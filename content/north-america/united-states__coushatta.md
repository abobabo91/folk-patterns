## Overview

The Coushatta are a Muskogean-speaking Native American people living primarily in Louisiana, Oklahoma, and Texas. Their earlier homelands were in Tennessee, Georgia, and Alabama, and they were closely allied and intermarried with the Alabama people. Coushatta communities are represented today by the Alabama-Quassarte Tribal Town in Wetumka, Oklahoma, the Coushatta Tribe of Louisiana, and the Alabama-Coushatta Tribe of Texas. The Koasati language belongs to the Apalachee-Alabama-Koasati branch of the Muskogean languages. Historically, Coushatta people farmed maize, beans, and squash, supplemented these foods through hunting and fishing, and became known for basketry. The sources also document language preservation, tribal self-government, and reservation-based farming and gaming.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Basketry** — The sources identify basketry as a skill for which the Coushatta are known.

## Foodways

Historically, the Coushatta were farmers who grew maize, beans, and squash. They supplemented their diet by hunting game and fish. In the twentieth century, Coushatta people in Louisiana began cultivating rice and crawfish on tribally owned farms on the reservation.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They do document language-preservation efforts: in 2007, the Coushatta Tribe of Louisiana, McNeese State University, and the College of William and Mary began the Koasati (Coushatta) Language Project as part of broader language revitalization efforts.

## Language & religion

Koasati, also called Coushatta, is a Native American language of Muskogean origin. It belongs to the Apalachee-Alabama-Koasati branch of the Muskogean languages and is related to Alabama and Mikasuki. The language is written in the Latin script. The sources describe a tribal orthographic system developed and approved by the Coushatta Tribe of Louisiana in 2007; long vowels are written by doubling the vowel, while nasalized vowels are underlined. Koasati is polysynthetic, generally follows subject–object–verb word order, and has singular, dual, and plural forms in its verbs.
