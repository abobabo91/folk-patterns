## Overview

The Klickitat lived north of the Columbia River in the Pacific Northwest. They traded between coastal peoples and communities east of the Cascade Mountains. Most Klickitat are now part of the Yakama Nation, while some are part of the Grand Ronde Community. The profile records basketry, painted hide objects, stone tools, villages, and language names, but gives little information about clothing, religion, music, or ceremonies.

## Material culture

The recorded material culture includes woven baskets, painted buffalo-hide and rawhide objects, stone arrowheads, a smoking pipe, and a deer-toe necklace.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The profile does not document named textile traditions, but it records basketry and painted geometric designs.

- **Cooking basket** — A woven osier-stem basket with leather straps was used for cooking.
- **Basketry bag** — A basketry and cord bag is recorded in the museum collection.
- **Water basket** — A basket made of osier is recorded as a water basket.
- **Parfleche envelope** — A buffalo-hide envelope has red, black, and faded blue diamond and triangular designs, with heavy hide fringes.
- **Parfleche wallet** — A rawhide wallet carries painted geometric designs in green, red, and black.

### Clothing & dress

The sources do not describe everyday or ceremonial clothing, but they record one dance necklace associated with a woman.

- **Woman’s dance necklace** — A woman’s necklace made of deer toes is described in the catalogue as a “brave’s” dance necklace.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Klickitat buildings, but they name several villages and their locations.

- **White Salmon Landing village** (*Itkilak; Ithlkilak*) — Itkilak, also called Ithlkilak, was at White Salmon Landing and was jointly occupied with the Chilluckquittequaw Tribe.
- **Underwood village** (*Nanshuit*) — Nanshuit was at Underwood and was jointly occupied with the Chilluckquittequaw Tribe.
- **Village below Memaloose Island** (*Shgwaliksh*) — Shgwaliksh was below Memaloose Island.
- **White Salmon Landing village** (*Tgasgutcu*) — Tgasgutcu was near White Salmon Landing and was jointly occupied with the Chilluckquittequaw Tribe.
- **Unknown-location village** (*Wiltkun*) — Wiltkun is named as a village whose exact location is unknown.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded everyday objects include stone arrowheads, a smoking pipe, and several kinds of baskets.

- **White stone arrowhead** — One arrowhead is flaked, white, triangular, and flat.
- **Light beige stone arrowhead** — Another arrowhead is flaked, flat, light beige, and nearly translucent.
- **Smoking pipe** — A smoking pipe was made of stone and white metal.
- **Basketry bag** — A basketry bag was made from basketry and cord.
- **Basket with thong** — A basket made of basketry and leather had a thong.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The only recorded adornment is a deer-toe necklace linked in the catalogue with a woman and with dance.

- **Woman’s dance necklace** — The necklace was made of deer toes, but the sources do not explain its meaning or use.

## Music & performance

The sources name no Klickitat instruments, songs, or musical traditions.

- **Welcoming chiefs** — Two chiefs welcomed Lewis and Clark when the expedition encountered the Klickitat in 1805, but no music or ceremony is described.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not document named dances or theatre forms, but one necklace is associated with dance.

- **“Brave’s” dance necklace** — A woman’s deer-toe necklace is described as a “brave’s” dance necklace, without details about the dance.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not describe Klickitat festivals or rituals, but they record major political events in 1855.

- **Yakima treaty** — The Klickitat joined the Yakima treaty at Camp Stevens on June 9, 1855, after the Klickitat War began.
- **Klickitat War** — The Klickitat War erupted in 1855, followed by the cession of Klickitat lands to the United States.

## Foodways

The Klickitat traded salmon, roots, and berries, and used recorded baskets connected with cooking and water.

- **Salmon trade** — The Klickitat traded salmon.
- **Roots trade** — The Klickitat traded roots.
- **Berries trade** — The Klickitat traded berries.
- **Cooking basket** — A woven osier-stem cooking basket had leather straps, but the sources do not say what it held.
- **Water basket** — A water basket made of osier is recorded without details about its use.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not present Klickitat stories, but they list a book of Klickitat legends and record traditional names.

- **Legends of the Klickitat** — Clarence Orvell Bunnell’s book presents a Klickitat version of the story of the Bridge of the Gods, but the profile does not summarize it.
- **Prairie people** (*Qwû'lh-hwai-pûm; χwálχwaypam; X̣ʷáɬx̣ʷaypam*) — These names are used by the Klickitat for themselves and mean “prairie people.”
- **Yakama name for the Klickitat** (*Xwálxwaypam; L'ataxat*) — These names were used by the Yakama for the Klickitat.

## Language & religion

The Klickitat were a Shahaptian people connected with Sahaptin, while the sources give no information about their religion.

- **Sahaptin language** (*Ichishkiin*) — Sahaptin, also called Ichishkiin, belongs to the Sahaptian branch of the Plateau Penutian family.
- **Traditional language name** (*Ichishkíin Sɨ́nwit*) — The Yakama Nation cultural resources program has promoted Ichishkíin Sɨ́nwit as the traditional name of the language.
- **Language dialect name** (*Ichishkíin*) — Ichishkíin is one of the dialect names given for Sahaptin.
- **Language dialect name** (*Ichishkínk*) — Ichishkínk is one of the dialect names given for Sahaptin.
- **Language dialect name** (*Chishkín; Čiškíin; Ičiškíin*) — Chishkín, Čiškíin, and Ičiškíin are also listed as dialect names.
