## Overview

The Osage Nation is a federally recognized tribe now based in Oklahoma whose ancestors lived in the Ohio and Mississippi river valleys before moving west in the 1600s. By the early 1800s they controlled a vast territory between the Missouri and Red rivers, living by hunting buffalo and farming corn and squash. Their culture centers on a cosmos divided between sky (masculine, creative) and earth (feminine, material), expressed through clan organization, ceremonial naming, and ritual leadership. Today about 25,000 people are enrolled, with ongoing efforts to preserve the Osage language and traditional knowledge.

## Material culture

Osage material culture is known chiefly for beadwork and fingerweaving in geometric and floral patterns, along with shell and animal-claw ornaments, and for ceremonial objects including pipes, bundles and rattles.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Osage weavers made narrow beaded bands and fingerwoven bags with geometric and floral patterns in wool and plant fibre.

- **Beaded bands and sashes** — Narrow bands worn at the waist and below the knee, made of beads on wool or cloth backings, with floral motifs and geometric patterns in black, blue, red, yellow, white and green.
- **Fingerwoven bags** — Carrying bags made from dyed wool and plant fibre, decorated with chevrons, hatched designs, and crossed diagonals, with braided fringes and beaded accents.

### Clothing & dress

Osage men wore blankets, cloth or fringed hide shirts, leggings, and various headgear, with heavy ornament at neck and ears.

- **Hide shirt** — Decorated with porcupine quills or fringes, worn over a blanket.
- **Cloth turban or headband** — Common headgear in nineteenth-century portrait photographs.
- **Embroidered skin leggings** — Worn with the blanket and shirt ensemble.

### Architecture

Osage villages were organized to express the tribe's division into sky people and earth people, with spiritual leaders' lodges placed between the two sides.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Osage ceremonial objects include pipes, bundles, rattles, and tomahawks; ceramics are not documented.

- **Dew claw strung rattle** — A ceremonial rattle combining dewclaws, leather, rawhide, brass, an eagle's claw, feathers, and a deer's tail.
- **Ceremonial pipe** — Used with tobacco as offerings in seeking Wakonda's aid.
- **Bundle** — A ritual apparatus used in ceremonies by medicine people and spiritual leaders.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Shell and animal claw dominate Osage jewelry; face painting marked tribe and clan identity, especially in death.

- **Bear-claw necklace** — Worn by men as a status ornament.
- **Shell necklace and choker** — Shell ornaments worn at the neck.
- **Shell ear ornaments** — Shell pieces worn in the ears.
- **Peace medal** — A trade and diplomatic ornament worn at the chest.
- **Face painting** — Stripes and motifs applied to the face to signal tribe and clan, especially used in death ceremonies.

## Music & performance

Osage ceremonial songs documented spiritual knowledge and were shared only among proven community members.

- **Ceremonial songs** — Songs taught by spiritual leaders and used in ceremonies for adoption, marriage, war, agriculture, and honoring the sun.

## Dance & theatre

Dance and dramatic traditions are not documented in the sources.

## Festivals & rituals

The Osage ritual year centers on clan responsibilities and cosmology, with ceremonial naming as the central life-cycle rite.

- **Ceremonial naming** — A child born into a clan was formally named to introduce them to the community; without a ceremonial name an Osage child could not take part in ceremonies.
- **Red Cedar clan** (*Hon-tse-shu-tsy*) — One of several clans named for elements of nature, each with its own responsibilities.
- **Travelers in the Mist clan** (*Moh-sho-tsa-moie*) — A clan named for a weather phenomenon.
- **Deer Lungs clan** (*Tah-lah-he*) — A clan named for an animal.
- **Elk clan** (*O-pon*) — A clan named for an animal.

## Foodways

The Osage depended on semi-annual buffalo hunts and on farming corn, squash and other vegetables near their villages.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Osage had no written language until recently; ceremonial songs recorded spiritual knowledge, and origin accounts told of westward migration from the Ohio valley.

- **Daposka Ahnkodapi** — The Nation's language immersion school, closed in 2026 for budget reasons.
- **Osage Language Program** — Created in 2003 to provide audio and video learning materials online.
- **Dhegiha Gathering** — In 2012, the 2nd Annual gathering brought Osage, Kaw, Quapaw, Ponca and Omaha speakers together for language revitalization.

## Language & religion

Osage is a Dhegihan language with a writing system created in 2006; traditional religion centers on Wakonda and the sky–earth division.

- **Wakonda** (*Wah'Kon-Tah*) — The supreme life force whose spirit is in everything from trees and sky to animals and humans.
- **Osage script** — A writing system created in 2006, one of the few developed for an Indigenous language in the United States.
- **Hominy dialect** — Distinguished by its treatment of /ð/ and pronunciation of šc as šč.

## Glossary

- *Dhegihan* — A branch of the Siouan language family, shared with Kansa, Ponca, Omaha and Quapaw
- *Wakonda* — The supreme life force in traditional Osage spirituality
- *Wah'Kon-Tah* — Alternative name for Wakonda
- *Sky people* — One half of the Osage tribe's social division
- *Earth people* — The other half of the Osage tribe's social division
- *Hon-tse-shu-tsy* — Red Cedar clan
- *Moh-sho-tsa-moie* — Travelers in the Mist clan
- *Tah-lah-he* — Deer Lungs clan
- *O-pon* — Elk clan
- *Ceremonial naming* — A child's formal introduction to the community and assignment of a spiritual name
- *Bundle* — A ritual apparatus used in Osage ceremonies
- *Dew claw strung rattle* — A ceremonial rattle made from animal parts and metal
- *Osage script* — A writing system created in 2006 for the Osage language
- *Daposka Ahnkodapi* — The Osage Nation's language immersion school
- *Osage Language Program* — A program created in 2003 to provide online language learning materials
- *Hominy dialect* — A regional variety of Osage distinguished by certain sound changes
