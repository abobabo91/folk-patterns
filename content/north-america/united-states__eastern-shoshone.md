## Overview

The Eastern Shoshone are Shoshone who primarily live in Wyoming and in the northeast corner of the Great Basin, where Utah, Idaho and Wyoming meet. They primarily settled on the Wind River Indian Reservation after Washakie signed the Fort Bridger Treaty in 1868. They lived in the Rocky Mountains during the 1805 Lewis and Clark Expedition and adopted Plains horse culture, unlike Western Shoshone communities that maintained a Great Basin culture. Their history includes bison hunting, the Rocky Mountain Fur Trade, bison-hide trade, negotiations at Fort Bridger, and relations with the Blackfoot, Crow, Bannock, Northern Arapaho, Utes, Flathead, Nez Perce, and others.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do describe trade fairs held at rendezvous sites along the Wind River Range, in areas previously used by the Shoshone for trade fairs.

## Foodways

The sources describe bison hunting as an important activity and identify several Shoshone bands by primary food sources. The *Kuccuntikka* or *Kuchun-deka*, also called *Guchundeka'*, *Kutsindüka*, or Buffalo Eaters, lived along the upper Green River Valley, Big Sandy River, and Wind River and across parts of Wyoming, Idaho, and Utah. The *Haivodika* or *Haiwodekanee*, called Dove Eaters, were described as middlemen who exchanged skins for agricultural products and textiles at Great Salt Lake. The *Tukkutikka*, also called *Tukudeka*, *Dukundeka'*, Sheep Eaters, or Mountain Sheep Eaters, lived in mountain regions of Wyoming, Idaho, and Montana. The *Boho'inee*, also called *Pohoko’ikkatee*, *Pohogwe*, *Pohoini*, Sage Grass people, or Sagebrush Butte People, lived in southeastern Idaho, along the Snake River Plain, and near Salmon Falls, and claimed the Camas Prairie as home.

## Language & religion

Eastern Shoshone speak the Shoshone language, a Central Numic language in the Uto-Aztecan language family. The language is spoken on the Wind River Indian Reservation.
