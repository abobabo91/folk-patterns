## Overview

The Miniconjou (*Mnikowoju*, *Hokwoju*, “Plants by the Water”) are a Native American people and a subdivision of the Lakota people. They formerly inhabited an area in western present-day South Dakota extending from the Black Hills to the Platte River. Their contemporary population lives mostly in west-central South Dakota. The sources used identify the Miniconjou as a Lakota subdivision and document historical bands, hereditary leaders, and war chiefs.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources state that recognized war chiefs wore shirts decorated with scalps.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources name *Inyan ha oin* (“Musselshell Earring”) and *Wagleza-oin* (“Gartersnake Earring”) as historic Miniconjou bands. The sources also state that war chiefs wore shirts decorated with scalps, but provide no further account of adornment.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that, prior to being confined to the reservation in the late 19th century, the Miniconjou recognized six hereditary leaders within their tribe, chosen from each clan.

## Foodways

The sources name the historic bands *Unkche yuta* (“Dung Eaters”), *Shunka Yute Shni* (“Eat No Dogs”), and *Nige Tanka* (“Big Belly”).

## Language & religion

The sources identify Lakota terms associated with the Miniconjou, including *Mnikowoju* and *Hokwoju*.
