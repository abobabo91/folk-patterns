## Overview

The Kickapoo are an Algonquian-speaking Native American tribe and Indigenous people whose communities are located in the United States and Mexico. In the United States, three federally recognized tribes are located in Kansas, Oklahoma, and Texas. The Kickapoo originated in the region south of the Great Lakes and were associated historically with the southern Wabash River, the Upper Iowa River region, northeast Iowa, southeast Minnesota, Illinois, Missouri, Kansas, and Texas. The Texas and Mexican branches form the cross-border nation called the Kickapoo of Coahuila/Texas. Around 3,000 people are enrolled tribal members according to the source, while the Kickapoo Tribe of Oklahoma has 2,719 enrolled members. Their language belongs to the Central Algonquian subgroup of the Algonquian languages.

## Music & performance

 The sources do identify Kickapoo whistled speech as a related topic and state that the Kickapoo in Mexico are known for their whistled speech. The language article also reports that texts, recordings, and a vocabulary of Kickapoo are available.

## Dance & theatre

 The sources mention the film *The Only Good Indian* (2009), directed by Greg Wilmott and starring Wes Studi, but describe it as a fictionalized account rather than a Kickapoo theatrical tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

 They do describe religious revivals among the Kansas Kickapoo during the 1820s and 1830s. Kennekuk combined Protestantism and Catholicism in his teaching and instructed his followers and white audiences to obey God’s commands.

## Foodways

 They state that, after relocation to Kansas, the Kickapoo focused more on farming so they could provide food for the rest of the tribe.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They describe Kennekuk as a prominent, nonviolent spiritual leader among the Kickapoo and identify him as the “Kickapoo Prophet.” He led followers during the Indian Removal in the 1830s to their current tribal lands in Kansas and died there of smallpox in 1852. The language article states that texts, recordings, and a vocabulary of Kickapoo are available. The Kickapoo Nation’s School in Horton, Kansas, began a language-immersion program in 1985 for elementary school grades, and language-education efforts continue at most Kickapoo sites.

## Language & religion

Kickapoo is an Algonquian language. It is described as either a dialect of the Fox language or a closely related language, mutually intelligible with the dialects spoken by the Sauk and Meskwaki. It belongs to the Central Algonquian subgroup of the Algonquian languages family, itself part of the Algic language family. The language’s endonym is *Metotheeneniaatoweeheni*. A Kickapoo alphabet was developed by Paul Voorhis in 1974 and revised in 1981; a new orthography is used by the Kickapoo Language Development Program in Oklahoma. The sources state that there are no children who are first-language users of Kickapoo, as they choose to speak English instead. Mexico’s Instituto Nacional de Antropología e Historia participated in developing a Kickapoo alphabet in 2010.

Kennekuk’s religious teaching combined Protestantism and Catholicism.
