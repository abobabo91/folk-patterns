## Overview

The Alsea are a Native American tribe of Western Oregon. They lived on the western coast of Oregon, around what is now Alsea Bay at the mouth of the Alsea River. Since 1856, they have been confederated with other Tribes on the Siletz Reservation, Oregon, and are members of the Confederated Tribes of Siletz. Their origin story says that the Yaquina, Alsea, Yachats, Lower Umpqua, and Siuslaw people are one tribe and speak the same language. The Alsean people practiced forehead flattening until about 1860. Their subsistence included seal and sea-lion hunting, salmon fishing, and gathering camas roots. Their language was closely related to Yaquina and was almost extinct by 1910.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources name clothing and basketry materials.

**Basketry** — The Alsea wove baskets.

**Grass raincoats** — The Alsea made raincoats from grass.

**Seal-skin robes** — The Alsea wore robes of seal skin.

### Clothing & dress

The Alsea wore robes of seal skin and made grass raincoats. The sources do not distinguish everyday and ceremonial clothing, or document separate men’s and women’s garments.

### Architecture

They record villages located along the Alsea River and its tributaries and around the river’s mouth.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They record baskets, canoes used in funerary practice, seal-skin robes, and grass raincoats, without describing their manufacture in detail.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The Alsean people practiced forehead flattening by slight pressure applied in a baby’s cradleboard until about 1860.

## Music & performance

Alsea shamans promoted good salmon runs, and the Alsea appealed to animal spirits and powers in nature for aid.

## Festivals & rituals

Very little is known about Alsea religion. It is thought to be similar to that of the Coos. Alsea shamans promoted good salmon runs, and the Alsea appealed to animal spirits and powers in nature for aid. The Alsea placed their dead in canoes on isolated land jutting into estuaries.

## Foodways

The Alsea hunted seals and sea lions, fished for salmon, and gathered camas roots.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Alsea origin story says that the Yaquina, Alsea, Yachats, Lower Umpqua, and Siuslaw people are all one tribe and speak the same language. Leo J. Frachtenberg wrote “Myths of the Alsea Indians of Northwestern Oregon.”

## Language & religion

Alsea was an Alsean language very closely related to Yaquina. Alsea and Yaquina were two closely related speech varieties spoken along the central Oregon coast until the early 1950s; both are now extinct. Alsea was last recorded in 1942 from the last speaker, John Albert, and Albert died in 1951. Yaquina was last recorded in 1884 by James Owen Dorsey. Alsea is commonly held to be part of a language isolate and is also usually considered to belong to the Penutian phylum. They state that very little is known about Alsea religion and that it is thought to be similar to that of the Coos.
