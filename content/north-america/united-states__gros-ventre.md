## Overview

The Gros Ventre, also known as the A'aninin, Atsina, or White Clay, are an historically Algonquian-speaking Native American people located in northcentral Montana. Today they are enrolled in the Fort Belknap Indian Community of the Fort Belknap Reservation of Montana, alongside the Assiniboine people. The community has 7,000 members. Their ancestors lived in the western Great Lakes region approximately 3,000 years ago, where they cultivated maize, and later lived along the Red River Valley in present-day Minnesota and North Dakota. They migrated toward the upper Missouri River Valley in the early 18th century. Their language, Gros Ventre or Atsina, is a nearly extinct Plains Algonquian language grouped with Arapaho as Arapaho-Atsina. Their cultural record includes oral history, sacred pipes, ceremonial suppression and revitalization, and historical relationships with the Arapaho, Cheyenne, Blackfoot, Crow, Assiniboine, Sioux, Ojibwe, Cree, and others.

### Architecture

The sources describe several buildings connected with Gros Ventre history. Fort Browning was established in 1868 near the mouth of Peoples Creek on the Milk River as a trading post for the Gros Ventres and Assiniboines. Fort Belknap was later built on the south side of the Milk River, about one mile southwest of the present town site of Harlem, Montana; it included a trading post, a block house, a warehouse, and an issue building. St. Paul's Mission was established in 1887 at the foot of the Little Rocky Mountains near Hays.

## Music & performance

The sources provide limited information about Gros Ventre performance. They identify an Owl Dance Song associated with George Chandler and mention Jamie Fox, a Métis fiddler, among notable Gros Ventre people.

The sources do record that the Gros Ventre maintained sacred pipes, the Feathered Pipe and the Flat Pipe, as central elements of traditional spiritual beliefs. The mission established at Fort Belknap repressed traditional ceremonies and culture.

## Festivals & rituals

 They do record that Jesuits came to Fort Belknap in 1862 to convert the Gros Ventre people to Roman Catholicism and that St. Paul's Mission was established in 1887. The mission repressed traditional ceremonies and culture.

Two sacred pipes, the Feathered Pipe and the Flat Pipe, remained central to traditional spiritual beliefs.

## Foodways

The sources state that the Gros Ventre lived an agrarian lifestyle in the western Great Lakes region approximately 3,000 years ago and cultivated maize.

Historical accounts also describe the importance of hunting territories. In 1855, a territory near the Three Fork area became a common hunting ground for the Gros Ventre and the Blackfoot, while a common hunting ground north of the Missouri River on the Fort Peck Indian Reservation included the Assiniboine and Sioux.

## Oral tradition & literature

In Ojibwa oral history, the Gros Ventre are known as the “men of the olden time” who occupied lands surrounding the head waters of the Mississippi River. The sources also state that the Gros Ventre formed a single Algonquian-speaking people with the ancestors of the Arapaho and were closely associated with the ancestors of the Cheyenne. They do record language-preservation work. Theresa Lamebull taught the language at Fort Belknap College, now Aaniiih Nakoda College, and helped develop a dictionary using the Phraselator. The White Clay Immersion School at Aaniiih Nakoda College was teaching the language to 26 students in 2012, compared with 11 students in 2006.

## Language & religion

Gros Ventre, or Atsina, is the ancestral language of the Gros Ventre people of what is today Montana. It is also known as Aaniiih, Ananin, Ahahnelin, Ahe, A’ani, and ʔɔʔɔɔɔniiih. Atsina and Arapaho are dialects of a common language usually designated Arapaho-Atsina; specialists have also used the designation Arapaho-Atsina-Nawathinehena. Historically, the language had five dialects. The last fluent speaker, Theresa Lamebull, died in 2007, while revitalization efforts continue. They state that Jesuits sought to convert the Gros Ventre to Roman Catholicism and that a mission repressed traditional ceremonies and culture. The Feathered Pipe and the Flat Pipe remain central to traditional spiritual beliefs.
