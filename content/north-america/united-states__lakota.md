## Overview

The Lakota are a Sioux people of the northern Great Plains. Their history is linked with the Missouri River, the Black Hills, horse-mounted buffalo hunting, winter counts, and treaty diplomacy. Lakȟótiyapi has about 2,000 speakers and is being taught through immersion programs. Lakota communities practice traditional religion, Christianity, or both.

## Material culture

Lakota material culture includes hide clothing, beadwork, porcupine quills, feathers, pipes, tobacco pouches, weapons, bundles, and sacred stones.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Lakota textiles and clothing use hide, beads, porcupine quills, pigments, fringe, and geometric designs.

- **Tobacco pouch** — A Lakota pipe bag from about 1870–1880 used hide, glass beads, porcupine quills, pigments, painted geometric designs, and fringe.
- **Warrior’s shirt** — A Sioux warrior’s shirt from the Yellowstone River area was linked to a chief’s garment during the war of 1867.
- **Beaded moccasins** — Lakota or Sioux moccasins were embroidered with beads and commonly had hard rawhide soles.
- **Deerskin war shirt** — A man’s shirt made from brain-tanned deer skin followed a poncho form and had porcupine quills and long fringe.
- **Sacred bundles** (*wašicu*) — Ceremonial or medicine bundles contain objects seen as material forms of sacred power.

### Clothing & dress

Lakota clothing used deer skin, hide, cloth, beads, quills, metal ornaments, feathers, and ribbons.

- **War bonnet** — An Oglala man was recorded wearing a war bonnet with a decorated scalp shirt, leggings, and moccasins.
- **Leggings** — Lakota clothing records include leggings, including leggings with embroidered flaps.
- **Decorated shirts** — Museum records describe decorated shirts made with materials such as hide, beads, quills, metal ornaments, and feathers.
- **Women’s decorated clothing** — Records show women wearing decorated clothing with shawls, necklaces, bangles, and finger rings.

### Architecture

Lakota seasonal life included winter camps, tipis, dispersed bands, and a large summer gathering.

- **Tipi** — Tipis were used in Lakota camps.
- **Tipi circle** (*cʽągléška wakʽą*) — The sacred hoop was a camp circle formed by a ring of tipis and symbolized harmony and connected life.
- **Winter camp** — Lakota seasonal life included sheltered winter camps.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources name pipes, tobacco pouches, weapons, breast plates, medicine bundles, and sacred stones but give no general account of pottery.

- **Bone breast plate** — Breast plates used bone tube beads, glass and metal beads, brass beads, leather, eagle claws, and old United States one-cent coins.
- **Pipe** — Pipes were used in Lakota smoking rituals and prayers.
- **Tobacco pouch** — Tobacco pouches are recorded among Lakota everyday and ritual objects.
- **Sacred stone bundle** (*wašícų tʻųká*) — A bundle could hold stones believed to be animate and able to possess a šicų spirit.
- **War bundle** — War bundles are mentioned among Lakota objects.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Lakota adornment included shell, metal, bone, feathers, ribbons, hair ornaments, and finger rings.

- **Dentalium and abalone ear ornaments** — A British Museum portrait records dentalium and abalone ornaments worn on the ears.
- **Shell choker** — Shell chokers are recorded as Lakota adornment.
- **Hair-plate ornament** — Metal hair-plate ornaments were worn as body adornment.
- **Eagle feather** — An eagle feather was worn in the hair in a recorded portrait.
- **Tattoos for the Sky-Road** — Some afterlife traditions say an old woman admits only people with proper tattoos to the Sky-Road.

## Music & performance

Lakota music centers on the human voice with drumming, repeated phrases, vocables, and honor beats.

- **Lakota Flag Song** — The Lakota Flag Song begins powwows and other special events and is not danced.
- **Dance song** — A traditional dance song may begin high, move lower, repeat its phrase, and include four honor beats.
- **Vocables** — Many songs use syllables without lexical meaning.
- **Lakota lullaby** — A Lakota lullaby is described as a calming hum.
- **Yuwipi song** (*Yuwipi*) — Yuwipi is one of the named Lakota song types.

## Dance & theatre

The sources describe the Ghost Dance and ritual dances but do not name theatrical forms or detailed secular dances.

- **Ghost Dance** — The Ghost Dance spread across reservations in 1890 and promised the return of the old world and the disappearance of white settlers.
- **Sun dance** — The sun dance is an important Lakota ritual tradition.
- **Vision quest** — The vision quest is an important Lakota ritual tradition.
- **Sweat lodge ceremony** — The sweat lodge is used for purification.
- **Yuwipi** — Yuwipi is an important ritual tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

Lakota ritual life includes seven ceremonies, sacred pipes, offerings, seasonal gatherings, and healing rites.

- **Seven ceremonies** — Lakota traditions say White Buffalo Calf Woman gave seven ceremonies.
- **Sacred pipe prayer** — Prayers may be supported by smoking a sacred pipe or making offerings of cotton flags or tobacco.
- **Ritual specialist** (*wičháša wakhá*) — A wičháša wakhá is a ritual specialist.
- **Yuwipi healing rite** (*yuwípi wičháša*) — A yuwípi wičháša performs Yuwipi, which usually calls on spirits for healing.
- **Summer gathering** — A great summer gathering brought people together for ceremony and communal hunting.

## Foodways

Buffalo supplied meat, hide, clothing, and shelter, and hunting and butchery had ceremonial elements.

- **Buffalo hunting** — Lakota life was closely connected to buffalo hunting.
- **Horse-mounted buffalo hunt** — Horse-mounted buffalo hunting developed after horses were obtained through trade with the Cheyenne along the Missouri River.
- **Buffalo butchery** — Traditional buffalo butchery included ceremonial elements.
- **Tobacco offering** — Tobacco was used as an offering and in smoking rituals.

## Oral tradition & literature

Lakota history and sacred stories were preserved in winter counts, origin traditions, and stories of powerful beings.

- **Winter count** (*waníyetu wówapi*) — Winter counts are pictorial calendars painted on hides or later recorded on paper.
- **Battiste Good winter count** — The Battiste Good winter count records Lakota history to 900 CE and links that time with White Buffalo Calf Woman giving the White Buffalo Calf Pipe.
- **Iktomi** — Iktomi is a trickster spider god.
- **Unhcegila** — Unhcegila is a serpent monster in Lakota mythology.
- **Wakinyan** — Wakinyan is the Thunderbird.

## Language & religion

Lakȟótiyapi is a Siouan language, while Lakota religion centers on sacred power, spirits, ceremonies, and internal variation.

- **Lakota language** (*Lakȟótiyapi*) — Lakȟótiyapi is mutually intelligible with the two Dakota dialects and has about 2,000 speakers.
- **Sacred power** (*wakʽą*) — Wakʽą is an energy or power that permeates the universe.
- **Great Mystery** (*Wakʽą Tʽąką*) — Wakʽą Tʽąką is the unified totality of sacred power and is translated as the Great Mystery.
- **Sacred spirits** (*wakʽąpi*) — Wakʽąpi are spirits in Lakota traditions.
- **Sacred pipe** — Sacred pipes are part of Lakota religious traditions.
