## Overview

The Quinault are a group of Native American peoples from western Washington in the United States. They are a Southwestern Coast Salish people and are enrolled in the federally recognized Quinault Indian Nation. Their traditional name, *kʷínayɬ*, was the name of a village at the mouth of the Quinault River, today called Taholah. The Quinault Indian Reservation lies on the Pacific coast, primarily in northwestern Grays Harbor County, with small parts extending into southwestern Jefferson County. Its reported resident population was 1,370 persons in the 2000 census. Quinault culture is documented here through basketry, weaving, longhouse housing, and efforts to revitalize the Quinault language.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Basketry and weaving** — Quinault basket makers and weavers used locally available reeds and grasses, spruce, maple, and red cedar. Baskets were made in styles suited to particular tasks. Burden baskets used for gathering oysters and other shellfish had an open weave for drainage and were made from water-resistant materials such as cedar bark. Archaeology at the Ozette Indian Village Archeological Site preserved baskets and blankets in a mudslide. Quinault basket artifacts are held in museums in the Northwest and around the world. Traditional basketmaking techniques have been subject to preservation efforts on the Quinault reservation, although the style has been intermixed with that of other tribes.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no motifs.

### Architecture

Traditionally, Quinault people lived in longhouses.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that the Quinault people settled onto reservation lands after signing the Quinault Treaty with the former Washington Territory in 1856.

## Foodways

They state that burden baskets were used for gathering oysters and other shellfish, but provide no further account of Quinault foodways.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do document efforts to revitalize the Quinault language, including the use of community classrooms described in a cited doctoral thesis.

## Language & religion

Quinault, also written *Kʷínaył*, belongs to the Tsamosan, or Olympic, branch of the Coast Salish family of Salishan languages. It is extinct, but efforts are being made to revitalize it. The sources describe its phonology, including the sounds /w, j, l/, the possible pronunciation of /xʷ/ as [ʍ], and vowel distinctions represented by /i, ɛ, ə, a, ɔ, u/ and /iː, uː, aː/.
