## Overview

The Kalaallit are an ethnographic group concentrated in western Greenland and the largest such group there. The term is also used in Greenlandic for the Greenlandic Inuit of Kalaallit Nunaat. The Kalaallit are Inuit and part of the circumpolar peoples of the Arctic. Their language is known as Kalaallisut, or Greenlandic in English. About 80% to 88% of Greenland’s population, approximately 44,000 to 50,000 people, identify as Inuit. Kalaallit are descended from the Thule people but probably not from their predecessors in Greenland, the Dorset culture. The group matters in folk-culture terms through its artistic traditions, including sewing animal skins, making masks, tupilaq figures, and carving sperm whale ivory.

## Language & religion

The language spoken by Inuit in Greenland is known as Kalaallisut and in English as Greenlandic. Northern and Eastern Greenlanders call themselves Inughuit and Tunumiit, respectively, while the Kalaallit are concentrated in the west.
