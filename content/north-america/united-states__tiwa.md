## Overview

The Tiwa, less commonly called Tigua, are related Tanoan Pueblo peoples in New Mexico. They traditionally speak Tiwa, a Tanoan language, although some speakers have shifted to Spanish and/or English. The people are divided into Northern Tiwa groups at Taos and Picuris and Southern Tiwa communities at Isleta and Sandia, around what is now Albuquerque, and at Ysleta del Sur near El Paso, Texas. Southern Tiwa is spoken in Isleta Pueblo, Sandia Pueblo, and Ysleta del Sur Pueblo, while Northern Tiwa includes Taos and Picuris. The Tiwa are historically documented in the Rio Grande region and were involved in the history of Tiguex and the Pueblo Revolt.

## Oral tradition & literature

The supplied material does not describe Tiwa folktales, epic poetry, proverbs, riddles, or Indigenous storytelling genres. It mentions several works concerning Tiwa or Isleta contexts. *The Padre of Isleta: The Story of Father Anton Docher* describes the everyday life of Tiwas Indians of Isleta Pueblo during the end of the 19th century. Samuel Gance’s *Anton ou la trajectoire d'un père* is another story of Isleta’s Padre Anton Docher. Cormac McCarthy’s *Blood Meridian* includes a band of peaceful Tiwa called Tigua and refers to a period around 1849–50; the supplied material presents this as a literary depiction rather than as Tiwa oral tradition.

## Language & religion

Tiwa is a Tanoan language with Northern and Southern dialects. The Northern dialect is spoken at Taos Pueblo and Picuris Pueblo, while the Southern dialect is spoken at Sandia Pueblo, Isleta Pueblo, and Ysleta del Sur Pueblo. Northern and Southern Tiwa are no longer mutually intelligible, although Tiwa people continue to regard the language as unified. The Southern Tiwa language is spoken by around 1,600 people in the three Southern Tiwa pueblos named above. Taos is spoken by 800 people in Taos Pueblo and Picuris is spoken by around 220 people in Picuris Pueblo. The extinct dialect of Piro Pueblo was a southern Tiwan dialect similar to the dialect of Isleta, although it is not known whether the Piro Pueblo language was Tiwa as well.

Both dialects are said to originate from Taos Pueblo. After the Pueblo Revolt against the Spanish conquistadors in 1680, some Tigua and Piro peoples fled south with the Spanish to El Paso del Norte, identified in the source as present-day Ciudad Juárez, Mexico. They founded Ysleta del Sur and Socorro in Texas and Senecú del Sur in Mexico. Spanish or Comanche was used as a lingua franca when Northern and Southern Tiwa people interacted. The sources state that most Indigenous Taos continue to practice their ancient religion.
