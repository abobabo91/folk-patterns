## Overview

The T'satsąot'ınę, identified in the source as the Yellowknives, Yellow Knives, Copper Indians, Red Knives, or T'atsaot'ine, are Indigenous peoples of Canada and one of the five main groups of the First Nations Dene who live in the Northwest Territories. The historic Yellowknives lived north and northeast of Great Slave Lake around the Yellowknife River and Yellowknife Bay, northward along the Coppermine River, northeast to the Back River, and east to the Thelon River. Their name derives from the colour of tools made from copper deposits.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention cabins, a schoolhouse, villages, and a community. Chief Snuff had a cabin beside the water near Rocher River, and the Dene village of Ndilǫ was developed in the mid 1950s on the tip of Latham Island.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Yellowknives are associated in the source with copper tools. The historic Yellowknives helped mine and trade copper deposits for tools, and the name Copper Indians refers to this activity.

## Festivals & rituals

They do record political and historical events including the signing of Treaty 8 by Chief Snuff in 1899 and the formation of the Yellowknives Dene First Nation in 1991.

## Foodways

The source states that the name *Lutselk'e* means “place of the Lutsel,” referring to the cisco, a type of small fish.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention encounters with Samuel Hearne in the 1770s and describe the legendary copper deposits that the Yellowknives helped search for.

## Language & religion

The source identifies the T'atsaot'ine as a Dene people and names Wíílíídeh as a dialect spoken in Dettah and Ndilǫ. Wíílíídeh developed from intermarriage between Yellowknives and Tłı̨chǫ peoples. The source gives Tetsǫ́t'ınę as the Wíílíídeh form of the name and provides several other Indigenous place names, including Tinde'e, Wíílíídeh cho, and Thlewechodyeth.
