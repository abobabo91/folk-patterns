## Overview

The Coree were a small Native American tribe who occupied a coastal area south of the Neuse River in southeastern North Carolina, in the area now covered by Carteret and Craven counties. English colonists did not describe them until 1701, when their population had already declined to as few as 125 members, probably because of infectious-disease epidemics and warfare. In the early 18th century, the Coree allied with the Tuscaroras and others against the colonists. In 1711, they participated in the Tuscarora War and the Coree War. After the alliance failed, surviving Coree were merged into the Machapunga by 1715 and later adopted by the Tuscaroras. Their language remains uncertain: reports describe it as unlike Carolina Algonquian, Iroquoian Tuscarora, and Cawtaban Woccon.

The sources used describe warfare, alliance, migration, absorption, and adoption.

## Language & religion

The Coree language is uncertain. The ethnographer James Mooney speculated that the Coree were related to the Iroquoian-speaking Cherokee, but the source says that he had no convincing evidence. Limited colonial reports describe a language that did not appear to be mutually intelligible with Carolina Algonquian, Iroquoian Tuscarora, or Cawtaban Woccon, possibly Waccamaw. John Lawson recorded that, while living with the Coree for 2 years, he spoke with a woman from “beyond the mountains” whose language he considered completely mutually intelligible with Coree apart from a few loan words.
