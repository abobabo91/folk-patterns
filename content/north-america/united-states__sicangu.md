## Overview

The Sicangu, also spelled Siċaƞġu, Sičháŋǧu, or Sičhą́ǧu, are one of the seven oyates, nations, or council fires of the Lakota people, an Indigenous people of the Northern Plains. Many Sicangu live on the Rosebud Indian Reservation in southwestern South Dakota and are enrolled in the federally recognized Rosebud Sioux Tribe, also known in Lakȟóta as the Sičhą́ǧu Oyáte. Other Sicangu live on the Lower Brule Indian Reservation, on the west bank of the Missouri River in central South Dakota, and on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation.

## Music & performance

The sources identify several Sicangu performers and cultural figures, including Paul Eagle Star, a performer with Buffalo Bill’s Wild West Show; Eddie Spears and Michael Spears, both actors; and Frank Waln, a rapper. They also identify Leonard Crow Dog as a spiritual leader and American Indian Movement activist.

## Dance & theatre

The sources state that Arnold Short Bull, described as a Sicangu holy man, brought the Ghost Dance to the Lakota in South Dakota in 1890.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources mention the Ghost Dance in connection with Arnold Short Bull, a Sicangu holy man who brought it to the Lakota in South Dakota in 1890. They also state that a possible origin of the name Sičhą́ǧu concerned an incident in which people fleeing through a grass fire on the plains crossed the Coteau des Prairies while migrating from the Mississippi to the Missouri in 1762–1763, according to Brown Hat’s winter count.

## Foodways

The sources document three ethnobotanical practices. The Sicangu give pulverized roots of green comet milkweed to children with diarrhea, and nursing mothers take an infusion of the whole plant to increase their milk flow. They brew the leaves of prairie redroot into a tea.

## Oral tradition & literature

Brown Hat’s winter count is cited in the account of the possible origin of the name Sičhą́ǧu and the migration from the Mississippi to the Missouri in 1762–1763. The sources also identify Mary Brave Bird as an author, Nellie Star Boy Menard as a quiltmaker, Moses Stranger Horse as an artist, Albert White Hat as a Lakota language teacher, and Rosebud Yellow Robe as a folklorist, educator, and author.

## Language & religion

The Sicangu Lakota are known as Sičhą́ǧu Oyáte in Lakȟóta, translated in the source as “Burnt Thighs Nation.” The term is described as a compound word of the Thítȟųwą Lakȟóta dialect meaning “burned thigh.” The source records several spellings, including Siċaƞġu, Siċaŋġu, and Sičháŋǧu, and discusses the orthographies associated with the Rosebud Sioux Tribe, Albert White Hat, and the Lakota Language Consortium. The sources identify Leonard Crow Dog as a spiritual leader and Arnold Short Bull as a holy man.
