## Overview

The Western Shoshone comprise several Shoshone tribes indigenous to the Great Basin. Their lands are identified in the Treaty of Ruby Valley of 1863, and they resided in Idaho, Nevada, California, and Utah. They are closely related culturally to the Paiute, Goshute, Bannock, Ute, and Timbisha tribes. They speak the Western dialect of the Shoshone language. Other Shoshone-speaking groups named in the source are the Goshute, Northern Shoshone, and Eastern Shoshone.

## Festivals & rituals

The source does record political and legal activity connected with the protection of Western Shoshone property, including opposition to nuclear testing within their country and injunctions against gold mining that would result in dewatering of Mount Tenabo, Nevada.

The Western Shoshone have been involved in legal battles with the federal government over rights to their land since an erroneous filing of a claim in 1951. In 1863, the Treaty of Ruby Valley was entered into with the Western Bands of the Shoshone Nation and identified the boundaries of their 40,000 mi2 territory. The treaty provided for payment of $5,000 per year for 20 years. The Western Shoshone Claims Distribution Act of 2004 made a payment of $160 million to the Great Basin tribe for the perceived acquisition of 39,000 mi2. The source states that the tribes wanted the United States to abide by the 1863 treaty.

## Foodways

The names of several Western Shoshone bands refer to their traditional geographical homelands and primary food sources. These include *Kuyatikka* or Kuyudikka, “Bitterroot Eaters”; *Mahaguadüka*, “Mentzelia Seed Eaters”; *Painkwitikka* or Penkwitikka, “Fish Eaters”; *Tipatikka*, also recorded as Tepattekka'a, Tetadeka, Pinenut Eaters, and Bia Tevateka; *Tsaiduka*, “Tule Eaters”; *Waitikka*, “Ricegrass Eaters”; *Watatikka*, “Ryegrass Seed Eaters”; and *Wiyimpihtikka*, “Buffalo Berry Eaters.”

The source associates these names with places including Halleck, Mary's River, Clover Valley, Smith Creek Valley, Reese River, Ruby Valley, Cache Valley, Railroad Valley, Ione Valley, Big Smoky Valley, Battle Mountain, Elko County, Nevada, and northern Utah.

## Language & religion

The Western Shoshone speak the Western dialect of the Shoshone language. The sources also name the Goshute, Northern Shoshone, and Eastern Shoshone as other Shoshone-speaking groups.
