## Overview

The Nisqually are a Lushootseed-speaking Native American tribe and a Southern Coast Salish people in western Washington state in the United States. The tribe lives in the Nisqually River valley near the river delta, on the Nisqually Indian Reservation in western Pierce County and eastern Thurston County. The reservation comprises 20.602 km2 of land on both sides of the river. In the 2000 census, its resident population was 588 persons, while in 2005 the tribe had a service-area population of 5,719 Native Americans, including 600 people living on the reservation. The Nisqually originally inhabited the interior woodlands and coastal waters from Mount Rainier west to Puget Sound. Their culture has been closely connected with fishing, especially salmon, which has been described as both a dietary mainstay and a foundation of Nisqually culture.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They identify settlements at Skate Creek, near the Mashel River, and at Kalama.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They state that the Nisqually operated fish hatcheries on Clear Creek and Kalama Creek.

## Music & performance

The sources do state that Lushootseed has been spoken during the annual Tribal Canoe Journeys throughout the Salish Sea.

## Festivals & rituals

They record several political and historical events: the Medicine Creek Treaty was signed in late 1854; the original reservation was established by the Medicine Creek Treaty of December 26, 1854; an executive order enlarged the reservation on January 20, 1856; and the tribe’s constitution and bylaws were approved on September 9, 1946 and amended in 1994. These are historical and governmental events rather than descriptions of ritual practice.

## Foodways

Fish, both fresh and smoked, is an important staple in Nisqually cuisine, especially salmon. The sources also name cod, eulachon, halibut, herring, sturgeon, and trout. Shellfish, deer, elk, and sea mammals were traditionally hunted for food. Camas, wild berries, crab apples, and other wild plants were traditionally gathered. The sources emphasize that salmon was not only a mainstay of the diet but also the foundation of Nisqually culture.

## Oral tradition & literature

According to legend, the Squalli-absch, described as ancestors of the modern Nisqually Indian Tribe, came north from the Great Basin, crossed the Cascade Mountain Range, and established their first village in a basin then known as Skate Creek, outside the southern boundary of the Nisqually River Watershed. A later major village was located near the Mashel River. They do document Lushootseed preservation and revival efforts, including language classes, immersion programs, instructional materials, a dictionary, readers, and research involving Vi Hilbert, who was described as the last speaker with a full native command of Lushootseed.

## Language & religion

The Nisqually speak a subdialect of the southern dialect of Lushootseed, called Twulshootseed, a Coast Salish language. Lushootseed belongs to the Central Coast Salish subgroup of the Salishan language family and has two main dialects, Northern Lushootseed and Southern Lushootseed, which are divided into smaller subdialects. The Nisqually subdialect is included within Southern Lushootseed. In Lushootseed, the Nisqually name is *dxʷsqʷaliʔabš*, meaning “people of the grass.” The language has historically been known by names including Puget Salish, Puget Sound Salish, Skagit-Nisqually, Niskwalli/Nisqually, Pugué, Squaxon, and Skagit-Nisqually. Lushootseed is now primarily a ceremonial language, with language revitalization programs and classes offered across the region.
