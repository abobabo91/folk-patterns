## Overview

The Mattaponi are an Indigenous people of Virginia whose reservation lies in King William County along the Mattaponi River, near West Point. The Mattaponi Indian Tribe has held reservation land since the colonial era and maintains a traditional government, the Mattaponi Tribal Council. The Mattaponi were one of the tribes associated with the Powhatan Confederacy and spoke an Algonquian language. Their history includes towns and settlements along the Mattaponi *yeokanta*, treaties with English colonial authorities, annual tribute payments, and continued defense of their land and political identity. A separate Upper Mattaponi Tribe, organized in 1921, lives near the upper reaches of the river in the settlement recorded as Adamstown.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The Mattaponi reservation is described as land held in common and assigned in plots for members’ use. The sources mention the Indian View Baptist Church, built by the Upper Mattaponi in 1942, and the Sharon Indian School, whose original one-room building was constructed in 1917. The school was replaced by an eight-room structure in 1952, closed in the 1960s, and returned to tribal jurisdiction and use in 1987 as a community center.

## Music & performance

The sources state that the Mattaponi spoke an Algonquian language and preserve Mattaponi and Pamunkey oral history concerning Pocahontas.

## Festivals & rituals

The Mattaponi maintain an annual tribute payment to the Governor of the Commonwealth of Virginia under the Treaty of Middle Plantation of 1677 and the treaties of 1646 and 1677. The sources describe this as a continuing political and historical obligation. The sources also mention the Indian View Baptist Church as the heart of the Upper Mattaponi faith community.

## Foodways

The treaties required an annual tribute payment of fish and game.

## Oral tradition & literature

Mattaponi and Pamunkey oral history states that Pocahontas was half Mattaponi through her mother and half Pamunkey through her father, Wahunsenacawh. The sources also state that Mattaponi and Pamunkey communities preserved this account. No other Mattaponi folktales, epic poetry, proverbs, riddles, written literature, or contemporary literary preservation projects are described.

## Language & religion

The Mattaponi spoke an Algonquian language, like other members of the Powhatan Confederacy. The Upper Mattaponi Indian View Baptist Church, built in 1942, is identified as the heart of their faith community.
