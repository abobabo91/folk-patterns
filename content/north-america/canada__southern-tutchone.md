## Overview

The Southern Tutchone are a First Nations people of the Athabaskan-speaking ethnolinguistic group living mainly in southern Yukon, Canada. Their communities and First Nations governments include Champagne and Aishihik First Nations, the Ta'an Kwach'an Council, Kluane First Nation, and Kwanlin Dün First Nation. Southern Tutchone communities named in the language record include Aishihik, Burwash Landing, Champagne, Haines Junction, Kloo Lake, Klukshu, Lake Laberge, and Whitehorse. The Southern Tutchone language is part of the Athabaskan language family, although Northern and Southern Tutchone are sometimes considered separate languages. The sources document language endangerment, literacy work, oral-history projects, immersion programs, school teaching, dance festivals, and other revitalization efforts.

## Music & performance

The sources document stories and songs among the materials recorded by the Yukon Native Language Center, as well as language lessons and multimedia learning resources. In 2015, Champagne and Aishihik First Nations launched the first *Da Ku Nän Ts'tthèt* (“Our House is Waking Up the Land”) dance festival, which focused on Southern Tutchone language and culture.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources identify the *Da Ku Nän Ts'tthèt* (“Our House is Waking Up the Land”) dance festival, launched in 2015 by Champagne and Aishihik First Nations and hosted at the cultural centre named *Da Ku*. The sources also record language and cultural camps, workshops, conferences, school programs, immersion programs, and community collaborations. The Southern Tutchone Tribal Council held its first language conference, *Kakwaddhin – Marking the Trail*, in 1996, to review language programs and draft long- and short-term strategic plans. The Kluane First Nations participated in “Working Together to Pass it on” sessions in 1995 and 1996, and a “Following your Grandfather trail” camp took place in Klukshu in 1997.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Yukon Native Language Center describes Southern Tutchone documentation beginning in the 1970s, including stories and songs, a basic noun dictionary, and language lessons. In 1994, the Ta'an Kwach'an Council took part in an oral-history and language-preservation project in which traditions, genealogy, and other information were documented. The sources also mention the Southern Tutchone textbook and audio made by Margaret Workman, a native Southern Tutchone speaker. In 2019, Cole Pauls published the science-fiction comic-book *Dakwäkãda Warriors*, which contains one hundred and ten Southern Tutchone words and phrases.

## Language & religion

Southern Tutchone is an Athabaskan language spoken in Yukon communities including Aishihik, Burwash Landing, Champagne, Haines Junction, Kloo Lake, Klukshu, Lake Laberge, and Whitehorse. The language is also called *Dän kʼe*, meaning “our way,” or *Dän k'e kwänje*, meaning “our way of speaking.” The sources identify the Aisihilik, Tàaʼan, Klukshu, and Kluane dialects. Northern and Southern Tutchone are sometimes considered separate languages, although speakers are generally able to understand one another with moderate difficulty. The language is endangered: the sources record fewer than one hundred speakers in one account, one hundred and forty speakers in a 2011 census, and four hundred and four first-language speakers and six hundred and forty-five people with some knowledge in 2004.
