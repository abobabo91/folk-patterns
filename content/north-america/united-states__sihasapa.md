## Overview

The Sihásapa, also called the Blackfoot Sioux, are a division of the Lakota people, Titonwan, or Teton. *Sihásapa* is the Lakota word for “Blackfoot.” They lived in the western Dakotas on the Great Plains and are consequently among the Plains Indians. Their official residence today is the Standing Rock Reservation in North and South Dakota and the Cheyenne River Reservation in South Dakota.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They record only the band name *Kangi-shun Pegnake* or *Kanxicu pegnake*, glossed as “Crow Feather Hair Ornaments” or “Wear raven feathers in their hair,” and *Wamnuga Owin* or *Wamnugaoin*, glossed as “Cowrie-Shell Earrings” or “Shell ear ornaments or Pendants.”

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources used mention the band name *Wamnuga Owin* or *Wamnugaoin*, glossed as “Cowrie-Shell Earrings” or “Shell ear ornaments or Pendants.” They also mention *Kangi-shun Pegnake* or *Kanxicu pegnake*, glossed as “Crow Feather Hair Ornaments” or “Wear raven feathers in their hair.” No further jewelry, body adornment, tattooing, henna, or hair practices are documented.

## Language & religion

The sources identify *Sihásapa* as the Lakota word for “Blackfoot” and describe the Sihasapa as a division of the Lakota people, Titonwan, or Teton.
