## Overview

The Mi'kmaw are a people of Canada known for their skilled work with natural materials. What survives in museum collections from the 1840s–1860s shows a sophisticated material culture centered on birchbark, porcupine quill, spruce root, and ash splint. A single photograph shows Mi'kmaw people wearing western-style clothing. The records that survive are museum catalogues only, so they show what Mi'kmaw makers produced rather than the full scope of their life and culture.

## Material culture

Mi'kmaw makers worked primarily with bark, plant fibers, and quills rather than textiles, creating decorated containers, furniture, and functional objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Pattern and decoration appear mainly in bark, quill, and splint rather than woven cloth.

- **Etched birchbark box** — A circular container with birchbark sides bearing repeating eight-pointed asterisk designs and a lid decorated with vertical zig-zags.
- **Quillwork on bark** — Porcupine quill worked onto birchbark over wooden frames, found on trunks, chairs, and canoe models.
- **Coiled ash splint basket** — A lidded basket made from coiled ash splints decorated with alternating bands of birchbark and sweetgrass.
- **Glass beadwork** — Beaded designs including crowns on leather moccasins.

### Clothing & dress

The only documented garment is a pair of beaded moccasins; a photograph shows Mi'kmaw people in western-style clothing.

- **Moccasins** — Leather moccasins trimmed with velvet and cotton, decorated with glass beadwork in crown designs.

### Architecture

No documentation of Mi'kmaw house form or building traditions survives in the sources.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Containers dominate this category, made from birchbark and wood with plant fiber stitching; metal appears only in repair.

- **Mocock** — A birchbark container that is wide and rectangular at the base and tapers to a small circular opening at the top, with etched linear designs and evidence of interior use, possibly for maple syrup.
- **Lidded trunk** — A wooden and birchbark trunk decorated with porcupine quillwork, dated 1840–50.
- **Canoe model** — A birchbark canoe model from about 1860, accompanied by dolls made of silk, cotton, wool, glass and resin.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Glass beadwork on moccasins is the only recorded adornment in the sources.

## Language & religion

The only recorded Mi'kmaw term is *mocock*, a birchbark container; religion is not documented.

- **Mocock** — A birchbark container, the only Mi'kmaw language term recorded in the museum catalogues.

## Glossary

- *mocock* — A birchbark container that tapers from a wide rectangular base to a small circular opening at the top
- *birchbark* — Tree bark used to make waterproof containers and decorative surfaces
- *splint* — Thin strips of wood woven or coiled to make baskets
- *ash splint* — Thin strips of ash wood used in coiled basketry
- *sweetgrass* — A fragrant grass used in decorative bands on baskets
- *spruce root* — Roots used as cordage for stitching birchbark containers
- *etched* — Carved or incised lines into a surface for decoration
- *moccasin* — A soft leather shoe, often decorated with beadwork
- *beadwork* — Decoration made by stitching glass beads onto leather or cloth
