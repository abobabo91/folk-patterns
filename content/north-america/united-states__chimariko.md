## Overview

The Chimariko are an Indigenous people of California who originally lived in a narrow, 20-mile section of canyon on the Trinity River in Trinity County in northwestern California. Originally hunter-gatherers, they relied especially on salmon, which was disrupted by gold-seeking environmental practices during the California Gold Rush. Their population declined sharply after conflict with white miners in the 1860s. The Chimariko language was an extinct language isolate formerly spoken in northern Trinity County. Estimates place the population at 250 in 1849, 200 in 1852, 20 in 1880, and none by 1900; in the 2010 census, 60 people claimed Chimariko ancestry, 19 of them full-blooded. Descendants and revitalization efforts continue.

## Music & performance

The language documentation includes recordings made by John Paul Marr with speaker Martha Ziegler, and the Smithsonian Institution holds slides of John Peabody Harrington’s unpublished work.

## Foodways

The principal food identified in the sources is salmon, which was the main food source of the Chimariko.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention Chimariko traditional narratives but provide no descriptions, titles, plots, genres, or performance contexts. The language was extensively documented in unpublished fieldnotes obtained by John Peabody Harrington from Sally Noble in the 1920s. Recent efforts to revitalize the Chimariko language among descendants are also reported, and a Chimariko language dictionary was published in August of 2026.

## Language & religion

Chimariko is an extinct language isolate formerly spoken in northern Trinity County, California. Victor Golla distinguished three local dialects: Trinity River Chimariko, South Fork Chimariko, and New River Chimariko. The language was spoken along the Trinity River, around the junction of South Fork and Hayfork Creek, and along New River. Proposals linking it with the Hokan, Shastan, Palaihnihan, Pomoan, Karuk, and Yana languages remain undemonstrated, and specialists consider Chimariko best treated as an isolate.
