## Overview

The Choctaw are Indigenous people of the Southeastern Woodlands. Their communities are now centered mainly in Oklahoma, Mississippi, and Louisiana. Choctaw culture is expressed through song, dance, dress, beadwork, pottery, basketry, cooking, and stickball. Storytelling preserves history and passes cultural legacies to younger generations.

## Material culture

Choctaw material culture includes handmade clothing, river-cane basketry, beadwork, pottery, textiles, and traditional cooking.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Choctaw textile arts use river cane, plant fibers, bison fur, beads, and stickball-related designs.

- **River-cane carrying basket** — This basket has a square base, circular top, circular handle, and diagonal weave, with some cane dyed pink.
- **Beaded sash** (*isht ʋskufʋchi*) — Beaded sashes are part of collective Choctaw culture.
- **Bison-fur and plant-fiber textiles** (*nan tʋnna*) — Choctaw textile artisans make traditional textiles from bison fur and plant fibers.
- **Full-diamond motif** — This dress motif represents a stickball stick.
- **Cross motif** — This dress motif represents a stickball stick.

### Clothing & dress

Choctaw dresses are handmade from ancestral designs adapted from nineteenth-century European-American styles.

- **Choctaw dress** — Choctaw dresses are often decorated with full-diamond, half-diamond, circular, and cross motifs representing stickball sticks.
- **Cloth shirt and skirt** — A museum portrait shows a Choctaw woman wearing a cloth shirt and skirt.

### Architecture

Traditional Choctaw families built summer houses, corn houses, and circular winter houses.

- **Summer house** — The summer house had an oblong-square form.
- **Corn house** — The corn house also had an oblong-square form.
- **Winter house** (*hot house*) — The winter house was circular and was commonly called the hot house.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Choctaw everyday arts include pottery, basketry, textiles, beadwork, and cooking.

- **Pottery** — Pottery remains a continuing Choctaw tradition.
- **River-cane carrying basket** — This basket uses river cane and diagonal weaving.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The profile documents Choctaw beadwork and beaded sashes but no named jewelry types.

- **Beaded sash** (*isht ʋskufʋchi*) — Beaded sashes are culturally distinctive Choctaw objects.

## Music & performance

Choctaw performance traditions include song, prayer, dancing, entertainment, and stickball events.

- **Song at Nanih Waiya** (*Nanih Waiya*) — Song is associated with prayer and pilgrimage at Nanih Waiya, the Mother Mound.
- **Choctaw Indian Fair** — The mid-summer fair includes hospitality, traditional cooking, entertainment, dancing, and stickball.
- **Stickball** — Stickball is played at Choctaw community events and festivals.

## Dance & theatre

Dance appears in community events, pilgrimage, and accounts of Choctaw warfare.

- **Pilgrimage dance** (*Nanih Waiya*) — Women visit Nanih Waiya for prayer, song, and dance.
- **War-trophy dance** — Women danced with war trophies until tired, after which the trophies were placed on hot houses.

## Festivals & rituals

Choctaw festivals and rituals connect community gatherings, stickball, prayer, song, dance, and respect for the sun.

- **Choctaw Indian Fair** — The mid-summer fair brings together Choctaw people from around the world.
- **Choctaw Labor Day festival** — The festival in Tuskahoma, Oklahoma, includes stickball played on a modern football field.
- **Female pilgrimage** (*Nanih Waiya*) — Nanih Waiya, the Mother Mound, remains a sacred place for female pilgrimage, prayer, song, and dance.
- **Sunny-day diplomacy** — Choctaw diplomats traditionally met only on sunny days because the sun was believed to ensure honesty.

## Foodways

Choctaw foodways include shared harvests, communal labor, traditional cooking, and medicinal plant use.

- **Shared harvests** — Early Choctaw communities practiced communal labor and shared their harvests.
- **Traditional cooking** — Traditional cooking is part of Choctaw culture, and the Choctaws are described as cuisine specialists.
- **Leaf and blossom decoction** (*Pseudognaphalium obtusifolium*) — A decoction of leaves and blossoms was taken for lung pain and colds.

## Oral tradition & literature

Choctaw storytelling preserves origins, migrations, heroes, animals, sacred traditions, and history.

- **Bohpoli** — Bohpoli, also called Kowi anukasha, was a little man who lived in the woods and helped doctors make medicines.
- **Emergence and migration stories** (*Chata and Chicksah*) — Stories recount the emergence of the Choctaw and the migration of Chata and Chicksah.
- **Animal stories** — Choctaw stories include Possum and Raccoon, the dog’s lifespan, and grasshoppers and ants.
- **Sun Hunter story** (*Hunter of the Sun*) — The Hunter of the Sun is among the named Choctaw stories.
- **Choctaw Tales** — Choctaw Tales by Tom Mould and Chief Phillip Martin is mentioned as a work about Choctaw storytelling.

## Language & religion

Choctaw is a Muskogean language with several dialects, a Roman-alphabet writing system, and a traditional religion centered partly on the sun and prophets.

- **Choctaw language** (*Chahta anumpa*) — Choctaw is closely related to Chickasaw and has three commonly identified dialects.
- **Prophets** (*hopaii*) — Hopaii served as prophets in Choctaw traditional religion.
- **Great Spirit names** (*Nanapesa*) — Nanapesa is named as a term associated with the Great Spirit.
- **Great Spirit names** (*Ishtahullo-chito*) — Ishtahullo-chito is named as a term associated with the Great Spirit.
- **Great Spirit names** (*Shilup chitoh osh*) — Shilup chitoh osh is named as a term associated with the Great Spirit.
