## Overview

The Comanche are a Native American tribe from the Southern Plains. Their historic homeland was called Comanchería. Their culture was shaped by horses, bison hunting, trade, warfare, and social groups. Today, language and cultural institutions support preservation and public gatherings.

## Music & performance

Comanche gatherings include annual dances, and the language was used by Comanche Code Talkers during World War II.

- **Comanche Homecoming Annual Dance** — The Comanche Homecoming Annual Dance takes place in mid-July in Walters, Oklahoma.
- **Comanche Homecoming powwow** — Comanches gather from across the United States each July in Walters for the annual homecoming powwow.
- **Comanche Little Ponies dances** — The Comanche Little Ponies host two annual dances, one over New Year’s Eve and one in May.
- **Comanche Code Talkers** — Seventeen young Comanche men sent sensitive wartime messages in Comanche so the enemy could not decipher them.

## Festivals & rituals

Annual gatherings celebrate Comanche heritage, support tribal government, and bring people together.

- **Comanche Nation Fair** — The Comanche Nation Fair takes place every September.
- **Annual General Council** — The Annual General Council meets on the third Saturday in April to approve the budget, nominate leaders, and vote on resolutions.
- **Comanche National Museum and Cultural Center** — The Lawton museum opened to the public in 2007 and has permanent and changing exhibitions on Comanche history and culture.
- **Comanche Homecoming powwow** — The annual July powwow brings Comanches together to celebrate their heritage and culture.

- **Burnt Meat** (*Itehtah'o*) — This nickname refers to meat thrown out in spring that dried and became black.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources preserve group-name explanations and language-learning work.

- **The people** (*Nʉmʉnʉʉ*) — This Comanche autonym means “the people” or “the human beings.”
- **Movers, Returners** (*Nokoninʉʉ*) — This name is recorded as meaning “Movers, Returners.”
- **Language preservation materials** — The Comanche Language and Cultural Preservation Committee and Comanche Nation Language Department offer dictionaries and learning materials.
- **Language recording project** — The former Comanche Nation College conducted a project to record the Comanche language.

## Language & religion

Comanche is a Numic language with its own name, writing system, and ongoing preservation efforts, while the profile gives no religious description.

- **Language of the people** (*nʉmʉ tekwapʉ*) — This is the Comanche name for the language and means “language of the people.”
- **The human beings** (*nʉmʉnʉʉ*) — This Comanche autonym means “the human beings” or “the people.”
- **Comanche Alphabet** — Dr. Alice Anderton developed the alphabet, which the Comanche Nation adopted officially in 1994.
- **Numic language** — Comanche belongs to the Numic branch of the Uto-Aztecan language family.
