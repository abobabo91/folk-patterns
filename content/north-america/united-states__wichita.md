## Overview

The Wichita are Southern Plains Native American peoples with ancestral lands in Oklahoma, Texas, and Kansas. They lived in settled villages, farmed, hunted bison, traded, and made pottery. Their culture also includes tattooing, grass lodges, and distinctive social and political organization. Today the Wichita and Affiliated Tribes are headquartered in Anadarko, Oklahoma.

## Material culture

Wichita material culture centers on tanned hides, grass lodges, pottery, bison products, tattoos, and ear ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Wichita women made clothing from tanned hides, and surviving records show blankets and woven waistcoats.

- **Tanned-hide clothing** — Wichita women prepared and sewed clothing from tanned hides, often decorating dresses with elk canine teeth.
- **Grass-lodge models** — Museum collections include models of Wichita grass lodges.
- **Tattoo marks** — Solid and dotted lines and circles were used as tattoo marks on the body.

### Clothing & dress

Wichita dress combined sewn hide clothing, blankets, tattoos, and later western-style clothing.

- **Hide dresses** — Women made dresses from tanned hides and often decorated them with elk canine teeth.
- **Blankets** — Blankets were wrapped around the waist, torso, or shoulders.
- **Western-style clothing** — Museum records show shirts, waistcoats, jackets, trousers, shoes, suits, ties, and hats.
- **Tattooing** — Men and women tattooed their faces and bodies with solid and dotted lines and circles.

### Architecture

Wichita villages were known for large domed grass lodges and other grass-covered houses.

- **Domed grass lodges** — Settled villages used domed grass lodges that could reach 30 feet in diameter.
- **Forked-pole huts** — For much of the year, people lived in huts made from forked cedar poles covered with dry grasses.
- **Granaries** — Round grass-thatched houses were surrounded by large granaries for corn, beans, and squash.
- **Council circles** — Some Great Bend settlements had central patios surrounded by four semi-subterranean structures called council circles.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Wichita households relied heavily on locally made pottery, crops, and many products from bison.

- **Wichita pottery** — Wichita people made local ceramic pottery that was traded and compared with Osage and Pawnee pottery.
- **Bison products** — Bison supplied clothing, food, cooking fat, shelter, leather, sinew, medicine, and armor.
- **Crop granaries** — Historical accounts mention granaries used to store crops.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Tattooing and ear ornaments were important recorded forms of Wichita adornment.

- **Tattooing** (*kirikir?i:s*) — The name kirikir?i:s is linked to tattoo marks around the eyes and means “raccoon-eyed people.”
- **Bone ear ornaments** — Museum photographs show people wearing bone ear ornaments.
- **Hooped ear ornaments** — Some Wichita people wore hooped ear ornaments.
- **Bear claw pendants** — Museum records include bear claw pendants.
- **Feathered hat** — A photograph shows a feather worn in a hat.

## Music & performance

The Wichita Annual Dance is a contemporary public powwow held every August.

- **Wichita Annual Dance** — The Wichita Annual Dance is a powwow held at Wichita Tribal Park north of Anadarko.

## Dance & theatre

The main recorded dance event is the annual Wichita powwow.

- **Wichita Annual Dance** — The Wichita Annual Dance is held every August at Wichita Tribal Park on US-281.

## Festivals & rituals

Wichita public cultural life includes an annual powwow, a history center, and older religious traditions.

- **Wichita Annual Dance** — The annual powwow is held every August at Wichita Tribal Park.
- **Wichita Tribal History Center** — Opened in 2018 in Anadarko, the center shares Wichita history, archaeology, visual arts, and culture.
- **Great Spirit worship** — Earlier sources describe the Wichita as worshipping a Great Spirit.
- **Religious tobacco use** — Tobacco was important for religious purposes in earlier agricultural communities.
- **Creation account** — A creation account says that a man and woman dreamed things were made for them and that the woman received an ear of corn for future generations.

## Foodways

Wichita foodways combined farming, bison hunting, other hunting, gathering, fishing, and mussel collecting.

- **Three Sisters** — Maize, beans, and squash were grown together as the Three Sisters.
- **Bison foods** — Bison was especially important for food and cooking fat, and all parts were used.
- **Gathered nuts and fruits** — People gathered walnuts, hickory nuts, plums, hackberries, and grapes.
- **River foods** — People caught fish and harvested mussels from rivers.
- **Game animals** — Historical hunting included deer, rabbits, turkey, bison, elk, pronghorn, and dog.

## Oral tradition & literature

The main recorded Wichita oral tradition is a creation account preserved from Tawakoni Jim.

- **Wichita creation account** — The account tells of a man and woman who dreamed that things were made for them and of corn given as food for future generations.
- **Wichita Documentation Project** — The University of Colorado, Boulder, documents the Wichita language.
- **Wichita dictionary and language CDs** — David S. Rood worked with Wichita speakers to create a dictionary and language CDs.
- **Quivira accounts** — Historical descriptions include statements attributed to Francisco Vásquez de Coronado about the people of Quivira.
- **Etzanoa accounts** — Historical descriptions also include accounts by Juan de Oñate concerning Etzanoa.

## Language & religion

Wichita is a Caddoan language with several named dialects, but it is now dormant or functionally extinct.

- **Wichita language** — Wichita is an agglutinative and polysynthetic Caddoan language with no gender distinction and with singular, dual, and plural forms.
- **Waco dialect** (*Waco*) — Waco is one of the identified Wichita dialects.
- **Tawakoni dialect** (*Tawakoni*) — Tawakoni is one of the identified Wichita dialects.
- **Wichita Proper** (*Kirikirʔi꞉s*) — Kirikirʔi꞉s, also called Wichita Proper, is an identified Wichita dialect.
- **Language classes** — The tribe offers classes and has created immersion classes for children and adults.
