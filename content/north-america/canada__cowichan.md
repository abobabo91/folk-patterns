## Overview

The Cowichan are a Coast Salish people of Vancouver Island whose territory covered the Cowichan Valley, the country around Cowichan Lake and Shawnigan Lake, and extended into the Gulf Islands and to the Fraser River. Cowichan Tribes is the largest First Nations band in British Columbia with over 3,800 registered members. They speak Halkomelem, a Central Salish language. They are known above all for a distinctive type of knitting called the Cowichan sweater and for carved spindle whorls, horn rattles and spoons.

## Material culture

Wood and horn dominate the documented object repertoire, with spindle whorls as the largest single class of objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Cowichan knitting, especially the Cowichan sweater, is their most distinctive textile tradition.

- **Cowichan sweater** — A distinctive type of knitted garment that the Cowichan are known for above all.
- **Spindle whorl** — A weighted disc that steadies a hand spindle, cut from wood and often worked with carving, engraving, openwork or red paint.
- **Cedar-bark canoe bailer** — A broad flat piece of cedar bark gently curved with ends turned up and gathered into vertical uprights, bound with cherry bark onto a horizontal handle.

### Clothing & dress

Cowichan dress combined imported trade blankets, neighboring peoples' hats, settler-made cloth garments, and knitted sweaters.

- **Cowichan sweater** — A knitted garment distinctively made by the Cowichan and worn as dress.
- **Haida hat** — A woven brimmed hat made by a neighbouring people and worn by Cowichan women.
- **Hudson's Bay blanket** — An imported trade blanket worn over the shoulders as part of Cowichan dress.

### Architecture

Seven traditional villages and nine reserves totalling 24 square kilometres lie within a core traditional territory of about 3,750 square kilometres.

- **Quamichan** (*Kwaʼmutsun*) — One of seven traditional villages of the Cowichan.
- **Comiaken** (*Qwumʼyiqunʼ*) — One of seven traditional villages of the Cowichan.
- **Koksilah** (*Xwulqwʼselu*) — One of seven traditional villages of the Cowichan.
- **Cowichan Bay** (*Tlʼulpalus*) — One of seven traditional villages of the Cowichan.
- **Skutz Canyon** — Location of reserves Skutz 7 and 8.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Wood and horn dominate the documented object repertoire, with no ceramics among the objects shown.

- **Spindle whorl** — A disc cut from wood, often maple, pierced by an axial hole and worked with carving, engraving, openwork or red paint.
- **Horn rattle** — A folded piece of mountain goat horn filled with unidentified material, with a wooden handle and wool tassels or abalone accents.
- **Horn spoon** — A spoon of mountain goat horn with a pointed handle and an engraved crest design on the bowl.
- **Wooden comb** — A carved comb featuring a xwe-xwe mask figure.
- **Salmon fish-lure** — A wooden lure used for fishing.

### Jewelry & body adornment

- **Abalone shell** — An adornment material used as an accent on a horn rattle.

## Music & performance

Horn rattles are the only documented sound-producing instruments, filled with unidentified material or objects to produce sound.

- **Horn rattle** — A sound-producing instrument made of mountain goat horn with a wooden handle, wool tassels, and sometimes abalone accents.
- **Potlatch** — A ceremonial gathering at Duncan where Quamichan men danced in ceremonial costume before a gathered audience.

## Dance & theatre

Potlatch dancing is the only dance event described, with men in ceremonial costume performing before a watching community.

- **Potlatch dance** — A ceremonial performance at Duncan where three Quamichan men danced in a clearing in ceremonial costume before other Quamichan people.
- **Xwe-xwe mask** (*xwe-xwe*) — A mask figure carved on a wooden comb, though how or when it was worn or danced is not described.

## Festivals & rituals

The potlatch is the one named ceremonial occasion, involving dancing, ceremonial dress and a gathered community.

- **Potlatch** — A ceremonial gathering with dancing, ceremonial dress and a watching community.
- **Arranged marriage** — Arranged marriages between children in different Halkomelem-speaking language areas were common and helped establish a regional social network.
- **War canoe raid** — Before and during early contact, Cowichan warriors travelled long distances in large war canoes to raid other villages, seize goods and take captives.

## Foodways

The food economy turned on salmon, with Island Halkomelem speakers crossing the Strait of Georgia each summer to camp along the Fraser River.

- **Salmon** — The foundation of the food economy, with Island people crossing the Strait of Georgia each summer to camp along the Fraser River for the salmon runs.
- **Salmon fish-lure** — A wooden lure used for fishing salmon.
- **Mountain goat horn spoon** — A spoon used for eating or serving food.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources contain no Cowichan folktales, epics, proverbs or riddles, but document language archiving through FirstVoices.

- **FirstVoices Halkomelem archive** — Holds 1,754 archived words and 690 archived phrases of Halkomelem, delivered through an iPhone app released in 2011 and an Android version in 2016.

## Language & religion

Cowichan speak Halkomelem, a Central Salish language near extinction with fewer than twenty-five fluent speakers estimated in 2000.

- **Halkomelem** — A Central Salish language spoken by Cowichan, with tense, sharply articulated glottalized resonants as a key feature of Island speech.
- **Island dialect** (*Hulʼqumiʼnumʼ*) — Also called Cowichan, this Island dialect of Halkomelem is shared with eight other Coast Salish peoples.
- **FirstVoices language documentation** — Language archiving with 1,754 words and 690 phrases available through mobile apps and online.
