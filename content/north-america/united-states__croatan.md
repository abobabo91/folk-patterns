## Overview

The Croatan, also known as Hatteras, were a small Native American ethnic group living in the coastal areas of what is now North Carolina. Their territory included the Alligator River, Croatan Sound, Roanoke Island, Ocracoke Island, and parts of the Outer Banks, including Hatteras Island. In 1701, their population was estimated to be 80 people. The Croatan spoke a language in the Algonquian language family. They first had contact with English settlers, notably John White, in 1585, and were extinct as a tribe by the mid-18th century. State-recognized tribes claiming descent from the Croatan people have members who live predominantly in Cumberland, Sampson, and Harnett counties.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe religious beliefs among Native Americans living in coastal North Carolina, including belief in “one only chief and great God,” the immortality of the soul, heaven, and a place near the setting sun called Popogusso, where souls were said to burn for eternity in a huge pit of fire. Conjurors and priests were distinctive spiritual leaders: priests were chosen for knowledge and wisdom and led the organized religion, while conjurors were chosen for magical abilities and were thought to possess powers from a personal connection with a supernatural being, mostly spirits from the animal world.

They state that the concepts of heaven and hell were impressed upon common people to encourage respect for leaders and a life that would produce rewards in the afterlife.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources connect Croatan history with accounts of the Lost Colony of Roanoke. Some survivors of the Lost Colony may have joined the Croatan. When Governor White reached Roanoke Island on August 18, 1590, he found the colony deserted, with “CROATOAN” carved into a palisade. Croatoan was the name of a nearby island, likely modern-day Hatteras Island, as well as the local Native American tribe.

In 1709, John Lawson wrote that the “Hatteras Indians” said several of their ancestors were white people who could “talk in a Book.” The sources present this account as part of the later speculation surrounding the fate of the Lost Colony. They also describe later research by the Croatoan Archaeological Society and the Lost Colony Center for Science and Research, including the Lost Colony DNA Project. As of 2019, the project had not identified any living descendants.

## Language & religion

The Croatan spoke a language in the Algonquian language family.

According to Thomas Harriot, Native Americans living in coastal North Carolina believed in “one only chief and great God,” who made petty gods to be used in creation and government. They believed in the immortality of the soul. After death, the soul either entered heaven to live with the gods or went to Popogusso, near the setting sun, where it burned for eternity. Priests led organized religion, while conjurors were associated with magical abilities and supernatural beings, mostly spirits from the animal world.
