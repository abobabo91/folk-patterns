## Overview

The Dakelh are a First Nations people of central British Columbia whose territories stretch along the Fraser River through forested lake country. They organized their year around harvesting salmon and berries, and maintained trade routes called Grease Trails to exchange furs and dried foods for coastal goods like eulachon oil. Their language, Dakelh, is severely endangered, with only about 10% of Dakelh people speaking it today. The community has worked since the 1980s to document and teach the language through institutes and community programs.

## Material culture

Dakelh material culture centered on tools for fishing, hunting and food processing made from stone, bone, wood and bark.

### Textile & pattern traditions

No weaving or embroidery tradition is documented.

- **Birchbark berry basket** — A container made of birchbark bound with split root, used to gather and store berries in summer.

### Clothing & dress

Clothing came from hunting furs of deer, caribou, moose, elk, black bear, beaver and rabbit.

### Architecture

No house forms or structures are described in available sources.

- **Keyoh** — A territory, village and trapline held under a hereditary chief.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Tools for fishing, hunting and food processing were made from stone, bone, wood and bark.

- **Stone arrow-head** — Flaked and ground stone points, including triangular and leaf-shaped forms.
- **Stone adze blade** — A grooved stone tool with ridges, used for woodworking.
- **Bone fishing cylinder** — A hollow bone tube plugged at both ends with wood and decorated with concentric circles.
- **Wooden netting-needle** — A tool used for making fishing nets.

## Festivals & rituals

The year was organized by a seasonal round with greatest activity in summer for fishing and berry gathering.

- **Grease Trails** — Trade routes to the Pacific coast where Dakelh exchanged hides, dried meat and berry mats for eulachon oil and seaweed.

## Foodways

Salmon, hunted game and berries formed the core diet, supplemented by trade goods from the Pacific coast.

- **Smoked salmon** — Various varieties of salmon were smoked and stored in large quantities for winter.
- **Eulachon oil** (*grease*) — Oil rendered from eulachons by letting them rot, adding boiling water and skimming the oil, highly nutritious and a prized trade good.
- **Lingonberry jam** (*Vaccinium vitis-idaea*) — Berries made into jam, a traditional food still eaten today.
- **Lodgepole pine sap** — The sap and cambium of lodgepole pine were used as food.

## Oral tradition & literature

Dakelh was written in syllabics and later in Roman orthography, with modern Bible translation and community-authored books.

- **Carrier syllabics** — A writing system inspired by Cree syllabics, developed in 1885 and widely used but fading by the 1930s.
- **Carrier Linguistic Committee orthography** — A Roman-based writing system developed in the 1960s using digraphs and apostrophes for glottalization.
- **Carrier Bible Translation** — A New Testament published in 1995 with a Blackwater dialect adaptation in 2002.
- **The Carrier, My People** — A community-authored book by Lizette Hall published in 1992.
- **Ulkatcho: Stories of the Grease Trail** — Community stories collected by Sage Birchwater about the trade routes and Dakelh history.

## Language & religion

Dakelh is a Northern Athabaskan language with a complex verb system and is now severely endangered; Catholic missionary activity shaped religious life.

- **Dakelh language** (*Dakelh*) — A Northern Athabaskan language split into Stuart-Trembleur and Southern dialects, with hundreds of thousands of verb forms.
- **Gender classifiers** — Grammatical markers indicating whether objects are round, stick-like or areal.
- **Yinka Dene Language Institute** — Founded in 1988 to handle research, archiving, curriculum and teacher training for Dakelh language preservation.

## Glossary

- *Dakelh* — The First Nations people, their territory and language; means 'the people on the land'
- *keyoh* — A hereditary territory, village and trapline held by a chief
- *Yinka Dene* — What the Dakelh call themselves; means 'the people on the land'
- *grease* — Eulachon oil, a nutritious rendered fat from candlefish
- *Grease Trails* — Trade routes from the interior to the Pacific coast for exchanging furs and dried foods
- *Carrier syllabics* — A writing system inspired by Cree syllabics, developed in 1885 for writing Dakelh
- *Carrier Linguistic Committee orthography* — Roman-based writing system for Dakelh developed in the 1960s
- *Vaccinium vitis-idaea* — Lingonberry, used to make jam by the Dakelh
- *Northern Athabaskan* — A language family to which Dakelh belongs
