## Overview

The Saulteaux, also known as the Plains Ojibwe, Nakawe, or Nakawē, are a First Nations band government in Canada. They are a branch of the Ojibwe who moved west and developed a mixed culture of woodlands and plains Indigenous customs and traditions. Historically they lived around Lake Superior and Lake Winnipeg, particularly near Sault Ste. Marie and northern Michigan. Today they are found principally in Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, Ontario and British Columbia, including the Interlake District, Swan River, Duck Bay, Camperville, Kamsack and surrounding areas, and Moberly Lake. The sources list numerous communities and population figures. The Saulteaux are primarily hunters and fishers and historically maintained trading relations with the French, British and later Americans.

## Language & religion

Saulteaux is a branch of the Ojibwe language and belongs to the Algonquian language family. Western Ojibwa, also known as *Nakawēmowin*, Saulteaux or Plains Ojibwa, is spoken in southern Manitoba and southern Saskatchewan, west of Lake Winnipeg. The Western Saulteaux call themselves *Nakawē*; the neighbouring Plains Cree call them *Nahkawiyiniw*. The Western dialect is also called *Nakawēmowin* or Western Ojibwa and has ISO 639-3 code OJW. Ontario Saulteaux use a form of Anishinaabemowin sometimes called Northwestern Ojibwa language, with ISO 639-3 code OJB.

Saulteaux is described as an agglutinating or polysynthetic language with relatively free word order. It distinguishes animate and inanimate categories rather than masculine and feminine, and uses the Standard Roman Orthography. Some speakers represent long vowels with double vowels, while others use a macron or acute accent.
