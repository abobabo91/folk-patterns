## Overview

Isleta Pueblo was established around the 14th century on a knife-shaped lava flow. Its people traditionally speak Isletan Tiwa and practice irrigated farming. Cultural life includes matrilineal organization, kiva rituals, Kachina traditions, Catholic mission history, pottery, and distinctive clothing. The pueblo is also known as Shiewhibak in Southern Tiwa.

## Material culture

The documented material culture includes pottery, patterned blankets, clothing, leg ornaments, and a turquoise cross set in silver.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not document a named Isleta textile tradition, but they record patterned blankets, cloth clothing, leg ornaments, and pottery designs.

- **Patterned blankets** — Patterned blankets were worn around the waist, upper torso, or body in studio portraits.
- **Cloth shirts and jackets** — Isleta men wore cloth shirts and jackets in photographic portraits.
- **Leg ornaments** — Alejandro Padilla wore leg ornaments in several photographic records.
- **Red diagonal diamonds** — Pottery records show red diagonal diamonds along a bowl rim.
- **Red and brown ornaments** — Pottery records name red and brown ornaments on a white interior ground.

### Clothing & dress

Museum portraits show Isleta men wearing shirts, jackets, leggings, boots or moccasins, blankets, and sometimes hats.

- **Alejandro Padilla’s outfit** — Alejandro Padilla wore a cloth shirt, jacket, leggings, leg ornaments, boots or moccasins, and a patterned blanket around his waist.
- **Ambrosio Abeita’s outfit** — Ambrosio Abeita wore cloth shirts, jackets, leggings, boots or moccasins, and a patterned blanket around his waist.
- **Hats** — Hats appear on a knee or on a table in several portraits.
- **Piece of cloth** — In one portrait, Alejandro Padilla held a piece of cloth.

### Architecture

Isleta Pueblo stands on a lava flow and includes a historic kiva and Spanish mission churches.

- **Kiva** — The tribe maintains and operates a kiva as a sacred space for rituals and ceremonies.
- **Spanish Mission of San Agustín de la Isleta** — The Spanish Mission of San Agustín de la Isleta was built in 1613 by the Franciscan friar Juan de Salas.
- **St. Agustin Mission Church** — The historic St. Agustin Mission Church was built by the Tiwa under the direction of a Spanish missionary in 1612.
- **Knife-shaped lava setting** — The pueblo was built on a knife-shaped lava flow crossing an ancient Rio Grande channel.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Isleta pottery includes painted earthenware bowls and vessels, while a turquoise cross was made in silver.

- **Cross-handle vessel** — Museum records document a small vessel with a cross handle from Isleta.
- **White-ground bowl with diamonds** — One earthenware bowl has exterior painting, a white ground, and red diagonal diamonds along the rim.
- **Red-and-brown bowl** — Another bowl is red outside and has a white interior decorated in red and brown with a wavy edge.
- **Painted pottery** — A British Museum postcard shows an Isleta woman seated before painted pottery placed on the ground.
- **Silver-mounted turquoise cross** — Isletans made a turquoise cross mounted in silver, which Pablo Abeita gave to the King of Belgium in 1919.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources document a silver-mounted turquoise cross and leg ornaments.

- **Silver-mounted turquoise cross** — Pablo Abeita gave a turquoise cross mounted in silver to the King of Belgium during the 1919 royal visit to Isleta.
- **Leg ornaments** — Alejandro Padilla wore leg ornaments in studio portraits.

## Music & performance

The sources do not identify Isleta instruments or song types, but they record dances and celebrations.

- **Dance of the kings** — The dance of the kings was held in January and overseen by a war captain and other officials.
- **Tortoise dance** — The tortoise dance was held in February and overseen by a war captain and other officials.
- **Kachina dancers** (*Kachina*) — Masked Kachina dancers represent Kachinas at religious ceremonies.

## Dance & theatre

Isleta dance traditions include seasonal dances and masked Kachina performances connected with religion.

- **Dance of the kings** — The dance of the kings was named as a January celebration.
- **Tortoise dance** — The tortoise dance was named as a February celebration.
- **Kachina dancers** (*Kachina*) — Masked community members represent Kachinas during religious ceremonies.
- **Kachina dolls** (*Kachina*) — Small dolls carved in the likeness of Kachinas are given as gifts to children.

## Festivals & rituals

Ritual life includes Kachina ceremonies, kiva use, seasonal dances, winter and summer traditions, and Catholic mission history.

- **Kiva ceremonies** (*kiva*) — A kiva is used as a sacred space for particular rituals and ceremonies.
- **Kachina ceremonies** (*Kachina*) — Traditional religion involves Kachinas, spiritual beings connected with the immaterial and natural worlds.
- **Dance of the kings** — The dance of the kings takes place in January.
- **Tortoise dance** — The tortoise dance takes place in February.
- **Winter and summer moieties** (*moiety*) — One moiety is connected with winter practices and the other with summer practices and traditions.

## Foodways

Isleta foodways are based on irrigated farming, with maize, squash, and beans as important foods.

- **Maize** — Maize, also called corn, was cultivated.
- **Squash** — Squash was a staple Pueblo food throughout the region.
- **Beans** — Beans were a staple Pueblo food throughout the region.
- **Comanche Ranch cattle** — The Pueblo’s Comanche Ranch is described as running 1,000 head of cattle.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not record a named Isleta oral-literary tradition, but they preserve the name Shiewhibak and language-education efforts.

- **Shiewhibak** — The Southern Tiwa name Shiewhibak means “a knife laid on the ground to play whib,” a traditional footrace.
- **Tiwa language classes** (*Tiwa*) — The tribe announced that Tiwa would be taught to children at Isleta Elementary School in 2015.
- **Tiwa-English program** (*Tiwa*) — A 2016 grant supported development of a dual-language Tiwa-English program for young children.
- **Whib footrace** (*whib*) — Whib is named as a traditional footrace in the meaning of Shiewhibak.

## Language & religion

Isleta traditionally speaks Isletan Tiwa and follows both Kachina religious traditions and Catholicism.

- **Isletan Tiwa** — Isletan Tiwa is one of the two varieties of Southern Tiwa.
- **Southern Tiwa** — Southern Tiwa belongs to the Tanoan branch of the Kiowa-Tanoan language family.
- **Shiewhibak** — Shiewhibak is the Southern Tiwa name for Isleta.
- **Dîiw'a'ane** — Dîiw'a'ane is the Western Keres name for Isleta.
- **Naatoohó** — Naatoohó is the Navajo name for Isleta.
