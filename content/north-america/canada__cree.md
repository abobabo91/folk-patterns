## Overview

The Cree are an Indigenous people of Canada with settlements across vast territories from the east coast to the northwest. They traditionally lived as hunter-gatherers in mobile bands, with those on the Great Plains hunting bison. The Cree maintain a living oral tradition and animistic worldview centered on respectful hunting practices and the cooperation of prey animals.

## Material culture

Cree material culture emphasizes sewn and woven work—beadwork, embroidery, and plaited cord—combining native materials like mooseskin and birchbark with imported glass beads, silk, and trade cloth.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Flowering designs in glass beads and silk embroidery on smoked mooseskin are the Cree's most distinctive textile work.

- **Floral beadwork** — Glass seed beads worked in flowering sprays on dark blue velvet bags and smoked mooseskin panels cut from coats.
- **Silk embroidery on smoked mooseskin** — Mittens and skin boots embroidered with silk flowers, fringing, and plaited wool cord with pompoms.
- **Birchbark biting** — Pictures made by biting folded birchbark to create designs of birds with outstretched wings.
- **Quillwork** — Panels sewn of native-tanned skin and worked with quill alongside glass beads and metal.
- **Plaited and finger-woven cordwork** — Multicoloured wool cords with pompoms join mittens and tie boots, often in red and blue.

### Clothing & dress

Everyday wear centered on the smoked mooseskin jacket with hood and paired mittens, while ceremonial dress featured feathers, horns, and trade cloth.

- **Smoked mooseskin jacket** — Pull-over garment with chest pockets, fringing, buttons at the neck, and a separate sewn hood with cotton lining.
- **Skin boots** — Embroidered uppers and leg fringe, worn with moccasins and decorated leggings.
- **Paired mittens** — Joined by a wool strip so they hang from the neck, often embroidered with floral designs.
- **Shot pouches** — Sewn from felted wool or trade cloth, embroidered with flowers and closed with metal zippers or buttons.
- **Infant caps** — Red cloth printed with blue flowers, lined in white cotton, with ruffled borders framing the face.

### Architecture

The Cree built mobile dwellings designed to be struck and moved with hunting bands rather than permanent houses.

- **Wigwam** — A domed tent housing eight to a dozen people, usually two related married couples and their families.
- **Tipi** — A conical tent used as a residential unit in mobile bands, visible in photographs from Battleford and Saskatchewan encampments.
- **Sun Dance lodge** — A purpose-built structure with long supporting beams and a shrub screen, used for the two-day Sun Dance ceremony.
- **Moose Factory** — A Hudson's Bay Company post founded in 1672–73 around which many Cree communities grew.
- **Fort Rupert** — Colonial post established at the mouth of the Rupert River in 1668, also known as Charles Fort or Waskaganish.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Cree produced no documented ceramics or metalwork, instead focusing on sewn and woven objects incorporating imported materials.

- **Birchbark canoe** — A watercraft photographed upside down beside tipis at lakeshore camps.
- **Rifle-cases** — Tailored from felted wool or denim, closed with moose skin and buttons or plastic fasteners.
- **Bitten birchbark pictures** — Rectangles of folded birchbark bearing bird or other designs created by biting.
- **Shot pouches** — Sewn pouches with embroidered decoration and hanging straps, often using metal zippers.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment used animal materials and trade goods, with feathers and horned headdresses distinguishing chiefs and ritual leaders.

- **Gorget choker** — A bead and shell necklace worn as a sign of status.
- **Arm band** — Made of deer hoof and skin, worn as a mark of distinction.
- **Eagle feathers** — Two feathers worn in the hair by chiefs and Sun Dance participants.
- **Two-horned headdress** — A chief's headdress with fur, worn in formal and ceremonial contexts.
- **Feather fan** — Carried by Sun Dance participants and ceremonial leaders.

## Music & performance

The Sun Dance is the only documented performance tradition, with no Cree instruments or song genres recorded in the sources.

- **Sun Dance** — A two-day ceremony held in a purpose-built lodge, with participants in headdresses and feathers holding sticks and feather fans, photographed at Battleford in 1895.

## Dance & theatre

The Sun Dance is the sole documented dance tradition, with no theatrical or masked traditions described.

- **Sun Dance** — A two-day ceremony believed to originate with the Cree and later adopted by neighbouring Ojibwe communities.

## Festivals & rituals

There was no annual festival calendar; instead, the hunting year structured community rhythms, with the Sun Dance as the named ceremonial gathering.

- **Sun Dance** — A two-day ceremonial gathering held at different times, with a purpose-built lodge and specific ritual roles.
- **Hunt** — The central ritual practice governed by ethics of respect: the animal should be killed without waste and with regard for the species' wider population.
- **War leader** (*okimahkan*) — A temporary commander appointed during conflict, distinct from the peace chief or diplomat.
- **Council of allied bands** — Questions of war and peace settled by consensus, with each clan sending a representative with a vote.

## Foodways

The sources record subsistence practices rather than cuisine: the Cree were hunter-gatherers who hunted bison on the plains or caribou in the north.

- **Bison hunting** — Plains Cree bands adopted bison hunting and became known as the Plains Cree.
- **Caribou hunting** — Naskapi communities remained caribou hunters and more nomadic than many of the Innu.
- **Fur trading** — After acquiring firearms, Cree men moved as traders onto the plains and became important intermediaries for the Hudson's Bay Company and First Nations networks.
- **Iron Confederacy** — Alliance formed with the Assiniboine, Métis Nation and Saulteaux that became a major force in the fur trade from the 1730s to the 1870s.

## Oral tradition & literature

A living narrative and oratorical tradition is documented in published texts and modern revitalization efforts.

- **Thunderbirds** (*pinesiwak*) — Beings sometimes called pinesiwak among the Cree and northern Ojibwe.
- **The Counselling Speeches of Jim Kâ-Nîpitêhtêw** — A 1998 text collection of oratory using full punctuation in Cree.
- **Cree Language Resource Project** — An online bilingual dictionary compiling Cree words across dialects.
- **Cree School Board reports** — Annual reports published in Cree as well as English.
- **Joshua Whitehead** — A writer using the Cree language in poetry.

## Language & religion

Cree is an Algonquian language spoken by about 86,475 people, making it Canada's most-spoken Indigenous language; religion is animistic with no formal structure.

- **Cree language** (*nêhiyawêwin (Plains), nīhithawīwin (Woods), ininîmowin (Eastern Swampy), ililîmowin (Moose), iyiyiu-Ayamiwin (Northern East Cree), nehirâmowin (Atikamekw)*) — An Algonquian dialect continuum with eight recognized groupings and approximately 86,475 speakers in 2021.
- **Canadian Aboriginal syllabics** — A writing system with Western and Eastern forms used by speakers of western and central Cree dialects.
- **Standard Roman Orthography** — A spelling system serving Plains and Swampy Cree alongside the Latin script used by eastern dialects.
- **Cree religion** — An animistic worldview with no formal leadership, centring on the ethics of the hunt and respect for animal cooperation.
- **Contact languages** — Michif, Northern Michif, Bungi, Oji-Cree and Nehipwat all have Cree as a foundation language.

## Glossary

- *nêhiyawêwin* — The Cree language as spoken by Plains Cree
- *wigwam* — A domed tent dwelling housing multiple families
- *tipi* — A conical tent dwelling used by mobile hunting bands
- *okimahkan* — A temporary war leader appointed during conflict
- *pinesiwak* — Thunderbirds, spiritual beings in Cree belief
- *Plains Cree* — Cree bands who adopted bison hunting on the Great Plains
- *mooseskin* — Smoked hide used for clothing and embroidery
- *birchbark biting* — Art made by biting folded birchbark to create designs
- *beadwork* — Decorative work using glass seed beads in floral patterns
- *shot pouch* — A sewn container for hunting ammunition
- *gorget* — A decorative necklace or choker worn at the throat
- *Sun Dance* — A two-day ceremonial gathering and ritual performance
- *Iron Confederacy* — Alliance of Cree, Assiniboine, Métis and Saulteaux in the fur trade
- *Hudson's Bay Company* — European fur trading enterprise and colonial posts
- *animistic* — Worldview that conceives all entities as living with relationships and spirits
- *Michif* — A contact language with Cree and French origins
- *syllabics* — A writing system using symbols for syllables rather than individual sounds
