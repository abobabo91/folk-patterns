## Overview

The Kaska, or Kaska Dena, are a First Nations people of the Athabaskan-speaking ethnolinguistic group living mainly in northern British Columbia and the southeastern Yukon in Canada. Their region also extends into a small area of the southwestern Northwest Territories. Kaska Dena communities include Ross River, Watson Lake, Upper Liard, Good Hope Lake, Lower Post, Fireside, Muncho Lake, and Kwadacha First Nation. The people traditionally followed a seasonal round, hunting caribou and other game, gathering berries, fishing at salmon runs, and travelling by waterways and bush trails. The Kaska language is an endangered Athabaskan language. The Kaska are significant to folk-culture study through their named local bands, place-based knowledge, seasonal movements, language, and efforts to revitalize Kaska cultural and oral materials.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe a traditional seasonal round in which Kaska people followed game such as caribou, gathered seasonal foods such as berries, travelled along waterways, and made seasonal migrations to salmon runs at *Tu disdis Tue* (Pelly River). Seasonal fishing also took place at *Tuts Algua* (Watson Lake), or *Lu cho*, and game was harvested along *Agedze Tue* (Hyland River).

## Foodways

They state that Kaska people followed seasonal food resources, including caribou, berries, salmon, and other game. The Kaska used waterways such as the Liard River and Frances River to travel, and seasonal fishing was undertaken at Tuts Algua, or Lu cho.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kaska was traditionally an oral aboriginal language used by the Kaska Dena people. They state that Kaska Dena communities have worked toward building Kaska-language written and oral materials, including programs such as culture camps and training programs. The Kaska language article also notes that Kaska narratives use grammatical distinctions involving aspect and mood: imperfective forms commonly accompany a humorous tone, while perfective forms tend to express a more serious tone.

## Language & religion

Kaska is an endangered Athabaskan language traditionally spoken by the Kaska Dena people. The Kaska Dene region includes the southeastern Yukon, northern British Columbia, and a small area in the southwestern Northwest Territories. The language is made up of eight dialects, described as having similar pronunciations and expressional terms. The sources state that there were around 200 speakers as of 2011, and that Kaska is listed as Status 7, “shifting.” Kaska Dena Elders are mostly the fluent speakers, although the language is taught in schools in Good Hope Lake, Lower Post, Watson Lake, and Ross River.

Kaska is polysynthetic, commonly features sentence words, and has Subject-Object-Verb structure when a sentence contains two independent nominals. It is head-final and uses nine prefix positions before a stem verb morpheme. The language does not mark control or grammatical gender. Its vowels include /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, and /u/, which produce about 60 vowel sounds through combinations of tone, lengthening, and nasalization. Kaska expresses time primarily through aspect marking, including imperfective, perfective, and optative aspect.
