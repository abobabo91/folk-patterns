## Overview

The Monacan are an Indigenous people of the Northeastern Woodlands and members of the federally recognized Monacan Indian Nation. Their traditional territory lay in the Piedmont west and upland of the Tidewater, between the Fall Line and the Blue Ridge Mountains, with historic settlements along the James and Rivanna rivers. Today the nation is located primarily in its traditional Piedmont region, particularly in Amherst County near Lynchburg, with satellite groups in West Virginia, Maryland, Tennessee, and Ohio. As of 2018, the Monacan Indian Nation had approximately 2,000 citizens. Their native language belongs to the Siouan-Catawban language family. Their history includes severe disruption through land loss, warfare, intermarriage, discrimination, colonial displacement, and the interruption of historical records, followed by cultural reorganization and recognition as a Native American people.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources identify historic Monacan towns and settlements. A model of an ancient Monacan village has been constructed as part of Natural Bridge State Park in Rockbridge County.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They state that men in the Monacan towns of Mowhemencho and Mahock possessed muskets in 1670 and that Monacans and the Erie were historically trade allies, especially in copper.

## Music & performance

The supplied article titled “Monacan music” concerns Monaco, a sovereign city-state on the French Riviera, rather than the Monacan people.

## Festivals & rituals

The Monacan Indian Nation operates a yearly powwow in May and a homecoming celebration in October.

## Foodways

The sources record a Monacan tradition that the Doeg occupied the area before the Monacans displaced them and that the Monacans taught them the art of growing corn.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources record several Monacan traditions. Lederer wrote that the Monacans said they had settled in the area because of an oracle four hundred years earlier and had been driven from the northwest by an enemy nation. They also told him that they had found the area occupied by the Doeg and eventually displaced them, while teaching them to grow corn. Another recorded tradition states that the human race derived from four women, Pash, Sepoy, Askarin, and Maraskarin, whose names distinguished four tribes.

## Language & religion

The Monacan language is identified as part of the Siouan-Catawban language family. The sources state that Peter Wynne reported in November 1608 that some members of the Jamestown expedition thought the language resembled “Welch” or Welsh, although the language was not Welsh and Wynne could not understand it.
