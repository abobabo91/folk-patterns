## Overview

The Mandan lived in permanent villages along the Upper Missouri River and nearby rivers. They built large, round earth lodges and farmed corn. Their corn surpluses were traded for bison meat and fat, horses, guns, and other goods. Their language and ceremonies are closely linked to long contact with Hidatsa and Crow.

## Material culture

Mandan material culture centered on earth lodges, farming, storage pits, canoes, paddles, robes, rattles, and ceremonial objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources give little detail about Mandan textile traditions, but they mention painted buffalo robes and figures.

- **Buffalo robes** — Buffalo Dance figures wore buffalo robes that were elaborately painted.
- **Painted buffalo figures** — Painted buffalo figures appeared in British Museum records of the Buffalo Dance.

### Clothing & dress

The sources provide very little information about everyday Mandan clothing.

- **Mandan shoe** — A museum record identifies a shoe from the Plains United States as associated with the Mandan.
- **Buffalo Dance robe** — Buffalo Dance figures wore elaborately painted buffalo robes.

### Architecture

Mandan villages had large, round earth lodges around a central plaza, with defenses around some settlements.

- **Earth lodge** — Permanent villages contained large, round earth lodges about 40 feet in diameter.
- **Double Ditch Village** — Double Ditch Village had two defensive trenches, former lodge depressions, and cache pits.
- **Huff Village** — Huff Village had stronger fortifications around the settlement.
- **Like-a-Fishhook Village** — Like-a-Fishhook Village was built upriver by Mandan survivors and Hidatsa with common protection in mind.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources name storage pits, canoes, paddles, rattles, long sticks, and the Ark or Big Canoe, but give little detail about their making.

- **Cache pit** — Cache pits stored dehydrated corn.
- **Canoe paddle** — A paddle from the Fort Berthold Indian Agency was intended for a canoe.
- **Rattle** — Rattles were used in Buffalo Dance scenes.
- **Long stick** — Long sticks were carried by buffalo figures in Buffalo Dance scenes.
- **Big Canoe** (*Ark*) — The Ark or Big Canoe stood in the centre of the village during the Medicine Lodge ceremony.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention tattooing but do not describe Mandan jewelry or other adornment.

- **Those who tattooed themselves** (*Is’tope*) — The Is’tope was a band glossed as “those who tattooed themselves.”

## Music & performance

Mandan ceremonial performance used rattles and a medicine pipe, while dancing and singing were described as orderly amusements.

- **Buffalo Dance rattle** — Buffalo figures held rattles during the Buffalo Dance.
- **Medicine pipe** — The Master of Ceremonies held the Medicine Pipe during the Medicine Lodge ceremony.
- **Dancing and singing** — David Thompson recorded dancing and singing as Mandan amusements conducted with order and decorum.

## Dance & theatre

The Buffalo Dance used painted buffalo figures, an Evil Spirit, and a dramatic defeat scene.

- **Buffalo Dance** (*Bel-lohk Na-pic*) — Eight buffalo figures performed a dance connected with the supply of food during the coming year.
- **Evil Spirit** (*O-ke-hee-de*) — The Evil Spirit entered the dancing area after being defeated by the medicine pipe.
- **Defeat of the Evil Spirit** — Women and children surrounded and pelted the Evil Spirit with sticks and dirt.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources document the annual Okipa, the Medicine Lodge ceremony, and the Buffalo Dance.

- **Okipa ceremony** (*Okipa*) — The Okipa was a sacred annual ceremony that George Catlin watched in 1832.
- **Medicine Lodge ceremony** (*Religious Ceremony*) — The annual “Religious Ceremony” of the Mandans was held in the Medicine Lodge.
- **Water-producing turtles** — Mandan tradition says turtles used in the Okipa produced water that protected people when Nuptadi Village was burned.
- **Buffalo creation ceremony** — The Buffalo Dance was connected with the creation of the Buffalos and food for the coming year.

## Foodways

Mandan foodways centered on women’s farming, especially corn, and on trade in corn and bison products.

- **Corn farming** — Women carried out extensive farming, and corn was a major crop and principal trade good.
- **Dehydrated corn** — Villages stored dehydrated corn in cache pits.
- **Bison meat and fat** — Mandan corn surpluses were exchanged for bison meat and fat.
- **Horse use** — Horses were used for transportation, carrying packs, pulling travois, and hunting.

## Oral tradition & literature

Mandan traditions tell of ancestral origins, the teaching of farming, protective turtles, and remembered warfare.

- **Hidatsa origin story** — Mandan oral tradition says the Mandan taught the once-nomadic Hidatsa to build villages and farm.
- **Eastern place of origin** — Mandan tradition refers to an eastern place near a lake as an ancestral origin.
- **Turtle protection story** — A story says turtles produced water to protect people when Nuptadi Village was burned.
- **Winter count of Butterfly** — Butterfly’s winter count records Mandan warfare in 1835–1836.

## Language & religion

Mandan is a Siouan language with named dialects, and its documented religious practices include the Okipa and Medicine Lodge ceremony.

- **Mandan language** (*Núu’etaa íroo*) — Mandan, also called Núu’etaa íroo, belongs to the Siouan language family.
- **Main dialects** (*Núu’etaa/Nuu’etaare; Rúptaa/Rúptaare*) — The language has two main dialects, and the sources also name Nuptare and Nuetare.
- **Nuptare variety** (*Nuptare*) — Only the Nuptare variety survived into the 20th century, and its speakers were bilingual in Hidatsa.
- **Secret language of medicine men** — The sources say medicine men had their own secret language.
