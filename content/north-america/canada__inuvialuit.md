## Overview

The Inuvialuit, also called Western Canadian Inuit, are Inuit who live in the western Canadian Arctic region. Their homeland, the Inuvialuit Settlement Region, follows the Arctic Ocean coastline from the Alaskan border through the Beaufort Sea and beyond the Amundsen Gulf, including parts of the western Canadian Arctic Islands, Aklavik, and part of Yukon. Inuvialuit number approximately 3,100. Their traditional language is Inuvialuktun, which consists of three or four dialects. Inuvialuit culture is documented through hunting, fishing, trapping, seasonal subsistence, named games, regional settlement histories, and continuing relationships with places such as Herschel Island and Tarium Niryutait.

## Material culture

### Architecture

 They identify former and current settlements, including Kitigaaryuit, the villages of Kitigaaryuk and Tchenerark, the adjacent village of Kuugaatchiaq, Inuvik, Aklavik, Tuktoyaktuk, Sachs Harbour, Paulatuk, and Ulukhaktok.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They do mention wooden handles and sharp nails as equipment in the game *napataak*, and rocks used in *iglukisaaq*.

## Music & performance

 They do record traditional Inuit games, including *akimuq*, a high kick game; *ayahaaq*, a string game; *iglukisaaq*, juggling rocks; *mak*, played by trying to make a person laugh; and *napataak*, darts played with a wooden handle and sharp nail.

## Festivals & rituals

 They do describe a seasonal cycle of subsistence activities. In spring, Inuvialuit fish and hunt geese and grizzly. In summer, they whale, fish, gather berries, roots, and medicinal plants. In autumn, they fish, seal, hunt geese, and gather plants. In winter, they fish, seal, and hunt polar bear.

## Foodways

 They document hunting, fishing, trapping, whaling, sealing, geese hunting, polar bear hunting, caribou hunting, and the gathering of berries, roots, medicinal plants, and other plants as subsistence activities. Inuvialuit hunt caribou year-round from the Cape Bathurst and Bluenose herds and have also shared the Porcupine herd with the Gwichʼin.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They do record regional naming practices: people living in the west are called *Ualinirmiut* or *Ualiniq* by people of the east, while people occupying the east are known as *Kivaninmiut* or *Kivaliniq* by people of the west.

## Language & religion

The traditional language is known as Inuvialuktun and is made up of three or four dialects. Uummarmiutun is spoken by the Uummarmiut of Aklavik and Inuvik; it is an Iñupiaq dialect but is usually associated with Inuvialuktun. Sallirmiutun, formerly Siglitun, is spoken by the Siglit of Sachs Harbour, Paulatuk, Tuktoyaktuk, and Inuvik. Kangiryuarmiutun is used by the Kangiryuarmiut of Ulukhaktok and is essentially the same as Inuinnaqtun, also used in Nunavut communities including Kugluktuk, Bathurst Inlet, and Cambridge Bay. Natsilingmiutut is used by the Netsilik of Gjoa Haven, Taloyoak, Kugaaruk, and Naujaat in Nunavut. Uummarmiutun, Siglitun, and Inuinnaqtun, or Kangiryuarmiutun, are written using Latin script, while Natsilingmiutut is written in Inuktitut syllabics.
