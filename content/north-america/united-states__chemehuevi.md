## Overview

The Chemehuevi are an Indigenous people of the Great Basin and the southernmost branch of Southern Paiute. Their traditional territory extended across the High Desert from the Colorado River to the Tehachapi Mountains, and from the Las Vegas area and Death Valley to the San Bernardino and San Gabriel Mountains. They lived primarily in the eastern Mojave Desert, Cottonwood Island in Nevada, and the Chemehuevi Valley along the Colorado River in California. They traditionally lived in small nomadic groups because of the sparse desert resources. The Chemehuevi language belongs to the Colorado River Numic branch of the Uto-Aztecan language family and is now near extinction. The people call themselves *Nüwüvi*, “The People,” or *Tantáwats*, meaning “Southern Men.”

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources describe one documented material tradition:

**Coiled willow baskets** — Chemehuevi makers used a three-rod foundation of willow. Split willow formed most of the weaving, while darker patterns were made with devil's claw and yucca. The style continues to be practiced by a small group of weavers.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name darker patterns.

## Music & performance

The sources state that Carobeth Laird described Chemehuevi people as able to “sing strange songs,”. The sources also mention that Chemehuevi people were willing to speak strange tongues and visit other tribes, but provide no further account of musical practice.

## Foodways

The interpretation of the name “Chemehuevi” as a Mojave term meaning “those who play with fish” does not provide a description of foodways.

## Oral tradition & literature

The most comprehensive collection of Chemehuevi history, culture, and mythology was gathered by Carobeth Laird and George Laird. Carobeth Laird’s *The Chemehuevis*, published in 1976, presents an account of traditional culture and language as George Laird remembered it and is described as the first and, at the time of the source’s writing, only ethnography of Chemehuevi traditional culture.

## Language & religion

Chemehuevi is a Colorado River Numic language in the Numic branch of the Uto-Aztecan language family. Colorado River Numic is described as a dialect chain extending from southeastern California to Colorado, with Chemehuevi, Southern Paiute, and Ute as its three major dialect groups. Chemehuevi is in danger of extinction; during the filming of the 2008 documentary *The Linguists*, Greg Anderson and K. David Harrison interviewed and recorded one of the last 3 remaining speakers. The language was first transcribed by John P. Harrington and Carobeth Laird, studied in the 1970s by Margaret L. Press, and taught to children at Siwavaats Junior College in Havasu Lake, California, established in 2015.
