## Overview

The Slavey, also known as Awokanak, Slave, and South Slavey, are a First Nations group of Indigenous peoples in Canada and part of the Dene people. Their homelands include the Great Slave Lake region, Canada's Northwest Territories, northeastern British Columbia, and northwestern Alberta. They speak Slavey, a group of Athabaskan languages. The South Slavey live in northwestern Alberta, northeastern British Columbia, and the southern Northwest Territories, while the Sahtu, or North Slavey, live exclusively in the Northwest Territories.

## Foodways

They state that the K’ashógot’įne, or Hare dialect, was spoken by the Gahwié got’iné, “Rabbitskin People,” or K’áshogot’ıne, “Great Hare People,” referring to their dependence on the varying hare for food and clothing. ## Language & religion

Slavey is a group of Athabaskan languages spoken among the Dene peoples of Canada, particularly in the Northwest Territories, or central Denendeh, where it has official status. North Slavey is spoken by the Sahtu people around Great Bear Lake, the middle Mackenzie River, and the Mackenzie Mountains; South Slavey is spoken in the region of Great Slave Lake, the upper Mackenzie River and its drainage, northwest Alberta, and northeast British Columbia. The dialects include Hare, Bear Lake, and Mountain varieties. North and South Slavey are official languages of the Northwest Territories and may be used in court and in debates and proceedings of the Northwest Territories legislature. The languages are primarily written using a modified Latin script, with some using Canadian Aboriginal syllabics.
