## Overview

The Westo were an Iroquoian Native American tribe encountered by Europeans in what became the Southeastern U.S. during the 17th century. Their first historical appearance was as a powerful tribe in colonial Virginia, where they had migrated from the north into the region around present-day Richmond. They later moved south to the Savannah River, near what became Augusta, Georgia, and their principal village was called *Hickauhaugau*. Estimates of their population range from 600 to 2,000 warriors, with the likelihood that they numbered 700 to 900 warriors and 3,000 or more persons. They probably spoke an Iroquoian language. The Westo are documented chiefly through colonial warfare, migration, raiding, and participation as middlemen in the Indian slave trade.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Westo architecture is documented only briefly. Their principal village, *Hickauhaugau*, stood near the Savannah River near present-day Augusta, Georgia, although its precise location has not been determined. The village had longhouses and palisades typical of Iroquoian villages further north. These features strengthened the view that the Westo were Erie.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do mention firearms, guns, ammunition, canoes, deerskins, and furs in connection with warfare and trade.

## Music & performance

No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription in the supplied material concerns this ethnic group.

## Festivals & rituals

The historical record does describe military actions and raids, including the destruction of the Spanish mission of Santo Domingo de Talaje on July 20, 1661, and a raid on the Spanish Mission of Santa Catalina de Guale in 1680, but these are historical events rather than documented festivals or rituals.

## Foodways

The records mention deerskins and furs as trade goods and state that the Westo captured slaves and exchanged them for guns, ammunition, and other items.

## Oral tradition & literature

The supplied material records historical interpretations of Westo origins, including the hypothesis that they were a group of Erie displaced south during the Beaver Wars, but it does not preserve Westo oral narratives.

## Language & religion

The Westo probably spoke an Iroquoian language. The Spanish called them *Chichimeco* or *Chichimecos*, and Virginia colonists may have called the same people *Richahecrian* or *Rickohockans*. They mention the Haudenosaunee League, the Seneca, the Muscogee Creeks, and Spanish missionary provinces in the historical setting.
