## Overview

The Potawatomi lived first in Lower Michigan, then moved through northern Wisconsin to northern Indiana and central Illinois. Today, federally recognized tribes are found in Wisconsin, Michigan, Kansas, Oklahoma, and Illinois, with related First Nations in Ontario. They call themselves Neshnabé and Bodéwadmi, meaning “keepers of the fire” in the Council of Three Fires. Their language and cultural knowledge are being preserved through recordings, books, and a master-apprentice program.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

### Clothing & dress

Museum records show Potawatomi people in both traditional-looking clothing and European-style suits.

- **Councillor's clothing** (*O-ta-wa*) — The Sucker, a Potawatomi councillor, wears a cloth shirt, leggings, moccasins, a cloth turban, and a blanket while holding a pipe.
- **Chief's clothing** (*Mzhik-ki-an*) — Thunder Coming Down To The Ground, a Potawatomi chief, is shown in clothing ranging from a cloth shirt and jacket to a three-piece suit, with a blanket.
- **Chief's formal clothing** (*Se-nadz-win*) — White Bear, a Potawatomi chief, wears a bow tie, shirt, waistcoat, jacket, and trousers.
- **Formal suit** (*Wa-wa-suk*) — The Flash Of A Sturgeon In Turning wears a three-piece suit and neck tie.
- **Hiawatha play clothing** — A man dressed for a Hiawatha play wears a porcupine hair roach, eagle feather, shell gorget, fringed shirt, beaded bandolier bag, quill-work armbands, beaded ling covering, and fringed trousers.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The profile names a few museum objects but gives little detail about their everyday use.

- **Web-woven bowl** — In the Water Spider story, a bowl woven from spider web carries a hot coal.

## Festivals & rituals

Potawatomi religious life includes the Seven Grandfather Teachings and the Water Spider's celebration, but no annual calendar is given.

- **Three Fires** — The Potawatomi, Odawa, and Ojibwe form a confederation called the Three Fires.
- **Seven Grandfather Teachings** — Children are taught wisdom, respect, love, honesty, humility, bravery, and truth toward their people and all creation.
- **Water Spider celebration** — After Water Spider brings back a hot coal, the animals honor her bravery in a great celebration.

## Foodways

The profile names two plants used in traditional Potawatomi cuisine.

- **Wild leek** (*Allium tricoccum*) — Allium tricoccum is eaten in traditional Potawatomi cuisine.
- **Velvetleaf blueberry** (*Vaccinium myrtilloides*) — Vaccinium myrtilloides is eaten fresh, dried, and canned, and its root bark is used for an unspecified ailment.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Water Spider story teaches patience, listening, and bravery, while language workers preserve Potawatomi knowledge.

- **Water Spider story** — The Water Spider listens to the other animals, retrieves a hot coal in a web-woven bowl, and is honored for her bravery.
- **Potawatomi videos and books** — Donald Neaseno Perrot made Potawatomi videos and books to preserve language and cultural knowledge.
- **Master-apprentice program** — The Pokagon Band began a program in 2013 pairing language apprentices with fluent Potawatomi speakers.

## Language & religion

Potawatomi is a declining Central Algonquian language, and its traditional religion has no formal leadership or organization.

- **Northern Potawatomi** — Northern Potawatomi is spoken in Ontario, Michigan, and Wisconsin.
- **Southern Potawatomi** — Southern Potawatomi is spoken in Kansas and Oklahoma.
- **Pedagogical System** — The Pedagogical System is the most commonly used writing system for Potawatomi.
- **BWAKA system** — The BWAKA system is used for Potawatomi in Kansas.
- **Traditional syllabic writing** — Potawatomi communities have also used a traditional syllabic writing system that is not included in Unicode standards.
