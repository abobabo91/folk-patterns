## Overview

The Quechan, also called Yuma, are a Native American tribe living on the Fort Yuma Indian Reservation along the lower Colorado River in Arizona and California, just north of the Mexican border. The reservation lies near the city of Yuma, Arizona, and includes land in southeastern Imperial County, California, and western Yuma County, Arizona. The federally recognized Quechan tribe’s main office is in Winterhaven, California. The tribe had a resident reservation population of 2,376 in the 2000 census, while about 4,000 active members were reported in 2023. The Quechan language belongs to the Yuman language family. Historically, Quechan-speaking people were skilled warriors and active traders whose exchange networks connected them with the Pima, New Mexico, and peoples of the Pacific coast.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They mention that Juan Bautista de Anza gave Palma’s people clothes, together with horses, steel weapons, and iron, as a token of allegiance.

### Architecture

They mention Spanish mission settlements at San Pedro y San Pablo de Bicuñer and Puerto de Purísima Concepción.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Historical accounts in the sources mention horses, steel weapons, clothes, and iron as gifts given to Palma’s people by Juan Bautista de Anza.

## Music & performance

The sources identify the Quechan language as part of the Yuman language family and mention a language guide created by the Quechan tribe in partnership with linguists, but provide no musical information.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that after 1840, Quechan people near La Frontera returned to their original ways of religious practice after the mission priests left and no one replaced them. The sources also describe the baptism of the chief Palma and three companions in Mexico City on February 13, 1777, when Palma received the Spanish baptismal name Salvador Carlos Antonio.

The sources record a major historical conflict associated with Spanish settlement: the Quechan rebellion lasted from July 17–19, 1781, during which four priests and thirty soldiers were killed and the Spanish mission settlements of San Pedro y San Pablo de Bicuñer and Puerto de Purísima Concepción were attacked and damaged.

## Foodways

They state that in 1780 livestock sent by the Spanish trampled most or all of the Quechan crops, and that severe lack of rain that year forced the Quechan to raid the Maricopa.

## Oral tradition & literature

They list *Xiipúktan (First of All): Three Views of the Origins of the Quechan People*, by George Bryant and Amy Miller, as a work in the World Oral Literature Series, but the supplied sources do not summarize its narratives.

The Wikipedia article lists Quechan traditional narratives among related subjects and identifies the Quechan language guide as a collaborative project of the Quechan tribe and linguists. The guide includes sections on the alphabet, actions, animals, the body, colors, directions, family and friends, house, money, nature and the environment, numbers, place names, plants, time, and shapes.

## Language & religion

The Quechan language belongs to the Yuman language family. The Quechan tribe has created a fully detailed language guide in partnership with linguists; the guide covers the alphabet and vocabulary relating to actions, animals, the body, colors, directions, family and friends, house, money, nature and the environment, numbers, place names, plants, time, and shapes.

The sources state that Quechan people near La Frontera returned to their original ways of religious practice after 1840, when the mission priests left and no one replaced them.
