## Overview

Nambé Pueblo is a federally recognized tribe of the Tewa Pueblo people in Santa Fe County, New Mexico. The Pueblo has existed since the 14th century and belongs to the Eight Northern Pueblos. It was a primary cultural, economic, and religious center when Spanish colonists arrived in the very early 17th century, and it organized and participated in the Pueblo Revolt of 1680. The 2010 census recorded 1,818 people living in the Nambé census-designated place; United States reports counted 568 people as exclusively Nambé and 723 as Nambé exclusively or in combination with another group. The Nambé language is a dialect of Tewa, also called Tano, within the Kiowa-Tanoan language family.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Pueblo refers both to the physical settlement and to a Southwestern style of architecture. The sources identify the first church, San Francisco de Nambé, built in Nambé Pueblo in the early 1600s.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention Nambé Pueblo Pottery as an external resource and identify Lonnie Vigil as a pottery artist.

## Festivals & rituals

Nambé Pueblo was a primary religious center at the arrival of Spanish colonists and participated in the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, which expelled the Spanish from New Mexico for twelve years. Catholic missionaries threatened native religious beliefs, renamed pueblos with saints’ names, and established San Francisco de Nambé in the early 1600s.

## Foodways

The sources state that the Spanish introduced peaches, peppers, and wheat to native communities. Nambé Pueblo’s earlier economy included cotton, crops, and labor demanded as taxes by Juan de Oñate after his arrival in 1598.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources explain that the term Anasazi is a Navajo term loosely translated as “Enemy Ancestors,” and that contemporary Puebloans do not want the term used. They also state that scholars believe Pueblo peoples descended from the Ancestral Pueblo people, possibly from the Mogollón and other ancient peoples, and that the Nambé emerged as a culture in present-day New Mexico after migration from canyon homelands.

## Language & religion

Nąnbeˀ Ówîngeh is given as a Tewa name for Nambé Pueblo. Nambé is the Spanish version of a similar-sounding Tewa word that can be interpreted loosely as “rounded earth.” The Nambé language is a Tewa dialect, also called Tano, belonging to the Kiowa-Tanoan language family. Nambé was historically a cultural, economic, and religious center, while Catholic missionaries threatened native religious beliefs after Spanish contact.
