## Overview

The Ponca homeland was near the mouth of the Niobrara River. Today, Ponca communities are based in Oklahoma and Nebraska. Their history includes forced removal, the Ponca Trail of Tears, and the court case Standing Bear v. Crook. Cultural work continues through language classes, learning materials, and revival efforts.

## Material culture

Ponca material culture is best documented through clothing, jewelry, blankets, weapons, and other personal objects in museum records.

### Textile & pattern traditions

- **Quill work** — Quill work appears as decoration on hide leggings.
- **Floral cloth** — Floral cloth appears in museum records as an object and clothing material.
- **Beadwork** — Beadwork appears in descriptions of Ponca objects and clothing.

### Clothing & dress

Museum records describe shirts, blankets, leggings, moccasins, waistcoats, and headdresses.

- **Hide leggings with quill work** — Iron Whip wore hide leggings decorated with quill work.
- **Trailer warbonner** — White Tail wore a trailer warbonner.
- **Cloth shirt and waistcoat** — White Tail was recorded wearing a cloth shirt and waistcoat.
- **Wool blankets** — A portrait of four Ponca chiefs records wool blankets.
- **Moccasins** — Ponca men in the records wore moccasins.

### Architecture

A prehistoric Ponca village had large circular homes along the Niobrara River.

- **Circular village homes** — The homes were up to sixty feet across and stood along almost two miles of the riverbank.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

- **Tomahawks** — Tomahawks appear in a portrait of four Ponca chiefs.
- **Sword** — A sword appears in museum records of Ponca personal possessions.
- **Blankets** — Blankets appear among the objects recorded in photographic portraits.
- **Furs** — Furs appear in museum records of Ponca possessions.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ponca adornment included necklaces, chokers, ear ornaments, medals, arm ornaments, feathers, and headdresses.

- **Bear claw necklace** — Iron Whip wore a bear claw necklace.
- **Diadanum shell necklace** — One catalogue record describes a diadanum shell necklace.
- **Bead choker** — White Tail wore a bead choker.
- **Peace medal** — White Tail wore a peace medal.
- **Fur-wrapped hair** — White Tail’s hair was arranged in two bunches wrapped in fur.

## Music & performance

The sources identify three Ponca co-founders of the Native American Church.

- **Native American Church** — Frank Eagle, Louis McDonald, and McKinley Eagle helped found the Native American Church in 1918.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not give a festival calendar or detailed life-cycle rites, but they record Standing Bear’s burial promise and its legal consequences.

- **Standing Bear’s burial journey** — Standing Bear left the Oklahoma reservation to bury his eldest son, Bear Shield, in the ancestral homeland.
- **Standing Bear v. Crook** — Standing Bear’s arrest during the journey led to the 1879 case that recognized Native Americans as persons under United States law.

## Foodways

The Ponca grew maize, kept vegetable gardens, and hunted buffalo.

- **Maize growing** — The Ponca grew maize, unlike most other Plains Indians.
- **Vegetable gardens** — The Ponca kept vegetable gardens.
- **Last successful buffalo hunt** — The Ponca’s last successful buffalo hunt took place in 1855.

## Oral tradition & literature

Ponca tradition says the people came to the Niobrara from east of the Mississippi shortly before Columbus’s arrival.

- **Migration tradition** — Ponca tradition holds that the people moved to the mouth of the Niobrara River from east of the Mississippi.
- **Louis Headman’s dictionary** — Louis Headman’s dictionary of the Ponca People supports language preservation.

## Language & religion

Omaha–Ponca has two closely related dialects, few elderly fluent speakers, and active revitalization work.

- **Omaha–Ponca** (*Páⁿka*) — Omaha–Ponca is a Dhegihan language spoken by Omaha and Ponca people in Nebraska and Oklahoma.
- **Ponca dialect** — As of 2008, the sources reported 35 fluent Ponca speakers, all elderly.
- **Language classes** — Language classes and Internet-based learning materials support revitalization.
- **Umónhon Nation Public School** — The Umónhon Nation Public School is mentioned in language-revitalization work.
- **Native American Church** — Three Ponca men helped co-found the Native American Church in 1918.
