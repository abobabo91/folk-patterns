## Overview

The Klickitat, also spelled Klikitat, are a Native American tribe of the Pacific Northwest. Most Klickitat are enrolled in the federally recognized Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakama Nation, while some are part of the Confederated Tribes of the Grand Ronde Community of Oregon. Their ancestral lands lay north of the Columbia River, at the headwaters of the Cowlitz, Lewis, White Salmon, and Klickitat rivers, in present-day Klickitat and Skamania Counties. They were a Shahaptian tribe whose eastern neighbors were the Yakama and whose western neighbors included Salishan and Chinookan tribes. The Klickitat were active and enterprising traders, serving as intermediaries between coastal tribes and communities east of the Cascade Mountains. Their name remains attached to Klickitat County, Klickitat, the Klickitat River, and other places and objects.

### Architecture

The historical sources identify Klickitat villages and locations, including Itkilak, also given as Ithlkilak, at White Salmon Landing; Nanshuit at Underwood; Shgwaliksh below Memaloose Island; Tgasgutcu near White Salmon Landing; and Wiltkun, whose exact location is unknown. ## Festivals & rituals

The historical account states that the Klickitat joined in the Yakima treaty at Camp Stevens on June 9, 1855, after the Klickitat War erupted in 1855. They ceded their lands to the United States, and most settled upon the Yakama Indian Reservation, with a minority on the Grand Ronde Community. The sources do document historical naming and language preservation. The Klickitat call themselves *Qwû'lh-hwai-pûm* or *χwálχwaypam*, meaning “prairie people,” with the form *X̣ʷáɬx̣ʷaypam* also given. The Yakama called them *Xwálxwaypam* or *L'ataxat*. These names are linguistic evidence rather than a described oral-literary tradition.

## Language & religion

The Klickitat are identified as a Shahaptian tribe. Their eastern neighbors, the Yakama, speak a closely related language. The supplied language article describes Sahaptin, also called Ichishkiin, as belonging to the Sahaptian branch of the Plateau Penutian family. It is spoken in the northwestern plateau along the Columbia River and its tributaries in southern Washington, northern Oregon, and southwestern Idaho, and is used by tribes connected with the Washington Reservations, including Yakama, Warm Springs, and Umatilla.

The Yakama Nation tribal cultural resources program has promoted *Ichishkíin Sɨ́nwit* as the traditional name of the language. The sources describe dialect names including *Ichishkíin*, *Ichishkínk*, *Chishkín*, *Čiškíin*, and *Ičiškíin*. They also state that Sahaptin has split ergative syntax, direct-inverse voicing, and applicative constructions. The sources do not discuss Klickitat religion, Christianity, ceremonial specialists, cosmology, or spiritual practices.
