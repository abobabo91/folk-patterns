## Overview

The Pawnee organized their year between river-bottom farming and long-range bison hunts, with a cosmology centered on stars and the cosmos. Their earth lodges were built as scale models of the sky, with smoke holes aligned to observe the Pleiades and doors facing east to catch the vernal equinox sunrise. They cultivated corn, beans and squashes using varieties maintained in sacred bundles, while hunting bison, elk and other game across hundreds of miles. Their culture was shaped by star gods and the four cardinal directions, with ceremonies tied to the agricultural and hunting calendar.

## Material culture

Pawnee material culture emphasizes hide, leather, wood and shell rather than pottery, with quillwork and decorative techniques applied to practical items like shields, reins and clothing.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Pawnee applied quillwork and flannel trim to hide and leather gear rather than weaving cloth.

- **Quilled and flannel-trimmed leather rein** — Brown leather rein ornamented with red flannel, quills and a brass bell, with a leather disc perforated at the centre with a star.
- **Painted hide shield** — Leather shield painted green with a large red disc on the upper part and further devices below.
- **Corn color system** — Black, spotted, white, yellow and red corn, with the four unspotted colours corresponding to the four semi-cardinal directions.

### Clothing & dress

Men wore decorated fringed or tasselled shirts, embroidered leggings and moccasins, with blankets or buffalo robes, while women wore blankets with shell ornaments.

- **Warrior shirt with tassels** — Fringed or tasselled shirt worn with embroidered and tasselled leggings, documented in studio portraits.
- **Fur turban** — Headgear made of fur with two shells and two eagle feathers attached.
- **Shell ear ornaments** — Near-universal adornment worn by both men and women in portraits.
- **Bear-claw-and-fur necklace** — Neck ornament combining bear claws with fur.
- **Peace medals** — Government gifts worn as insignia by chiefs during negotiations.

### Architecture

The Pawnee built earth lodges on river banks with oval plans, central smoke holes and doors facing east, designed as scale models of the sky.

- **Earth lodge** — Oval structure with ten to fifteen posts set about ten feet apart outlining a central room, smaller poles tied with willow withes, thatch and earth, with a recessed floor about three feet below ground.
- **Cardinal direction posts** — Four, eight or twelve centre poles in the lodge, with four painted to stand for the four cardinal directions and the four major star gods.
- **Smoke hole** — Central hole serving as chimney, smoke vent and skylight, aligned to allow sighting of the Pleiades.
- **Hinged buffalo-skin door** — Door facing east and the rising sun, opening onto a long low passageway and entry room, able to be wedged shut at night.
- **Buffalo skull display** — Buffalo skull with horns displayed on the west side as great medicine.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pawnee everyday objects were made from hide, wood, metal and sinew rather than pottery.

- **Baby-carrier board** — Large flat board of brownish-yellow wood carved at the larger end with a square enclosing a circle.
- **Hunting arrow** — Combination of wood, metal, feather and sinew, used from horseback with a single arrow into the flank between ribs and hip capable of incapacitating a bison.
- **Tomahawk** — Hand weapon carried by men in portraits and during negotiations with the government.
- **Tomahawk pipe** — Hybrid tool combining a pipe and tomahawk, appearing in chief portraits.
- **Sacred bundle** — Bundle holding an ear of holy corn among other materials, kept to replenish the seasonal supply.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Shell, beads and natural materials like bear claws and eagle feathers dominated Pawnee adornment.

- **Shell necklace** — Necklace made of shells, recurring across studio portraits.
- **Shell choker** — Choker of shells worn around the neck.
- **Shell ear ornaments** — Shell earrings appearing near-universally in portraits on both men and women.
- **Eagle feather headdress** — Single eagle feather worn in the hair or attached to fur hats and turbans.
- **Beaded and shell jewelry** — Beaded necklaces, chains, pendants, earrings and finger rings worn by individual sitters.

## Music & performance

Pawnee music centered on ceremonial songs led by priests, with documented genres tied to specific rituals.

- **Spring Awakening songs** — Songs performed by a priest to open the Spring Awakening ceremony when Swimming Ducks stars were first spotted and rolling thunder heard from the west.
- **Morning Star ceremony songs** — Five-day sequence of songs describing the advancing stages of the Morning Star ritual.

## Dance & theatre

The Ghost Dance movement is the only named dance tradition documented for the Pawnee.

- **Ghost Dance** — Movement in which the Pawnee participated in the 1890s after relocating to Oklahoma.

## Festivals & rituals

The ceremonial year tracked the sky and crops, with major ceremonies tied to planting, harvesting and bison hunts.

- **Spring Awakening ceremony** (*Thunder ceremony*) — Most important ceremony meant to wake the earth and ready it for planting, opened by a priest when Swimming Ducks stars appeared on the north-eastern horizon and rolling thunder was heard from the west.
- **White Beaver ceremony** — Held by the Chawi band with the purpose of revitalizing hibernating animals rather than renewing the corn crop.
- **Morning Star ceremony** — Performed only by Village Across a Hill, standing outside the ceremonial calendar and involving an actual sacrifice when a man dreamed the Morning Star commanded it, in which a captured girl was ritually transformed into the Evening Star and killed at the star's rising.
- **Swimming Ducks stars** (*ickariʾ*) — Two small twinkling stars on the north-eastern horizon near the Milky Way that marked the opening of the Spring Awakening ceremony.
- **Holy Corn** (*Wonderful Corn*) — Archaic breed of corn grown solely for sacred bundles, with strains kept separate during cultivation.

## Foodways

The Pawnee cultivated ten varieties of corn, seven of squashes and pumpkins and eight of beans in river bottomlands, supplemented by bison hunting.

- **Holy Corn** (*Wonderful Corn*) — Archaic corn breed grown expressly to replenish sacred bundles for the winter and summer seasons.
- **Bison meat processing** — Women sliced flesh into strips and dried it on poles over slow fires, keeping it usable for months.
- **Eagle Corn soup** — Soup made from Pawnee Eagle Corn unavailable for ceremonies for 125 years, revived in November 2010 after sprouting the last 25 remaining seeds in 2005, with a taste described as almonds with cream.
- **Complete protein crops** — Combination of corn, beans and squashes planted in fertile river bottomlands that together made whole proteins.

## Oral tradition & literature

Pawnee cosmology centered on star gods and was recorded primarily by anthropologist James Rolfe Murie.

- **Morning Star and Evening Star creation narrative** — Creation story in which the male Morning Star in the east was made first as a warrior, and the female Evening Star in the west resisted the plan to create humankind until Morning Star overcame forces in the western sky, producing the first human being, a girl.
- **Tirawahut teachings** (*Atíʼas Tirawa, Our Father Above*) — Father god who taught the people tattooing, fire-building, hunting, agriculture, speech, clothing, ritual and sacrifice.
- **Mother Corn** (*Atira*) — Earth goddess and wife of Tirawahut.
- **Spirit animals** (*nahurac*) — Intermediaries relied upon by South Band Pawnee traditions, with five named lodges at Pahuk, Lalawakohtito, Ahkawitakol, Kitzawitzuk and Pahur.
- **Northeast Star** (*Big Black Meteoric Star*) — Major star served under Tirawahut, described by some Skidi as a buffalo bull carrying the heaven on his back.

## Language & religion

Pawnee is a Caddoan language with South Band and Skiri dialects, ergative-absolutive and polysynthetic, with only a handful of native speakers remaining.

- **Skiri dialect** (*pâri pakûru'*) — South dialect of Pawnee that became extinct in 2001 with the death of Lula Nora Pratt.
- **Sacred bundles** — Priest-tended bundles holding sacred corn and other materials, central to religious practice.
- **Star gods** — Six major stars serving under Tirawahut, forming the center of Pawnee religious life.
- **Four cardinal directions** — Fundamental organizing principle in Pawnee cosmology and lodge architecture.

## Glossary

- *ickariʾ* — Loup River
- *Chatiks si chatiks* — Men of Men, the Pawnee name for themselves
- *pâri pakûru'* — Pawnee language
- *Swimming Ducks* — Two small twinkling stars marking the Spring Awakening ceremony
- *Atíʼas Tirawa* — Our Father Above, the creator god who taught the people
- *Atira* — Mother Corn, earth goddess
- *Pah* — The Moon
- *Morning Star* — Male warrior star in the east, deity in cosmology
- *Evening Star* — Female star in the west involved in creation and ritual sacrifice
- *Big Black Meteoric Star* — Northeast Star serving under Tirawahut, described as a buffalo bull carrying heaven
- *nahurac* — Spirit animals that serve as intermediaries in South Band traditions
- *Holy Corn* — Archaic corn variety grown solely for sacred bundles
- *Pahuk* — One of five named spirit animal lodges
- *Skiri* — One of two main Pawnee dialects, now extinct
- *South Band* — One of two main Pawnee dialects
- *sacred bundle* — Priest-tended bundle holding sacred corn and ritual materials
- *earth lodge* — Oval dwelling with central smoke hole and posts representing cosmic directions
