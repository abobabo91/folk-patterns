## Overview

The Cheyenne are an Indigenous people of the Great Plains. The Tsétsėhéstȧhese and Só'taeo'o joined together in the early 19th century. Today they live mainly in Oklahoma and Montana. Their culture includes horse equipment, decorated clothing, sacred objects, and buffalo-hide bags.

## Material culture

Cheyenne material culture is known for quillwork, beadwork, painted buffalo hide, clothing, horse equipment, and sacred bundles.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Cheyenne decoration uses porcupine quills, glass beads, paint, leather, and buffalo hide.

- **Porcupine-quill decoration** (*quillwork*) — Quills were sorted, dyed, softened, flattened, and attached to clothing, moccasins, bags, and horse equipment.
- **Bead decoration** (*beadwork*) — Glass beads were sewn onto leather tobacco bags, pipe bags, leggings, moccasins, shirts, and other clothing.
- **Painted buffalo-hide bag** (*parfleche*) — A parfleche was a stiff untanned buffalo-hide bag for storing dried meat, sometimes painted red and green.
- **Leather pipe bag** (*pipe bag*) — Leather pipe bags could have beadwork, glass beads, fringe, and quillwork.
- **Saddle equipment** — A saddle with stirrups was recorded as Northern Cheyenne horse equipment.

### Clothing & dress

Cheyenne clothing included decorated shirts, leggings, moccasins, blankets, jackets, and head ornaments.

- **Decorated shirt** — Shirts could have quillwork, embroidery, beadwork, tassels, and fringe.
- **Decorated leggings** — Leggings could be made from hide and decorated with beadwork or embroidery.
- **Wool blanket** — Little Robe wore a wool blanket with quillwork around his waist.
- **Warrior shirt** — High Toe wore a warrior shirt with quillwork leggings and moccasins.
- **Military jacket** — Military jackets are included in the clothing records.

### Architecture

Older Cheyenne settlements used earth lodges, but portable tipis replaced them after the move to nomadic horse culture.

- **Earth lodge** — Earth lodges were formerly part of Cheyenne settlements.
- **Tipi** — Portable tipis became part of Cheyenne life after the adoption of nomadic Plains horse culture.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded everyday objects include bags, pipes, rattles, saddles, and other horse equipment.

- **Smoking pipe** — Museum records describe Cheyenne smoking pipes.
- **Medicine rattle** — A medicine rattle appears in the museum records.
- **Storage bag** — Storage bags were made from untanned buffalo hide and used for dried meat.
- **Horse equipment** — Horse equipment included saddles with stirrups.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Cheyenne adornment included shell, metal, feather, and hair ornaments.

- **Dentalium necklace or choker** (*dentalium*) — Records mention dentalium shell necklaces and chokers.
- **Abalone ear ornament** (*abalone shell*) — Abalone shell ear ornaments appear in the catalogue records.
- **Metal ear ornament** — Metal ear ornaments were worn with decorated clothing.
- **Gorget** — Gorgets are listed among the recorded forms of adornment.
- **Feather ornament** — Feather ornaments and eagle feathers were worn in the hair.

## Music & performance

The sources focus on sacred objects and ceremonies rather than detailed music traditions.

- **Sacred Arrows** (*Maahótse*) — The Sacred Arrows formed one of the two great covenants of the Cheyenne Nation.
- **Sacred Buffalo Hat** (*Ésevone*) — The Sacred Buffalo Hat formed the other great covenant of the Cheyenne Nation.
- **Ceremonial pipe** — A ceremonial pipe was associated with the Sacred Hat Bundle.

## Dance & theatre

- **Sun Dance** — The Sun Dance was given to the people by Tomȯsévėséhe and was connected with the Sacred Buffalo Hat.

## Festivals & rituals

Cheyenne ritual life centers on the Sacred Arrows, the Sacred Buffalo Hat, their keepers, and sacred lodges.

- **Sacred Arrows** (*Maahótse*) — The Sacred Arrows were received by Motsé'eóeve at Nóávóse, or Bear Butte, and kept in the maahéome.
- **Arrow Lodge** (*maahéome*) — The maahéome was the Arrow Lodge or Arrow Tepee where the Sacred Arrows were kept.
- **Sacred Buffalo Hat** (*Ésevone*) — The Sacred Buffalo Hat was received by Tomȯsévėséhe at Tȯhóonévose and symbolized female power.
- **Sacred Hat Lodge** (*vonȧhéome*) — The vonȧhéome, also called hóhkėha'éome, was the Sacred Hat Lodge or Sacred Hat Tepee.
- **Sacred Buffalo Hat Keeper** (*Tséá'enōvȧhtse*) — The Sacred Buffalo Hat Keeper had to belong to the Só'taeo'o.

## Foodways

Cheyenne foodways changed from fishing and farming foods to a mainly bison-based Plains diet.

- **Bison hunting** — The Cheyenne hunted bison and later relied mainly on bison after adopting Plains horse culture.
- **Wild rice gathering** — They formerly collected wild rice while living between the Mississippi River and Mille Lacs Lake.
- **Wild fruits and vegetables** — After moving onto the Plains, wild fruits and vegetables became part of their diet.
- **Dried meat storage** — Painted buffalo-hide bags were used to store dried meat.

## Oral tradition & literature

Cheyenne oral history tells how prophets received sacred objects and shaped Cheyenne society.

- **Sacred Arrows story** (*Motsé'eóeve*) — Motsé'eóeve received the Sacred Arrows and organized military societies, legal justice, and the Council of Forty-four peace chiefs.
- **Sacred Buffalo Hat story** (*Tomȯsévėséhe*) — Tomȯsévėséhe received the Sacred Buffalo Hat and gave the people ceremonies and the Sun Dance.
- **Sweet Medicine prophecy** (*Sweet Medicine*) — Sweet Medicine predicted the coming of the horse, the cow, the white man, and other new things.

## Language & religion

Cheyenne is an Algonquian language, and Cheyenne spiritual life centers on sacred objects, prophets, and ceremonies.

- **Cheyenne language** (*Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse*) — Cheyenne is spoken in Montana and Oklahoma and has an alphabet of 14 letters.
- **Só'taeo'o language** (*Só'taéka'ėškóne*) — The former Só'taeo'o language was so close to Cheyenne that it was sometimes called a Cheyenne dialect.
- **Creator** (*Ma'heo'o*) — Ma'heo'o was the divine figure connected with the sacred objects received by the prophets.
- **Sacred Hat Bundle** (*Hóhkėha'e*) — Hóhkėha'e was another name for the Sacred Buffalo Hat and its sacred tradition.
