## Overview

The Mdewakanton, also spelled Mdewákhaŋthuŋwaŋ and currently pronounced Bdewákhaŋthuŋwaŋ, are one of the sub-tribes of the Isanti (Santee) Dakota (Sioux). Their historic home is Mille Lacs Lake, called Mde Wákhaŋ or Bde Wákhaŋ, “Spirit/Mystic Lake,” in central Minnesota. Together with the Wahpekute, they formed the Upper Council of the Dakota or Santee Sioux. Their descendants are members of federally recognized tribes in Minnesota, South Dakota, and Nebraska, and of First Nations in Manitoba, Canada. The sources describe decentralized villages, named divisions and bands, treaty history, reservations, and continuing organized groups.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources describe Mdewakanton villages and reservations. One historical band, the Tintaotonwe, is glossed as “village on the prairie,” while the Kiyuska village Keoxa was at the side of today’s Winona, Minnesota.

## Music & performance

The historical material identifies leaders and chiefs, including Wabasha, Takopepeshene, Taoyateduta, Shakopee, and Marpiyawicasta.

## Festivals & rituals

The historical account records that the Mdewakanton negotiated an “Indian Removal” treaty with the U.S. government in the fall of 1837. It states that dwindling game populations associated with the American fur trade and the threat of starvation motivated the signing, and that the agreement concerned their lands east of the Mississippi River, including all islands in the river.

## Foodways

They state that declining populations of game and the threat of starvation were among the factors involved in the 1837 treaty negotiations.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that tradition presents the Mdewakanton as the leading tribe of Očhéthi Šakówiŋ. They also describe the alliance of seven Sioux tribes, called Oceti Sakowin or Očhéthi Šakówiŋ, “The Seven Council Fires.”

## Language & religion

The sources record Mdewakanton and Dakota terms, including Mde Wákhaŋ or Bde Wákhaŋ, “Spirit/Mystic Lake,” Isáŋyáthi, “Knife Makers,” Očhéthi Šakówiŋ, “The Seven Council Fires,” thuŋwaŋ, “settlers,” and towan, “village.” They identify the Mdewakanton as a sub-tribe of the Isanti (Santee) Dakota (Sioux). The name of Mille Lacs Lake is also explained through Dakota Mde Wákhaŋ/Bde Wákhaŋ, “Spirit/Mystic Lake.”
