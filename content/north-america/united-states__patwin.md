## Overview

The Patwin, also called Patween and Southern Wintu, are the southern branch of the Wintun group and are Indigenous to Northern California. Their historical territory included the western Sacramento Valley and areas between what is now Suisun, Vacaville, and Putah Creek. The Patwin were bordered by the Yuki, Nomlaki, Konkow, Nisenan, Plains Miwok, Bay Miwok, Coast Miwok, Wappo, Lake Miwok, and Pomo. Today, Patwin people are enrolled in the Cachil DeHe Band of Wintun Indians of the Colusa Indian Community of the Colusa Rancheria, the Kletsel Dehe Band of Wintun Indians, and the Yocha Dehe Wintun Nation. Their language belongs to the Wintuan branch of the Penutian language family. Patwin culture is documented through language work, basketry, healing, archaeology, and cultural reclamation.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Archaeological remains associated with the Patwin were discovered at the Mondavi Center construction site beginning in 1999, after which the University of California, Davis, built a Native American Contemplative Garden within the Arboretum.

## Music & performance

The available material identifies language and cultural preservation as important contemporary concerns, including teaching Patwin to the younger generation through the work associated with Bertha Wright Mitchell, also known as Auntie Bertha.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do identify a Native American Contemplative Garden at the University of California, Davis, as a project honoring the Patwin.

## Foodways

Before the Gold Rush, Patwin communities are described as living in settled villages throughout the western Sacramento Valley and engaging in hunting, gathering, and fishing. Acorn gathering and access to water were important resources.

## Oral tradition & literature

Contemporary cultural preservation is connected in the sources with the reclamation of the Yocha Dehe Wintun Nation and with the teaching and documentation of the Patwin language. As of 2012, the Tewe Kewe Cultural Center of the Yocha Dehe Wintun Nation had a California Indian Library Collection and an extensive Patwin language and history research section.

## Language & religion

Patwin, also called Patween and *Wintun Tʼewe*, is a critically endangered Wintuan language of Northern California. The sources state that one Patwin person was a documented first-language speaker as of 2021. Patwin has River Patwin, also called Valley Patwin, traditionally spoken along the Sacramento River in Colusa County, and Hill Patwin, spoken in the plains and foothills west of the river. The Patwin language is related to Nomlaki and Wintu; these languages belong to the Penutian language family and have close relations to Maiduan, Miwokan, Ohlone, and Yokuts. Southern Patwin became extinct shortly after contact and is very poorly attested.
