## Overview

The Secwépemc, also known by the exonym Shuswap, are a First Nation people residing in the interior of British Columbia in Canada. Their traditional territory, Secwepemcúĺecw, extends from the eastern Chilcotin Plateau, bordering Tŝilhqot'in Country, through the Cariboo Plateau and Thompson Country to Kamloops. It includes the Selkirk Mountains, the Big Bend of the Columbia River, and the northern part of the Columbia Valley region. Their historical territory covers approximately 145,000 square kilometres. The Secwépemc speak Secwepemctsín, an Interior Salish language. Traditionally, hunting, trading, and fishing supported their communities, while their oral history extends back approximately 10,000 years.

## Foodways

The sources state that the Secwépemc traditionally depended on hunting, trading, and fishing to support their communities. They also state that captive slaves were historically an important commodity and that captives were exchanged for salmon.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Secwépemc have an oral history that spans back approximately 10,000 years. The sources identify Mary Augusta Tappage as a storyteller and list Vera Manuel as a poet and playwright, Bev Sellars as a writer and activist, Phyllis Webstad as an author and creator of Orange Shirt Day, and Tania Willard as a curator and artist.

## Language & religion

Secwepemctsín, also called Shuswap, is an Interior Salish language and one of the Salishan languages. Based on 2006 census statistics, there were roughly 1,600 speakers. As of 2026, the number of fully fluent speakers had diminished to fewer than 100 individuals, all over the age of 70. Secwepemctsín is taught at Chief Atahm School through an immersion program for students from K–9, and Simon Fraser University’s Indigenous Language Program offers certificates, diplomas, and a post-graduate Master’s program in language proficiency.
