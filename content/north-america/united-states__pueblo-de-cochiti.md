## Overview

The Pueblo of Cochiti is a federally recognized tribe of Pueblo people associated with Cochiti, a historic pueblo in Sandoval County, New Mexico, United States. Cochiti is part of the Albuquerque metropolitan area and lies 22 miles southwest of Santa Fe. In the 2020 census, the community had a population of 479; the 2010 census recorded 528 people in the census-designated place. The Cochiti are a Keres people who speak Eastern Keres, a dialect of the Keresan language, which is a language isolate. Their cultural documentation in the sources emphasizes pueblo history, pottery, storytelling, language preservation, religious persecution and resistance, and the continuation of artistic practices from the early twentieth century into the twenty-first century.

### Architecture

Cochiti is described as a historic pueblo and as a historic district listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The sources also describe earlier settlements associated with the Cochiti people: cliff dwellings at Rito de los Frijoles in present-day Bandelier National Monument, the pueblo of Katishtya, Potrero Viejo on the Pajarito Plateau, and the temporary pueblo known as Hanut Cochiti. The San Buenaventura Mission at Cochiti was constructed during the period of Spanish Catholic missionary activity.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Potters of Cochiti and Kewa Pueblo, formerly Santo Domingo Pueblo, have made traditional pots for centuries. They developed styles for different purposes and expressed deep beliefs in their designs. Since the early decades of the twentieth century, these pots have been appreciated by audiences outside the pueblos. In the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, potters continued to use traditional techniques while expanding their designs and repertoire, and their pottery entered an international market.

## Festivals & rituals

The Pueblo celebrates the annual feast day of its patron saint, San Buenaventura, on July 14. The San Buenaventura Mission at Cochiti was part of the Spanish Catholic program of church construction and religious conversion. Spanish Catholic missionaries attacked Pueblo religion, renamed pueblos with Catholic saints’ names, routinely tortured tribes for practicing their traditional religion, and forced them into labor and/or slavery.

The Cochiti pueblo people took part in the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, an uprising of Native Americans against the Spaniards. After Spanish Governor Antonio de Otermin reconquered New Mexico, the Cochiti retreated with the Keresan tribes of San Felipe and Santo Domingo, now called Kewa, to Potrero Viejo. The Cochiti remained there until 1693, when they were forced to flee Spanish Governor Don Diego de Vargas and his troops.

## Oral tradition & literature

Storytelling is documented as an important occupation and artistic practice among people from Cochiti Pueblo. Helen Cordero is identified as a storyteller and potter. Laurencita Herrera, who lived from 1912 to 1984, is identified as a storyteller associated with pottery and vessels. The Herrera-Ortiz family of Cochiti Pueblo is known for traditional pottery and other forms of art.

The sources also describe a Keres Children’s Learning Center, an independent Keres immersion school founded in the early twenty-first century to aid preservation of the Keres language and culture. The school has added grades since its founding.

## Language & religion

The Cochiti speak Eastern Keres, a dialect of the Keresan language, which is a language isolate. The Western Keresan name for the people of Cochiti Pueblo is *K’úutìim’é*, translated as “People from the Mountains.” The article also records Eastern Keresan *Kotyit* and Navajo *Tǫ́ʼgaaʼ* as names associated with Cochiti.

The sources describe Spanish Catholic missionary efforts to attack Cochiti religion, rename pueblos with Catholic saints’ names, construct churches, and force religious and labor practices. They also describe the Pueblo Revolt of 1680 as resistance to Spanish rule. The annual feast day for the patron saint San Buenaventura is celebrated on July 14.
