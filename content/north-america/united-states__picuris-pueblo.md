## Overview

Picuris Pueblo is a historic pueblo in Taos County, New Mexico, United States, located on the western slopes of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains, 18 miles south of Taos Pueblo. The federally recognized Tiwa Pueblo people of the same name inhabit the community, which is a member of the Eight Northern Pueblos. Their own name for the pueblo is *P'įwweltha*, meaning “mountain warrior place” or “mountain pass place.” They speak the Picuris dialect of the Northern Tiwa language, part of the Kiowa-Tanoan language family. The 2020 census recorded a population of 83. Picuris matters in folk-culture terms through its continued customs, dances, micaceous pottery, weaving, beadwork, sculpture, jewelry, and its museum and annual arts-and-crafts fair.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Weaving** — The Picuris Pueblo Museum displays and sells works by local weavers. An Arts & Crafts fair of weaving, sculpture, beadwork, and jewelry is held the first weekend in July.

### Architecture

Picuris Pueblo is a historic pueblo on the western slopes of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains. The San Lorenzo de Picurís church was built of adobe by 1776 and restored in the 21st century. Excavated dwellings are located within the pueblo.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Picuris is particularly known for its micaceous pottery. The Picuris Pueblo Museum displays and sells the works of local potters, and the pueblo is associated with micaceous potters including Anthony Durand, Cora Durand, Maria Ramita Martinez, and Rosalie Simbola Aguilar.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Jewelry and beadwork are represented among the works displayed and sold at the Picuris Pueblo Museum and at the Arts & Crafts fair held the first weekend in July.

## Music & performance

The sources describe dances and public or private performance contexts. The major feast day is San Lorenzo’s Day on August 10, during which people of all ages engage in races, while the Sunset Dance is held on August 9. Corn Dances and Buffalo Dances take place in June and August and may be private ceremonies. Dances are also held on New Year’s Day and in late January and early February; these are open to the public.

## Dance & theatre

The named dances are the Sunset Dance, Corn Dances, and Buffalo Dances. The Sunset Dance is held on August 9, in connection with the major feast day of San Lorenzo’s Day on August 10. Corn Dances and Buffalo Dances are held in June and August and may be private ceremonies. Other dances take place on New Year’s Day and in late January and early February and are open to the public.

## Festivals & rituals

Picuris Pueblo’s major feast day is San Lorenzo’s Day on August 10. During the feast, people of all ages engage in races, and the Sunset Dance is held on August 9. Corn Dances and Buffalo Dances occur in June and August; the source states that these may be private ceremonies. Dances held on New Year’s Day and in late January and early February are open to the public. An Arts & Crafts fair of weaving, sculpture, beadwork, and jewelry is held the first weekend in July.

The Picuris people were influenced by Spanish colonialism, particularly through the adoption of Christian religious practices and the abandonment of traditional rituals and ceremonies. They later resumed their traditional customs, which are performed throughout the year, and became self-governing again in the mid-1920s.

## Foodways

The sources mention the Amaya Restaurant in Santa Fe, New Mexico, which opened in 1991 and served Native American cuisine.

## Language & religion

The Picuris people speak the Picuris dialect of the Northern Tiwa language, which belongs to the Kiowa-Tanoan language family. The pueblo’s own name is *P'įwweltha*, meaning “mountain warrior place” or “mountain pass place.” Spanish explorer Don Juan de Oñate named the pueblo “Pikuria,” meaning “those who paint.” The pueblo was influenced by Spanish Christian religious practices, and the San Lorenzo de Picurís church was built of adobe by 1776.
