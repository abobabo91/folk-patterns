## Overview

Jemez Pueblo is a federally recognized tribe and census-designated place in Sandoval County, New Mexico, United States. Among Jemez people, the pueblo is known as *Walatowa*. The population of the census-designated place was 1,963 at the 2020 census, while the tribe has about 3,400 citizens. Jemez Pueblo is part of the Albuquerque metropolitan area and lies in a rural setting near the Red Rocks formation. The pueblo is associated with the Towa language, an unwritten member of the Tanoan language family. Its cultural life includes religious leadership, ceremonial practices, agriculture, customary practices, and running traditions. The sources also connect Jemez Pueblo with the surviving remnant of the Pecos Pueblo population, which fled to Jemez Pueblo in 1838.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Jemez Pueblo is described as a rural pueblo and census-designated place located near the Red Rocks formation. The sources identify the pueblo at the center of the community and state that the place was likely the location of the Franciscan Mission San Diego de la Congregacion.

## Music & performance

 They do describe running as an old Jemez pastime and ceremonial activity. Before the advent of television at Jemez, tales of running feats were a major form of entertainment on winter nights. Races retained a ceremonial purpose connected with assisting the movement of the sun and moon or hastening the growth of crops, while also becoming a popular secular sport.

## Dance & theatre

 They describe ceremonial and secular running, but do not characterize it as dance or theatre.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources identify religious leadership and ceremonial practices. The leadership of the Pueblo of Jemez is annually selected by Jemez religious leaders. *Carex* is sacred and used in ceremonies. Running also has a ceremonial role: races were held to assist the movement of the sun and moon or to hasten the growth of crops.

## Foodways

The sources state that Jemez residents continued to raise chile peppers, corn, and wheat in the early 1970s. The sources also state that the Jemez Pueblo owns a convenience store.

## Oral tradition & literature

Before television arrived at Jemez, tales of running feats were a major form of entertainment on winter nights. N. Scott Momaday is identified as a Kiowa novelist, short story writer, essayist, and poet who won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction, but the sources do not present him as a Jemez Pueblo literary figure; he is listed as a notable resident.

## Language & religion

Jemez Pueblo people have historically spoken the Towa language, an unwritten language belonging to the Tanoan language family. They state that the leadership of the Pueblo of Jemez is annually selected by Jemez religious leaders. *Carex* is described as sacred and used in ceremonies. The sources also describe races as having ceremonial purposes connected with the movement of the sun and moon and the growth of crops.
