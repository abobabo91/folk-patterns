## Overview

The Lekwungen are a Coast Salish people who reside on southeastern Vancouver Island, British Columbia, in the Greater Victoria area. They are represented by the Songhees First Nation and Esquimalt First Nation. Their traditional language is Lekwungen, a dialect of the North Straits Salish language. The name “Lekwungen” is a contemporary designation for Indigenous peoples who speak this language and refers specifically to six families historically residing on southern Vancouver Island. Their traditional territory included most of the present-day Greater Victoria area, as well as the Discovery, Chatham, and San Juan islands. The sources describe Lekwungen social organization, village life, treaty history, architecture, ceremonial practices, and population changes.

### Architecture

The Coast Salish traditionally lived in bighouses, large rectangular communal houses made from cedar planks. These houses were adorned with carved and jointed totem posts. The sources also provide evidence of a fortified village at Finlayson Point in Beacon Hill Park before the arrival of Europeans. A major Coast Salish seaport community called Sitchanalth was located in the area now known as Willows Beach in Oak Bay, British Columbia.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

## Festivals & rituals

The Lekwungen practiced potlatch and ceremonial gift distribution, as did other north-west coast tribes. Before European contact, much of the government was organized through a clan system, with twelve clans each having its own fishing and hunting territory. Chiefship was hereditary in the male line, and society was divided into nobles, commons, and slaves.

The sources describe burial practices rather than an annual ritual calendar. The dead were buried in canoes or boxes upon the surface of the ground, or laid away in trees. Mentioning the names of a dead person was taboo.

The Lekwungen were one of the few First Nations in British Columbia to have a treaty with the British. Sir James Douglas negotiated a treaty with the Lekwungen in 1850. The Lekwungen considered that the government of British Columbia had failed to honour the treaty and commenced legal action against the province and the Government of Canada. A settlement was announced in November 2006.

During the 1862 Pacific Northwest smallpox epidemic, the Lekwungen were largely spared because of smallpox vaccines given by Hudson’s Bay Company physician Dr. John Helmcken and because Lekwungen people self-quarantined on Discovery Island.

## Language & religion

Lekwungen is the traditional language of the Lekwungen and is described as a dialect of the North Straits Salish language. The term “Lekwungen” is used to describe a group of Indigenous peoples who speak the Lekwungen language. The people associated with the term have also been referred to as the Songhees or Songish, although “Songhees” is now primarily used for the Songhees Band government.

The sources describe clan organization, hereditary chiefship, potlatch, ceremonial gift distribution, burial practices, and a taboo against mentioning the names of the dead.
