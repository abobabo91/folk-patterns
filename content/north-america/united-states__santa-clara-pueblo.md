## Overview

Santa Clara Pueblo, also known in Tewa as *Khaˀpʼoe Ówîngeh*, is a census-designated place and federally recognized tribe of Native American Pueblo people in Rio Arriba County, New Mexico, United States. It lies on the Rio Grande, approximately 1.5 miles south of Española, between Ohkay Owingeh and San Ildefonso Pueblo. The people belong to the Tewa ethnic group and speak the Rio Grande Tewa language. As of the 2020 census, Santa Clara Pueblo had a population of 930. The pueblo is a member of the Eight Northern Pueblos and is known especially for hand-crafted pottery, including blackware and redware with deep engravings. Its name is translated as “singing water village” or “rose water village,” and it is also known as the Village of Wild Roses.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Pottery decoration** — Santa Clara Pueblo ceramics use painted designs, impressed patterns, incised designs, and resist-firing with incised or sgraffito designs. The sources identify black polished and red polished pottery, especially pottery with incised work, as distinctive features of the pueblo’s ceramic production.

**Motif vocabulary.** “Knife-wing” or eagle feather designs.

### Architecture

The sources document chapel buildings and a church. A chapel was built in the area by 1617, Fray Alonso de Benavides established a mission in 1628, and the current church replaced the former church in 1918. The original and unoccupied chapel was destroyed during the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, and two other chapel buildings were later constructed there.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pottery is one of the best-known arts practiced at Santa Clara Pueblo. It was traditionally made primarily by girls and women, although many men also make pottery today. Santa Clara potters are known for black polished and red polished pottery in a distinctive style, particularly the use of incised work. Ceramic decoration includes painted designs, impressed patterns, incised designs, and resist-firing with incised or sgraffito designs.

## Festivals & rituals

 They do describe the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, when this Pueblo joined forces with nearby pueblos and fought against the Spanish Royal Government. The sources also record the construction and abandonment of missions and chapels, including the abandonment of the mission before the Great Pueblo Revolt of 1680.
