## Overview

The Bannock were originally Northern Paiute but became more culturally affiliated with the Northern Shoshone. Their traditional lands reached across parts of the Great Basin and northern Rocky Mountains. Today the Shoshone-Bannock Tribes are based at the Fort Hall Indian Reservation in Idaho. The profile covers their material culture, ceremonies, hunting, fishing, and food gathering.

## Material culture

Bannock material culture included geometric beadwork, leather and rawhide objects, plant-fiber bags, pottery, horn utensils, and tule reed rafts.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Bannock makers used geometric designs on beadwork and bags, along with leather, plant fiber, salmon skin, and rawhide.

- **Geometric beadwork** (*beadwork*) — After glass beads arrived, Bannock people transferred geometric designs from petroglyphs to beadwork.
- **Cornhusk bag** — A flat plant-fiber bag, possibly made from corn, had leather handles and colorful geometric patterns.
- **Salmon-skin carrying bags** — Bannock people traditionally made carrying bags from salmon skin.
- **Rawhide cradle** — A Bannock rawhide cradle held a doll in partly untreated leather.
- **Beaded needle housing** (*needle housing*) — A 1960 leather needle housing from Fort Hall was embroidered with blue, yellow, and red glass beads.

### Clothing & dress

Records show cloth tunics, blankets, moccasins, and baby carriers, but they do not give a full account of Bannock dress.

- **Cloth tunic** — Museum portraits show Bannock people wearing cloth tunics.
- **Moccasin** — A Bannock moccasin from Fort Hall was given to the Museum of Ethnography in 1958.
- **Blanket** — A photograph shows a Bannock man seated with a blanket over his legs.
- **Baby carrier** — A museum record shows a Shoshone or Bannock baby in a carrier with a beaded bag in front.

### Architecture

Before the late nineteenth century, Bannock people used buffalo-hide tipis in winter and brush-and-grass dwellings in summer.

- **Winter tipi** (*tipi*) — Bannock people lived mainly in buffalo-hide tipis during winter.
- **Summer brush dwelling** (*conical dwelling*) — During summer they built conical dwellings from brush and grass.
- **Sweat lodge** — Sweat lodges were one of the Bannock building types.
- **Menstrual hut** — Menstrual huts were also identified as Bannock building types.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Bannock people made pottery, horn utensils, tule reed rafts, leather bags, cradles, and other everyday objects.

- **Pottery** — The Bannock traditionally made pottery.
- **Bighorn horn utensils** (*bighorn sheep horn utensils*) — They made utensils from bighorn sheep horns.
- **Tule reed raft** — They made tule reed rafts for water transport.
- **Plant-fiber bag** — A museum record identifies a plant-fiber bag with leather handles and geometric patterns.
- **Rawhide cradle** — Museum records identify a rawhide cradle containing a doll.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources do not document Bannock jewelry or named body-adornment practices, but they do show glass beads used on objects.

- **Glass beads** — Glass beads were used in Bannock beadwork.
- **Beaded baby bag** (*beaded bag*) — A beaded bag was shown in front of a Shoshone or Bannock baby in a carrier.

## Music & performance

The sources name several dances and ceremonies but do not describe Bannock instruments, songs, or the music used in them.

- **Scalp Dance** — The Scalp Dance was one of the named Bannock ceremonies.
- **Grass Dance** — The Grass Dance was one of the named Bannock ceremonies.
- **Circle Dance** — The Circle Dance was one of the named Bannock ceremonies.
- **Bear Dance** — The Bear Dance was one of the named Bannock ceremonies.
- **Sun Dance** — The Bannock practiced the Sun Dance after adapting to reservation life.

## Dance & theatre

Bannock named dances included the Scalp Dance, Grass Dance, Circle Dance, Bear Dance, and later the Sun Dance.

- **Scalp Dance** — The Scalp Dance was a named Bannock dance.
- **Grass Dance** — The Grass Dance was a named Bannock dance.
- **Circle Dance** — The Circle Dance was a named Bannock dance.
- **Bear Dance** — The Bear Dance was a named Bannock dance.
- **Sun Dance** — The Sun Dance was practiced after acclimation to reservation life.

## Festivals & rituals

Bannock ritual life included spirit appeals, healing, death rites, named dances, and later the Sun Dance.

- **Spirit worship** (*worship*) — Bannock religious practices included worship and appeals to spirits.
- **Medicine-men healing** (*medicine men*) — Healing arts involved medicine men.
- **Soul journey** (*land of the dead*) — The Bannock believed the soul journeyed to the land of the dead after death.
- **Funeral rites** (*minor self-mutilation*) — The dead were wrapped in blankets and placed in rock crevices, while mourners practiced minor self-mutilation.
- **Bannock War** — The Bannock War took place in 1878.

## Foodways

Bannock people fished for salmon, hunted buffalo, and gathered food outside the reservation when government provisions were unreliable.

- **Salmon fishing** — Before the late nineteenth century, Bannock people fished for salmon on Idaho's Snake River.
- **Buffalo hunting** — They hunted buffalo herds in the fall.
- **Food gathering** — In the 1870s they continued to gather food outside the reservation.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not record Bannock stories or literature, but they mention a travel route used for buffalo hunting.

- **Bannock Trail** — The Bannock Trail connected Oregon, northern Nevada, southern Idaho, and western Wyoming with Montana and Canada for buffalo hunting.

## Language & religion

The Bannock were originally Northern Paiute, culturally close to the Northern Shoshone, and followed religious practices centered on spirits, healing, and the soul after death.

- **Northern Paiute name** (*panaki*) — The Wikipedia source gives panaki as a Northern Paiute term for the Bannock.
- **Northern Paiute name** (*kutsutɨkaˀa*) — The Wikipedia source also gives kutsutɨkaˀa as a Northern Paiute term for the Bannock.
- **Appeals to spirits** — Bannock religious practices included appeals to spirits.
- **Medicine men** — Medicine men were involved in healing arts.
- **Soul journey** — The Bannock believed the soul journeyed to the land of the dead after death.
