## Overview

The Zia are a Keres Pueblo people centered at Zia Pueblo in New Mexico. They speak Eastern Keres and trace their ancestry to Ancestral Puebloan people who moved to the Jemez River Valley in the 13th century. Zia culture is known for pottery, farming, religious ceremonies, kachina traditions, and the sacred sun symbol. Their main crops are corn, beans, and squash.

## Material culture

Zia material culture is especially known for painted pottery, adobe pueblo homes, farming tools, and objects used in religious ceremonies.

### Clothing & dress

The sources document only religious men dressing as kachinas during ceremonies.

- **Kachina dress** (*kachina*) — Religious men dressed as kachinas, danced among the people for three days, and then returned to the mountains.

### Architecture

Zia pueblo homes were multi-level adobe buildings arranged like apartment complexes.

- **Pueblo homes** — Pueblo homes had stacked box-like levels, ladders, storage spaces, and rooms for families.
- **Adobe building** (*adobe*) — Adobe was made from clay, sand, water, and organic materials such as sticks, straw, and dung, then formed into blocks and dried.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pottery was central to daily life and trade, and Zia potters made painted vessels from clay tempered with basalt.

- **Painted pottery** (*pottery painting*) — Dried vessels were painted with geometric, plant, animal, bird, nature, and religious designs.
- **Polychrome clay vessel** — A museum record describes a Zia vessel with two red birds outlined in black, including the sacred water bird.
- **Storage jar** — A large coiled clay storage jar was painted with orange and black slips and decorated with two birds.
- **Water jar** (*olla*) — A large pottery water jar had black, white, and red floral and geometric patterns and was repaired with native sinew.
- **Earthenware bowl** — Small and large earthenware bowls had white or red grounds with black and red decoration along the rim.

## Music & performance

Music accompanied kachina ceremonies and the grinding of sacred corn.

- **Kachina ceremony music** (*kachina*) — Drums and rattles were used during dances in ceremonies asking for rain and crop growth.
- **Corn-grinding songs** (*sacred corn*) — Women sang religious songs while grinding dried corn because they considered corn sacred.

## Dance & theatre

The sources document ceremonial kachina dancing but no separate theatrical tradition.

- **Kachina dancing** (*kachina*) — Religious men dressed as kachinas danced among the people for three days before returning to the mountains.

## Festivals & rituals

Zia ceremonies center on kachinas, rain, crops, the sacred Sun, and the number four.

- **Kachina ceremonies** (*kachina*) — The Zia asked ancestral kachina spirits to bring rain and make crops grow.
- **Sun ritual** (*Sun*) — The sacred Sun was honored with a red four-directional symbol painted on ceremonial vases, drawn around campfires, and used to introduce newborns to the Sun.
- **Four sacred obligations** (*four*) — The sacred number four represented a strong body, a clear mind, a pure spirit, and devotion to the welfare of others.
- **Religious suppression** (*traditional Zia religious ceremonies*) — Spanish religious orders outlawed traditional Zia religious ceremonies, although tribal members had built a church and convent by 1613.

## Foodways

Zia foodways center on farming corn, beans, and squash in response to the dry climate.

- **Three sisters** — Corn, beans, and squash were planted together on shared land and formed the staple of Zia and Pueblo diets.
- **Corn tortilla** (*tortilla*) — Women made dough from corn flour and water, flattened it, and baked it on hot rocks over a fire.
- **Water farming** (*cisterns*) — People dug paths and trenches to move and collect rainwater for fields, while women carried clay pottery to gather water.
- **Hunting** — The Zia mainly ate plants but hunted rabbits, gophers, squirrels, deer, antelope, mountain lions, and sometimes bison.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not document Zia folktales or written literature, but they record religious songs and knowledge.

- **Corn-grinding songs** (*religious songs*) — Women sang religious songs while grinding sacred corn into flour.
- **Kachina knowledge** (*kachinas*) — Religious knowledge included teachings about kachinas, the Sun, and the duties represented by four.

## Language & religion

The Zia speak Eastern Keres and follow traditions centered on the sacred Sun, kachinas, rain, and fourfold order.

- **Eastern Keres** — The Zia are one of the Keres Pueblo peoples and speak Eastern Keres.
- **Kachinas** — Kachinas are understood as ancestral spirits associated with rain and clouds.
- **Sacred sun symbol** (*solar symbol*) — The Zia solar symbol is a red circle with groups of rays pointing in four directions.
- **Fourfold sacred order** (*four*) — Four represents the compass points, seasons, periods of the day, seasons of life, and four sacred obligations.

## Glossary

- *Tsʾíiyʾamʾé* — Zia name for the people
- *Tsi'ya* — another name for Zia Pueblo
- *Eastern Keres* — language spoken by the Zia
- *kachina* — ancestral spirit or ceremonial figure
- *kachinas* — ancestral spirits linked with rain and clouds
- *pueblo homes* — multi-level adobe homes
- *adobe* — dried clay building material
- *olla* — pottery water jar
- *basalt* — hard volcanic rock used to temper pottery
- *pottery painting* — painting dried pottery with designs
- *sacred water bird* — bird believed to bring water and life
- *solar symbol* — red circle with rays in four directions
- *three sisters* — corn, beans, and squash
- *tortilla* — flat baked corn dough
- *cisterns* — trenches for collecting rainwater
- *sacred corn* — corn treated as sacred
- *four* — sacred number in Zia traditions
