## Overview

The Yurok homeland follows the Klamath River from near Trinidad toward Crescent City. Fishing, hunting, gathering, basket weaving and woodcarving remain important. The Yurok language is being revitalized through classes, master-apprentice teams and singing. Ceremonial life includes the World Renewal Ceremony, Jump Dance and Brush Dance.

## Material culture

Yurok material culture uses local wood, roots, grasses, ferns, hides, shells, bone and stone, especially in baskets, ceremonial objects and household tools.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Yurok basketry uses twined plant fibers and appears in many vessel and ceremonial forms.

- **Yurok basketry** — Twined baskets were made from bear grass, spruce or willow roots, willow or hazel sticks and maidenhair fern.
- **Ceremonial cap** — This twined basketry cap used local plant materials and could show an obsidian-blade design with lightning.
- **Dance hat** — Dance hats were made from wood, coniferous root, maidenhair fern and bear grass.
- **Basketry medallion** — Medallions used willow, coniferous root, maidenhair fern, woodwardia fern, bear grass and dyed quill.
- **Basket bowl** (*Korgfat*) — The Korgfat is a basket bowl associated with Hupa, Yurok and Karok people.

### Clothing & dress

The sources mainly describe special clothing and regalia worn for ceremonies.

- **Ceremonial dance regalia** — Dancers wore elaborate clothing, including hide fringed with grass in a quillwork-like manner.
- **Ceremonial headdresses** — Headdresses were among the sacred objects returned for use in Yurok ceremonies.
- **Ceremonial deerskins** — Deerskins were returned with other sacred regalia for ceremonies and cultural display.

### Architecture

Yurok villages had separate family homes along with sweat houses and menstrual huts.

- **Village** (*ʼoʼloolekw*) — A village consisted of separate single-family homes, sweat houses and menstrual huts.
- **Sumeg** — Sumeg is a traditional hamlet of wooden plank buildings built in 1990.
- **Redwood plank house** — Plans for ʼO Rew include traditional redwood plank houses.
- **Sweat house** — Men of an extended patrilineal family gathered in sweat houses.
- **Menstrual hut** — Women stayed in separate underground huts for ten days during menstruation.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources describe household objects made from wood, roots, ferns and grasses, but no Yurok ceramics or metalwork.

- **Cooking basket** — Cooking baskets were among the recorded Yurok household objects.
- **Berry-washing tray** — Berry-washing trays were made as everyday basketry objects.
- **Glass bottle with wooden cover** — Museum records include a glass bottle with a wooden lid and cover.
- **Stone-tipped spear** — Museum records list eight stone-tipped spears associated with the Yurok.
- **Basket bowl with step pattern** — A bowl-shaped basket had white and black step-pattern ornament.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Dentalium shells were the major Yurok currency and also appeared in adornment.

- **Dentalium shell** (*terkwterm*) — Dentalium shells were the major currency of the Yurok nations.
- **Shell medallion necklace** — A necklace used a shell medallion with skin, grass, beads, twine, seeds and basketry.
- **Basketry earring** — Earrings could be made from basketry, skin and metal.
- **Wooden medallion** — Wooden medallions incorporated roots, ferns, bear grass and dyed quill.

## Music & performance

Songs and dances carry Yurok ceremonial knowledge across generations.

- **World Renewal songs** — Songs passed through many generations were performed during the World Renewal Ceremony.
- **Language singing** — Singing helps pass the Yurok language from one generation to another.
- **Master-apprentice language teaching** — Master-apprentice teams teach the language along with classes and immersion camps.
- **Yurok Language Project recordings** — The Yurok Language Project created an audio dictionary and a database of compiled texts.

## Dance & theatre

The Jump Dance and Brush Dance remain important Yurok tribal ceremonies.

- **Jump Dance** — The Jump Dance remains part of Yurok tribal ceremonies.
- **Brush Dance** — The Brush Dance remains part of Yurok tribal ceremonies.
- **World Renewal Ceremony** — The World Renewal Ceremony brings together songs and dances passed down through generations.
- **Peyerk ceremony hosting** (*peyerk*) — Peyerk from many villages hosted ceremonies and supplied food, shelter and special clothing for dancers.

## Festivals & rituals

Yurok ceremonies renew community ties and connect songs, dances, healing and the natural world.

- **World Renewal Ceremony** — Held every year, it included Yurok songs and dances.
- **Shamanic healing** — Women became shamans after dreams calling them to heal, with help from another shaman in a ritual dance.
- **Healing ritual** — Shamans used plants, prayer and rituals to heal people.
- **Successful-harvest ceremonies** — Shamans performed ceremonies for successful hunting, fishing and gathering.
- **Sacred-artifact repatriation** — In 2010, the Smithsonian returned 217 sacred artifacts to the Yurok tribe.

## Foodways

Salmon is the vital food and a foundation of Yurok identity.

- **Salmon fishing** — The Yurok traditionally fished for salmon along rivers.
- **Ocean fishing and shellfish gathering** — They gathered ocean fish and shellfish.
- **Game hunting** — They hunted game and ate varied meats.
- **Plant and berry gathering** — They gathered plants and varied berries.
- **Drift-whale meat** — Whale meat was prized, but Yuroks waited for a dead whale to wash ashore and dried the flesh.

## Oral tradition & literature

Yurok traditions describe the homeland and dentalium shells, while modern projects preserve the language in books and recordings.

- **Dentalium-world tradition** — Traditions describe dentalium shells as living at the downstream and upstream ends of the world.
- **Compiled texts** — The Yurok Language Project created a database of compiled texts.
- **Yurok-language graphic novel** (*Soldiers Unknown*) — Soldiers Unknown was partly written in Yurok, translated by James Gensaw and illustrated by Rahsan Ekedal.
- **Language recording work** — Archie Thompson helped record and teach Yurok before his death in 2013.

## Language & religion

Yurok is an Algic language with several traditional names, and its spiritual life centers on the Great Spirit and ritual practice.

- **Yurok language** (*Oohl*) — The people call themselves Oohl, meaning “the people,” and speak Yurok, an Algic language.
- **Downstream people** (*Puliklah*) — Puliklah comes from pulik, “downstream,” and -la, “people of.”
- **Yurok language names** (*Chillula, Mita, Pekwan, Rikwa, Sugon, Weitspek, Weitspekan*) — The language is also known by these names in the source material.
- **New Yurok Alphabet** — Modern Yurok is written with Latin characters in the New Yurok Alphabet.
- **Yurok Unifon** — Yurok was previously written in the Yurok Unifon.
