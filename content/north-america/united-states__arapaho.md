## Overview

The Arapaho traditionally lived across parts of the northern and central Great Plains. Horse travel, bison hunting, trade, and warrior societies shaped their life. Sacred ceremonies included the Sun Dance, Ghost Dance, and peyote ceremonies. Arapaho people continue efforts to preserve and teach their language.

## Material culture

Arapaho material culture includes robes, moccasins, blankets, decorated bags, saddlebags, weapons, jewelry, and ceremonial clothing.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not name Arapaho textile patterns, but they describe decorated clothing, robes, blankets, moccasins, and bags.

- **Ceremonial regalia** — Portraits show eagle-feather headdresses, embroidered moccasins, cloth shirts, vests, wool blankets, necklaces, hoop earrings, and decorated bags.
- **Buffalo robes** — A museum record shows four Arapaho men wearing buffalo robes, cloth shirts, and moccasins.
- **Embroidered moccasins** — Bird Chief is recorded as wearing embroidered moccasins.
- **Wool blankets** — Portraits show Arapaho men wearing wool blankets around their shoulders, waists, or bodies.
- **Decorated bag** — Heap of Bears is recorded holding a decorated bag.

### Clothing & dress

Arapaho men wore cloth shirts, vests, jackets, blankets, robes, moccasins, and jewelry, with special clothing for chiefs and ceremonies.

- **Eagle-feather headdress** — Yellow Calf wore an eagle-feather headdress with bead embroidery.
- **White tunic** — Yellow Calf wore a white tunic with tassels.
- **Cloth scarf and vest** — Left Hand wore a cloth scarf, cloth shirt, vest, and wool blanket.
- **Hoop earrings** — Bird Chief appears in portraits wearing hoop earrings, a cloth shirt or vest, and a wool blanket.
- **Braided or cloth-wrapped hair** — Portraits show hair braided or wrapped in cloth, sometimes in two bunches.

### Architecture

The sources give little detail about Arapaho buildings, but they mention temporary brush shelters.

- **Temporary brush shelter** — The Beesowuunenno’ used temporary brush shelters similar to dome-shaped shade or sweat lodges of Great Lakes Algonquian peoples.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources document hide objects, a saddlebag, and many weapons, but no Arapaho ceramic or metalworking tradition.

- **Saddlebag** (*sadelväska*) — A museum catalogue identifies a sadelväska, or saddlebag, as an Arapaho object from the western United States prairie region.
- **Handled hide scraper** — A hide scraper with an elk-horn handle came from the Cheyenne and Arapaho Reservation in Oklahoma.
- **War-club** — War-clubs were among the weapons used by Arapaho warriors.
- **Tomahawk** — Tomahawks were used by Arapaho warriors, and Left Hand is shown holding one with fur tails on its handle.
- **Bow** — Bows were among the weapons used by Arapaho warriors.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Arapaho adornment included earrings, necklaces, hair wrapping, feather headdresses, decorated clothing, and body tattoos.

- **Necklace** — Arapaho men are recorded wearing necklaces.
- **Pocket-watch chain** — Portraits show men wearing pocket-watch chains.
- **Ear ornament** — The sources mention ear ornaments as part of Arapaho adornment.
- **Bead embroidery** — Yellow Calf’s eagle-feather headdress had bead embroidery.
- **Tattooed circles** — Arapaho people traditionally tattooed small circles on their bodies.

## Music & performance

Arapaho music includes sacred and secular songs with distinctive melodies, rhythms, and ceremonial uses.

- **Sacred songs** — Sacred songs are part of Arapaho music and include songs connected with the Sun Dance.
- **Guardian-spirit songs** — Songs taught by guardian spirits were supposed to be sung only when the recipient was near death.
- **Round-dance songs** — Round-dance songs use triple meter.
- **Peyote songs** — Peyote songs accompany peyote ceremonies and use rapid rhythm, vocables, and closing formulas.
- **Historical songs** — Historical songs are one of the named secular and traditional Arapaho song forms.

## Dance & theatre

Named Arapaho dances include round dances, the snake dance, the rabbit dance, and the turtle dance.

- **Round dance** — Round dances are secular dances and songs in triple meter.
- **Snake dance** — The snake dance is a named Arapaho dance form.
- **Rabbit dance** — The rabbit dance is a partner dance introduced after European contact.
- **Turtle dance** — The turtle dance is a named Arapaho dance form.

## Festivals & rituals

The main documented ceremonies are the summer Sun Dance, the Ghost Dance, and peyote ceremonies.

- **Sun Dance** — The Sun Dance brings Arapaho bands together in summer and guides warriors toward a vision and guardian spirit.
- **Ghost Dance** — The Ghost Dance reached the Arapaho in the 1880s through tribes farther west and used music as an integral part of the movement.
- **Peyote ceremony** — Peyote ceremonies use peyote songs, and chewed peyote buttons act as a hallucinogen.

## Foodways

Arapaho foodways changed from farming in the western Great Lakes to horse-based hunting on the Great Plains.

- **Maize** — In the western Great Lakes region, Arapaho people grew crops including maize.
- **Bison hunting** — After moving to the Great Plains and acquiring horses, Arapaho hunters pursued bison.
- **Trade meat and hides** — Arapaho people traded meat and hides with the Arikara, Mandan, and Hidatsa.
- **Corn, squash, and beans** — In trade, Arapaho people received corn, squash, and beans.
- **Beaver furs** — Beaver furs were important trade goods.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources name historical traditions and many song types, but no epic, folktale cycle, or proverb tradition.

- **Arapaho Historical Traditions** (*Hinono'einoo3itoono*) — Arapaho Historical Traditions: Hinono'einoo3itoono is a named work about Arapaho historical traditions.
- **The Arapaho Project** — The Arapaho Project promotes and restores the traditional language and culture.
- **Lullabies** — Lullabies are among the named Arapaho song forms.
- **Children’s songs** — Children’s songs are among the named Arapaho song forms.
- **Courtship songs** — Courtship songs are among the named Arapaho song forms.

## Language & religion

Arapaho is an endangered Algonquian language supported by language programs, while religious life includes the Sun Dance, guardian spirits, Ghost Dance, and peyote ceremonies.

- **Arapaho language** (*Hinónoʼeitíít*) — Hinónoʼeitíít is the Arapaho endonym for the Arapaho language.
- **Northern Arapaho language programs** — Language preservation includes the Language and Culture Commission, language instruction, language camps, bilingual education, and the Arapaho Language Lodge.
- **Immersion preschool** — Dr. S. N. Greymorning established a 1995 immersion preschool program.
- **Besawunena** — Besawunena is described as a dialect attested from a wordlist collected by Alfred Kroeber.
- **Guardian spirits** — Guardian spirits are connected with visions, the Sun Dance, and sacred songs.
