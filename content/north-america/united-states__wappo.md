## Overview

The Wappo, who call themselves *Micewal*, are an Indigenous people of northern California. Their traditional homelands include Napa Valley, the south shore of Clear Lake, Alexander Valley, and Russian River valley. Wappo villages existed near the present-day towns of Yountville, St. Helena, and Calistoga when Mexicans arrived to colonize California. Alfred L. Kroeber estimated the Wappo population in 1770 at 1,000; Sherburne F. Cook later estimated 1,650. The Wappo language is an extinct member of the Yukian language family and was distinguished by influence from nearby Pomoan languages. Wappo culture is documented here especially through hunting and gathering, small settlements, water-holding baskets, and an endangered language whose last fluent speaker, Laura Fish Somersal, died in 1990.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Woven baskets* were made so carefully that they could hold water. The sources describe this basketry.

### Architecture

Before European colonization, the Wappo lived in small groups without centralized political authority. Their homes were built from branches, leaves, and mud.

## Music & performance

The Wappo language article records linguistic examples such as *méhwa* “wild grape vine” and *kálkuʔ* “greyhound,”.

## Festivals & rituals

The historical record states that people from the south shore of Clear Lake were absorbed and dispersed to the Spanish missions in California, where the mission recorded at least 550 Wappo baptisms.

## Foodways

Before European colonization, the Wappo lived by hunting and gathering. The Wappo language article gives *čičaloʔ* “pea” and *háros* “rice” as examples of words borrowed from Spanish.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do identify Wappo traditional narratives as a related subject and record scholarly work on the language. Paul Radin published the first texts on Wappo grammar in the 1920s. Jesse O. Sawyer published *English–Wappo Vocabulary* in 1965 and continued to study Wappo grammar. The sources also mention the Wappo Language Project at the Western Institute for Endangered Language Documentation and state that, as of the 21st century, there were plans and efforts toward language revival.

## Language & religion

Wappo is an extinct language of the Yukian language family. It was spoken in what is now known as the Alexander Valley north of San Francisco, and it was influenced by nearby Pomoan languages. The people called themselves *Micewal*; the Pomoan name for them was *Ashochimi*, meaning “northerners.” The language had five varieties: Clear Lake Wappo, Russian River Wappo, Northern Wappo, Central Wappo, and Southern Wappo. It had predicate-final word order, a rich case system, and five categories of tense or aspect: habitual/progressive, stative, past, inchoative, and future. The sources describe Spanish and English influence on Wappo.
