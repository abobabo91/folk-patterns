## Overview

The Nanticoke are a Native American, Algonquian-speaking people whose traditional homelands were in the Chesapeake Bay area, including Delaware. Their historic territories included areas along the Nanticoke River, Broad Creek, the Susquehanna River, and the Delmarva Peninsula. Today, descendants live in Oklahoma among the Delaware Nation and the Delaware Tribe of Indians, and on the Six Nations of the Grand River reserve in Ontario, Canada. The Nanticoke name for themselves is *Nentego*, meaning “Tidewater People.” The Nanticoke consisted of several related tribes, including the Choptank, Assateague, Piscataway, and Doeg. The sources identify the Nanticoke as important to the history of Indigenous communities in the Chesapeake Bay area, their migrations, language revival, and state-recognized tribal organization.

## Festivals & rituals

They record the annual powwow organized by the Nanticoke Indian Association, which continued until the mid-1930s and was revived in 1977. The sources also describe seasonal activities: while settled along the Susquehanna River, the Nanticoke used an established path to return to the Delmarva Peninsula for seasonal gathering of fruits, nuts, and roots, as well as fishing.

The sources describe a gathering in the early summer of 1742 involving Nanticoke, Shawnee, and Choptank people on Winnesoccum Island in the Pocomoke Swamp. Chiefs Robin Hood, Hopping Sam, Simon Alsechqueck, and Messowan gathered their people for six days to discuss plans of attack and their adverse encounters with the English. Following the United States gaining independence, the federal government made an official treaty of peace with the tribes, signed on July 24, 1792.

## Foodways

They state that Nanticoke people seasonally gathered fruits, nuts, and roots on the Delmarva Peninsula and practiced fishing. The sources also say that, during the Winnesoccum Incident, members of the Nanticoke, Shawnee, and Choptank tribes told English interrogators that they had gathered in the swamp to hunt, although other explanations were also given.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do record historical accounts of the Winnesoccum Incident, including the names of Chiefs Robin Hood, Hopping Sam, Simon Alsechqueck, and Messowan.

The Nanticoke language has been the subject of preservation and revival efforts. The Nanticoke Indian Association and linguists from Georgetown University have worked to revive the language since the late 20th century. In 2007, the Nanticoke Indian Association in Millsboro, Delaware, assembled with assistance from Myrelene Ranville née Henderson of the Sagkeeng First Nation in Manitoba, Canada, who speaks Anishinaabemowin, a similar language. The revival used the vocabulary list of Thomas Jefferson. In 2023, a book for the revitalization of the Nanticoke language was published.

## Language & religion

Nanticoke is an extinct Algonquian language spoken historically in Delaware and Maryland, United States. It was distinct from the Algonquian languages spoken by tribes on the Western Shore of Maryland and along the Potomac River, and its vocabulary was distinct from Delaware. Similar languages were likely spoken by neighboring tribes, including the Choptank, Assateague, Piscataway, and Doeg.

The last fluent speaker identified by the Nanticoke Indian Association was Lydia E. Clark, who died in 1856. Two surviving wordlists are described: a 146-word list compiled by Moravian missionary John Heckewelder in 1785, and a list of 300 words obtained in 1792 by William Vans Murray from a Nanticoke speaker in Dorchester County, Maryland.
