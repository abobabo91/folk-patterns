## Overview

The Aleuts are Indigenous people of the Aleutian Islands. Their communities are divided between Alaska and Kamchatka Krai. Their culture centers on the sea, hunting, skin clothing, boats, basketry, carving, and bone and ivory work. They are also known as Unangan, Unangas, and Unangax̂.

## Material culture

Aleut material culture relies on marine hunting and local materials such as skin, grass, wood, stone, bone, ivory, and animal parts.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Aleut women made waterproof clothing and fine grass baskets, while weavers used local materials and distinctive animal and hunting motifs.

- **Eagle-skin parka** (*Kameikas*) — Kameikas were parkas made from downy eagle skin, and the term means “rain gear” in English.
- **Gutskin raincoat** (*Kamleikas*) — Kamleikas were waterproof parkas or shirts made from sewn strips of intestine, sometimes decorated with colored gut-skin strips, wool yarn, and animal hair.
- **Grass basket** (*Qiigam aygaaxsii*) — Qiigam aygaaxsii is the Aleut term for a grass basket made from sea-lyme grass.
- **False-embroidered basket** — Aleut basket makers overlaid strands of grass or reeds on a woven surface to create false embroidery.
- **Woven grass pieces** — Aleut weavers made cloth-like pieces from grass and also used birch bark, puffin feathers, and baleen.

### Clothing & dress

Aleut clothing was made for cold, wet conditions from seal, sea-otter, bird, and gut materials.

- **Women’s skin parka** — Women wore parkas made from seal or sea-otter skin that extended below the knees.
- **Men’s bird-skin parka** — Men wore bird-skin parkas with the feathers turned inward or outward according to the weather.
- **Waterproof hunting parka** — Men wore hooded parkas made from seal, sea-lion, bear, walrus, or whale entrails, with wrist openings that could be tightened against water.
- **Seal-skin breeches** — Men wore breeches made from the esophageal skin of seals.
- **Child’s eagle-skin parka** (*Kameikas*) — Children wore parkas made from downy eagle skin and tanned bird-skin caps.

### Architecture

Aleuts lived in partly underground houses insulated with grass and earth.

- **Partly underground house** (*Barabara*) — A barabara was made by digging an oblong pit, framing the roof with driftwood, and covering it with grass and earth.
- **Interior trench** — Trenches along the inside walls were covered with mats to keep them clean.
- **Shared lodge** — Several families could live in one house, with each family having a designated area.
- **Hanging lanterns** — Lanterns were hung inside instead of using a central fireplace or bonfire.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Aleut tools and household objects were made from stone, wood, bone, ivory, horn, and other animal materials.

- **Stone blade** — Archaeological tools included blades made from obsidian and chert.
- **Bone needle and awl** — Aleuts made needles and awls from bird bone.
- **Harpoon point** — Harpoon points were made from mammal bone.
- **Harpoon mouthpiece** — A walrus-ivory mouthpiece was used for a harpoon bladder.
- **Horn spoon** — A spoon or ladle was carved from Mountain Sheep horn.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Aleut adornment used ivory, beads, feathers, hair, whiskers, shells, bones, and tattoos to show identity, achievement, and status.

- **Tooth neck ornament** — Museum records include a neck ornament made from carved teeth.
- **Sea-lion whisker ornament** — Some ornaments were made from bundled sea-lion whiskers threaded with beads.
- **Nose pin** — Nose pins could be made from bark, bone, or an eagle’s feather shaft and decorated with amber and coral.
- **Dentalium ear ornament** — Ears were pierced with dentalium shells, bone, feathers, dried bird wings or skulls, and amber.
- **Lip ornament** — Walrus ivory, beads, or bones were worn through the lower lip.

## Music & performance

The sources do not describe Aleut instruments or song traditions, but they say masks were used in dances and praises.

- **Ceremonial mask** — Masks were used in ceremonies ranging from dances to praises, and each mask had its own meaning and purpose.

## Dance & theatre

Aleut masks represented mythic and historical figures and were used in ceremonies with dances and praises.

- **Myth figure mask** — Wooden masks represented anthropomorphic beings from myths and oral history.
- **Ancient-people mask** — Atka masks portrayed ancient people whom the Atka believed had lived in their land before them.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not describe an annual festival calendar, but they record body-piercing, tattooing, and mask ceremonies.

- **Newborn nose piercing** — Nose piercing was usually performed a few days after birth.
- **Women’s tattooing** — Women began tattooing after physical maturity and menstruation, at about age 20.
- **First-animal tattoo** — Men received their first tattoo after killing their first animal.
- **Mask ceremony** — Masks were used in ceremonies involving dances and praises.

## Foodways

Aleut foodways depended on fishing, marine hunting, gathering, and preserving local foods.

- **Berry, fat, and fish mixture** (*Alutiqqutigaq*) — Alutiqqutigaq was made from berries, fat, and fish.
- **Dried fish** — Fish such as salmon and cod were dried, smoked, or roasted.
- **Preserved sea-mammal food** — Seal, walrus, whale, and other sea mammals were dried, smoked, roasted, or preserved.
- **Boiled whale skin and blubber** — Boiled whale skin and blubber were described as delicacies.
- **Walrus skin and blubber** — Walrus skin and blubber were also described as delicacies.

## Oral tradition & literature

Aleut oral tradition is reflected in myths, oral history, and stories about earlier peoples and encounters with outsiders.

- **Ancient people of Atka** — The Atka people believed that another people had lived in their land before them.
- **Anthropomorphic myth figures** — Masks represented ancient figures from myths and oral history as anthropomorphic creatures.
- **Aleut uprising account** — A Japanese account records the Aleut uprising against Russian traders in 1784.
- **Dreams of Russia** — The 1992 Japanese film Dreams of Russia depicted Daikokuya Kōdayū’s encounter with Russians and Aleuts.

## Language & religion

Aleut is called Unangam Tunuu, has three dialects, and exists alongside Christian and older spirit beliefs.

- **Aleut language** (*Unangam Tunuu*) — Unangam Tunuu is the Aleut language of the Eskaleut language family.
- **Eastern dialect** — Eastern Aleut was spoken on the Eastern Aleutian, Shumagin, Fox, and Pribilof Islands.
- **Atkan dialect** — Atkan was spoken on Atka and Bering islands.
- **Attuan dialect** — Attuan was formerly spoken in the western islands and is now extinct.
- **Spirit beliefs** — Aleut religious beliefs included spirits, evil entities, and animal spirits, as well as ideas about tattoos and piercings.
