## Overview

The Shoshone are an Indigenous people with four large cultural and language divisions. They traditionally speak Shoshoni, also called a Numic language. Their material culture includes hunting weapons, bags, clothing, and drums. The sources also record language-preservation work and the protection of holy land at the Bear River Massacre site.

## Material culture

The documented material culture includes horn bows, salmon-skin bags, clothing, jewelry, drums, and homes made from high-growing grasses.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources document a salmon-skin bag but few other Shoshone textile traditions.

- **Salmon-skin bag** — This hard, stiff bag was made about 1960 by Cora George at Fort Hall, Idaho, for the Tukuryka Shoshone, with a decorated opening band and a light-skin strap.

### Clothing & dress

Museum records show Shoshone people wearing tunics, leggings, moccasins, dresses, and other modern clothing.

- **Cloth tunic dress** — Four young Shoshone women and a child were photographed wearing cloth tunic dresses.
- **Baby carrier** — A photograph shows a Shoshone woman carrying a baby in a baby carrier.
- **Moccasins** — Museum records depict Shoshone men wearing moccasins.
- **Hide leggings** — Museum records depict Shoshone men wearing hide leggings.
- **Neck tie** — Museum records depict Shoshone men wearing neck ties.

### Architecture

Traditional Shoshone homes were made from sosoni, a tall grass.

- **Grass house** (*sosoni*) — Some neighboring tribes called the Shoshone “Grass House People” because their traditional homes used high-growing grasses called sosoni.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources highlight a bighorn-sheep horn bow and a wooden frame drum.

- **Horn bow** — This 1854 bow was made from bighorn sheep horn and covered inside with sinew, and horn bows occurred chiefly among the Shoshone and some other peoples.
- **Frame drum** — A 19th-century frame drum was made of wood, possibly ash, and sheepskin.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Photographs show Shoshone people wearing neck ornaments, ear-rings, and decorated hair coverings.

- **Large neck disc** — Chief Washakie wore a large round disc on his neck.
- **Shell neck ornament** — One Shoshone man wore a shell neck ornament.
- **Metal neck-tie fastening** — Another portrait subject wore a circular metal fastening on a neck tie.
- **Decorated hair wraps** — One photograph shows hair in two bunches wrapped in decorated fabric.

## Music & performance

The sources do not document Shoshone music traditions, but they list one frame drum.

- **Frame drum** — A 19th-century wooden and sheepskin frame drum is listed, but the sources do not connect it to a specific Shoshone performance tradition.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not document named Shoshone dances, theatre, or other performance traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not describe a festival calendar or detailed rites, but they record the protection of holy land at the Bear River Massacre site.

- **Bear River Massacre site protection** — The Northwestern Band of the Shoshone Nation acquired the site in 2008 to protect the holy land and build a memorial.

The sources do not describe Shoshone dishes or cooking, but they record band names linked to food sources.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not record traditional Shoshone stories, but they document language-preservation work.

- **Newe** — The Shoshone call themselves Newe, meaning “People.”
- **Sosoni** — The name “Shoshone” comes from Sosoni, a Shoshone word for high-growing grasses.
- **Shoshoni language video game** — A Shoshone language video game was released in August 2013.
- **Digital language archive** — Contemporary preservation work includes digital language archiving, dictionaries, recordings, and Shoshoni classes.

## Language & religion

Shoshoni is a threatened Numic language with several regional dialects and two main writing systems.

- **Western Shoshoni** — Western Shoshoni is spoken primarily in Nevada.
- **Gosiute** — Gosiute is spoken in western Utah.
- **Northern Shoshoni** — Northern Shoshoni is spoken in southern Idaho and northern Utah.
- **Eastern Shoshoni** — Eastern Shoshoni is spoken in Wyoming.
- **Crum-Miller system** — The Crum-Miller system is one of the two main Shoshoni writing systems in use.
