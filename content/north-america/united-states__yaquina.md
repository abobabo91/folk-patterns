## Overview

The Yaquina, or Yaqo'n, were a tribe of Native Americans associated with the central Oregon coast. They lived around the Yaquina River and Yaquina Bay, places whose names derive from them, and the town of Yaquina, Oregon, was also named after the tribe. There were 19 Yaquina in 1910. Their language was one of the Yakonan languages. After the tribe was decimated by disease, remaining Yaqo'n people lived on the Coastal Indian Reservation, whose territory was greatly reduced to the Siletz Indian Reservation. Descendants of the Yaqo'n are members of the Confederated Tribes of Siletz Indians. The sources document the Yaquina primarily through their historical location, population, language, and relationship to reservation history.

## Language & religion

Yaquina and Alsea, also called Alsean or Yakonan, were two closely related speech varieties spoken along the central Oregon coast until the early 1950s. They are sometimes treated as different languages, although the sources state that the poor state of attestation makes this difficult to determine; Mithun considered them probably dialects of a single language. They are commonly held to be components of a language isolate. Alsea is also considered a language isolate and is usually classified within the Penutian phylum; it may form part of a Coast Oregon Penutian subgroup with Siuslaw and the Coosan languages. Both varieties are now extinct. Yaquina was last recorded in 1884 by James Owen Dorsey.
