## Overview

The Wasco–Wishram are two closely related Chinook Indian tribes from the Columbia River in Oregon. They lived along the riverbanks near The Dalles, a major trading location within a vast trade network. The Wasco traditionally lived on the south bank, while the Wishram settled in permanent villages along the north bank. Today they are part of the Confederated Tribes of Warm Springs in Oregon and the Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakama Nation in Washington. Their language belongs to the Upper Chinookan, or Kiksht, division of the Chinookan language family. The tribes are known for wood carving, beadwork, basketry, and fishing traditions connected with salmon and steelhead.

### Textile & pattern traditions

### Architecture

Wishram village, called *Nixlúidix* by its residents, was the main summer and winter village of the Wishram on the Columbia River in Washington. It is described as the largest prehistoric Chinook village site and is now part of Columbia Hills State Park.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They do describe intricate wood carving, beadwork, and basketry as arts associated with both tribes.

## Music & performance

 They state that the Wasco–Wishram language has had no fluent speakers since 2012 and that the tribe has a language program intended to revive its use among tribal members of all ages.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state that fishing for salmon and steelhead held cultural and religious importance for the tribes. The 1855 treaties preserved fishing at “all ... usual and accustomed stations in common with the citizens of the United States,” while the Bonneville Dam, Grand Coulee Dam, and The Dalles Dam later caused severe damage to native fisheries. In 1974, a landmark court case confirmed the rights of Northwest Coast tribes to fish as they had historically done.

## Foodways

 They identify salmon and steelhead fishing as culturally and religiously important, and state that government payments for the loss of fish did not compensate for this importance.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They state that the tribe has a language program to revive the Wasco–Wishram language among tribal members of all ages.

## Language & religion

Wasco–Wishram belongs to the Upper Chinookan, or Kiksht, division of the Chinookan language family, which is itself described as a branch of the proposed Penutian language family. The sources state that there have been no fluent speakers since 2012. The tribes maintain a language program for members of all ages. The sources also record the cultural and religious importance of fishing for salmon and steelhead.
