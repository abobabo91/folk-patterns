## Overview

The Yuki, also known as Yukiah, are an Indigenous people of California. They were traditionally divided into three groups: Ukomno'om, or “Valley People”; Huchnom, or “Outside the Valley”; and Ukohtontilka or Ukosontilka, the “Ocean People” or Coast Yuki. Their territory included Round Valley and much of northern Mendocino County and Lake County, and Yuki tribes are thought to have settled as far south as Hood Mountain in present-day Sonoma County. Today, Yuki people are enrolled members of the Round Valley Indian Tribes of the Round Valley Reservation. In the 2010 census, 569 people claimed Yuki ancestry, including 255 full-blooded people. Yuki culture is documented here through its language, counting system, basketry, and history.

## Language & religion

Yuki is an isolate and is unrelated to other Native American languages according to the source. It is described as distantly related to Wappo, with the two languages making up the entirety of the Yukian language family. The Yuki language has been extinct since its last speaker, Arthur Anderson, died in 1983. The Yuki people had a quaternary, or 4-based, counting system based on counting the spaces between the fingers rather than the fingers themselves.
