## Overview

The Muscogee are Indigenous peoples of the Southeastern Woodlands who were largely forced to move to Indian Territory in the 1830s. Their society included autonomous towns, farming, hunting, trade, craft work, ceremonial grounds, and ballparks. Their history includes Mississippian culture, earthwork mounds, political organization, and rich oral traditions. Muscogee language classes and immersion camps continue today.

## Material culture

Recorded material culture includes hide, skin, worsted cloth, beads, silk thread, pottery, trade goods, and a nineteenth-century drum.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The recorded chief’s dress combines hide, worsted cloth, beads, fringe, and silk-thread decoration.

- **Chief’s dress** — A chief’s dress included a fringed hide jacket, cloth belt, beaded bag, beaded garters, and decorated skin moccasins.
- **Beaded accessories** — Beads appeared on the chief’s bag and garters.
- **Silk-thread moccasin decoration** — The chief’s skin moccasins were sewn with silk thread.
- **Fringe** — Fringe appeared on the hide jacket and garters.
- **Cloth and hide materials** — The recorded clothing used hide, skin, worsted cloth, beads, and silk thread.

### Clothing & dress

Recorded clothing ranges from a chief’s traditional dress to nineteenth-century coats, shirts, and suits.

- **Chief’s dress** — The chief’s dress included a hide jacket, worsted-cloth belt, tasselled bag, fringed garters, and silk-thread-decorated moccasins.
- **Hilis Hadjo’s red coat** — A drawing shows Hilis Hadjo wearing red head-gear, a military red coat with epaulettes, a tasselled waist belt, and spurred shoes.
- **Creek men’s suits** — Other records show Creek men wearing shirts, trousers, jackets, waistcoats, bow ties, watch chains, and suits.

### Architecture

Muscogee towns centered on plazas, ballparks, and square ceremonial dance grounds.

- **Ceremonial dance grounds** — Square ceremonial dance grounds were part of Muscogee town life.
- **Ballparks** — Ballparks were important spaces in Muscogee town networks.
- **Earthwork mounds** — Complex cities included earthwork mounds and networks of satellite towns and farmsteads.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The profile records pottery, traded goods, specialized crafts, and a nineteenth-century drum.

- **Woodland pottery** — Pottery developed during the Woodland period alongside small-scale horticulture.
- **Trade goods** — Trade included firearms, gunpowder, axes, glass beads, cloth, rum, and European goods.
- **Nineteenth-century drum** — A museum catalogue records a drum made of wood, skin, and various materials.
- **Craft specialization** — Precontact Muscogee polities had specialized craft production.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Recorded adornment centers on beads, silk thread, fringe, and tassels.

- **Chief’s bag beads** — Beads decorated the chief’s bag.
- **Garter beads** — Beads were used on the chief’s garters.
- **Moccasin decoration** — Silk thread decorated the chief’s moccasins.
- **Bag and belt tassels** — Tassels appeared on the chief’s bag and belt.

## Music & performance

Muscogee performance life included ritual leaders, ceremonial grounds, ballparks, stories, and a recorded drum.

- **Medicine maker** (*heles hayv*) — The medicine maker officiated at rituals and provided black drink for purification ceremonies.
- **Warrior adviser** (*tustunnuggee*) — The tustunnuggee was a ranking warrior and principal military adviser.
- **Nineteenth-century drum** — A recorded drum was made of wood, skin, and various materials.
- **Creation stories and legends** — Spoken narratives concern human emergence, the Maker of Breath, the Sun, the Moon, Thunder, the Tie Snake, the Horned Serpent, and Corn Woman.

## Dance & theatre

Muscogee public life included ball-play and square ceremonial dance grounds.

- **Ball-play** — In 1790, the Muscogee and Choctaw settled a land dispute through a game involving nearly 10,000 players and bystanders.
- **Square ceremonial dance grounds** — Confederated town networks were organized around square ceremonial dance grounds.

## Festivals & rituals

Ritual life included purification with black drink and stories about cosmic balance.

- **Purification ceremony** — The medicine maker provided black drink for purification ceremonies.
- **Three Worlds** — Muscogee cosmology divided the universe into the Upper World, Middle World, and Lower World.
- **Tie Snake legend** — Tie Snake stories concern prohibited food, transformation, and cosmic balance.
- **Horned Serpent legend** — Horned Serpent stories concern prohibited food, transformation, and cosmic balance.
- **Corn Woman story** — Corn Woman stories say that corn came from her body and that her sacrifice allowed crops to grow.

## Foodways

Corn, farming, hunting, deer-hide exchange, and black drink are central food-related themes in the profile.

- **Corn** (*maize*) — Corn was central to Mississippian agriculture, surpluses, and population growth.
- **Farming** — Muscogee communities practiced agriculture.
- **Hunting** — Hunting was part of Muscogee life.
- **Deer-hide exchange** — The sources describe exchange involving deer hides.
- **Black drink** — Black drink was used in purification ceremonies.

## Oral tradition & literature

Muscogee oral traditions tell of migration, creation, powerful beings, and the origins of corn.

- **Migration story** — Muscogee oral history describes migration from west of the Mississippi River to the east bank of the Ocmulgee River.
- **Creation stories** — Creation stories say that humans emerged from beneath the earth in the West and traveled eastward.
- **Maker of Breath** (*Hesaketvmesē*) — Hesaketvmesē means “Maker of Breath” or “the One Above who holds life’s breath.”
- **Creator** (*Epohfvnkv*) — Epohfvnkv means “the Creator” or “the One Above us.”
- **Language classes and immersion camps** — The Muscogee Nation supports language classes and immersion camps through educational institutions.

## Language & religion

Muscogee has several dialects, a traditional alphabet, complex grammar, and a cosmology of three worlds.

- **Muscogee language** (*Mvskoke*) — Muscogee, also called Mvskoke or Muskogee, belongs to the Eastern Muskogean branch of the Muskogean family.
- **Related languages** — Related languages and varieties include Hitchiti-Mikasuki, Koasati, and Alabama.
- **Main dialects** — The three main dialects are Muscogee proper, Oklahoma Seminole Muscogee, and Florida Seminole Muscogee.
- **National Alphabet** — Interpreters and chiefs adopted a traditional alphabet as the National Alphabet in 1853.
- **Three Worlds cosmology** — Muscogee mythology describes the Upper World, Middle World, and Lower World.
