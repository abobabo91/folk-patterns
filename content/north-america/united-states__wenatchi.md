## Overview

The Wenatchi are Native Americans who originally lived near the confluence of the Columbia and Wenatchee Rivers in Central Washington state. Their language is Interior Salish, a variant of Salish. The Wenatchee River was their main food source and had one of the greatest salmon runs in the world before numerous hydroelectric dams were built downstream on the Columbia, together with pollution and other issues. Wenatchi foodways also included camas, biscuitroot, berries, deer, sheep, and whatever else could be hunted or caught. The Wenatchi were organized into individually distinct bands associated with Lake Chelan, the Entiat River, the Columbia, and different parts of the Wenatchee River.

## Music & performance

A Powwow was held in 1931 on the land of Mary Felix, a Wenatchi woman living in Yaksum Canyon in Cashmere, Washington; nearly 700 Native Americans attended, including 250 Wenatchi and people traveling from the Colville and Yakama reservations.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do record a Powwow held in 1931 on the land of Mary Felix in Yaksum Canyon in Cashmere, Washington. In 2024, the Colville Confederated Tribes acquired 11 acres of land near Cashmere along the Wenatchee River as part of an effort to reclaim ownership of ancestral lands.

## Foodways

Traditionally, the Wenatchi ate salmon, starchy roots such as camas and biscuitroot, berries, deer, sheep, and whatever else they could hunt or catch. The Wenatchee River was their main food source because of its great salmon runs before numerous hydroelectric dams were built downstream on the Columbia, together with pollution and other issues.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do record the history of the Wenatchapam Fishery, an important cultural site at the confluence of the Wenatchee River and Icicle Creek near Leavenworth. The fishery was named as a reservation site in the Yakama treaty from the Walla Walla Council in 1855. Wenatchi Chief John Harmelt later sought federal protection of Wenatchi rights to the fishery, with support from Chief Joseph of the Nez Perce people.

## Language & religion

The Wenatchi language is Interior Salish, a variant of Salish. The Wenatchi are closely related by language to the Entiat and Chelan peoples and through marriage and culture to Upper Yakima bands.
