## Overview

The Nooksack are an Indigenous people of the Pacific Northwest Coast. Today, most are federally recognized as the Nooksack Indian Tribe, located in the mainland northwest corner of Washington state along the Nooksack River near Deming, in western Whatcom County, 12 miles south of the Canadian border. As of 2008, the tribe had more than 1,800 enrolled citizens. The Nooksack are part of the Coast Salish people and traditionally spoke Nooksack, a Salishan language closely related to Halkomelem of coastal British Columbia. Their historic territory extended from the mountains to the coast of present-day northwest Washington state and into British Columbia. Their cultural history includes fishing, hunting, clamming, gathering root vegetables and berries, seasonal camps, and the continuing organization of a federally recognized tribal community.

## Music & performance

The sources do identify local language revitalization work connected with cultural activities. In the Lhéchelesem Teacher Training Language Immersion Project, students spend mornings in language immersion and afternoons working on special projects focused on language use in one aspect of local native culture, such as fishing or crafts. The project provides a certificate after two years and fully qualified language teachers after four years, with the equivalent of a Bachelor of Arts. Its aim is to revive Lhéchelesem in all aspects of daily life. As of 2020, one fluent speaker remained, and the annual budget was $110,000, with 60 percent funded by the Administration for Native Americans and 40 percent by the Nooksack Indian Tribe.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe seasonal camps for hunting and fishing, including timing activities to harvest salmon runs. The sources also describe General Council as an ancient tradition in which the tribe acts together as a single unit and, in extreme cases of misuse of authority, can revoke authority given to its leaders. In July 2016, Tribal Elder George Adams called a General Council meeting to conduct a special election of four open council seats. More than 200 attendees met off the reservation, elected four interim council members, and invalidated the recall of Carmen Tageant from the Tribal Council.

## Foodways

Nooksack subsistence historically depended on fishing, hunting, clamming, gathering root vegetables and berries, and processing these resources for sustenance. Their territory included the watershed of the Nooksack River and extended from the mountains to the coast of present-day northwest Washington state and into British Columbia, Canada. The sources mention seasonal timing for salmon runs and federal court decisions in the 1970s affirming Native American rights in Washington state to fish and gather food in traditional ways from historic territories.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do document linguistic preservation. In the 1970s, the linguist Brent Galloway worked closely with the fluent native speaker Sindick Jimmy to compile a dictionary of the Nooksack language. Galloway’s book, *Nooksack Place Names: Geography, Culture, and Language*, was published in 2011. The sources also state that the name Nooksack comes from a Nooksack place name meaning “always bracken fern roots.” The Nooksack Indian Tribe’s Lhéchelesem Teacher Training Language Immersion Project continues efforts to revive use of the language in daily life.

## Language & religion

Nooksack is a Coast Salish language of the Salishan language family. It is spoken by the Nooksack people, who reside primarily along the Nooksack River in Whatcom County, Washington. The language is closely related to Halkomelem and is also described as most closely related to the Squamish, shíshálh, and Halkomelem languages. Some researchers questioned whether Nooksack was a divergent dialect of Halkomelem, but the source states that research established it as a distinct language. Nooksack is also called Lhéchelesem; another source gives the forms Lhéchalosem, Noxwsʼáʔaq, and Nuxwsá7aq. In 1977, Nooksack became extinct with the death of Sindick Jimmy, while another source states that one fluent speaker remained as of 2020.
