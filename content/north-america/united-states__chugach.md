## Overview

The Chugach, also known as Chugach Sugpiaq or Chugachigmiut, are an Alaska Native people of the Kenai Peninsula and Prince William Sound on the southern coast of Alaska. They are an Alutiiq (Pacific Native) people who speak the Chugach dialect of the Alutiiq language. Their villages include Chenega Bay, Eyak, Nanwalek (English Bay), Port Graham, and Tatitlek. The Chugach have lived around Prince William Sound for millennia, according to archaeological finds, and encountered the Russian explorer Vitus Bering in 1741.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do record historical accounts of androgynous third-gender or two-spirit individuals known as *aranu'tiq*. Anthropologists writing in the 1950s described these individuals as being considered male on one side of their bodies and female on the other. Some had descriptive names such as “Tyakutyik,” translated as “What Kind Of People Are These Two?”, although the description was given to many types of people in the community and was not related to gender expression.

## Foodways

The sources state that the Chugach economy was based on fishing and that it was badly affected by environmental damage caused by the Exxon Valdez oil spill in 1989.

## Language & religion

Chugach people speak one of two dialects of the Pacific Gulf Yupik language; the other is Koniag. These Central Yupik languages belong to the Alaskan Yupik language family. The language was once written in Cyrillic script and is now written in the Latin script.
