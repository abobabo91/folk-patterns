## Overview

The Assiniboine were a Plains people who hunted bison and became key traders in the northern fur trade, exchanging beaver and hides for Hudson's Bay goods. Their population crashed from disease in the 1800s. Today they live mainly in Canada with smaller U.S. communities. They are known for hide-work, beaded decoration, and horsemanship.

## Material culture

Assiniboine material culture centers on hide-work and glass-bead decoration, with metal and wool goods acquired through the fur trade.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Women processed hides for clothing and worked every part of hunted animals into useful things.

- **Hide-work** — Women cured and processed skins into clothing, bedding, tools and cord; every part of the animal was used.
- **Glass-bead decoration on tanned skin** — Glass beads from the fur trade were sewn onto native-tanned skin to make ornaments and decorated bags.
- **Pigmented rawhide and sinew assembly** — Binding and coloring with rawhide, sinew and pigment finished weapons and tools.

### Clothing & dress

Assiniboine dress in the 1800s mixed traditional hide garments with fur-trade wool and cloth.

- **Hide tunic** — Men and women wore fringed or plain tunics made from tanned hide.
- **Wool coat** — Men's outer garments acquired from the fur trade, often worn over inner hide garments.
- **Hudson's Bay point blanket** — Trade blankets worn wrapped or draped, often with leggings.
- **Moccasins** — Footwear worn by men and women.
- **Studded waist belt** — A belt carrying a knife sheath or pouches, often studded with metal.

### Architecture

The Assiniboine lived in portable tipis as they followed the bison herds across the Plains.

- **Tipi** (*Wiʼį́kceya tíbi or įkcéwąga*) — A cone-shaped portable dwelling that housed a nuclear family or extended family.
- **Camp** — Multiple tipis arranged together formed a band settlement that moved with the herds.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

No pottery appears in the record; metal goods and stone tools came from hunting and the fur trade.

- **Metal pot** — Cooking vessels acquired through the fur trade to replace hide containers and stone-boiling methods.
- **Metal tomahawk** — A weapon acquired through the fur trade alongside guns and ammunition.
- **Club (skullcracker)** — A weapon made from stone, wood, rawhide, sinew, pigment and feathers, finished about 1850–60.
- **Tobacco bag** — A pouch combining native-tanned skin, glass beads and wool, typical of 1860–70.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment was worn at the neck and in the hair, with feathers and beaded ornaments as status markers.

- **Neck-ornament** — Decorative items worn around the neck or across the chest, including beaded ornaments.
- **Feather in hair** — Feathers arranged in the hair as personal adornment.

## Music & performance

The surviving record does not describe Assiniboine instruments or song genres.

- **Wichiyabina band** (*Wiciyabina or Wįcį́jana*) — A band name meaning 'Ones That Go to the Dance' or 'Girl Band,' the only musical reference in the sources.

## Dance & theatre

The Sun Dance was the main named dance and ritual practice.

- **Sun Dance** — A collective rite performed by the Assiniboine as by other Plains peoples.

## Festivals & rituals

Religious life centered on the Sun and Thunder gods, with the Sun Dance as the central collective ceremony.

- **Sun Dance** — The main collective religious rite honoring the Sun god.
- **Vision quest** — An individual spiritual practice in which a person sought guidance from personal visions.
- **Medicine man** (*Wócegiye įtącą*) — A religious leader and healer who used plants and herbs to treat illness and provide spiritual guidance.
- **Band chief** (*Hųgá or Hunga*) — The leader of a local group or band, advised by an advisory council of little chiefs.
- **War chief** (*įtą́cą*) — A leader who took charge of men in battle.

## Foodways

Bison hunted from horseback was the staple; women processed and preserved meat and gathered plants.

- **Bison hunt** — Men hunted plains bison from horseback using bow and arrows, providing the main food source.
- **Stone-boiling** — A cooking method in which hot stones were dropped into water to boil meat; the practice gave the Assiniboine their best-known name.
- **Meat preservation** — Women processed and preserved meat for winter storage.
- **Plant gathering and cultivation** — Women gathered and cultivated plants for food and medicine.

## Oral tradition & literature

The creation myth of Iktome, an amoral trickster-creator, is the central narrative.

- **Iktome** — A creator-trickster figure in an earth-diver creation story who sends animals to find land beneath a primeval sea.
- **Earth-diver story** — A creation narrative in which the muskrat dives to the bottom of a sea and returns with earth to form land.
- **Band names** — Assiniboine band names carried their own stories, such as Wadópaȟna Tųwą, 'Canoe Paddlers Who Live on the Prairie.'

## Language & religion

Assiniboine is a Nakotan Siouan language with 27 consonants and 8 vowels; fewer than 150 fluent speakers remain.

- **Nakotan language** (*Nakʰóta, Nakʰóda, or Nakʰóna*) — An n-variety Siouan language closer to Sioux dialects than to Stoney, with no articles, case marking or tense marking.
- **Numerals** — Count words: wąži (1), nųba (2), yamni (3), tópa (4), záptą (5), šákpe (6), iyušna (7), šaknoğą (8), napcuwąga (9), wikcémna (10).
- **Sun god and Thunder god** — The most important manifestations of the Great Spirit in Assiniboine belief.

## Glossary

- *Nakoda* — Assiniboine people; their name for themselves
- *Wiʼį́kceya tíbi* — tipi; a portable dwelling
- *Wócegiye įtącą* — medicine man; religious leader and healer
- *Hųgá* — band chief; leader
- *įtą́cą* — war chief; leader in battle
- *Iktome* — creator-trickster figure in creation myth
- *Wiciyabina* — band name meaning Ones That Go to the Dance
- *vision quest* — spiritual practice of seeking personal guidance
- *earth-diver* — creation story type in which animals dive for land
- *Nakotan* — n-variety of Siouan languages including Assiniboine
