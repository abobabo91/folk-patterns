## Overview

Paiute is a historical label for three non-contiguous Indigenous groups of the Great Basin. These are Northern Paiute, Southern Paiute, and Mono. Their languages are related but do not form one single subgroup. Museum records connect Paiute objects with the western United States and people near Pyramid Lake, Nevada.

## Material culture

The records focus on basketry, water-bottles, clothing, blankets, moccasins, beads, and hair ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources give little detail about named textile or pattern traditions, but they describe distinctive basketry and basket foundations.

- **Pah-Utes basketry** (*Pah-utes*) — Catalogue records say Navajos later traded textiles for baskets made by the Pah-utes.
- **Triangular basket foundation** (*Rhus*) — Ute and Paiute basket makers use a triangular foundation of three sumac rods, unlike the Navajo two-rod foundation.
- **Navajo Wedding basket** (*Pah-utes*) — One basket made by the Pah-utes is identified in a record as a so-called Navajo Wedding basket.
- **Watertight plant-fiber bottle** — The records include a completely watertight bottle made of plant fibers.
- **Twigs-and-pitch water-bottle** — Another recorded water-bottle is made of twigs and pitch.

### Clothing & dress

Paiute clothing records show cloth garments, blankets, leggings, moccasins, fringed tunics, loin coverings, and beadwork.

- **Warrior shirt** — One Paiute man wears a warrior shirt with beaded decoration and tassels.
- **Fringed tunic** (*Won-si-vu*) — Young Antelope, or Won-si-vu, is shown wearing a fringed tunic with a loin covering and cloth headband.
- **Fibre loin covering** (*Ka-ni*) — Ka-ni is described as wearing a fibre loin covering.
- **Moccasins** — Photographic records show Paiute men, women, and children wearing moccasins or other recorded clothing.
- **Children’s cloth clothing** — Children are shown wearing cloth tops, jackets, necklaces, and blankets.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The records describe containers, baskets, a cradle or cot, and a bear basket, but give little detail about other everyday objects.

- **Twigs-and-pitch water-bottle** — This bottle is made of twigs and pitch.
- **Watertight fiber bottle** — This bottle is made of plant fibers and is completely watertight.
- **Cradle or cot** — The museum catalogue includes a cradle or cot.
- **Bear basket** — The museum catalogue includes a bear basket.
- **String basket** — The catalogue records include baskets made of basketry and string.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment records emphasize ear and hair ornaments, necklaces, beads, embroidered clothing, and wrapped hair.

- **Ear ornaments** — Paiute photographic records show men wearing ear ornaments.
- **Hair ornaments** — The records describe hair ornaments and beads worn in the hair.
- **Beaded necklace** — Paiute people are shown wearing beaded necklaces and other necklaces.
- **Cloth-wrapped hair plait** — One man has part of his hair in a plait wrapped in cloth.
- **Cloth headband** — Young Antelope is shown wearing a cloth headband.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not describe a festival calendar or named rites, but one Pyramid Lake legend teaches respect for the water.

- **Pyramid Lake water-respect tradition** — A record says lake spirits provide water and that people must show the water proper respect.
- **Crying children of Pyramid Lake** — The sound of crying children is said to tempt people into the lake, where strong underwater currents cause them to drown.

## Oral tradition & literature

The catalogue preserves one Pyramid Lake legend about crying children, lake spirits, drowning, and respect for water.

- **Pyramid Lake crying-children legend** — The legend says crying children are heard from the lake and draw people into dangerous underwater currents.
- **Pyramid Lake spirits** — The record says that without the spirits there would be no water and that people must respect the water.

## Language & religion

Paiute languages belong to the Numic group of Uto-Aztecan languages, while the sources describe lake spirits and respect for Pyramid Lake water.

- **Northern Paiute language** (*Numu*) — Northern Paiute language is also called Numu and Paviotso.
- **Paviotso** — Paviotso is another name for Northern Paiute language.
- **Mono language** (*Western dialect*) — Mono language in California includes a Western dialect also known as Owens Valley Paiute.
- **Owens Valley Paiute** — Owens Valley Paiute is another name for the Western dialect of Mono language.
- **Colorado River Numic language** (*Ute-Chemehuevi*) — Colorado River Numic language is also known as Ute, Southern Paiute, Ute–Southern Paiute, or Ute-Chemehuevi.
