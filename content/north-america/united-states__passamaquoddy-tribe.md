## Overview

The Passamaquoddy, whose endonym is *Peskotomuhkat* and whose plural form is *Peskotomuhkatiyik*, are a Native American/First Nations people living in northeastern North America. Their traditional homeland, *Peskotomuhkatikuk*, straddles New Brunswick and Maine in a region called Dawnland. They are one of the constituent nations of the Wabanaki Confederacy. In Maine, the Passamaquoddy Tribe is federally recognized; the Peskotomuhkati Nation in Canada does not have official First Nations status. The total Passamaquoddy population is around 3,576 people. Their language belongs to the Algonquian branch of the Algic language family. Their cultural history includes seasonal settlement, seafood harvesting, spear-fishing, oral history, birchbark etching, and petrographs.

## Foodways

The Passamaquoddy name reflects the importance of pollock in their culture. *Peskotomuhkat* literally means “pollock-spearer” or “those of the place where pollock are plentiful.” Their method of fishing was spear-fishing rather than angling or using nets.

The sources describe seasonal harvesting rather than named dishes or recipes. In summer, the Passamaquoddy primarily harvested seafood on the coast and islands, including marine mammals, mollusks, crustaceans, and fish. In winter, they dispersed and hunted inland.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Passamaquoddy have an oral history supported with visual imagery, including birchbark etching and petrographs before European contact.

The University of Maine published a comprehensive *Passamaquoddy Dictionary* in 2008. Another language resource is the online Passamaquoddy-Maliseet Language Portal, which includes videos of native speakers conversing in Passamaquoddy and English. The sources also list *An Upriver Passamaquoddy* by Allen J. Sockabasin, published in 2007 by Tilbury House.

## Language & religion

The Passamaquoddy language is shared, apart from minor dialect differences, with the neighboring and related Wolastoqiyik people. About 500 people, most if not all over the age of 50, speak the Maliseet-Passamaquoddy language. It belongs to the Algonquian branch of the Algic language family. Most Passamaquoddy people speak English as their first language. In Canada, most people of the Peskotomuhkati Nation speak French and English.
