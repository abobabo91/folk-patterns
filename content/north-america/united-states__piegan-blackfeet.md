## Overview

The Piegan are the largest of the three Blackfeet-speaking groups of the Blackfeet Confederacy. They dominated much of the northern Great Plains during the nineteenth century. Their homelands were divided between Canada and the United States, forming the Blackfeet Nation in Montana and the Piikani Nation in Alberta. They practiced some agriculture, were partly nomadic, and expanded their bison-hunting range after adopting horses and guns.

## Material culture

Museum records show decorated hide and cloth clothing, beadwork, feathered headdresses, and personal ornaments, but document little else about material culture.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources describe decorated clothing and beadwork but name no textile traditions or motifs.

- **Decorated hide clothing** — Big Spring wore a decorated hide shirt and leggings, a loin covering, and moccasins.
- **Beadwork** — Beadwork appears on a woman’s cloth dress, Big Spring’s hide shirt, and men’s bracelets and chokers.
- **Feathered headdresses** — Museum records show feather headdresses worn by Big Spring, Acustie, and members of Many Mixtures, Jim Little Leaf, and Joe Calf Child.
- **Decorated cloth tunic** — Acustie wore a decorated cloth tunic with leggings and several bead, feather, fur, and shell ornaments.
- **Hide shirt** — Records describe Big Spring’s decorated hide shirt and Two Bears’s hide shirt with bead decoration.

### Clothing & dress

The records show varied clothing and adornment.

- **Big Spring’s outfit** — Big Spring wore a feather headdress, necklaces, a decorated hide shirt and leggings, a loin covering, and moccasins.
- **Woman’s cloth dress** — Big Spring’s wife wore a brimmed felt hat, a cloth scarf, and a decorated cloth dress with beadwork and horizontal cloth strips.
- **Acustie’s outfit** (*Many Diving*) — Acustie wore a decorated cloth tunic, leggings, a fur and shell neck ornament, a bead and feather choker, beaded bracelets, and a horned ermine-trimmed headdress.
- **Two Bears’s outfit** — Two Bears wore an eagle feather headdress tipped with horse hair and a hide shirt with bead decoration.
- **Horseback costumes** (*Many Mixtures; Jim Little Leaf; Joe Calf Child*) — Three photographed people wore assorted feathered headdresses and costumes while on horseback.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Personal adornment included necklaces, beadwork, feathers, fur, shells, and horse hair.

- **Necklaces** — Big Spring wore necklaces.
- **Fur and shell neck ornament** — Acustie wore a fur and shell neck ornament.
- **Bead and feather choker** — Acustie wore a bead and feather choker.
- **Beaded bracelets** — Acustie wore beaded bracelets, and a man’s bracelets are also recorded.
- **Eagle feather headdress** — Two Bears wore an eagle feather headdress tipped with horse hair.

## Music & performance

The sources name no instruments or song traditions, but describe women who could sing alone in a men’s style.

- **Manly-hearted women** — Some Blackfeet women took on many social roles of men.
- **Solo singing** — These women were willing to sing alone, which was usually considered immodest.
- **Men’s singing style** — These women could use a men’s singing style.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources give no festival calendar or ritual procedures, but describe a sacred force and creation traditions.

- **Sacred force** — The Blackfeet believe in a sacred force that permeates all things.
- **Sun symbolism** — The sun represents this sacred force because its light sustains all things.
- **Blackfoot creation story** — The creation story is set near Glacier National Park in the area now called the Badger-Two Medicine.
- **Tribal oral histories** — Oral histories place humans near the Rocky Mountain Front for thousands of years before European contact.

## Foodways

The sources describe agriculture, nomadic movement, bison hunting, and starvation but name no dishes or cooking methods.

- **Agriculture** — The people practiced some agriculture.
- **Bison hunting** — Adopting horses and guns gave them a larger range for bison hunting.
- **Last buffalo hunt** — The last buffalo hunt failed in 1882.
- **Starvation year** — The failed 1882 hunt became known as the starvation year.

## Oral tradition & literature

Piegan traditions include creation stories and oral histories, while later writers have written fiction and history about the Blackfeet.

- **Blackfoot creation story** — The creation story is set near Glacier National Park in the Badger-Two Medicine area.
- **Tribal oral histories** — Tribal oral histories say that humans lived near the Rocky Mountain Front for thousands of years before European contact.
- **Killing Custer** — James Welch wrote this non-fiction historical account of the Battle of Little Bighorn and the fate of the Plains Indians.
- **Fools Crow** — James Welch’s award-winning novel is based on the Blackfeet tribe and its culture.
- **The Buffalo Hunter Hunter** — Stephen Graham Jones’s novel centers on a Piegan Blackfeet man who becomes a vampire and seeks revenge.

## Language & religion

The Piegan speak Blackfoot, an Algonquian language, and describe a sacred force represented by the sun.

- **Blackfoot language** (*ᑯᖿᖹ / Piikáni / ṗiik̇ǔni*) — Blackfoot is an agglutinative language in the Algonquian family.
- **Piegan names** (*Pikuni; Piikuni; Piikani; Piikáni*) — The people are known by several related names, including Piegan, Pikuni, Piikuni, Piikani, and Piikáni.
- **Siksika** (*ᓱᖽᐧᖿ*) — Siksika is one of the other Blackfeet-speaking groups in the Blackfeet Confederacy.
- **Siksikáíkoan** (*ᓱᖽᐧᖼᖾᖳᐡ*) — The name Siksiká is derived from Siksikáíkoan.
- **Sacred force and sun** — Blackfeet belief centers on a sacred force that permeates all things and is symbolized by the sun.
