## Overview

The Tuscarora Nation (*Skarù·ręʔ Kayedá·kreh*) is a federally recognized tribe of Tuscarora people. Its headquarters is on the Tuscarora Reservation in Lewiston, New York, where its elected chief is Tom Jonathan. The Tuscarora migrated in ancient times from the New York area to the Carolinas. After their defeat in the Tuscarora War at the beginning of the 18th century, most migrated north to Haudenosaunee territory beginning in 1722. They first settled in the territory of the Oneida people in central New York and declared their nation fully relocated by 1765. The sources do not state the population, language family, or broader folk-culture significance of the Tuscarora Nation.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Beaded floral motif decoration** — The museum catalogue records baby carriers decorated with a beaded floral motif. The records do not identify a vernacular name, technique, or comparison with neighboring cultures.

**Cloth dresses** — The catalogue records Tuscarora women and girls wearing cloth dresses. The sources do not describe their construction, patterns, or distinction from other clothing traditions.

**Baby carriers** — The catalogue records baby carriers in which babies were wrapped in bundles. Several carriers are described as having beaded floral motif decoration.

### Clothing & dress

The museum catalogue records a Tuscarora woman wearing a cloth dress and holding a baby in a baby carrier. Another catalogue record describes a Tuscarora (?) woman wearing a cloth head covering and cloth dress, while a girl beside her wears a hat, necklace, cloth dress, and boots. The babies are described as wrapped in bundles attached to baby carriers. The sources do not describe men’s dress, ceremonial clothing, belts, or distinctions between everyday and ceremonial dress.
