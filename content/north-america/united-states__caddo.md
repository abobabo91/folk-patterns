## Overview

The Caddo are a Native people whose homeland included parts of present-day Texas, Louisiana, Arkansas, and Oklahoma. Their ancestors formed the Caddoan Mississippian culture, built large earthwork mounds, and developed extensive maize farming. Caddo culture includes strong oral traditions, seasonal ceremonies, body adornment, pottery, songs, and dances. The Caddo Nation and community groups continue language and cultural preservation.

## Material culture

Caddo material culture included earthwork mounds, pottery, wooden tools, bows and arrows, body painting, tattoos, jewelry, and decorated hair.

### Clothing & dress

Caddo clothing work was associated with women, while body painting, jewelry, hair decoration, and tattoos were important forms of appearance.

- **Clothing making** — Zacado was said to have taught the Caddo how to make clothing.
- **Three-piece suits** — Museum portraits show Caddo chiefs wearing western-style three-piece suits.
- **Beaded waistband** — Little Boy, also called Show-e-tat, is shown wearing a beaded waistband.

### Architecture

Caddo settlements included villages, hamlets, and farmsteads arranged around platform mounds and open ceremonial plazas.

- **Platform mounds** — Leaders directed the building of flat-topped earthworks used as temple mounds and elite residential platforms.
- **Open plazas** — Large leveled plazas around the mounds were usually swept clean and used for ceremonies.
- **Bayous** — Caddo communities lived in the Piney Woods, a landscape of hills, river valleys, and intermittent wetlands called bayous.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Caddo people made pottery, wooden tools, bows, arrows, and other objects for daily work, hunting, and ceremony.

- **Red ochre vessel** — A Caddo bottle found near Fayetteville was colored red with iron ochre by dipping the vessel in ochre.
- **Wooden mortars** — Caddo women made wooden mortars, pots, and other utensils for daily tasks.
- **Bows and arrows** — Handcrafted bows and arrows were used for hunting.
- **Ceremonial drum and pipe** — The creation story says an old man carried a drum and a pipe.
- **Fire** — Fire was carried in the creation story and held continuing religious importance.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Caddo adornment included painted skin, tattoos, pierced ears, jewelry, decorated hair, feathers, and animal fur.

- **Painted skin** — Men favored painted skin as part of their body ornamentation.
- **Facial and body tattoos** — Women were especially associated with tattoos that could cover most of the body, including the face.
- **Metal ear ornaments** — Museum portraits of Little Boy, White Deer, and Warloupe show metal ear ornaments.
- **Feather and fur hair decorations** — Braids were decorated with bird feathers or animal fur.

## Music & performance

Caddo songs and dances are taught and performed by the Hasinai Society and the Caddo Culture Club.

- **Caddo songs** — The Hasinai Society and Caddo Culture Club teach and perform songs to continue Caddo culture.
- **Drum and pipe** — A drum and a pipe have continuing religious importance in Caddo tradition.
- **Big Moon ceremony** (*Big Moon*) — John Wilson introduced the Big Moon peyote ceremony in 1880.
- **Half Moon ceremony** (*Half Moon*) — The Caddo already knew the Half Moon peyote ceremony before the Big Moon ceremony was introduced.

## Dance & theatre

Caddo dances are taught and performed for cultural preservation, and the Ghost Dance was practiced in the late nineteenth century.

- **Ghost Dance** — Caddo people practiced the Ghost Dance in hopes of restoring traditional ways and stopping European-American intrusions.
- **Caddo cultural dances** — The Hasinai Society and Caddo Culture Club teach and perform Caddo dances.

## Festivals & rituals

Caddo religious life follows seasonal ceremonies connected with corn cultivation and centers on Kadhi háyuh, the Leader Above.

- **Leader Above** (*Kadhi háyuh*) — Caddo religious practice centers on Kadhi háyuh, translated as Leader Above or Leader in the Sky.
- **Head priest** (*xinesi*) — The xinesi was a head priest who was said to commune with spirits near Caddo temples.
- **Place of crying** (*Chahkanina*) — The creation story says the Caddo emerged from Chahkanina, or the place of crying.
- **Purifying drink** — Early priests drank a purifying sacrament made from wild olive leaves.
- **Seasonal corn ceremonies** — Caddo ceremonies developed around important stages of seasonal corn cultivation.

## Foodways

Caddo foodways combined maize farming, other crops, hunting, gathering, and seasonal processing.

- **Maize** — Maize was especially important to Caddo farming and supported dense settlements.
- **Garden crops** — Caddo people cultivated sunflower, pumpkins, squash, beans, melons, and tobacco.
- **Cold-season foods** — Acorns and roots were gathered and processed when crops did not grow.
- **Wild turkey hunting** — Men hunted wild turkeys, which held cultural significance, with bows and arrows.
- **Purifying sacrament** — Early priests used a drink made from wild olive leaves as a purifying sacrament.

## Oral tradition & literature

Caddo oral tradition preserves creation stories, migration history, and teachings about everyday life.

- **Caddo creation story** — The creation story tells how the Caddo emerged from an underground place and includes Moon, the wolf, corn, pumpkin seeds, a drum, a pipe, and fire.
- **Westward movement** (*Bah'hatteno*) — The oral history recounts the westward movement of the Caddo along the Red River, called Bah'hatteno in Caddo.
- **Zacado's teachings** — Zacado's teachings concerned hunting, fishing, dwellings, and clothing.
- **Kiwat Hasí꞉nay archives** (*Kiwat Hasí꞉nay*) — The Kiwat Hasí꞉nay foundation worked to record and digitally archive Caddo oral traditions.

## Language & religion

Caddo is a Caddoan language with several named dialects, and its religious traditions include seasonal ceremonies, priests, spirits, peyote ceremonies, and the Ghost Dance.

- **Caddo language** (*Hasí꞉nay*) — Caddo, whose endonym is Hasí꞉nay, belongs to the Caddoan language family.
- **Hasinai dialect** (*Hasinai*) — Hasinai was one of the commonly used Caddo dialects.
- **Hainai dialect** (*Hainai*) — Hainai was another commonly used Caddo dialect.
- **Kadohadacho dialect** (*Kadohadacho*) — Kadohadacho was identified as a Caddo dialect.
- **Natchitoches and Yatasi dialects** (*Natchitoches; Yatasi*) — Natchitoches and Yatasi were also identified as Caddo dialects.
