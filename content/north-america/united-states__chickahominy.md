## Overview

The Chickahominy are a federally recognized tribe of Virginian Native Americans whose traditional territory followed the Chickahominy River from its mouth at the James River near Jamestown to what is now New Kent County, Virginia. Today, the Chickahominy primarily live in Charles City County, along the James River between Richmond and Williamsburg. The tribe consists of about 840 people living near the tribal center at Chickahominy Ridge, while several hundred more live elsewhere in the United States. The Eastern Chickahominy are a separate federally recognized tribe based in New Kent County and have about 132 members. Chickahominy history includes maize agriculture, shell-tempered pottery, collective ossuaries, intertown gathering places, fortified towns, fishing, and relations with the English and Powhatan Confederacy.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The Chickahominy constructed sites for intertown gatherings and burial grounds in marshy areas. The lower portion of the Chickahominy River had fortified towns and facilities for feasting. In the modern period, the tribal center stands on current tribal lands in the traditional territory of present-day Charles City County, and the annual Powwow and Fall Festival are held there.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

During their early history, the Chickahominy made shell-tempered pottery.

## Music & performance

## Festivals & rituals

The tribal center on the Chickahominy’s current lands is the location of an annual Powwow and Fall Festival. In earlier history, the Chickahominy constructed intertown gathering places and burial grounds in marshy areas, while the lower river had facilities for feasting. In 500 AD, they began burying their dead in collective ossuaries, centuries before neighboring nations.

## Foodways

The Chickahominy practiced Three Sisters agriculture with maize, beans, and squash. Corn possibly made up 50% of the Chickahominy diet. They also maintained abundant stocks of fish and migratory birds. During the first few winters after the English settlement at Jamestown, the Chickahominy traded food for English goods and taught the English how to grow and preserve crops in local conditions. The lower portion of the river had facilities for feasting.

## Oral tradition & literature

They record historical accounts of Chickahominy relations with the English and the Powhatan Confederacy, including differing claims about whether the Chickahominy joined the confederacy in 1616, in 1677, or never joined it.

## Language & religion

At contact, the Chickahominy were led by Mungai, described as “great men,” together with elders called Cawcawwasoughs and religious leaders. Most members of the Chickahominy Tribe are Christian, and many attend Samaria Baptist Church, formerly called Samaria Indian Church, in Charles City County. The church was built on tribal grounds and once served as a school for tribal children.
