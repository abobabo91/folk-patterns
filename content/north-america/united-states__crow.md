## Overview

The Crow are a Plains people of southern Montana whose historical territory was the Yellowstone River valley and surrounding ranges. They are documented as skilled horse breeders and bison hunters whose material culture—beadwork, painted hides, and ceremonial objects—remains in museum collections and continues on the reservation today. Their religious and ceremonial life centers on the Sun Dance, the Tobacco Society, and vision quests. The Crow Fair, begun in 1918, remains an annual gathering in August.

## Material culture

Crow material culture combines native-tanned skin and traditional forms with traded materials like glass beads, wool cloth, and metal.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Beadwork on cloth and skin, quillwork, and painted designs are the main textile arts.

- **Elk-tooth dress** — A woman's dress of blue felted wool decorated with elk teeth and red wool cloth at the neck, wrist and hem.
- **Beadwork** — Glass beads worked onto trade cloth and native-tanned skin appear on leggings, cuffs, moccasins, blanket strips and paint bags, often in floral designs.
- **Painted bison robe** — A buffalo hide robe painted with images of horses and riders.
- **Quillwork** — Porcupine quill decoration appears on men's shirts and armlets made of native-tanned skin.
- **Parfleche** — A bag of rawhide used for storage and transport.

### Clothing & dress

Men wore skin shirts and leggings decorated with beads and quills; women wore blue wool dresses ornamented with elk teeth.

- **Man's skin shirt** — A shirt of about 1860 combining native-tanned skin with factory-woven cloth, porcupine quill, glass beads, horsehair, wool yarn and ermine.
- **Leggings** — Men's leggings made of blue cotton or skin, decorated with glass beadwork, finished with skin tassels and metal bells.
- **Moccasins** — Footwear made of skin and beads.
- **Elk-tooth dress** — A woman's blue felted wool dress ornamented with elk teeth and red cloth, the main garment for women.
- **Tobacco dance dress** — A girl's cotton dress made specifically for the tobacco dance ceremony, marking ritual rather than daily use.

### Architecture

The Crow lived in tipis and also built two ceremonial structures: the Sun Dance lodge and the sweat lodge.

- **Tipi** — A hide tent used as the main dwelling, shown in photographs with the hide opened along one side or fully enclosed.
- **Sun Dance lodge** (*Ashé Isée*) — A large ceremonial structure with a forked central pole encircled by twelve cottonwood posts about forty feet out, with the doorway facing east.
- **Sweat lodge** (*Little Lodge*) — A ceremonial structure of twelve poles with an east-facing door and a two-foot-square pit at the centre for heated rocks.
- **Earth lodge** — In their earlier village phase, the Crow lived in settled villages of earth lodges and grew crops before moving west around 1675–1700.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Eating vessels are made of horn and antler; storage is in rawhide and skin; ritual objects include rattles, whips and ropes of natural materials.

- **Buffalo horn spoon** — A spoon carved from buffalo horn for eating.
- **Bighorn sheep horn spoon** — A spoon carved from bighorn sheep horn for eating.
- **Moose antler spoon** — A spoon carved from moose antler for eating.
- **Sun Dance rattle** — A ritual object made from a gourd, used in the Sun Dance ceremony.
- **Medicine rope** — A rope made of buffalo and horse hair with wool cloth and beads, a ritual object.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment centers on medicine necklaces made from natural materials and representing sacred power.

- **Horse-tooth necklace** (*Xapáaliia*) — A medicine necklace made of horse teeth, beads, claws and a leather cord, embodying sacred power granted by a spirit patron.
- **Feather and horse-hair necklace** (*Xapáaliia*) — A medicine necklace made of feathers, horse hair, stone and leather cord, representing a vision-quester's spirit patron.
- **Armlet** — A pair of bracelets made of hide and porcupine quills.
- **Shell choker** — A necklace of shells worn with blankets and feathers in the hair, as shown in the portrait of Lone Wolf.
- **Braided hair** — Hair worn in two braids, often adorned with feathers, as recorded in portraits of Crow men.

## Music & performance

Named instruments are used only in ceremony: the gourd rattle, eagle-bone whistles, and drums.

- **Sun Dance rattle** — A gourd rattle used by dancers and drummers during the Sun Dance ceremony.
- **Eagle-bone whistle** — Whistles blown by dancers during the Sunrise Ceremony to absorb the power of the sun.
- **Drum** — Central to the Sun Dance: regular rhythms help dancers sustain effort, and irregular beating exhausts them; drummers strike harder when a dancer enters a vision.
- **Sunrise song** — A named song sung at dawn facing east as the first rays appear, while participants dance, pray and blow bone whistles.

## Dance & theatre

Two dances are recorded: the Sun Dance, the major public ceremony, and the tobacco dance.

- **Sun Dance** (*aškišširissu-a*) — The major public ceremony danced for three days in the Big Lodge: dancers fast and keep their eyes on an idol on the central pole until they collapse into a vision.
- **Tobacco dance** — A ceremony attested by a girl's cotton dress made specifically for it, marking ritual rather than daily wear.

## Festivals & rituals

The Crow Fair began in 1918; the Sun Dance is the main summer ceremony; the sweat lodge purifies body and soul before the Sun Dance; vision quests send individuals alone into the mountains; the Tobacco Society tends the sacred plant.

- **Crow Fair** — An annual gathering begun in 1918, held on the third weekend of August, where Crow and members of other tribes display their culture.
- **Sun Dance** (*aškišširissu-a*) — The summer ceremony sponsored by an individual of repute who gathers materials and sets the agenda; sap from the cut forked tree is considered a good omen.
- **Sweat lodge** (*Little Lodge*) — A purification ceremony preceding the Sun Dance, with four rocks laid in a cross for the four directions and the circle of life, running in four quarters.
- **Vision quest** (*bilisshíissanne*) — A solo journey to a prominent place, often a peak in the Wolf Mountains, where an individual fasts from food and water for two or three days to receive spiritual instruction.
- **Tobacco Society** (*Bacu'sua*) — The organization that cultivates the sacred plant and keeps its rites correct, central to Crow spiritual life.

## Foodways

The American bison was the main food, hunted on foot before horses and from horseback after; the Crow also hunted other game and gathered wild foods.

- **Bison hunting** — Before horses, hunters stalked on foot disguised in a wolf pelt; with horses, riders panicked herds into stampedes and shot animals from horseback.
- **Buffalo meat preparation** — Meat was roasted or boiled in stew with prairie turnips; the rump, tongue, liver, heart and kidneys were all prized as delicacies.
- **Pemmican** — Dried bison meat ground with fat and berries, a preserved food for storage.
- **Gathered foods** — Wild elderberries and wild turnip were collected to supplement meat.
- **Tobacco** — Not a food but described by Crows themselves as their 'means of living', the sacred plant central to Crow life.

## Oral tradition & literature

Crow narrative preserves creation stories and sacred history through named creators and transformative tales.

- **Old Man Coyote** — One of the Creator's names; in one account he meets two male ducks who dive for soil and root to build islands and lands, plants and trees, and then moulds Man and Woman from clay.
- **Akbaatatdia** — One of the Creator's names, meaning 'One Who Has Made Everything'.
- **Iichíkbaalee** — One of the Creator's names, meaning 'First Doer'.
- **Story of the Seven Bison** — A sacred account where a man defeats the armoured bison Bones Together by turning into a feather; the bison ascend to become the Big Dipper and the man the Little Dipper.
- **Migration accounts** — Tribal history carried through stories of No Intestines' vision and the search for sacred tobacco, and No Vitals' discovery of the plant at Devils Lake.

## Language & religion

Crow is a Missouri Valley Siouan language endangered but still spoken; Crow religion centers on an omnipotent Creator, sacred power from spirit patrons, and tolerance of multiple paths.

- **Crow language** (*Apsáalooke*) — A Missouri Valley Siouan language related only to Hidatsa; UNESCO classified it as definitely endangered in 2012, though fluent speakers of all ages remain.
- **River Crow and Mountain Crow** — Two dialect regions with slight differences in speech.
- **Creator** — A single omnipotent being in Crow belief, addressed by multiple names.
- **Sacred power** (*Baaxpée*) — Power that comes from a spirit patron and is embodied in a physical object called a Xapáaliia.
- **Doing ceremony properly** (*diakaashe*) — Means 'he really did it'—the Crow commitment to performing ceremony with integrity and respect.
