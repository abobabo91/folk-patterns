## Overview

The Otoe lived semi-nomadically along the Missouri River in the Central Plains. They farmed in villages, lived in elm-bark lodges, and used tipis while traveling. Buffalo became central to their diet and culture. Today they belong to the federally recognized Otoe-Missouria Tribe of Indians in Oklahoma.

## Material culture

The documented material culture centers on chiefs’ clothing, beadwork, blankets, ornaments, weapons, and two types of dwellings.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources document wool blankets, beadwork, and hide leggings with fringe.

- **Wool blankets** — Wool blankets were worn around the waist by Otoe chiefs.
- **Beadwork** — Beadwork decorated the leggings of Medicine Horse and Buffalo Chief.
- **Hide leggings** — Little Pipe wore hide leggings with fringe.

### Clothing & dress

Museum portraits show Otoe chiefs in decorated turbans, feathered ornaments, beaded items, leggings, moccasins, and blankets.

- **Little Pipe’s chiefly dress** (*Ranunwe Inge*) — Little Pipe wore a decorated fur turban with an eagle feather, ear ornaments, a beaded choker, necklaces, a checked shirt, rings, leggings, and a wool blanket.
- **Medicine Horse’s chiefly dress** (*Shun-Gech-Hoy*) — Medicine Horse wore a decorated fur turban with an eagle feather, ear ornaments, beaded necklaces, a peace medal, wool leggings, leg bands, and moccasins.
- **Buffalo Chief’s chiefly dress** (*Cha-Wan-Na-Ga-He*) — Buffalo Chief wore a decorated fur turban with two eagle feathers, ear ornaments, a beaded choker, necklaces, a peace medal, wool leggings, a wool blanket, and moccasins.

### Architecture

The Otoe used elm-bark lodges in farming villages and tipis while traveling.

- **Elm-bark lodges** — The Otoe lived in elm-bark lodges while farming.
- **Tipis** — The Otoe used tipis when traveling.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources do not document Otoe ceramics or household crafts, but portraits show chiefs with tomahawks and peace medals.

- **Pipe tomahawk** — Museum portraits show chiefs holding a pipe tomahawk or tomahawks.
- **Peace medal** — Medicine Horse and Buffalo Chief wore peace medals.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Chiefly dress included ear ornaments, beads, rings, medals, and feathered fur turbans.

- **Ear ornaments** — Chiefs wore ear ornaments.
- **Beaded chokers** — Little Pipe and Buffalo Chief wore beaded chokers.
- **Beaded necklaces** — Medicine Horse and Buffalo Chief wore beaded necklaces.
- **Fur turbans with eagle feathers** — Little Pipe and Medicine Horse wore turbans with one eagle feather, while Buffalo Chief wore one with two.

## Foodways

Farming and buffalo hunting shaped Otoe foodways, with bison central to diet and culture.

- **Buffalo hunting** — The Otoe often left their settlements to hunt buffalo.
- **American bison** — The American bison became central to Otoe diet and culture.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not document Otoe folktales or storytelling, but they name two writers and a language project.

- **Annette Arkeketa** — Annette Arkeketa is identified as a poet and playwright.
- **Anna Lee Walters** — Anna Lee Walters is identified as an author.
- **Ioway-Otoe Language project** — The Ioway Cultural Institute is associated with the Ioway-Otoe Language project.

## Language & religion

Chiwere is a Siouan language related to Iowa, Missouria, and Ho-Chunk, while the sources give only limited information about Otoe religion.

- **Chiwere** (*Jiwére*) — Chiwere, or Jiwére, is the Otoe language.
- **Siouan language family** (*Siouan*) — Chiwere belongs to the Siouan language family.
- **Christian mission** — The sources mention Christian missionaries and a mission during the 19th century.
- **Otoe-Missouria Tribe of Indians** — Today Otoe people belong to the federally recognized Otoe-Missouria Tribe of Indians.
