## Overview

The Mohawk are an Indigenous people of the Northeastern Woodlands and the easternmost nation of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy. Their traditional territory centered on the Mohawk Valley of upstate New York. Today Mohawk communities span Canada and the United States, with ongoing efforts to preserve language and culture through immersion education and community initiatives.

## Material culture

Mohawk material culture is documented through basketry, dress, and everyday objects, with splint-and-sweetgrass baskets, beadwork, and carved wood pieces showing distinctive decorative techniques.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Splint and sweetgrass basketry is the documented pattern tradition, featuring three-dimensional raised curls rather than woven designs.

- **Splint and sweetgrass basket** — A lidded fancy basket made from black ash splints dyed fuchsia, worked with sweetgrass, featuring three-dimensional raised curls across the outside of both body and lid.

### Clothing & dress

Historic Mohawk dress included distinctive men's hairstyles and practical clothing adapted to season, with nineteenth and twentieth-century regalia documented in photographs.

- **Men's plucked hairstyle** — Mohawk men historically removed most head hair by plucking tuft by tuft, leaving a smaller section worn in community-specific styles.
- **Women's hairstyle** — Women wore their hair long, often dressed with bear grease or tied back in a single braid.
- **Traditional summer dress** — Women often went topless in summer and wore a skirt of deerskin, switching to deerskin clothing in colder seasons.
- **Feather headdress** — Worn in nineteenth-century regalia by men including Chief Poking Fire, often paired with beaded bandolier bags, tunics, fringed leggings and moccasins.

### Architecture

The sources record settlement patterns rather than house forms: three main villages along the Mohawk River consolidated after the 1635 smallpox epidemic.

- **Ossernenon** (*Asserué*) — One of three villages along the south side of the Mohawk River in 1642, recorded by Isaac Jogues and Johannes Megapolensis.
- **Andagaron** (*Banagiro*) — One of three villages along the south side of the Mohawk River in 1642, recorded by Isaac Jogues and Johannes Megapolensis.
- **Tionontoguen** (*Thenondiogo*) — One of three villages along the south side of the Mohawk River in 1642, recorded by Isaac Jogues and Johannes Megapolensis.
- **Canajoharie** (*Upper Castle*) — A major eighteenth-century settlement where most Mohawks in the Province of New York lived.
- **Tionondorage Castle** (*Fort Hunter, Lower Castle*) — A second major settlement about thirty miles downstream from Canajoharie in the eighteenth century.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Everyday objects include carved wood cradleboards and flint, which was the group's defining economic material and the source of their name.

- **Cradleboard** — A Mohawk cradleboard of about 1860 in the Metropolitan Museum of Art, made of wood, rawhide and pigment as a carrying frame for an infant.
- **Flint** (*Kanien꞉ke*) — The defining economic material that made the Mohawk wealthy traders, as other Confederacy nations needed their flint for tool-making.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment is documented through the distinctive plucked male hairstyle, bear-grease hairdressing, and beaded accessories worn in nineteenth-century photographs.

- **Beaded bandolier bag** — A beaded accessory worn across the body, documented in photographs of men including Chief Poking Fire.
- **Beaded collar** — A beaded accessory worn by men in nineteenth-century regalia portraits, including the performer Sio-he-ro-te.
- **Beaded vest** — A beaded garment worn over decorated jackets, documented in photographs of men including the performer Go-Won-Go.
- **Decorated headband** — A headband often carrying a feather, worn by performers in nineteenth-century photographs.

## Music & performance

The Thanksgiving Address is the documented spoken-word tradition, recited every morning at the Ahkwesásne Freedom School, with Mohawk language broadcast on CKON-FM.

- **Thanksgiving Address** (*Ohén꞉ton Karihwatékwen*) — The words before all else, recited every morning in the hallway by teachers and students together at the Ahkwesásne Freedom School.
- **CKON-FM radio station** — A Mohawk-language broadcast station licensed by the Ahkwesáhsne Mohawk Nation, on air at 97.3 in Hogansburg, New York and Saint Regis, Quebec, with programming in Kanienʼkéha.

## Dance & theatre

Mohawk performance is documented through screen and digital media, including film, television series, video games and animated shows featuring the language.

- **Film Mohawk** — A 2017 film in which Mohawk language appears.
- **Film Black Robe** — A 1991 film featuring Mohawk language.
- **Assassin's Creed III** — A 2012 Ubisoft Montreal game with a half-Mohawk protagonist Ratonhnhaké꞉ton who speaks Kanienʼkéha.
- **Go-Won-Go** — A Mohawk performer documented in a studio portrait held by the British Museum.

## Festivals & rituals

The annual calendar centers on a cycle of fifteen seasonal ceremonies that forms the basis of immersion education, alongside community gatherings and political resistance practices.

- **Fifteen seasonal ceremonies** — An annual cycle on which the Ahkwesásne Freedom School's immersion curriculum has been based since about 1985.
- **Strawberry Festival** — An annual festival held at Kanaʼtsioharè:ke, the community west of Fonda, New York re-established in September 1993, which also holds cultural presentations, workshops and academic events.
- **Mohawk Warrior Society** (*Rotisken'rakéhte*) — Founded at Kahnawà꞉ke in 1971, using roadblocks, occupations and evictions to assert rights and protect the environment from pollution.

## Foodways

For centuries the Mohawk cultivated productive maize fields on the fertile Mohawk River floodplains alongside hunting and fishing.

- **Maize cultivation** (*oʼnenste*) — The Mohawk maintained productive maize fields on the fertile floodplains along the Mohawk River, west of the Pine Bush, for centuries.
- **St. Lawrence River Valley hunting grounds** — The Mohawk maintained this region as hunting grounds for centuries.

## Oral tradition & literature

The verbal tradition anchors daily practice through the Thanksgiving Address and a fifteen-ceremony seasonal cycle, with substantial revival apparatus built around the language.

- **Thanksgiving Address** (*Ohén꞉ton Karihwatékwen*) — The words before all else, recited each morning at the Ahkwesásne Freedom School, foundational to the immersion curriculum.
- **Ahkwesásne Freedom School** — Founded by Mohawk parents in 1979 out of concern at the lack of culture-based education in public and parochial schools, with an immersion curriculum adopted in 1985.
- **Kanaʼtsioharè:ke** (*place of the clean pot*) — Conceived as a Carlisle Indian Boarding School in Reverse, with stated mission to preserve traditional values, culture, language and lifestyles under the Kaienerekowa.

## Language & religion

Kanienʼkéha is a threatened Iroquoian language with three dialects and roughly 3,500 speakers, while society is organized by three clans and religion blends traditional animism, the Longhouse Religion, and Catholicism.

- **Kanienʼkéha language** — A polysynthetic Iroquoian language classified as threatened, with around 3,500 speakers in 2011, three dialects, four tones on stressed vowels, and orthography standardized in 1993.
- **Bear clan** (*Ohkwa:ri*) — One of three Mohawk clans descended nominally from a single female ancestor, with women holding leadership roles and marriage within a clan forbidden.
- **Turtle clan** (*A'nó꞉wara*) — One of three Mohawk clans descended nominally from a single female ancestor, with women holding leadership roles and marriage within a clan forbidden.
- **Wolf clan** (*Okwaho*) — One of three Mohawk clans descended nominally from a single female ancestor, with women holding leadership roles and marriage within a clan forbidden.
- **Longhouse Religion** — A traditional religion followed by many Mohawks, built around a primordial conflict between good and evil.

## Glossary

- *Kanienʼkehá꞉ka* — People of the Flint Place, the Mohawk name for themselves
- *Kanien꞉ke* — Flint Stone Place, the source of the Mohawk name
- *Haudenosaunee* — Iroquois Confederacy, of which Mohawk are the easternmost nation
- *Keepers of the Eastern Door* — The Mohawk's role within the Haudenosaunee Confederacy
- *Kanienʼkéha* — The Mohawk language, a threatened Iroquoian language
- *Ohén꞉ton Karihwatékwen* — The Thanksgiving Address, the words before all else
- *Ohkwa:ri* — Bear clan
- *A'nó꞉wara* — Turtle clan
- *Okwaho* — Wolf clan
- *oʼnenste* — Corn, a staple crop with the natural-world prefix
- *Kaienerekowa* — The Great Law of Peace, guiding principle for cultural preservation
- *Rotisken'rakéhte* — Mohawk Warrior Society, founded in 1971 for rights and environmental protection
- *Akwesasne* — A major Mohawk community in Canada and the United States
- *Kahnawà꞉ke* — A Mohawk community in Quebec, also called Caughnawaga
- *Kanesatake* — A Mohawk community in Quebec
- *Tyendinaga* — A Mohawk community in Ontario
- *Kanaʼtsioharè:ke* — Place of the clean pot, a Mohawk community re-established in 1993
