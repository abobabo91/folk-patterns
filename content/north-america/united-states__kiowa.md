## Overview

The Kiowa moved south from western Montana to the Southern Plains. Their life centered on seasonal mobility, horses, bison hunting, tipis, warrior societies, music, and ceremony. They were forced onto a reservation in southwestern Oklahoma in 1867. Today the Kiowa Tribe is headquartered in Carnegie, Oklahoma, and works to preserve its endangered language.

## Material culture

Kiowa material culture centers on painted hides, beadwork, quillwork, tipis, weapons, ceremonial clothing, and objects linked to horses and ritual life.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Kiowa women painted hides and made decorated rawhide, beadwork, quillwork, blankets, and ceremonial garlands.

- **Rawhide containers** (*Parfleche*) — Kiowa women painted geometric designs on rawhide containers used for camping goods.
- **Hide painting** — Geometric hide paintings decorated some tipis and carried special meanings.
- **Beadwork** — Beadwork decorated moccasins, sashes, belts, and other ceremonial items.
- **Quillwork** — Kiowa women later decorated hides with beads and quills.
- **Ceremonial garlands** — Garlands and wreaths made from Plantago virginica were worn by old men during ceremonial dances as symbols of health.

### Clothing & dress

Kiowa dress used painted and decorated hides, beaded moccasins, leggings, belts, feathers, fur, and metal ornaments.

- **Black Leggings regalia** — This ceremonial outfit included black fringed leggings, a beaded sash and belt, black beaded moccasins, and an eagle feather headdress.
- **Eagle feather headdress** — The Kiowa headdress used imitation eagle feathers, imitation otter fur, felt, and beadwork.
- **Warrior costume** — A replica Warrior costume included a red textile backdrop dragger made with fake black otter fur and medal buttons.
- **Beaded moccasins** — Moccasins included multi-coloured beadwork, glass beads, brass buttons, leather, hide, and metal tinkling cones.
- **Horse adornment** — Kiowa horses could wear beaded masks, bison horns, and feathers in their manes.

### Architecture

The tipi was a portable, climate-friendly home made from bison hides and wooden poles.

- **Tipi** — The conical tipi used sewn bison hides, wooden poles, smoke flaps, an entrance flap, and animal skins on the floor.
- **Skin lodge** — Skin lodge was another name for the tipi.
- **Travois** — Tipi poles could form a travois for carrying belongings and children.
- **Painted tipi** — Some tipis had hide paintings inside and outside, with special meanings attached to certain designs.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources describe weapons, shields, belts, and a belt bag, but no Kiowa ceramics.

- **Wooden bow** — A museum record shows a dark-wood bow with a thin sinew cord and two green lines painted on its shaft.
- **Bison-hide shield** — Shields were made from tough bison hide stretched over a wooden frame or from bison skulls.
- **Decorated weapons** — Bows, arrows, lances, tomahawks, knives, war clubs, firearms, and swords could carry feathers, fur, and animal parts for ceremony.
- **Steel-medallion belt** — A leather belt was decorated with connected pressed steel medallions bearing floral and geometric designs.
- **Woman’s belt bag** (*skärppåse med nålhus*) — The catalogue lists this as a woman’s belt bag with a needle case.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Kiowa adornment included a distinctive hairstyle, jewelry, clothing ornaments, and ritual horse painting.

- **Horizontal haircut** — The ancient Kiowa hairstyle was cut from the lower outer edge of the eyes to the back of the ears to keep hair from tangling with a bowstring.
- **Ear ornaments** — Photographs show Kiowa people wearing ear ornaments.
- **Peace medals** — Photographs show chiefs and women wearing peace medals.
- **Pocket watches** — Some people wore pocket watches suspended from chains.
- **Ritual horse painting** — A medicine man painted horses for good fortune and protection in battle.

## Music & performance

Kiowa music is closely tied to dancing, warrior societies, ceremony, and songs passed down by Kiowa song makers.

- **Gourd dance songs** — The gourd dance follows Kiowa protocol and social rules.
- **Brush Dance songs** — Brush Dance songs commemorate the outlawed Kiowa Sun Dance.
- **Buffalo Dance songs** — Buffalo Dance songs also commemorate the outlawed Kiowa Sun Dance.
- **War Journey songs** (*49 songs*) — War Journey songs are also called 49 songs.
- **Flag songs** — Flag songs honor warriors of the past, the present, and the people.

## Dance & theatre

Kiowa performance centers on dances and ceremony; the sources do not describe a Kiowa theatre tradition.

- **Gourd dance** — The gourd dance is a major Kiowa performance tradition.
- **Sun Dance performance** (*K’áu:d̶ó*) — The Sun Dance included war ceremonies, dancing, prayers, speeches, body painting, eagle-whistle blowing, visions, and feasting.
- **K’aw-tow ceremony** (*k'aw-tow*) — The four-day k'aw-tow included dancing, speeches, ritual painting, eagle-whistle blowing, visions, and feasting.
- **War ceremonies** — The Kogui conducted war ceremonies during the annual Sun Dance.

## Festivals & rituals

The yearly Sun Dance was the central Kiowa gathering, with strict band duties, sacred objects, ceremony, and feasting.

- **Sun Dance** (*K’áu:d̶ó*) — The Sun Dance used a tipi ring, a sacred bundle, a holy lance, shields, prayers, ritual painting, dancing, visions, and feasting.
- **Sacred bundle** (*Tai-mé; Taimay*) — The Sacred or Medicine bundle was guarded during the Sun Dance and unveiled by its keeper.
- **Funeral mourning** — Families mourned through wailing, tearing clothes, shaving the head, and sometimes cutting the body or finger joints.
- **Prompt burial** — Bodies were washed and buried the same day when possible, or the next morning if death came at night.
- **Burning belongings** — After burial, most belongings were burned with the tipi, and surviving relatives moved if the home was shared.

## Foodways

The Kiowa relied mainly on bison hunting, gathered plants, and food prepared for travel.

- **Bison hunting** — Bison were hunted on foot before horses and on horseback later, using bows, arrows, and lances.
- **Pemmican** — Pemmican was made by grinding lean meat, mixing it with melted fat and sometimes berries, shaping it into bars, and storing it in pouches.
- **Gathered foods** — Women gathered berries, tubers, seeds, nuts, vegetables, and wild fruit.
- **Important plants** — Kiowa cuisine included pecans, prickly pear, mulberries, persimmons, acorns, plums, and wild onions.
- **Earth-pit cooking** — Before European metal pots, meat and vegetables were boiled in an earth-lined pit heated with fire-hot rocks.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kiowa oral history includes an origin story, named calendars, and language teaching materials.

- **Hollow-log origin story** — One origin story says the Kiowa came through a hollow log, but a pregnant woman became stuck and stopped more people from emerging.
- **Emerging name** (*Kútd̶àu; Kwu-da*) — Kútd̶àu or Kwu-da means “emerging” or “coming out rapidly” and relates to the origin story.
- **Coming-out name** (*Tépdàu; Tep-da*) — Tépdàu or Tep-da is another ancient name linked to the origin narrative.
- **Kiowa calendars** — Kiowa calendars recorded two events each year, creating twice as many entries for a period as other Plains winter counts.
- **Calendar keeping** — Little Bluff, Settan, Anko, and Silver Horn were named Kiowa calendar keepers.

## Language & religion

Kiowa is a severely endangered Kiowa-Tanoan language with active preservation work and spiritual traditions tied to ceremony, spirits, and sacred objects.

- **Kiowa language** — Kiowa is spoken mainly in Caddo, Kiowa, and Comanche counties in Oklahoma.
- **Kiowa alphabet** — Parker McKenzie developed a Kiowa alphabet, and the current alphabet uses barred letters such as B̶, D̶, G̶, and n̶.
- **Language preservation** — Preservation includes teaching grammars, children’s stories, language classes, and a Kiowa Language Department.
- **Medicine men** — Medicine men carried out spiritual work such as ritual horse painting.
- **Peyote songs and hymns** — Peyote songs and hymns are part of Kiowa spiritual and musical life.

## Glossary

- *Ǥáuigú* — Another name for the Kiowa
- *Parfleche* — Painted rawhide container
- *K’áu:d̶ó* — Kiowa name for the Sun Dance
- *Tai-mé* — Sacred or Medicine bundle
- *Taimay* — Another form of Tai-mé
- *k'aw-tow* — Four-day ceremonial event
- *pemmican* — Dried meat mixed with fat
- *Kútd̶àu* — Emerging or coming out rapidly
- *Kwu-da* — Another form of Kútd̶àu
- *Tépdàu* — Ancient name linked to emerging
- *Tep-da* — Another form of Tépdàu
- *skärppåse med nålhus* — Woman’s belt bag with needle case
- *Kogui* — Sun Dance band with war duties
- *49 songs* — Another name for War Journey songs
