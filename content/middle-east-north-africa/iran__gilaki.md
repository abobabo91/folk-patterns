## Overview

The Gilaks are an Iranian people native to the south of the Caspian Sea and one of the main ethnic groups in the northern parts of Iran. Together with the closely related Mazandarani people, they form part of the Caspian peoples inhabiting the southern and southwestern coastal regions of the Caspian Sea. Gilaks mainly live along the southwest Caspian coasts, in Gilan province, northern Mazandaran, and northern Qazvin province, including Alamut. Their population was estimated at between 3 and 4 million in 2006. They speak Gilaki, a Caspian language of the Northwestern branch of the Iranian languages. Their cultural profile in the supplied sources is documented chiefly through settlement history, agriculture, regional dialects, and linguistic relationships.

## Foodways

The sources describe agriculture and regional economic activity. Gilaks living along the northern side of the Alborz mountains tend to raise livestock, while those living in the plains farm. They supply agricultural staples including rice, grains, tobacco, and tea; fishing and caviar exports are also identified among the region’s major industries.

## Language & religion

Gilaki is an Iranian language belonging to the Caspian group of the Northwestern branch of the Iranian languages. It is spoken by the Gilak people in the south and west of the Caspian Sea and is closely related to Mazandarani; the two languages have similar vocabularies and share certain typological features with Caucasian languages, specifically Kartvelian languages. Gilaki has three dialects: Western Gilaki, Eastern Gilaki, and Deylami. The western and eastern dialects are separated by the Sefid River. Deylami is spoken in the mountains of eastern, western, and southern Gilan and western Mazandaran. Gilaki is also spoken in Ramsar, Tonekabon, the valley of the Chalus river, and Alamut.
