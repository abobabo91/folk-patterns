## Overview

Arab citizens of Israel are the largest ethnic minority in Israel. The population stood at 2.1 million people in 2023, accounting for 21% of Israel’s total population. Most identify as Arab or Palestinian by nationality and as Israeli by citizenship, although identity also includes religious and civic components. They mostly live in Arab-majority towns and cities, some of which are among the poorest in the country. The population includes Muslim Arabs, Christian Arabs, Druze, Bedouins and Circassians. Their history is connected to the Palestinians who remained within the territory that became Israel after the 1947–49 war and to their descendants. Their folk-culture significance in the sources lies chiefly in language, identity, poetry, songs and political memory.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources mention conservative Islamic dress as a feature encouraged by the Islamic Movement during the 1980s.

## Music & performance

The sources identify singing and reciting nationalist songs or poetry as public expressions of Palestinian identity. Between 1948 and 1967, such expressions were illegal. After the end of military administrative rule in 1966 and following the 1967 war, national consciousness and its expression among Arab citizens spread.

The sources also describe Arab citizens’ support for the First Intifada, including assistance to Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza Strip through money, food and clothes. They mention strikes held in solidarity with Palestinians in the occupied territories.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe public commemorations connected with political history. The date of the 1976 protest against land expropriations and house demolitions, 30 March, has since been commemorated annually as Land Day. The sources also state that most Jewish Israelis refer to the 1948 Arab–Israeli War as the War of Independence, while most Arab citizens refer to it as *al-Nakba* (the catastrophe).

The sources discuss religious affiliation, identifying Muslims as the majority and noting significant Christian and Druze minorities.

## Foodways

The sources mention food only in connection with assistance to Palestinians during the First Intifada, when Arab citizens provided money, food and clothes.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources identify nationalist songs and poetry as expressions of Palestinian identity. They state that public singing and reciting of nationalist songs or poetry were illegal between 1948 and 1967, and that expressions of national consciousness spread after the end of military administrative rule in 1966 and following the 1967 war.

The sources name the Arab poet Tawfiq Ziad, who was elected mayor of Nazareth in 1975 as a Maki member.

## Language & religion

Arab citizens of Israel speak both Arabic and Hebrew. Their traditional vernaculars are mostly Levantine Arabic dialects, including Palestinian Arabic in central Israel and Lebanese Arabic further north, particularly among the Druze. Negev Bedouins speak a Northwest Arabian dialect. These modern Arabic dialects have absorbed multiple Hebrew loanwords and phrases, and the resulting speech is sometimes called the Israeli Arabic dialect.

By religious affiliation, the majority are Muslims, with significant Christian and Druze minorities. The sources also discuss Circassians, whose origins are in the Caucasus but who are mostly Muslim.
