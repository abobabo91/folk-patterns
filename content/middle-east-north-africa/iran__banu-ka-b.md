## Overview

The Banu Kaʿb are a nomadic Arab tribe whose origins are placed in the Najd region of the Arabian Peninsula. They inhabit Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait, and Iran, especially Khuzestan near Al-Muhammarah. Other branches settled in Oman, the United Arab Emirates, and Morocco, while the tribe is also present in Qatar and Bahrain, where members are called Al-Kaaban. The group has a history of movement, raiding, settlement, political rule, and military activity across southern and central Ottoman Iraq and adjacent Khuzestan. In 1812, the Emirate of Muhammara emerged as an autonomous emirate under the Banu Kaʿb. The sources identify Sunni and Shia branches.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They record settlement in cities including Basra and Nasiriyah, and near Al-Muhammarah, but provide no architectural description.

## Music & performance

They state that the Banu Kaʿb had a strong navy in the mid-eighteenth century and sometimes attacked British ships.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that branches of the Banu Kaʿb in Oman, Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates practice Sunni Islam, while the Banu Kaʿb of Iraq practice Shia Islam. The Iraqi branch began converting from Sunni to Shia Islam from the early eighteenth century onwards.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources provide a lineage extending from Kaab bin Rabi’ah through Aamer bin Sa’sa’ah, Mu’awiyah, Bakr, Hawazin, Mansour, Ikrimah, Khasfa, Qais Ailan, Mudar, Nizar, Ma’ad, and Adnan. They also identify Al-Nābigha al-Jaʽdī as an early Islamic poet and Companion of Muhammad.

## Language & religion

The sources identify the Banu Kaʿb as an Arab tribe and give Arabic forms for the names Banu Kaʿb and Kaabi. They do not classify the group’s language within a language family or describe dialects, scripts, or historical writing practices. Religious affiliation differs by branch: the Banu Kaʿb of Oman, Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates practice Sunni Islam, while the Banu Kaʿb of Iraq practice Shia Islam. The sources state that conversion from Sunni to Shia Islam began among the Banu Kaʿb of Iraq from the early eighteenth century onwards.
