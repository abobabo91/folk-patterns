## Overview

The Bedouin, also called Beduin or Bedu, are pastorally semi-nomadic Arab tribes historically inhabiting desert regions in the Arabian Peninsula, North Africa, the Levant, and Mesopotamia. Their territory extends from the deserts of North Africa to those of the Middle East. The sources describe herding camels, sheep, and goats; tribal and clan organization; oral poetry; music and dances; hospitality; coffee preparation; and traditional tent knitting as important cultural practices.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

They mention traditional tent knitting and black goat-hair tents called *bayt al-shar*, but provide no further description of their techniques, motifs, or distinctions from neighboring cultures.

### Architecture

Bedouin family units traditionally lived in tents. In Saudi Arabia, the sources describe black goat-hair tents called *bayt al-shar*, divided by cloth curtains into rug-floor areas for males, family, and cooking.

## Music & performance

The sources describe music as part of traditional Bedouin culture. Oral poetry, also known as *nabati poetry*, is described as the most popular art form among Bedouins. Having a poet in one’s tribe was highly regarded, and poetry served both artistic and social functions, including conveying information and exercising social control. It is often recited in the vernacular dialect, in contrast with more common forms of Arabic poetry in Modern Standard Arabic.

Some urbanized Bedouins organize cultural festivals several times a year, where Bedouins gather to participate in and learn about traditions including poetry recitation, traditional sword dances, traditional instruments, and tent knitting.

## Dance & theatre

The sources mention traditional dances such as *saas* and traditional sword dances performed at cultural festivals.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that urbanized Bedouins may organize cultural festivals several times a year. These gatherings involve poetry recitation, traditional sword dances, traditional instruments, and classes teaching traditional tent knitting. Camel races are organized during celebratory occasions such as weddings and religious festivals.

The sources describe wedding celebrations and religious festivals. Coffee is associated with hospitality, especially among Bedouins of the Arabian Peninsula, the Levant, Egypt, and Iraq.

## Foodways

Traditional Bedouin livelihoods centered on goats, sheep, and dromedary camels. These animals supplied meat, dairy products, and wool, and dairy products formed most of the staple foods in the diet described by the source. Camel meat was occasionally consumed, while camels also served as an important source of milk and as transportation.

In areas with unpredictable rainfall, camps were moved according to the availability of green pasture. In regions where winter rainfall was more predictable, some Bedouin planted grain along migration routes as a winter resource for livestock. In western Africa, the sources describe transhumance, with crops planted near permanent homes and livestock moved to highland pastures.

Coffee is a major aspect of hospitality among Bedouins of Egypt and other parts of the Arab world. Traditionally, coffee beans are roasted in a *mihmas*, crushed in a *mihbaj*, boiled in a *dallah*, and served in a small handleless cup called a *finjan*.

## Oral tradition & literature

Oral poetry is described as the most popular Bedouin art form. A poet held high status within the tribe, and poetry communicated information and supported social control. *Nabati poetry* is commonly recited in the vernacular dialect. The sources also describe an early scholarly practice in which Bedouin were asked to recite poems to help determine the pronunciation and spelling of Arabic words.

Bedouin society preserved proverbs and oral formulas, including the saying that loyalty proceeds from oneself to one’s immediate family, then to one’s cousin, and finally to the stranger.

## Language & religion

The sources state that Bedouin poetry is often recited in the vernacular dialect, while more common forms of Arabic poetry use Modern Standard Arabic. The vast majority of Bedouins adhere to Islam, while a small number of Christian Bedouins were present in the Fertile Crescent until the 21st century. The sources also describe customary justice, including *bisha’a*, an ordeal by fire associated with lie detection.
