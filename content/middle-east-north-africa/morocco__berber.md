## Overview

The Berbers are the indigenous people of North Africa with the largest concentration in Morocco. Their material culture — geometric textiles, heavy engraved silver, mud-brick architecture — has survived largely intact through Roman, Arab, and French rule. A non-urban, non-courtly design vocabulary, transmitted through women and derived from tattoo motifs, shapes everything from carpets to granary locks. The three main dialect regions — Rif, Middle Atlas, and High Atlas — each have distinct material and musical traditions.

## Material culture

Berber material culture is geometric, hand-made, and built on a design vocabulary of symbols — lozenges, combs, fives, zigzags — that appear identically in tattoo, weaving, pottery, metalwork, and architecture.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Berber rugs are woven by women and range from thick ivory-ground pile carpets to rag rugs and flatweaves, all using a shared geometric vocabulary.

- **Beni Ourain rug** (*Beni Ourain*) — Thick cream-colored pile rugs from the eastern Middle Atlas with sparse dark lozenge patterns, made from undyed mountain sheep wool for sleeping in cold cedar forests.
- **Azilal rug** (*Azilal*) — High Atlas rugs with off-white ground carrying free-drawn, asymmetric figures in dyed wool without regard to symmetry, each one unique.
- **Taznakht rug** (*Ouaouzguite*) — Flatweave and mixed-technique rugs from the Siroua massif combining kilim, soumak wrapping, and knotted pile in a dense red-dominant field.
- **Boucherouite rug** (*bu sherwit*) — Twentieth-century rag rugs knotted from cut clothing, nylon, and plastic in aggressively bright colors, now collected as art.
- **Handira** — The bride's cape of the Middle Atlas, a cream or striped flatweave densely hung with sequins that glitter and rustle in movement.

### Clothing & dress

Men wear loose hooded robes; women's dress varies sharply by region but centers on wrapped and pinned cloth secured with paired silver fibulae.

- **Djellaba** — A hooded, loose, ankle-length robe worn by men in wool or cotton with a functional hood against sun and cold.
- **Burnous** (*selham / burnous*) — A semicircular hooded cloak worn over the djellaba, typically white or dark brown, worn by both shepherds and notables.
- **Fibulae** (*tizerzai*) — Pairs of triangular or disc-headed pins worn chained across a woman's chest to close her wrap, and given by the groom's family as the bride's inalienable property.
- **Fouta** — A striped red-and-white wrapper worn by Rifian women over the skirt, paired with a wide-brimmed straw hat with woollen pompoms.
- **Belgha** — A backless pointed leather slipper, yellow for men, worn as everyday footwear.

### Architecture

Berber architecture centers on the rammed-earth fortified house (tighremt) with patterned walls, and the collective stone granary (agadir) of the Anti-Atlas.

- **Kasbah** (*tighremt*) — A rammed-earth fortified house of two to four storeys with battered corner towers and walls incised with relief lozenges and chevrons matching carpet patterns.
- **Ksar** — Whole valley villages of stacked timermt entered through a single gate, found along the Drâa, Dadès, and Todgha valleys.
- **Agadir (Igherm)** (*agadir / igherm*) — A walled collective fortified granary of the Anti-Atlas and Souss with hundreds of stacked stone cells, each family's property, administered by elected assembly under written customary law.
- **High Atlas stone house** (*tabout*) — Dry-stone houses with juniper beams and flat earth roofs, stepped against mountain slopes so one house's roof is the next one's terrace.
- **Rif house** — Built in stone and mud with pitched thatched or tiled roofs, unique in Morocco for protecting against Mediterranean rain.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Rural Berber pottery is hand-built by women without a wheel and fired in open bonfires; metalwork centers on engraved brass, daggers, and carved wood.

- **Jebala-Rif pottery** (*tajine*) — Hand-built pottery painted in iron-red and manganese geometric bands on cream slip, using lozenge and comb signs identical to tattoo motifs.
- **Tajine** — Both the conical earthenware lid-and-base vessel and the slow stew cooked inside it.
- **Cedar and walnut woodwork** (*atlas*) — Carved cedar and walnut from the Middle Atlas made into doors, chests, ceiling panels, spoons, and carved bowls.
- **Koumiya dagger** (*koumiya*) — A curved dagger of the Souss with a silver-sheathed hilt, engraved and sometimes with niello and enamel work.
- **Brass trays and henna scabbards** (*nqash*) — Engraved brass trays and dagger scabbards decorated with henna-applied designs (nqash).

### Jewelry & body adornment

Moroccan Berber silver is heavy, low-purity, and worked by engraving, niello, enamel, and stone-setting; the fibula is the defining object.

- **Fibula** (*tazersit / tizerzai*) — A triangular or disc-headed pin worn in pairs and chained across the chest to close a woman's wrap; the triangle symbolizes the female.
- **Cloisonné enamel work** — Enamelled work in yellow, green, and blue on silver, set with cabochon glass and amber, a specialty of Tiznit and the Ida ou Nadif in the Anti-Atlas.
- **Khamsa hand pendant** (*khamsa*) — An open-hand pendant worn as an amulet, signifying five and warding against evil.
- **Taounza forehead band** (*taounza*) — A decorated band worn across the forehead as part of a woman's adornment.
- **Berber tattoo** (*oucham*) — Vertical chin lines and brow and ankle marks in indigo soot applied to women at puberty, with motifs identical to weaving signs; largely suppressed by Islamic reform.

## Music & performance

Moroccan Berber music is regional and village-based, centered on professional poet-musicians (rwais) and collective village drums.

- **Rwais poet-musician** (*rwais / rais*) — Professional Tashelhit poet-musicians traveling with a repertoire of composed, memorized, and politically pointed sung poetry, led by a rais playing single-string bowed rebab.
- **Rebab** (*rebab / ribab*) — A single-string bowed lute played by the rais to lead rwais performances.
- **Bendir drum** (*bendir*) — A large frame drum with a gut snare across the head, central to collective village music and village dances.
- **Ghaita shawm** (*ghaita*) — A shawm played at weddings and moussems as part of the village music ensemble.
- **Gnawa lila** (*lila*) — An all-night possession ceremony performed by Gnawa (descendants of enslaved West Africans) with guembri bass lute and iron qraqeb castanets.

## Dance & theatre

Village dances are central to Berber life, combining music, sung improvised poetry, and collective or solo movement.

- **Ahwash** — An emblematic Tashelhit evening dance where men form a ring of bendir players while women in facing lines answer with sung verse and slow dense steps accelerating over an hour.
- **Ahidous** — The Middle Atlas Tamazight counterpart to ahwash, danced by mixed lines swaying and stamping in a circle with bendir accompaniment, faster and more tightly interlocked.
- **Taskiwin** — A martial men's dance of the western High Atlas where dancers shake a powder-horn worn on the left shoulder in coordinated shoulder tremor.
- **Guedra** — A Saharan dance performed on the knees by a veiled woman, expressed entirely through hands, fingers, and torso over a cooking-pot drum.
- **Marketplace storyteller** (*hlaykia*) — A professional storyteller performing in Souss halqa circles, carrying narrative performance tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

Berber festivals and rituals follow the agricultural and Islamic calendars, marked by special foods, pilgrimage, and life-cycle ceremonies.

- **Yennayer (Amazigh New Year)** (*Yennayer / Id Yennayer*) — The Amazigh new year on 13–14 January by the agrarian calendar, marked by special couscous with seven vegetables and fruit, with a hidden date stone for luck; an official Moroccan holiday since 2024.
- **Boujloud** (*Boujloud / Bilmawn*) — A masked goat festival following Eid al-Adha in the Souss and Anti-Atlas where a man wrapped in sacrificial goat skins runs through the village striking people to drive out misfortune.
- **Moussem** — A saint's-day pilgrimage fair at the tomb (marabout) of a local holy figure, punctuating summer and autumn across the mountains.
- **Imilchil betrothal moussem** (*Imilchil*) — A Middle Atlas moussem in September where marriages among the Ait Haddidou are contracted at the shrine of Sidi Mohammed el Maghani.
- **Sbou naming ceremony** (*sbou*) — The naming ceremony held on the seventh day after birth, marked by the sacrifice of a sheep.

## Foodways

Barley, not wheat, is the Berber staple grain of the mountains, eaten as flatbread and couscous, with tajine stews and mint tea as the hospitality ritual.

- **Aghroum** — A flat, dense barley bread baked on a griddle or in a domed clay oven, the everyday staple grain of the mountains.
- **Tajine** — A slow stew cooked in a conical earthenware vessel; southern and Atlas versions use lamb with preserved lemon and olives, or chicken with almond and onion.
- **Amlou** — A Souss speciality of roast almond, honey, and argan oil beaten to a paste, dependent on the endemic argan tree.
- **Smen** — Clarified butter buried and aged for months or years as a prestige condiment.
- **Mint tea** (*atay*) — Chinese gunpowder green tea, spearmint, and sugar poured from height into small glasses in three servings; the compulsory rite of hospitality.

## Oral tradition & literature

Berber literary tradition centers on sung poetry by professional rwais, women's folktales told at night, and proverb and riddle exchange.

- **Rwais poetry** (*rwais*) — Composed, memorized Tashelhit verse performed and traveled by professional poet-musicians, commercially recorded from the 1930s onward.
- **Tinfusin folktales** (*tinfusin*) — Prose folktales told at night by women, featuring stock characters like the ogress Teryel and the trickster jackal Ouchen.
- **Izlan couplets** (*izlan*) — Short sung couplets of the Middle Atlas exchanged between men's and women's lines.
- **Tamawayt lament** (*tamawayt*) — A long, free-rhythm, highly melismatic solo lament of the Middle Atlas that opens a performance.
- **Proverb and riddle** (*inzan / tinfusin n imukan*) — Competitive evening genres of proverb and riddle exchange.

## Language & religion

Moroccan Imazighen speak three mutually unintelligible Afroasiatic languages and practice Sunni Islam with strong Sufi tradition and pre-Islamic survivals.

- **Tarifit** — The Berber language of the Rif mountains along the Mediterranean, spoken by Rifians.
- **Tamazight** — The Berber language of the Middle Atlas cedar plateaux, spoken by confederations including Zemmour, Zaiane, and Ait Bou Ichaouen.
- **Tashelhit** — The Berber language of the High Atlas, Anti-Atlas, and Souss plain, with major weaving centers at Taznakht and Azilal.
- **Tifinagh script** (*tifinagh*) — An ancient consonantal script revived and standardized as Neo-Tifinagh, adopted as Morocco's official Amazigh alphabet in 2003.
- **Zawiya lodges** (*zawiya*) — Sufi lodges that form the living center of mountain religion, alongside the veneration of local saints and marabout tombs.
