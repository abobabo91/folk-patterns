## Overview

The Qashqai are a large tribal confederacy who practice seasonal nomadism in southern Iran, moving between summer mountain pastures and winter lowlands. They are world-renowned for their woven textiles—especially pile rugs, gabbeh flatweaves, and saddlebags—made on portable ground looms. Their culture centers on pastoral life, distinctive dress, oral traditions, and celebration through music, dance, and horsemanship, though most now live in settled villages while maintaining migration routes and weaving practices.

## Material culture

Qashqai material culture emphasizes portable, woven, and handmade goods suited to nomadic life, with textiles at the center.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Qashqai weavers are world-famous for wool pile rugs, flatweaves, and decorated bags made on portable looms.

- **Qashqai carpet** (*gabbeh (coarse long-pile); kilim/gelim (flatweave)*) — Hand-knotted wool pile rugs with stacked diamond medallions, small animals, and rosettes; the fine saturation of their madder red and indigo blue is legendary.
- **Gabbeh** — A coarse long-pile sleeping rug in undyed or single-color wool with a few oversized figures, the opposite of formal city rugs; made famous internationally by the 1996 film.
- **Saddlebags** (*khorjin*) — Double bags with decorated faces and plainweave backs, joined by a woven bridge; Qashqai examples are considered superior to any others made.
- **Striped blankets** (*jajim*) — Narrow warp-faced striped bands woven separately and stitched together into blankets and covers.
- **Salt bag** (*namakdan*) — A keyhole-shaped bag with a narrow neck that closes against grazing animals, usually worked in sumak technique.

### Clothing & dress

Women wear the most conspicuous dress in southern Iran: multiple gathered skirts in clashing colors with a short tunic and long sleeveless coat, topped with a stiff headband and large draped scarf.

- **Women's gathered skirts** (*tuman*) — Several superimposed full skirts of printed or shot cloth in saturated colors, worn over wide-legged trousers.
- **Women's short tunic** (*arkhaloq*) — A waist-length tunic with an open front, worn under the long sleeveless coat.
- **Women's headband** (*lachak*) — A stiff band worn under a large gauzy square scarf (charqad) pinned to frame the face and decorated with coins and braid.
- **Men's felt cap** (*kolah-e do-gushi*) — A short brimless felt cap with two upward flaps over the ears, standardized as tribal identity badge in the early twentieth century.
- **Men's wool sash** (*shal*) — A long wrapped sash worn at the waist over a tunic and wide trousers under a long coat.

### Architecture

The traditional Qashqai dwelling is the black tent woven from goat hair, with interior screens for storage and division of space.

- **Black tent** (*siah chador*) — A tent woven by women from hand-spun goat hair in long panels, stretched over wooden uprights and ridge poles; goat hair swells when wet to seal the roof.
- **Tent panels** (*lat*) — Long woven strips of goat hair sewn together to form the tent structure.
- **Winter hut** (*kapar*) — A light hut of palm rib, reed, and brush on a stone footing, used in the warm winter lowlands near Bushehr.
- **Interior reed screens** — Stacked reed and brushwood screens line the interior walls to divide sleeping and cooking areas and store household weavings and bedding.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Nomadic Qashqai buy rather than make hard goods; their material culture centers on goatskin, wood, and tinned copper vessels.

- **Goatskin churning bag** (*mashk*) — A whole goatskin swung from a wooden tripod, used to churn milk into butter and buttermilk (dugh).
- **Convex iron griddle** (*saj*) — A convex sheet-iron plate set over a brush fire for baking bread.
- **Loom tools** — The distinctively Qashqai objects are ground-loom stakes, heavy iron weft-beating combs, and spindles (duk) carried while tending flocks.
- **Cotton-uppered shoe** (*giveh*) — Everyday footwear with a hard-pressed sole, replaced by rawhide during migration.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Silver jewelry and amulets dominate, with coin-strung necklaces, bangles, and protective talismans doubling as portable capital.

- **Coin necklaces and headbands** — Strung from Qajar and Pahlavi rial pieces worn from childhood.
- **Arm binding** (*bazuband*) — A tubular amulet bound to the upper arm, shared with wider Iranian tradition.
- **Amulet case** (*ta'viz*) — A flat case holding a folded Quranic text against illness and the evil eye.
- **Blue glass beads** — Strung as explicit defense against the evil eye (cheshm-e nazar); hung on children and valuable animals.
- **Henna night** (*hana-bandan*) — Henna is applied to hands and hair at the bride's night before marriage and before major festivals.

## Music & performance

Celebration music is loud and processional with shawm and drums; pastoral and domestic music features shepherd's flute, bowed fiddle, and women's unaccompanied song.

- **Double-reed shawm** (*sorna; karna (longer form)*) — Paired with cylindrical drums for outdoor weddings, circumcisions, and stick dances, played by professional musician families.
- **Cylindrical drum** (*dohol*) — Played alongside the sorna for celebration music and stick dances.
- **Paired kettledrums** (*naqareh*) — An alternative percussion accompaniment to the sorna for processional music.
- **Shepherd's flute** (*ney*) — An end-blown flute for pastoral and indoor music, played in quiet settings.
- **Bowed spike fiddle** (*kamancheh*) — Used for pastoral music and in women's indoor settings.

## Dance & theatre

Two dances define Qashqai celebration: a men's stick-fighting display and a women's handkerchief dance with flaring skirts.

- **Stick play** (*chub-bazi*) — A men's mock combat in which one fighter swings a long staff at an opponent's ankles while the opponent parries with a short stick, circling inside a ring of spectators.
- **Handkerchief dance** (*dastmal-bazi*) — A women's open circle or facing-line dance with a colored kerchief in each hand, the multiple skirts flaring with the step; the visual centerpiece of a wedding.
- **Mounted display** — The Darreshuri horsemen substitute horsemanship and racing displays for theatre at weddings and festivals.

## Festivals & rituals

The tribal year turns on two seasonal migrations (spring to highlands, autumn to lowlands) rather than harvest; major life events are marriage and circumcision.

- **Nowruz migration** (*Nowruz (21 March); kuch (migration)*) — The spring departure for highlands begins immediately after the Persian new year, merging the new year and road-opening into a single celebration.
- **Betrothal and bride-price** (*nishan*) — Marriage negotiations precede a wedding of up to three days with henna night for the bride and transfer of her dowry of woven goods.
- **Circumcision celebration** (*khatneh-suran*) — Boys' circumcision is celebrated with the same shawm-and-drum music and feasting as weddings.
- **Mourning cycle** — At death the body is buried quickly and mourned for three, seven, and forty days with halva and recitations; carved stone lions mark graves of honored men.
- **Stone lion monuments** (*shir-e sangi*) — Carved stone lions set over the graves of men who died with a reputation for courage across Fars province.

## Foodways

Bread and dairy are the nutritional base, with meat and rice reserved for feasts and guests.

- **Unleavened wheat bread** — Thin bread baked on the saj over brushwood, the daily staple eaten with dairy products.
- **Dried whey** (*kashk*) — Whey dried into hard pellets that keep through migration and are reconstituted into sauces and soups.
- **Thick pottage** (*ash*) — A thick stew of wild greens, mountain herbs, and gathered bulbs collected along the migration route.
- **Rice pilaf** (*polow*) — Festival food rather than daily fare, served at celebrations and important gatherings.
- **Grilled meat** (*kabab*) — Sheep meat grilled for guests, weddings, and funerals.

## Oral tradition & literature

Qashqai knowledge is explicitly oral and nocturnal, transmitted by elders around evening fires; genealogies, tribal history, and Oghuz epics form the core repertoire.

- **Genealogical narratives** — Elders recite descent from the Ak Koyunlu (White Sheep Tribe), settlement in Fars under Shah Ismail, and twentieth-century wars with the state.
- **Köroğlu epic cycle** (*Köroğlu*) — The prestige heroic narrative shared with Azerbaijan and Anatolia, sung by ashiq bards.
- **Animal tales and riddles** — Traditional narrative forms preserved in oral transmission.
- **Proverb stock** — A dense collection of traditional sayings encoding cultural wisdom.
- **Tent-school movement** — Mohammad Bahmanbeigi's educational program from the 1950s brought literacy to migrating camps and produced the first generation of Qashqai writers.

## Language & religion

Qashqai (Turki) is an Oghuz Turkic language closely related to Azerbaijani; the confederacy is Twelver Shi'a Muslim with light clerical footprint and historical tension with the clerical state.

- **Turki language** (*Turki; Qashqai*) — An Oghuz Turkic language with substantial Persian lexical and syntactic borrowing, written in Perso-Arabic script with no standardized orthography.
- **Tribal polyglotism** — The confederacy absorbed Lur, Kurdish, Arab, and Turkic clans (Khalaj, Afshar, Bayat, Qajar, Shamlu, Igdir), with Qashqai in urban areas shifting to Persian.
- **Shi'a Islam** (*Twelver Shi'a*) — The Qashqai practice Islam with the light clerical footprint typical of pastoral societies, emphasizing vows and pilgrimage over mosque attendance.
- **Protective rituals** (*nazr (vow); esfand (wild rue)*) — Religious practice runs through vows to shrines, burning of wild rue against the evil eye, and wearing of amulets.
- **Shrine pilgrimage** (*imamzadeh*) — Local shrine pilgrimage is a central religious practice.
