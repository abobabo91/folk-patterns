## Overview

The Milan, also called *Mîlan*, are described as a Kurdish tribe that historically stood at the head of a multi-confessional tribal confederacy. The confederacy was most active around Viranşehir, between Urfa, Mardin and Diyarbakir, although the Milan were also present in Dêrsim and other places. They describe the Milan as important to Kurdish origin legends, regional political history, and the history of relations between tribal communities and the Ottoman and Egyptian states.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources used mention that Ibrahim Pasha contributed to the building of Viranşehir.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The account of the Egyptian–Ottoman War states that Muhammad Ali gave Ayub Bey a pair of golden pistols and a gold sword hilt.

## Festivals & rituals

They do record a legend in which the Milan and Zilan are regarded by many tribes as legendary parental tribes. One version says that the Milan settled in Dêrsim, after which Sultan Selim ordered some to sedentarize and build houses and others to nomadize southward.

## Foodways

The historical account states that the Millis supplied food, livestock, and logistical support to the Egyptian army during the Egyptian–Ottoman War, but gives no further description of Milan foodways.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Milan are associated with Kurdish legends of origin involving the Milan and Zilan, and in one version a third branch called the Baba Kurdi. According to one account attributed to Sykes, Ibrahim Pasha said that the Kurds had once been divided into the Milan and Zilan, that there were 1,200 Milan tribes, and that they were later scattered. Another version, recounted by Celadet Bedirxan, says that the ancestor of the Kurds was a man named ‘Kurd’, whose two surviving sons were Mil and Zil. The sources also identify Derwêşê Evdî as a semi-historical Yazidi figure of Kurdish folklore associated with the Milan. No Milan literary revival, preservation project, proverb collection, or riddle tradition is described.

## Language & religion

The sources identify the Milan as Kurdish and describe their historical confederacy as multi-confessional. They also mention Kurdish, Yazidi, Christian, Ottoman, Egyptian, and Islamic contexts in connection with the group’s history. Ibrahim Pasha is described as having sheltered Christians during massacres of Christians in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries and as having revolted against the Ottomans.
