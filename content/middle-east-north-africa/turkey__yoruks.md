## Overview

The Yörüks, also called Yuruks or Yorouks, are a Turkish ethnic subgroup of Oghuz descent. They primarily inhabit the mountains of Anatolia and are also distributed across the Balkan peninsula, including eastern North Macedonia, parts of Bulgaria, Thessaly, and southern Thrace in Greece. Their name derives from the Old Turkish verb “yörü,” meaning “to walk,” and the group is associated with nomadic or formerly semi-nomadic ways of life. In Turkey, the terms “Türkmen” and “Yörük” indicate differing degrees of attachment to a semi-nomadic lifestyle. Yörüks are divided into named endogamous patrilineal tribes, which are further divided into clans or lineages called *kabile*, *sülale*, or *oba*.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Kara çadır* — The *kara çadır* is a dark wool tent in which Anatolian Yörüks lived for much of the year.

Carpet-weaving — Carpet-weaving is identified as an art through which sedentary Turkmen populations preserve parts of their heritage.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no motifs.

### Architecture

The principal built forms named in the sources are the dark wool *kara çadır* tents used by Anatolian Yörüks and the yayla houses maintained for summer use by some populations associated with Yörük heritage.

## Music & performance

A throat-singing tradition known as *Boğaz Havası* or *Boğaz Çalma* is an important aspect of Sarıkeçili Yörük culture. It is performed by pressing the throat with a finger while singing with a sound.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe a seasonal movement pattern among Anatolian Yörüks: during the summer, they went up to the mountains, while in the winter they came down to the coastal plains. Sarıkeçili Yörüks had winter camps on the coasts of Silifke, Gülnar, and Anamur, and lived in summer in the districts of Beyşehir and Seydişehir in Konya Province.

## Foodways

They state that Yörük groups kept goats, sheep, camels, and sometimes cattle.

## Oral tradition & literature

They mention folklore and traditions generally as ways in which sedentary Turkmen populations preserve parts of their heritage, and they identify Bedia Akartürk as a Turkish folk music singer.

## Language & religion

The Yörüks are described as being of Oghuz descent and as ethnically and linguistically related to Eastern and Western Turkomans. Dialects listed for Rumelian Turks include Gajal, Gerlovo Turk, Karamanli, Kyzylbash, Surguch, Tozluk Turk, Yuruk, Prizren, and Macedonian Gagauz. The sources explain that *Yörük* and *Yürük* forms reflect dialectal contractions influenced by Kipchak Turkic. Jean-Paul Roux found that the majority of the Anatolian Yörüks he visited in the late 1950s were Sunni Muslims. The sources also mention the Alevi community and distinguish Sunni Turkomans from Shiite Kızılbaş Turkomans in the historical discussion of the term Yörük.
