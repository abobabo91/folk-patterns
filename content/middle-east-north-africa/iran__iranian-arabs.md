## Overview

Iranian Arabs (Arabic: عرب إيران ʿArabu Īrān; Persian: عرب‌های ايران Arabhā-ye Irān) are citizens of Iran who are ethnically Arab. As of August 2026, their population is estimated at 4 million people, although other estimates in the supplied source range from about half a million to 12 million. They are primarily concentrated in Khuzestan province, especially in the lowlands associated with the Kārūn and Karkheh Rivers and the Persian Gulf coastal plains. Iranian Arabs also live in Fars, Hormozgan, and Khorasan. Their cultural profile includes Arabic-speaking communities, strong tribal loyalties, pastoral nomadism, farming, fishing, hospitality, Shia Islam, and distinctive women’s clothing.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Women in Khuzestan wear various types of scarves and dresses. The *aba* is a black, robe-like dress with long sleeves, leaving the hands exposed from the wrist. The *asabe* is a turban-like scarf whose cloth quality denotes the woman’s social rank. The *pooshie* is a mask-like silk cloth covering the face. Dresses include the *nefnef*, a long, loose dress, and the *thoub*, a loose, gauzy dress worn over the *nefnef*. *Ne’al* and *Kabkab*, also called *Karkab*, are two kinds of footwear.

## Music & performance

The sources do state that Iranian Arabs speak Arabic in some regions, that tribal loyalties influence socialization and politicization, and that Iranian Arabs are known for hospitality.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that Arab tribes in Khuzestan have retained identity in language, culture, and Shia Islam, but provide no specific ritual descriptions.

## Foodways

The sources identify farming and fishing as important occupations among Arabs in rural areas and describe Arabs living along the Persian Gulf coastal plains as mostly pastoral nomads.

## Oral tradition & literature

They mention Arab nationalism during the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88), stating that attempts by the Iraqi Hussein regime to generate Arab nationalism in the area had no palpable success.

## Language & religion

Most Iranian Arabs in Khuzestan are bilingual speakers of Arabic and Persian. The Arabic spoken there is Khuzestani Arabic, described as a mixture of Gulf Arabic and South Mesopotamian Arabic. Khorasani Arabs are described as descendants of Arab migrants from Arabia; very few speak Arabic as their mother tongue, and Khorasani Arabs are described as already almost totally Persianised in a 2013 article. The sources state that Arab tribes in Khuzestan have retained Shia Islam. Earlier Arabs in the Sasanian Empire included people who followed polytheistic religions, adopted Judaism, or were Christians, with most appearing to be Christians.
