## Overview

Turkish culture layers Central Asian pastoralism, Turco-Persian court forms, Byzantine-Greek and Armenian craft traditions, and four centuries of Ottoman imperial patronage. The heartland is the Anatolian plateau and its river valleys. Turkish folk culture is unusual in preserving both a courtly canon and a still-living nomadic (Yörük) tradition.

## Material culture

Ottoman craftwork standardized carpet, tile, and silk production into named workshop traditions that remain the foundation of material culture.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Flat-woven kilims and pile carpets knotted with the Turkish knot are the signature textiles, each region producing distinctive styles.

- **Kilim** — Flat-woven slit-tapestry rugs in wool made for floor, cradle, saddlebag and prayer use, distinguished by bold reciprocal-field geometry and the elibelinde goddess figure.
- **Turkish carpet** — Pile carpets knotted with the symmetrical Gördes knot, with regional schools including Uşak, Ladik, Ghiordes, Milas, Konya and the court manufactory at Hereke.
- **Çatma velvet** — Bursa-made silk pile velvet with brocaded metal-thread ground used for cushion covers, throne covers and kaftan panels.
- **Bursa silk and Ottoman brocade** (*kemha, seraser, serenk*) — Silk-and-metal lampas and metal-thread cloths woven in Bursa and Istanbul under palace specification with large-scale single motifs.
- **Yastık** — Small pile or flatwoven cushion face roughly 60 × 90 cm, the standard domestic weaving of Anatolian households preserving regional motif dialects.

### Clothing & dress

Ottoman dress combined long robes, baggy trousers, embroidered waistcoats and sashes, with headgear marking status and origin.

- **Entari** — Long open robe worn by men in Ottoman times.
- **Şalvar** — Baggy trousers gathered at the ankle worn by men and women.
- **Fes** — Crimson felt hat adopted after 1829 and abolished by the Hat Law of 1925.
- **Üç etek** — Long three-panel coat worn by women over the içlik shirt.
- **Nalın** — Wooden bath clogs inlaid with mother-of-pearl.

### Architecture

The classic Turkish house is a timber-frame two-storey structure organized around a central sofa hall with carved wooden elements and screened windows.

- **Türk evi** — Two-storey house with masonry ground floor and timber-frame upper floor with jettied projection over the street on brackets.
- **Sofa** — Central organizing hall from which square rooms open, each self-sufficient with built-in cupboards and divan seating.
- **Kafes** — Turned-wood lattice screens on upper windows.
- **Cumba** — Projecting bay window serving as the house's public face.
- **Kara çadır** — Black goat-hair tent still pitched by Yörük nomads.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

İznik ware established the Ottoman ceramic standard with white slip-covered body and raised red glaze, while tinned copper dominates functional metalwork.

- **İznik ware** — White slip-covered ceramic under clear alkaline glaze painted in cobalt, turquoise, sage green and raised sealing-wax red, used for mosque tiles and vessels.
- **Kütahya** — Ceramic industry continuing into the eighteenth century in a brighter, more provincial register including pilgrim flasks and coffee cups.
- **Cezve** — Tinned copper coffee pot for brewing Turkish coffee.
- **Kündekari** — Interlocking geometric joinery on doors and pulpits.
- **Ebru** — Marbled paper made through workshop traditions alongside calligraphy and inlay.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Silver dominates village adornment while gold marks urban and bridal wealth, with coin ornaments and belt clasps as the core forms.

- **Hasır** — Bridal collar made from strings of Ottoman altın and çeyrek coins.
- **Kemer tokası** — Belt clasp in filigree or niello, often the single most valuable object a woman owned.
- **Nazar boncuğu** — Blue glass amulet against the eye made by Görece and İzmir glassmakers.
- **Muska** — Small Qur'anic triangles in silver cases worn as amulets.
- **Telkâri** — Filigree work from Midyat and Trabzon's silver wire-knit traditions.

## Music & performance

Anatolian folk music centers on the saz lute and its relatives, while Ottoman classical music uses the makam modal system and the sacred Mevlevi âyin.

- **Saz** — Long-necked fretted lute played with bare fingers in bağlama düzeni tuning, the foundation of Anatolian folk music.
- **Kemençe** — Three-stringed bowed instrument of the Black Sea played for horon dancing.
- **Davul-zurna** — Double-headed drum paired with double-reed pipe, inseparable for announcing weddings, circumcisions and wrestling matches.
- **Âyin** — Sacred Mevlevi suite for ney and kudüm, the sacred branch of Ottoman classical music.
- **Makam** — Modal system used in Ottoman classical music played with usûl rhythmic cycles.

## Dance & theatre

Village dances are regional and named, from the line-and-circle halay to the slow solo zeybek, while theatre takes shadow-puppet and improvised comic forms.

- **Halay** — Line-and-circle dance of the southeast led by a handkerchief-holding halaybaşı to davul-zurna music.
- **Horon** — Tight shoulder-linked circle dance of Trabzon and Rize with rapid trembling steps to the kemençe.
- **Zeybek** — Slow, wide-armed solo dance in nine beats danced by efe men of the Aegean as an assertion of bearing.
- **Karagöz ve Hacivat** — Leather shadow-puppet play performed against a backlit muslin screen by a single hayalî voicing all characters, traditionally in Ramadan coffee-houses.
- **Semâ** — Turning ceremony of the Whirling Dervishes of Konya.

## Festivals & rituals

The Islamic calendar frames the year with Ramazan, Şeker Bayramı and Kurban Bayramı, overlaid with older solar festivals and life-cycle rites.

- **Ramazan** — Islamic month marked by pre-dawn sahur announced by drummers.
- **Kurban Bayramı** — Eid al-Adha celebrated with household animal sacrifice and distribution of thirds.
- **Nevruz** — Spring festival on 21 March marked with bonfire-jumping and sprouting wheat, especially in the east.
- **Yağlı güreş** — Oiled wrestling festival at Kırkpınar in Edirne held since the fourteenth century.
- **Kına gecesi** — Henna night in the wedding sequence with deliberate weeping songs and henna applied to the bride's palms.

## Foodways

Wheat and bulgur form the base, lamb and mutton dominate the protein, and dairy and preserves carry households through winter.

- **Bulgur** — Cracked and parboiled wheat used for kısır salad and raw-kneaded çiğ köfte.
- **Mantı** — Small lamb dumplings of Kayseri served under garlic yoghurt and chilli butter.
- **Kebap** — Lamb and mutton prepared in dozens of named forms including şiş, Adana, cağ and İskender varieties.
- **Beyaz peynir** — Brined sheep's-milk cheese.
- **Rakı** — Aniseed spirit clouded with water served at the rakı sofrası table with mezze.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Book of Dede Korkut is the great Anatolian narrative, with minstrel romances, quatrain traditions and the paradox-tales of Nasreddin Hoca forming the literary landscape.

- **Book of Dede Korkut** (*Kitab-ı Dedem Korkut*) — Twelve Oghuz heroic tales in prose and verse centred on the bard-sage Korkut Ata.
- **Köroğlu** — Cycle about an outlaw bard avenging his blinded father, the most widely sung successor to Dede Korkut.
- **Atışma** — Rhymed improvised duels between aşık minstrels judged on metre and wit.
- **Mâni** — Four-line quatrain used for courtship, teasing and riddling.
- **Nasreddin Hoca** — Akşehir figure whose thousands of paradox-tales function as proverb and argument.

## Language & religion

Turkish is Oghuz Turkic with vowel harmony; Sunni Islam of the Hanafi school dominates with a substantial Alevi minority whose cem ritual stands outside mosque Islam.

- **Turkish** (*Türkçe*) — Oghuz Turkic language, agglutinative with vowel harmony, mutually intelligible with Azerbaijani, Gagauz and Turkmen.
- **Hanafi school** (*Hanafi*) — Sunni Islamic school followed by the overwhelming majority of Turkish Muslims.
- **Alevi** — Substantial minority tradition with cem ritual, dede lineage guides, semah dance and veneration of Ali and the Twelve Imams.
- **Mevlevi** — Sufi order that shaped music, poetry and calligraphy, including the Whirling Dervishes.
- **Türbe** — Saint-tomb visited with wish-rags tied to trees, a pre-Islamic Inner Asian layer that persists.

## Glossary

- *elibelinde* — hands on hips, fertility motif
- *saz* — feathery serrated leaves style devised by painter Şahkulu
- *Gördes* — Turkish symmetrical knot used in carpets
- *yastık* — small cushion face, roughly 60 × 90 cm
- *sofa* — central hall organizing rooms in traditional house
- *kafes* — turned-wood lattice screens on windows
- *cumba* — projecting bay window on house facade
- *kündekari* — interlocking geometric joinery on doors
- *makam* — modal system in Ottoman classical music
- *davul-zurna* — drum-and-double-reed pair announcing ceremonies
- *halay* — line-and-circle dance of southeast
- *zeybek* — slow solo nine-beat Aegean dance
- *semâ* — turning ceremony of Whirling Dervishes
- *Karagöz* — shadow-puppet play with backlit screen
- *kına gecesi* — henna night in wedding sequence
- *bulgur* — cracked parboiled wheat used in dishes
- *rakı* — aniseed spirit clouded with water
- *aşık* — minstrel bard who performs romances and duels
- *cem* — Alevi-Bektaşi worship gathering with deyiş hymns
