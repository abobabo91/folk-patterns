## Overview

The Tats are an Iranian people living in northern Iran, especially in Qazvin province. They are mainly Shia Muslims and number about 300,000. The Tats use Tati, a group of northwestern Iranian dialects closely related to Talysh and Zaza. Persian and Iranian Azerbaijani are also spoken. The term Tati has been used since the Middle Ages for languages in the Caucasus and northwestern Iran, where it was extended to almost all local Iranian languages except Persian and Kurdish. Contemporary Tati refers to several northwestern Iranian dialects in Iranian Azerbaijan and in the provinces of Qazvin and Zanjan.

## Language & religion

The Tats use Tati, a group of northwestern Iranian dialects closely related to Talysh and Zaza. The dialects listed in the source are Chali, Danesfani, Hiaraji, Hoznini, Esfarvarini, Takestani, Sagzabadi, Ebrahimabadi, Eshtehardi, Hoini, Kajali, Shahroudi, and Harzani. Persian and Iranian Azerbaijani are also spoken. The sources describe the Tats as mainly Shia Muslims. They state that these dialects may be descendants of the extinct Old Azari language and have affinities with Talysh, Mazanderani, and the extinct Old Azari language.
