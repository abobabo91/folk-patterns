## Overview

A Ṣa'īdī is a person from Upper Egypt, known in Egyptian Arabic as صعيدي and in Coptic as ⲣⲉⲙⲣⲏⲥ (*Remris*). Upper Egypt is also called صعيد مصر and ⲙⲁⲣⲏⲥ (*Maris*). The term literally means “from Ṣa'īd,” a geographical term meaning “highland, upland, plateau.” Sa'idi people live in closely knit villages along the Nile Valley in Egypt, and most are fellahin. Approximately 30% of Egyptians live in Upper Egypt, where 80% of Egypt’s severe poverty is concentrated. Sa'idi identity is also associated with a dialect and with a form of music originating in Upper Egypt. The sources describe Egypt as culturally and linguistically diverse.

## Music & performance

Sa'idi can refer to a form of music originating in Upper Egypt. The sources also mention *Tahtib* in the “See also” section, without providing information about it. The 1998 film *Ṣa'īdi fil-Gama'a al-Amrikiya* (“A Ṣa'īdi in the American University”), starring Mohamed Henedi, is described as an example of stereotyping: its main character is portrayed as less fashionable than other Egyptian students at the American University in Cairo. No further performance tradition is documented.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that Upper Egyptians follow Islam and Christianity, and that Upper Egypt has a rich Coptic Christian history. Sahidic was the leading Coptic dialect in the pre-Islamic period and is used as a liturgical language by clergy and among Sa'idi Coptic Christians.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do document ethnic jokes and stereotypes concerning Ṣa'īdis and their dialect, mainly associated with upper-class Egyptians who own businesses in Egypt’s major cities. Sa'idi people are popularly assumed by other Egyptians to be rural simpletons. The 1998 film *Ṣa'īdi fil-Gama'a al-Amrikiya* is identified as an example of this stereotyping.

## Language & religion

Sa'idi identity is associated with Sa'idi Arabic, the dialect spoken by Sa'idis, and with Sahidic Coptic. The Coptic name for a person from Upper Egypt is ⲣⲉⲙⲣⲏⲥ, pronounced *rem.rīs*, meaning “person of the South,” or ⲣⲉⲙ(ⲡ)ⲙⲁⲣⲏⲥ, pronounced *rem.pma.rīs* or *rem.ma.rīs*, meaning “person of the place of the south.” The sources state that Upper Egyptians follow Islam and Christianity, and that a large proportion of Upper Egypt’s population are Christians. Sahidic Coptic is used liturgically by clergy and among Sa'idi Coptic Christians.
