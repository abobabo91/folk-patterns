## Overview

The Pontic Greeks are an ethnically Greek group indigenous to Pontus, in northeastern Anatolia, in what is now Turkey. Pontic Greek culture is distinguished by music, dances, cuisine, and clothing. Today, most Pontic Greeks live in Northern Greece, especially in and around Thessaloniki in Macedonia, while communities also exist around the Caucasus, including Georgia. Pontic Greeks traditionally speak Pontic Greek, a modern Greek variety that developed remotely in Pontus. It is commonly known as Pontiaka and traditionally called *Romeika* by native speakers. Most Pontic Greeks are Orthodox Christians; Muslim Pontic communities remain in Turkey’s Eastern Black Sea region and elsewhere. Their history includes Greek colonization, the Empire of Trebizond, Ottoman rule, genocide, and the 1923 population exchange.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources identify clothing as one of the features distinguishing Pontic Greek culture.

### Architecture

Pontic Greeks constructed buildings on the southern coast of the Black Sea over a presence lasting millennia. The sources describe churches, monasteries, castles, fortifications, and other religious and non-religious structures, often built in stone or incorporating carved stone and cave construction.

Named religious buildings include the Virgin Mary Monastery in Şebinkarahisar District, Giresun Province; Panagias Soumela Monastery, Saint George Peristereotas Monastery, and Vazelon Monastery in Maçka, Trabzon Province; Panagia Theoskepastos Monastery in Trabzon; Balatlar Church; St. Anne Church; Hagia Sophia; Hagios Eugenios Church; and Panagia Chrysokephalos church. Some churches later became mosques, including the Hagios Eugenios Church, now the New Friday Mosque, and Panagia Chrysokephalos church, now Fatih Mosque.

The sources also name Giresun Castle, Rize Castle, Kiz Castle, Zilkale Castle, Kov Castle, and fortifications at Sinope. In the 1600s and 1700s, coastal Pontians moved into the Pontic Mountains and established villages such as *Santa*.

## Music & performance

Music is one of the features distinguishing Pontic Greek culture. The principal named instrument in the sources is the *Pontic lyra*, a traditional musical instrument that remains important to Pontian diaspora communities.

Pontic Greeks traditionally speak Pontic Greek, also called *Pontiaka* and traditionally known by native speakers as *Romeika*. The sources state that Pontic communities share a common culture distinguished partly by music.

Pontic Greek communities have existed in Pontus, Northern Greece, the Caucasus, Georgia, Armenia, Russia, Ukraine, and Crimea.

## Dance & theatre

The sources name the *Serra*, also known as *Pyrrhichios*, as a Pontic folk dance. Folk dances such as the Serra remain important to Pontian diaspora communities.

## Festivals & rituals

They describe major historical events affecting Pontic Greeks, including the genocide perpetrated between 1913 and 1923 and the 1923 population exchange between Greece and Turkey defined by the Treaty of Lausanne. These were historical events rather than festivals. The sources also state that most Pontic Greeks are Orthodox Christians, while some communities in Turkey are Sunni Muslims.

## Foodways

The sources identify cuisine as one of the features distinguishing Pontic Greek culture. They name *havítz*, also called *kuymak*, as a food cooked by Pontians and Turks. They also state that Pontians cook *boortsog* and *İmam bayıldı*, and that their food culture shares aspects with Turks, Lazes, Persians, and Armenians.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources connect Pontus with Greek mythology, identifying the Black Sea region as the place where Jason and the Argonauts sailed in search of the Golden Fleece. They also state that the Amazons lived in Pontus, with a minority living in Taurica, also known as Crimea. The warlike characteristics of Pontic Greeks were once said to derive from the Amazons of Pontus.

The sources mention Xenophon’s *Anabasis*, which describes Greek mercenaries reaching the Euxine Sea after the failure of the rebellion of Cyrus the Younger. They also mention Apollonius of Rhodes and his *Argonautica*, which referred to Giresun Island. No Pontic Greek proverb, riddle, folktale cycle, oral performance context, or contemporary literary revival is documented.

## Language & religion

Pontic Greeks traditionally speak Pontic Greek, a modern Greek variety that developed remotely in the region of Pontus. It is commonly known as *Pontiaka* and traditionally called *Romeika* by native speakers. The sources describe Pontic Greek as a Greek language variety and state that Pontian Greeks share cultural traits with other Greeks.

Most Pontic Greeks are Orthodox Christians. A minority in Turkey are Sunni Muslims, and previously Islamized Greek settlements exist in the Eastern Black Sea region, including areas around Trabzon, Gümüşhane, Rize, Bayburt, and Artvin. During Ottoman rule, some Pontian Greeks converted to Islam and adopted Turkish.
