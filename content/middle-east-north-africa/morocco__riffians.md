## Overview

Riffians, also called Rifians, are a Berber ethnic group indigenous to the Rif region of northeastern Morocco and the Spanish enclave of Melilla. Communities also live in southern Spain, France, the Netherlands, Belgium, and elsewhere in Western Europe. They are predominantly Sunni Muslims and primarily speak Tarifit, a Zenati Berber language. Tarifit is spoken mainly in the Rif provinces of Nador, Al Hoceima, Driouch, and Berkane, and by some 1,200,000 Riffians, comprising 3.2% of Morocco’s population. The group’s folk-culture record in the supplied sources concerns clothing, agricultural life, language, social organization, and surviving pre-Islamic beliefs.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Haik* — Until the end of the nineteenth century, the haik was the main garment worn by Riffian women. Haiks of the Rif were plain or had a few simple cotton stripes at the end.

*Djellaba* — The Riffian djellaba was historically short, with narrower sleeves and hoods than those of the Jebala and the Ghomara, and it was unlined.

Cotton garments — Riffian clothing described in the sources includes cotton textiles, including the white cotton turban worn by men and the cotton stripes found on some women’s haiks.

Tie-dyed belts — Women’s haiks were worn with belts that were often decorated with tie-dye designs.

### Clothing & dress

Historically, Riffian men wore a short djellaba with narrower sleeves and hoods than the djellabas of the Jebala and the Ghomara; the garment was unlined. Today, Riffian men wear a white cotton turban called *ariziṯ*, tightly wrapped around the head and covering all of it. Until the end of the nineteenth century, the principal garment of Riffian women was the haik. Rif haiks were plain or had a few simple cotton stripes at the end, and they were worn with belts often decorated with tie-dye designs.

The sources mention that Tarifit has little written literature before the twentieth century and that the first written examples began appearing just before the colonial period.

## Festivals & rituals

They do state that modern-day Riffians are predominantly Muslim and that some pre-Islamic beliefs and practices survived among Riffian communities until relatively recently. In the early twentieth century, some Riffians traditionally distinguished nine types of supernatural beings: *Malakin*, angels of the Abrahamic religions; *Jnun*, jinn; *Khier*, ghosts believed to be tall and thin and to travel through cemeteries at night; *Thasadunt imthran*, a man-eating female mule believed to pass through cemeteries at night; *Er riah*, a baleful hot summer whirlwind; *Thamza*, also called Stut, a man-eating ogress believed capable of changing her form; *Amziw*, a man-eating ogre and the husband of Thamza; *Bu sibba ijitjen*, the father of the seven heads, a fire-breathing, mule-sized beast with seven human heads; and *Hajuj u Majuj*, described as dwarfs living in underground caves who sought to reach the surface and cause destruction. During the 1920s, some Riffians reportedly believed that Thamza, Amziw, and Bu sibba ijitjen had once existed but had died long ago and could no longer be found.

## Foodways

Riffians have lived a largely settled, agricultural lifestyle, using hand tools, oxen, and cattle to plow steeply terraced land in their valleys. Horticultural produce, sheep and goat meat, cheese, and milk provided traditional sustenance. Some Riffians practiced sardine seining along the Mediterranean coast. They identify modern-day Riffians as predominantly Muslim.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that Riffian Berber had little written literature before the twentieth century and that the first written examples of Tarifit appeared just before the colonial period. Texts by R. Basset from 1897 and S. Biarnay from 1917 were transcribed in the Latin alphabet, although the sources characterize their transcription as deficient. The sources also identify a sample text from *An introduction to Tarifiyt Berber (Nador, Morocco)* by Khalid Mourigh and Maarten Kossmann: *Sirkuḷasyun* (“trafic”).

## Language & religion

Tarifit, also known as Riffian, is a Zenati Berber language spoken in the Rif region of northern Morocco. Its traditional autonym is *Tmaziɣt*, also written Tamazight; Tarifit or Tarifect developed as a linguistic term used to distinguish the language from other Berber varieties. Tarifit has dialectal variation and is described through western, west-central, central, east-central, and eastern Rif Berber aggregates. It has been written in several systems: Latin transcription was used in early texts, Tifinagh became official throughout Morocco since 2003, and the Berber Latin alphabet continues to be widely used online and in publications. Riffians are predominantly Sunni Muslims, while some pre-Islamic beliefs and practices survived until relatively recently.
