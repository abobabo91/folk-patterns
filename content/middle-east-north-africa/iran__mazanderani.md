## Overview

The Mazanderanis, also known as Mazanis or Tabari people, are an Iranian ethnic group indigenous to Mazandaran province on the southern shores of the Caspian Sea. Their settled area lies between the Caspian coast and the Alborz Mountains and forms part of the historical region of Tabaristan, also called Tapuria or Tapurestan. The population was 4,480,000 in 2019; a 2006 estimate placed the number between 3 and 4 million. Mazanderani is a Northwestern Iranian language, closely related to Gilaki, and most Mazandaranis are also fluent in Persian. Farming and fishing are traditional professions. Their folk-cultural profile is especially documented here through regional foodways, language, historical traditions, and connections with neighboring Caspian and South Caucasian peoples.

## Music & performance

The sources identify Amir Pazevari as a Mazanderani poet and Farya Faraji as a musicologist and composer.

## Festivals & rituals

They do mention the Tabarian calendar in connection with *narenj vehar*, a traditional wine drink consumed after hard labor, particularly during summer and the month of Merdal. No further festival or ritual information is supplied.

## Foodways

Mazanderani foodways belong to the broader Caspian cuisine of northern Iran, found primarily in Mazandaran, Gilan, Alborz, and Golestan provinces. Coastal and mountainous environments produce different food practices: coastal populations prepare dishes with local fish, Caspian rice, and vegetables, while people settled in the Alborz use indigenous herbs. Rice is historically associated with Mazandaran and Gilan and is prepared locally in the *kateh* cooking style, in which rice is cooked with more water, butter, and salt. Rice crops are grown in the sloping regions of the Alborz mountain range, and seafood is an important component of coastal meals. Persian caviar (*ḵāvīar*) is used in dishes and served with egg dishes. Between 1400 and 1870, Mazandaran was the only place in Iran cultivating sugarcane, which was sometimes eaten with bread and rice.

Named dishes include *Aghouze-Messeama*, *Dewpetti*, *Ispina-Saek*, *Khali Aush*, *Naz Khatun*, *Ispinej-Mearji*, *Keahi-Heali*, *Mirza Ghasemi*, *Baghali ghatogh*, *Kateh*, *Keshmesh polo*, *Khoresh-e gol dar chaman*, *Khoresh-e torsh tareh*, *Kuku eshpel*, *Morgh-e torsh*, *Sirabij*, and *Sir Anar*. Local wild herbs include *zolang*, *anarijeh*, *ouji*, and *sersem*. Stinging nettles are used in Mazandarani nettle soup. Desserts and sweets include *Aghouzenoon*, *Peshtizik*, *Pisgendela*, *Nesseri*, *Kunak*, *Tunsernun*, *Red sugar* (*Serkh seker*), and *Reshteh Khoshkar*. Mazanderani and Gilani traditional wines have historically been made from wild local grapes; *narenj vehar* is described as a traditional wine drink.

## Oral tradition & literature

Mazanderani has one of the longest written traditions among living Iranian languages, extending from the tenth to the fifteenth century. Its literature includes *Marzban Nameh*, later translated into Persian, and the poetry of Amir Pazevari. The language’s literary and administrative prominence diminished in favor of Persian by the early seventeenth century, when Mazandaran was integrated into the national administration.

## Language & religion

Mazanderani, also called Mazani, Tabari, Taveri, Tati, Geleki, and Galeshi, is a Northwestern Iranian language of the northern branch of Western Iranian. It is closely related to Gilaki; the two languages have similar vocabularies and share typological features with Caucasian languages. As of 2023, Mazanderani had 1.35 million native speakers. The language has dialects including Saravi, Amoli, Baboli, Ghaemshahri, Chaloos and East Tonekabon, Nur and Nowshahr, Shahsavari, Behshahri, Kordkuy, Katouli, Ghasrani, Damavandi, and Shahmirzadi. It is commonly written in the Perso-Arabic script, although some speakers use the Roman alphabet in SMS messages. Mazanderani is inflected and genderless, and its usual word order is SOV, with SVO also occurring in some tenses and dialects.

The sources mention Mazanderani Shia clerics, Shia thinkers, and Shia political figures.
