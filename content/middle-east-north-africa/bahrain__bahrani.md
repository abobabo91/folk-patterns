## Overview

The Bahrani, or Baharna, are an ethnoreligious group of Shia Muslim Arabs associated with the historical region of Bahrain. Most Bahraini citizens are Baharna, and populations are also found in Qatif, al-Ahsa, Kuwait, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Oman, Iraq, Iran, Eastern Africa, Europe and America. In Bahrain, they are especially associated with rural areas and Shia villages, while Bahrani Arabic is also spoken in parts of Manama. Their cultural profile includes palm-leaf weaving, pottery, dhow building, metalwork, embroidery, distinctive foodways, Bahrani Arabic, and the Nashl dress. Their history is connected with agriculture, shipbuilding, crafts, trade, migration and the development of Shia communities in the Gulf.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Palm-leaf weaving** — Artisans use palm leaves to make mats, baskets and fans. The sources identify Karbabad and Jasra as places particularly associated with this craft.

**Embroidery and textiles** — Traditional textiles and embroidery are described as significant crafts among the Baharna.

**Nashl dress** — *Thob-el-nashal*, also called the “Nashl” dress, is a women’s garment associated especially with Bahrain and Eastern Arabia. It is made from specially purchased fabrics, decorated with golden embroidered patterns, and produced by women and artisans using needles and thread.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources mention golden patterns on the Nashl dress.

### Clothing & dress

The sources document women’s clothing rather than a complete everyday dress system. *Thob-el-nashal*, the Nashl dress, was worn by women during major occasions and was especially associated with Bahrain, Qatar, Kuwait, the UAE and the Eastern Province of Saudi Arabia. It is made in vivid colors including red, blue, purple and green; black is also described as distinctive. Golden embroidery enhances the garment’s appearance. The dress is handmade by women and artisans from villages in the region, using needles and thread after the fabrics have been purchased from traditional markets. The name is connected in the source to *manshal*, a brightly colored fabric covering the howdah used to transport a bride.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

**Pottery** — The village of Al-Ali is described as famous for its ceramics, alongside other pottery traditions across Bahrain.

**Metalwork** — Baharna artisans work in copper and gold, producing jewelry and decorative objects.

**Palm-leaf household goods** — Palm leaves are used to make mats, baskets and fans, especially in Karbabad and Jasra.

**Dhow building** — Traditional wooden boats are built in shipyards in Manama and Muharraq. Shipbuilding is described as a highly respected art form.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources state that Baharna artisans create intricate jewelry through copper and gold work.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe the Nashl dress as clothing worn by women during major occasions. The sources also identify Shia Islam as central to Baharna religious identity and describe religious and political developments connected with Bahrain, including the spread of Twelver Shia Islam through Persian influence and religious exchanges. No specific ritual practice is documented in the supplied material.

## Foodways

The sources name several dishes associated with the Baharna. *Machboos* is described as common around the Gulf region. Dried salted fish is called *Lehleh*. *Ṣāfī* is served with white rice or sweet rice, known as *Muhammar*, and is described as a very popular dish in Bahrain.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that the Baharnah have various local proverbs.

## Language & religion

Bahrani Arabic, also called Bahrani or Baharna Arabic, is a variety of Arabic spoken by the Baharna in Eastern Arabia and Oman. In Bahrain it is primarily associated with Shia villages and some parts of Manama; it is also spoken in Qatif Governorate and in the Omani governorates of Al Dhahirah and Al Batinah. The dialect has been influenced by ancient Aramaic, Syriac and Akkadian, as well as Persian, Urdu, Ottoman Turkish and English. The sources describe two forms: the traditional old form, known as *Halayliyya*, and a modern form. Bahrani Arabic includes regional varieties associated with Sitra, A’ali, Buri, Manama, Ras Rumman and Diraz. The Baharna are Shia Muslims, and the sources describe Persian influence and the spread of Twelver Shia Islam in Bahrain.
