## Overview

The Hemshin people are an ethnographic group of Armenians associated with the Hemşin and Çamlıhemşin districts of Rize Province, Turkey. Hemshin communities also live near Hopa and Borçka in Artvin Province, in Abkhazia, Russia’s Krasnodar Krai, Georgia, Armenia, Kazakhstan, and Kyrgyzstan. The present community in Hemshin is Muslim and Turkish-speaking, while the Hopa Hemşinli speak Turkish and Hemşince, also called Homshetsma or Homshetsi. Christian northern groups speak Homshetsi as well. The community developed from Armenian Christian populations and became distinct through geographical isolation, migration, conversion to Sunni Islam, and contact with Turkish and Laz speakers. Hemshin culture is documented through language, baking, tea cultivation, music, jokes, riddles, stories, dance, distinctive clothing, and regional occupations.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Hemshin traditional attire is described only in general terms. Women often wrap their heads with a *pushi*, a long piece of cloth adorned with beads. Men often wear hats and vests made of wool or cotton.

### Architecture

Hemshin architecture is not described in detail by the sources. The material mentions surviving Armenian architecture in Hamšēn and refers to Hemshin people and their mansions.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention beads as decoration on the *pushi* worn by women.

## Music & performance

Hemshin musical practice includes dances accompanied by instruments associated with different regional groups. The Western group uses the *parkapzuk*. The Eastern group uses the *şimşir kaval*, a flute made of buxus. The Northern group uses the *Hamshna-Zurna*, also called the Hamsheni zurna.

Music in Homshetsi has also been part of modern cultural activity. The eastern Hemshinli filmmaker Özcan Alper made *Momi* (“Grandma”), the first motion picture in Homshetsi, released in 2000. Hamsheni singer Gökhan Birben and Laz singer Kâzım Koyuncu sang in Homshetsi. In 2005, *Vova - Hamşetsu Ğhağ* was released as the first music album described as consisting exclusively of anonymous Hamshen folk songs and sung mostly in Homshetsi. In 2006, the Ensemble Caravan released the first music album in Homshetsma in Krasnodar.

## Dance & theatre

The sources state that Hemshin people accompany dances with their own music, using the *parkapzuk*, *şimşir kaval*, or *Hamshna-Zurna* according to regional group. The film *Momi* is identified as the first motion picture in Homshetsi.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that the community was originally Christian and belonged to the Armenian Apostolic Church, and that most Hemshin people later became Sunni Muslims.

## Foodways

Hemshin people are well known for baking and tea growing. The traditional occupations of Turkish Hemshinli include cultivating tea and maize, breeding livestock, and beekeeping. Northern Hamshenis of Russia and Georgia are described as growers of citrus, corn, tobacco, and tea, as well as fishermen.

## Oral tradition & literature

Hemshin people are known for clever jokes, riddles, and stories. Some anecdotes told by Muslim Hemshinli are described as being based on older Armenian ones.

Modern preservation efforts have focused on language and cultural heritage. The Hamshen Scientific, Information and Cultural Centre began exclusive projects intended to recover the cultural heritage of Hamshenis living in the region. An Armenian newspaper published in Sukhumi carries the name *Hamshen*. A Hamsheni international scientific convention took place in Sochi from October 13 to 15, 2005, with scholars from Armenia, Russia, the United States, Germany, and Iran discussing Hamsheni history.

## Language & religion

Homshetsi, also called Homshetsma or Hemşince, is described as an archaic Armenian dialect or language spoken by eastern and northern Hemshin groups in northeastern Turkey, Abkhazia, Russia, Central Asia, Armenia, and Georgia. It belongs to the Western Armenian dialect group and is generally not mutually intelligible with Armenian. Its long isolation preserved forms found in Classical and Middle Armenian, while Arabic and Turkish grammatical and lexical components also occur. It was not a written language until 1995, when Bert Vaux designed an orthographic system based on the Turkish alphabet. Christian immigrants from Hamshen used the Armenian alphabet.

Western Hemshinli in Rize now speak Turkish, with many Homshetsi loanwords. Hopa Hemşinli speak Turkish and Hemşince, which has been influenced by Turkish and Laz. Homshetsi is categorized as “Severely endangered.” Hemshin people were originally Christians of the Armenian Apostolic Church and mostly converted to Sunni Islam during Ottoman rule. The sources state that Islam did not become the general religion before the end of the 16th century.
