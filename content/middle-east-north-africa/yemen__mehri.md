## Overview

The Mahris, also known as the al-Mahra tribe, are a Yemeni ethnographic group primarily inhabiting South Arabia, especially the Al-Mahra Governorate in Yemen and the island of Socotra in the Guardafui Channel. They are also found in the Dhofar Governorate of Oman and in smaller communities in Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and Qatar. According to Ethnologue, there are around 115,200 Mahri speakers. The group is associated with the Mehri language, a Modern South Arabian language in the Semitic branch of the Afroasiatic family. Mahris have historical ties to the Horn of Africa, participated in the Ethiopian–Adal War and Islamic conquests, and are associated with fishing, desert life and Mahri camels.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that Mahris are predominantly Muslims and that a delegation from al-Mahra visited Muhammad and embraced Islam.

## Oral tradition & literature

Mehri possesses a rich oral tradition but little written vernacular literature, and there is almost no literacy in written Mehri among native speakers. The sources mention examples of Mehri poetry from a Hadramaut forum and an ELAR archive of Mehri language documentation materials.

## Language & religion

Mehri, also called *Mahrīyyt* in its endonym, is a Modern South Arabian language in the Semitic branch of the Afroasiatic family. It is spoken primarily in the Mahra Governorate of Yemen and the Dhofar Governorate of Oman, with smaller diaspora communities in Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar and Kuwait. The language group also includes Shehri, Harsusi, Hobyot, Bathari and Soqotri. Mehri is divided into Yemeni Mehri and Omani Mehri; Yemeni Mehri has western and eastern dialects, while Omani Mehri is also called Dhofari Mehri and Nagd Mehri. The language is primarily spoken, with writing using either the standard Arabic alphabet or modified Arabic alphabets that are not standardized. On Socotra, Mahri inhabitants speak Soqotri. Mahris are predominantly Muslims. Mahris consider 2 October to be the Mehri Language Day.
