## Overview

This gallery holds twenty-eight selected records: nineteen V&A objects, two British Museum textiles and seven Europeana photographs. The V&A catalogues identify the objects as made in Armenia, mainly in the nineteenth century. That place attribution does not identify every maker's ethnicity; several objects have no named maker. The selection is especially rich in embroidery and provides only a partial view of Armenian life.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The [V&A embroidered apron](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O361404) uses geometric and floral bands in wool, silk and cotton threads. A [second apron](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O361405), [bed cover](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O318206) and [embroidered linen cover](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O360692) show several materials and formats. The gallery has no firmly attributed Armenian carpet, so it cannot illustrate a complete carpet tradition.

### Clothing & dress

Aprons, a [belt](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O360697) and [1960s knitted socks](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O367802) come from different dates and contexts. They are examples of dress rather than parts of one uniform costume.

### Architecture

No building is pictured in the museum object selection. [UNESCO documents khachkar carving](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/armenian-cross-stones-art-symbolism-and-craftsmanship-of-khachkars-00434): carved cross-stones for worship and remembrance, with varied geometric and vegetal ornament.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

A [nineteenth-century earthenware jug](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O72362) has painted and incised decoration and an Armenian inscription. A [silver-gilt mitre](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O105458) and [staff](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O108696) represent church metalwork. [UNESCO records blacksmithing in Gyumri](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/tradition-of-blacksmithing-in-gyumri-01967), although no ironwork from that tradition appears here.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The V&A's [silver necklace](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O79189), [silver-gilt head ornament](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O79188) and [necklace or headband](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O80024) are dated 1850?1870. The last object's Armenian origin is qualified as probable in the museum record.

## Music & performance

[UNESCO's duduk entry](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/duduk-and-its-music-00092) describes an Armenian double-reed instrument, often made from apricot wood. The [Smithsonian catalogue](https://collections.si.edu/search/detail/edanmdm:siris_arc_315254) lists a *Songs and Dances of Armenia* recording. Neither is illustrated by an instrument in this object gallery.

## Dance & theatre

[Kochari](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/kochari-traditional-group-dance-01295) is a group dance performed on festive and family occasions. The [*Daredevils of Sassoun*](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/performance-of-the-armenian-epic-of-daredevils-of-sassoun-or-david-of-sassoun-00743) is an epic performed in narration and song.

## Festivals & rituals

UNESCO documents the [pilgrimage to St. Thaddeus Apostle Monastery](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/pilgrimage-to-the-st-thaddeus-apostle-monastery-01571) as a practice of Armenian communities in Iran and Armenia and Armenian Apostolic followers. Its description includes liturgy, processions and communal meals. The museum church objects are not documented as part of that pilgrimage.

## Foodways

[Lavash-making in Armenia](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/lavash-the-preparation-meaning-and-appearance-of-traditional-bread-as-an-expression-of-culture-in-armenia-00985) involves thin bread baked in a clay oven. UNESCO describes shared preparation and a place for lavash in weddings.

## Oral tradition & literature

The performed *Daredevils of Sassoun* has multiple versions. [Armenian letter art](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/armenian-letter-art-and-its-cultural-expressions-01513) links the alphabet and decorated writing to other artistic practices.

## Language & religion

Armenian has Eastern and Western literary forms. The church objects and UNESCO's St. Thaddeus account document aspects of Armenian Apostolic heritage; neither determines the beliefs of all Armenians.

## Glossary

- *khachkar* — a carved cross-stone used for worship or remembrance.
- *duduk* — an Armenian double-reed wind instrument.
- *kochari* — a group dance performed at social gatherings.
- *lavash* — a thin bread baked in a clay oven.
