## Overview

The Mozabite people, also called *At Weɣlan* or *At Mzab* in Tumzabt and Banī Mzāb in Arabic, are a Berber ethnic group inhabiting the M'zab natural region in the northern Sahara of Algeria. Their population is estimated at about 150,000 to 300,000 people. They mainly live in the oases of Ghardaïa, Beni Isguen, El Atteuf, Melika and Bounoura, as well as the isolated oases of Berriane and El Guerrara. Ghardaïa is the capital of the confederation, while Beni Isguen is its chief commercial centre. They primarily speak Mozabite, a Zenati language in the Berber branch of the Afroasiatic family. The supplied sources document their language, settlement history, religious affiliation and irrigation works, but provide little information about other folk-culture forms.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The supplied sources identify the M'zab as a natural region of the northern Sahara and name its oases. They state that Mozabite engineers built a system of irrigation works that made the oases more fertile than before.

## Festivals & rituals

The supplied sources state that Mozabites are primarily Ibadi Muslims and that there was also a small population of Mzabi Jews.

## Oral tradition & literature

The supplied sources mention Ibn Khaldun, Amhammad Atfayyash and the Mozabite anthropologist Brahim Chérifi in connection with explanations of the name Mzab.

## Language & religion

Mozabite, also known as Mzab, Tumẓabt or Ghardaia, is a Zenati language spoken by the Mozabites, an Ibadi Berber group inhabiting the seven cities of the M'zab natural region in northern Saharan Algeria. It is one of the Mzab–Wargla languages, a dialect cluster of the Zenati languages. Mozabites also use Algerian Arabic, and the Mozabite language was estimated by UNESCO to have about 150,000 speakers in 2010. The sources state that Mozabites are primarily Ibadi Muslims, while a small Mzabi Jewish population also existed.
