## Overview

The Banu Abs are an ancient Bedouin tribe originating in central Arabia and forming a branch of the Ghatafan tribes. The sources locate them historically around the outskirts of Medina in modern Hejaz, in an area now called Hurrat Bani Rasheed, and describe modern populations in Saudi Arabia, Oman, Eritrea, Palestine, Yemen, Sudan, Kuwait, Egypt, Jordan, the United Arab Emirates, Malaysia, Europe, the United States, and Brazil. The tribe is described as large and scattered. Its importance in the sources lies in its tribal history, traditions of independence, bravery, chivalry, honor, hospitality, genealogical memory, and the story of the warrior-poet Antarah ibn Shaddad.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources state that the main branch of the Banu Abs tribe has preserved its traditional dress despite the Westernization of some neighboring groups. A historical reference describes Harith ibn Rab'i al-Absi wearing a white turban while serving as a commander of a contingent of 1000 cavalrymen, but this is an individual account rather than a general description of Banu Abs dress.

### Architecture

They mention tribal Diwans as institutions maintained by branches of the Banu Abs outside Saudi Arabia.

## Music & performance

They state that branches of the tribe settled in different regions have incorporated local music, foods, traditions, stories, and legends from the lands where they live. The sources also mention modern cultural artistic works inspired by Antarah ibn Shaddad. These include Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov's *Symphony No. 2*, based on the legend of ʿAntar, and the first Palestinian opera composed in 1988 by Mustapha al-Kurd, whose primary subject was Antarah ibn Shaddad. No further information is provided about Banu Abs musical practice or performance.

## Dance & theatre

They mention the story of Antar and Abla and artistic works inspired by Antarah ibn Shaddad, including an opera.

## Festivals & rituals

They describe tribal traditions of honor, family loyalty, genealogical remembrance, and the maintenance of clan-based institutions. Branches outside Saudi Arabia are said to maintain a clan head, traditional codes of honor, family traditions, and tribal Diwans. The sources also describe private family genealogies and histories tracing patrilineal lines from modern members back to Abs, Adnan, Ishmael, Abraham, and ultimately Adam. These records can include births and deaths, wars and battles, deeds, honors, personalities, reputations, occupations, causes of death, and other notable events.

## Foodways

They state that branches of the tribe living outside Saudi Arabia have adapted to local cultures and incorporated foods from the lands where they settled. No named Banu Abs dish or food tradition is provided.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Banu Abs are associated in the sources with the famous classical love and war story of Antar and Abla. Antarah ibn Shaddad al-Absi, dated approximately 525–615, is described as one of the seven great pre-Islamic Arab poets and the protagonist of this story. He displayed chivalry, bravery, and eloquence in the Arabic language, earning among the Arabs of the Peninsula the name “Complete Knight.” He was also the author of the *Divan* and a warrior.

The sources record traditions about the war of al-Dahhas wal Ghabra between the Banu Abs and their brother tribe, the Banu Dhubyan. They state that the war lasted almost 40 years and ended when Zuhayr ibn Jadhima called for an end to the bloodshed and asked wealthy merchants of both tribes to pay the losses caused by the war. Absi traditions also recall 3,000 members of the tribe repelling a Sasanian attack of 20,000.

The sources mention fables, proverbs, stories, legends, and private genealogical histories.

## Language & religion

The sources describe the traditional dialect of the Banu Abs as a form of Hejazi and Najdi Arabic, specifically a mixture of the dialect of the northern peoples of Ha'il, the standard Bedouin dialect, and Hijazi Arabic.

The Banu Abs are described as Northern Adnanite Arabs, descending from Adnan. Absi traditions tell of the prophet Khalid ibn Sinan, who taught Biblical monotheism and the worship of God as “al-ahad al-samad” before Muhammad. The sources state that most men of the Abs rejected Khalid at the time, while later some Absis remembered him during the Arab conquests. Other members, including Ubayy ibn Amara ibn Malik, accepted Muhammad as prophet and are ranked as Companions. As Muslims, the Abs and some Berbers continued to venerate Khalid, although Muslims have historically been divided over his status.
