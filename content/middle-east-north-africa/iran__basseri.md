## Overview

The Basseri are a Persian nomadic and pastoral tribe of Fars province in Iran. Their migratory area is around Shiraz, and their traditional lands form a strip from the Kūh-e Būl mountain to the hills west of Lar. The tribe’s winter lands are in Jahrom, Evaz, and north of Larestan County; in summer, groups stay in Marvdasht, Pasargad, and Eqlid. The Basseri are one of the five tribes of the Khamseh confederation. Their economy stems mainly from sheep and goats. The tent is the basic unit of social organization, and each tent has a recognized head who deals with tribal officers, villagers, and strangers. The Basseri speak a dialect of Persian containing many Old Persian words not found in modern Persian.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Basseri tribe rugs** — Basseri rugs are asymmetrically knotted and are usually made by the women of the tribe. They are distinguished by brighter colours, more open space, smaller ornaments and figures, and orange as a specific colour.

**Locally made packing tools and rugs** — Packing tools and rugs are woven by Basseri women and are locally made.

### Clothing & dress

Women’s clothes are largely sewn from bought material, while male clothing is bought finished.

### Architecture

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe historical changes affecting Basseri mobility and settlement. In the 1930s, many Basseri were forced to settle by the policies of Reza Shah; in 1941, they again started to decamp. After the Land Reformations of Iran, many were settled in the cities and villages of Fars province, but some later resumed decamping. Following the Islamic Revolution of Iran, problems associated with nomadic life, including inaccessibility to hospitals and schools, successive droughts, and destruction of migration paths, contributed to movement toward the cities and villages of the province.

They state only that the Basseri economy stems mainly from sheep and goats.

## Oral tradition & literature

They identify Fredrik Barth’s *Nomads of South Persia: the Basseri tribe of the Khamseh Confederacy* as a work concerning the tribe, but the supplied material does not describe Basseri oral literature.

## Language & religion

The Basseri speak the Basseri dialect, which is a dialect of Persian containing many Old Persian words not found in modern Persian. The majority know only the Basseri dialect, while some also know English. The dialect is most similar to the Persian spoken in urban Shiraz.

The sources describe the Basseri as Shia Muslims. They state that the group became Sunni Muslims after Arabs invaded Iran in the 7th century CE and was force converted to Shi’ism following the Safavid invasion in the 16th century CE. Before the arrival of Islam in the region, the sources describe the Basseri as Zoroastrians, like the majority of Persians.
