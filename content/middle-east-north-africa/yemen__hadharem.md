## Overview

The Hadharem (*ḥaḍārim*; singular: Hadhrami, *ḥaḍramī*) are an Arab ethnographic group indigenous to the Hadhramaut region in the Arabian Peninsula, a region associated with modern-day Yemen, southern Oman, and southern Saudi Arabia. Their spoken language is Hadhrami Arabic, a dialect of Arabic. Among the two million inhabitants of Hadhramaut, there are about 1,300 distinct tribes. Hadharem history includes seafaring, trading, migration, religious change, and settlement around the Indian Ocean basin. Communities and people of Hadhrami origin or descent are also documented in western Yemen, the Arab states of the Persian Gulf, the Red Sea coast, the Horn of Africa, South Asia, Sri Lanka, and Maritime Southeast Asia.

The sources do record a long seafaring and trading tradition and Hadhrami movement around the Indian Ocean basin.

## Festivals & rituals

Hadhramaut was under Muslim rule and converted to Islam during the time of Prophet Muhammad. A religious leader from Iraq introduced the Hadharem to Ibadi Islam in the mid-eighth century; in 951 AD Sunnis took Hadhramaut and placed it under their domain. The Hadharem currently follow Sunni Islam, specifically the Shafi'i school.

## Language & religion

The Hadharem speak Hadhrami Arabic, a dialect of Arabic. Hadharem living in the diaspora who have acculturated mainly speak the local language of the region where they live. Hadhramaut was under Muslim rule and converted to the faith during the time of Prophet Muhammad. Ibadi Islam was introduced in the mid-eighth century, while Sunnis took Hadhramaut in 951 AD; the Hadharem follow Sunni Islam, specifically the Shafi'i school.
