## Overview

The Zayanes are a Berber population inhabiting the Khenifra region in the central Middle Atlas mountains of Morocco. They live in the Middle Atlas mountain range around Khénifra and belong to a large tribe that twice a year wanders toward Azaghar, where the climate is milder than in Adrar, where winter is very severe. They speak Central Atlas Tamazight, also called Tazayit, one of the Berber languages. The sources describe their attachment to ancestral land and their tenacity as warriors, especially during the colonization, under the leadership of Mouha ou Hammou Zayani. They prevented many invaders from easily seizing Khénifra.

## Foodways

The sources identify goat’s milk, ewe’s milk, and *Ahriche* as foods enjoyed by the Zayanes. *Ahriche* is a dish of tripe, usually consisting of ganglion, caul, lung, or heart of an animal wound with intestines on an oak stick and cooked on hot coals.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources name Central Atlas Tamazight, or *Tazayit*, as the language of the Zayanes and list works concerning Berber literature, Amazigh poetry, Zayanes customs, and resistance.

## Language & religion

The Zayanes speak Central Atlas Tamazight, or *Tazayit*, which the source identifies as one of the Berber languages. The source also gives *Aẓayyi* as the singular and *Iẓayyan* as the plural in Central Atlas Tamazight, and gives Arabic *زيان*, romanized as *Zayān*.
