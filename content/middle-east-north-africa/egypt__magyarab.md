## Overview

The Magyarabs are a small Hungarian subgroup living within Nubia along the Nile in Egypt and Sudan. They descend from Hungarians who intermarried with local Nubian people and are believed to date back to the late 16th century, when Hungary and Egypt were part of the Ottoman Empire. Around 400 Magyarabs live in Cairo, while others live in Sudan around Wadi Halfa and in villages around Aswan. They still consider themselves Hungarians and have been members of the World Federation of Hungarians since 1992. They do not speak Hungarian, although a list of non-Arabic words used on Magyarab island was recognized as similar to Hungarian words.

## Oral tradition & literature

According to local Magyarabs, their ancestor was Ibrahim el-Magyar, a general who came from Buda, present-day Budapest, in 1517. He married a local Nubian woman, and their son Ali had five sons: Selami, Mustafa, Djelal Eddin, Musa and Iksa.

## Language & religion

The Magyarabs do not speak Hungarian. Around 1934, Hansjoachim von der Esch recorded non-Arabic words used on Magyarab island, which he said were recognized by László Almásy as similar to Hungarian words. The name “Magyarab” combines “Magyar,” meaning Hungarian, with “Ab,” which in Nubian means “tribe”; together, the name translates as “Tribe of the Magyars.”
