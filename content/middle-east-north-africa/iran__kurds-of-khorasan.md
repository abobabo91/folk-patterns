## Overview

Khorasani Kurds (*Kurdên Xorasanê*; Persian: کردهای خراسان) live in the provinces of North Khorasan and Razavi Khorasan in northeastern Iran, along the Iran–Turkmenistan border. There are about 696 Kurdish villages in the two Khorasan provinces. They speak the Kurmanji dialect of Kurdish and are Shia Muslims. Many are bilingual in Khorasani Turkic, mainly because of intermarriages with Khorasani Turks, while Persian is the lingua franca. Their history includes deportations from present-day Turkish Kurdistan and the South Caucasus, the establishment of Kurdish domains in Khorasan, and sustained interaction with Khorasani Turkic and Turkmen tribes.

## Music & performance

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources identify Ja'far Qoli as the most famous Khorasani Kurdish poet discussed in the article. Some of his works were modelled on the verses of the 18th-century Turkmen ashik Magtymguly and on Khorasani Turkic verses.

## Language & religion

Khorasani Kurdish is a dialect of Kurmanji and lacks distinct sub-dialects. It is influenced by Persian, Khorasani Turkic, and Turkmen. Two of the four major Khorasani Kurdish tribes, Zafaranlu and Shadlu, mainly speak Khorasani Turkic. Khorasani Kurds are Shia Muslims, and Persian functions as the lingua franca.
