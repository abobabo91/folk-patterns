## Overview

The Iraqi Turkmens, also referred to as Iraqi Turks, are the third-largest ethnic group in Iraq. Their traditional homeland is in northern Iraq, mainly Kirkuk and Nineveh provinces, with Kirkuk regarded by them as their capital. They are closely related to Syrian Turkmen and have close cultural ties with Turkey. According to 2013 data from the Iraqi Ministry of Planning, their population was about 3 million out of Iraq’s total population of about 34.7 million, approximately 9% of the country’s population. Their dialects belong to the Western Oghuz branch of the Turkic languages.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They state only that some Iraqi Turkmens claimed their traditional clothes strongly resembled Sumerian clothes, alongside claims that their language contained Sumerian influence.

### Architecture

They mention physical infrastructure and major water projects in and around Baghdad and the construction of a dam in Karbala under Suleiman the Magnificent, but do not present these as Iraqi Turkmen architectural traditions.

## Music & performance

They mention Türkmeneli TV, launched in Kirkuk in 2004, which broadcasts programmes in Turkish and Arabic and had studios in Kirkuk, Baghdad, and the Çankaya neighbourhood in Ankara as of 2012.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that Iraqi Turkmens are predominantly Muslim, that Sunni Turkmens form the majority at about 60–70%, and that approximately 30% to 40% practice the Shia branch of Islam. The sources also describe sectarian violence between Sunni and Shia Turkmens.

## Foodways

They state that some Iraqi Turkmens claimed Sumerian influence in the names of foods.

## Oral tradition & literature

Iraqi Turkmen literature is described as combining loyalty to Iraq as a state with the maintenance of Turkish distinctiveness. For Mustafa Gökkaya, born in 1910, his community was Muslim and “my father is Turk, and the homeland [is] my mother.” For Reşit Ali Dakuklu, born in 1918, being part of “the Turks of Iraq” involved brotherly relations with every nation, unity with Iraq, and speaking in Turkish.

## Language & religion

Iraqi Turkmen dialects belong to the Western Oghuz branch of Turkic languages and are also called Iraqi Turkmen Turkish, Iraqi Turkish, and Iraqi Turkic. They have been influenced by Ottoman Turkish, Azerbaijani Turkic, Arabic, and Kurdish. The written language is based on Istanbul Turkish and uses the modern Turkish alphabet. Traditional Turkmeni used the Arabic script, but by 2005 Turkish had replaced it in Iraqi schools. The Turkish language was recognized as a minority language in Kirkuk and Kifri in 1930, banned by the Iraqi government in 1972, and recognized under the Iraqi constitution in 2005. Iraqi Turkmens are predominantly Muslim; Sunni and Shia communities coexist alongside a small Catholic minority. Historically, some followed Qizilbash doctrine, Bektashi Alevism, and later Twelver Shia Islam.
