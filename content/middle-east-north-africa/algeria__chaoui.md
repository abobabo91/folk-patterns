## Overview

The Chaoui people, also called Shawiya, are a Berber ethnic group native to the Aurès region of northeastern Algeria. Their area includes Constantine, Setif, Batna, Khenchla, Oum El Bouaghi and Tébessa provinces, within and around the Aurès Mountains. They call themselves *Išawiyen* or *Icawiyen* and traditionally speak Shawiya, also called *Tachawit* or *Tacawit*. Shawiya belongs to the Berber branch of the Afro-Asiatic family and is a variety of the Zenati languages. Estimates of the number of Shawiya speakers range from 1.4 to 3 million. The Chaoui are described as the second-largest Berber-speaking group in Algeria after the Kabyles. Their folk-culture profile includes Shawiya music, the Rahaba dance, fantasia horsemanship, oral poetry, and material practices associated with the Aurès.

## Music & performance

Chaoui music is described as a musical style of the Aurès in southern Algeria. It is also called Shawiya style in English or French Chaoui. The music combines sahraoui, or Saharan, music with marked danceable rhythms and is accompanied by flute and bendir. The sources name Houria Aïchi, Ali El-Khencheli, A'issa ÉI-Djermouni, Hadj Bouregaa, Teldja and Zoulikha as notable singers. Another source names Aïssa Djermouni, Ali Khencheli, Massinissa, Ishem Boumaraf, Djamel Sabri, Groupe Iwal and Houria Aïchi among twentieth-century singers.

The sources identify music as specifically Chaoui or Shawiya. The supplied material also does not describe professional musicians, musical training, funerary performance, court performance, or tea-house contexts.

## Dance & theatre

The Shawia dance is called *Rahaba*. Men and women dance it at weddings. The sources also describe the *fantasia* as a traditional exhibition of horsemanship in the Aurès, performed during cultural festivals.

## Festivals & rituals

The supplied sources mention weddings as a context for the Rahaba dance and cultural festivals as occasions for fantasia horsemanship.

The historical material describes resistance, religious change and political events rather than a ritual calendar. Christianity spread massively in the Aurès in the year 256, and the region became an important center of Christendom. The Synod of Hippo in 393 was held in Hippo Regius in modern-day Algeria and was attended by Augustine of Hippo. These historical statements do not constitute a description of contemporary Chaoui ritual practice.

## Language & religion

Shawiya, also called Shawiya Berber, Chaouïa, *Tachawit* or *Tacawit*, is a Zenati Berber language spoken in Algeria by the Shawiya people. Its primary speech area is the Awras Mountains and surrounding regions, including Batna, Khenchela, Sétif, Oum El Bouaghi, Souk Ahras, Tébessa, Biskra, Guelma, Mila and Constantine. It is closely related to the Shenwa language of Central Algeria. The language was until recently unwritten and rarely taught at school. Speakers have often code-switched to Algerian Arabic, French or English for non-traditional technology and sociological concerns.

The supplied sources describe Christianity in the Roman period and identify Augustine of Hippo and Donatus Magnus as important figures in Western Christianity.
