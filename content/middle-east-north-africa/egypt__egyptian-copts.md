## Overview

Copts are a Christian ethnoreligious group native to Egypt who have inhabited the area of modern Egypt since antiquity and are descended from the ancient Egyptians. They predominantly follow the Coptic Orthodox Church, with minorities belonging to the Coptic Catholic Church and the Evangelical Church of Egypt. Copts account for roughly 5 to 15 percent of Egypt’s population and form the largest Christian population in Egypt and the Middle East, as well as in Sudan and Libya. Most Copts live in Upper Egypt, while Copts are also prominent in urban cities such as Cairo and Alexandria. Their historic language, Coptic, is the final stage of the Egyptian language and a direct descendant of Demotic Egyptian. Their cultural distinctiveness includes religious traditions, Coptic music, and the continued use of the Coptic calendar.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention monasteries, cells, caves, churches, palaces, and mosques in connection with Coptic history.

## Music & performance

Coptic music is thought to incorporate some influences from earlier Egyptian musical traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

Coptic religious practice includes the Coptic calendar, which is based on the ancient Egyptian calendar and remains significant in liturgical practices. The sources state that the Council of Nicaea entrusted the Patriarch of Alexandria with calculating and annually announcing the exact date of Easter to the rest of the Christian churches. They also describe themes of persecution and martyrdom as a significant part of Coptic identity because of historic and current conflicts.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention Coptic writings, including a fragment of the Gospel of John written in Coptic and found in Upper Egypt, and New Testament writings found in Oxyrhynchus in Middle Egypt. They also describe the History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria as a source recounting fiscal oppression imposed by the caliphate against Copts.

## Language & religion

Coptic is the final stage of the Egyptian language and a direct descendant of Demotic Egyptian. Historically, Copts spoke Coptic, and Scripture was translated into Coptic in the second century. The language developed several forms, including Sahidic, Fayyumic, and Bohairic. Coptic writing became a coherent system for expressing the Egyptian language and helped distinguish the native population of Egypt from the ruling Greeks. The Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria is an Oriental Orthodox Church, while the Coptic Catholic Church is an Eastern Catholic Church in communion with the Holy See of Rome; others belong to the Evangelical Church of Egypt. The Council of Chalcedon in 451 AD produced a division between the church that accepted its decrees and the church rejected by the vast majority of native Copts.
