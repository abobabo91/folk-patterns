## Overview

The Kumzari or Kumzar are an Iranian ethnic group native to the Musandam Peninsula in northern Oman. They live principally in the village of Kumzar and in the Harat al-Kamazirah quarter of Khasab, with Kumzari speakers also found in Dibba, coastal villages, and on Larak Island in Iran. Kumzari is a Southwestern Iranian language with substantial Arabic influence and similarities to Persian, Luri, Achomi, and Balochi. The language survives with between 4,000 and 5,000 speakers and is described as the only Iranian language spoken exclusively in the Arabian Peninsula. Kumzari cultural life documented in the sources includes fishing, semi-nomadic travel, wedding dance, and the use of a Perso-Arabic script.

## Language & religion

Kumzari is a Southwestern Iranian, or Iranic, language spoken by the Kumzari people and the Bedouin Shihuh tribe. It has similarities with Persian, Luri, Achomi, and Balochi and has a substantial Arabic influence, along with Portuguese and English influence. It is usually written in the Perso-Arabic script. The alphabet has 33 letters and includes additional letters such as چ and ۺ, as well as the lam-Alif ligature and Hamza. Kumzari has eight vowels and a distinction between long and short vowels.
