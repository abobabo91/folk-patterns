## Overview

Iranian Turkmens are a branch of Turkmen people living mainly in the northern and northeastern regions of Iran. Their region is called Turkmen Sahra and includes substantial parts of Golestan Province. Their population is estimated at 0.5 to 2.4 million people; nearly one million live along the northern edges of Iran, just south of the Turkmenistan-Iran border. They have historically included semi-nomadic tribes, and many customs continue among modern Turkmens. The sources describe Yomut, Goklen, and Teke tribes, as well as agriculture, cattle breeding, extended-family households, and continued movement between permanent homes and tents.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources identify Dowletmammet Azady as a Turkmen poet, scholar, and Sufi, and Magtymguly Pyragy as a Turkmen spiritual leader, philosophical poet, and Sufi.

## Language & religion

Iranian Turkmens primarily speak Southern Turkmen, a variant of the Turkmen language also spoken in Afghanistan. Southern Turkmen is written mainly in the Perso-Arabic script, although some people use the Turkmen Latin script, which has been common in Turkmenistan since its independence in the 1990s. Southern Turkmen is mutually intelligible with the Turkmen variety spoken in Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan, borrows heavily from Persian, and has a strong Arabic influence. The sources identify Dowletmammet Azady and Magtymguly Pyragy as Sufis.
