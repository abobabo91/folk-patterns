## Overview

Syrian Turkmen, also called Syria Turks, are Syrian citizens of Turkish race who mainly trace their roots to Anatolia. Most live near the Syrian-Turkish border, in an area extending from Idlib and Aleppo to the Raqqa Governorate, while others live near Latakia, Homs, Hama, Damascus, Daraa, and Quneitra. They are also concentrated in the urban centres and countryside of Aleppo, Damascus, Homs, Hama, Latakia, and Quneitra. Their population is difficult to estimate because official censuses did not record ethnic origin or mother tongue; estimates cited in the sources range from 250,000 to 3 million. Turkish is widely used among them, alongside Arabic and Kurdish in Syria. Their folk-culture significance in the supplied sources lies chiefly in language, settlement, identity, and political history.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that the majority of Syrian Turkmen are Sunni Muslims, while a small minority are Shia Muslims, particularly Alevis and Bektashis; some Turkish-speaking people identified as Turkmen practice Christianity.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that Syrian Turkmen communities were banned from publishing works in Turkish under the rule of Hafez al-Assad.

## Language & religion

Syrian Turkmen speak Turkish, which the sources describe as the third most widely used language in Syria after Arabic and Kurdish. Turkish is spoken especially in villages east of the Euphrates, north of Aleppo, and along the northern coast near the Syrian-Turkish border; Turkish-speaking areas also occur in Qalamun and Homs. The sources name Kilis and Antep dialects in Aleppo, an Urfa dialect in Tell Abyad and Raqqa, a Hatay/Yayladağı dialect in Bayırbucak, and a Yörük dialect in Damascus. Rural Turkish-speaking Turkmen are described as constituting 30% of Syrian Turkmen, while urban Turkmen have become Arabic-speaking and Arabised. The majority are Sunni Muslims; a small minority are Shia Muslims, particularly Alevis and Bektashis, and some practice Christianity.
