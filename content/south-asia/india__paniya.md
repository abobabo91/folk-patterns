## Overview

The Paniya, also known as Paniyar, Panyer, and Paniyan, are an ethnic group of India and the single largest Scheduled Tribe in Kerala. They mainly live in villages around the edges of forestland in Kerala’s Wayanad, Kozhikode, Kannur, and Malappuram districts, with other communities in Tamil Nadu west of the Nilgiri Hills and in Karnataka’s Kodagu District. Nearly 67,948 Paniya live in the Kerala hills, according to the cited demographic information. Their livelihood has historically depended on agriculture and agricultural labour. The Paniya language belongs to the Dravidian family and is closely related to Malayalam. Their folk-cultural profile includes village settlements, distinctive clothing, local religious practices, funeral rites, and the continued use of the Paniya language in domestic and religious settings.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Paniya men wear a lengthy cloth wrapped around the waist, known as a *mundu*. A smaller mundu is slung over the shoulders to cover the body. Paniya women, or *panichi*, wear a long cloth, with a smaller cloth positioned above the breast area and around the armpits. Women also wear a red or black *aratti* scarf around the waist.

### Architecture

Paniya settlements are villages known as *padis*. A padi consists of a few huts, called *pire* or *chala*, arranged with courtyards. Each hut settlement contains 5 to 15 families.

## Festivals & rituals

Valliyoorkkavu, described as a temple and the centre of the bonding contracts, is associated with prominent Paniya festivals. These festivals can take place in February, March, and April. The temple is associated with the regional mother goddess and is located near Mananthavady. The modern Paniya practise a variety of faiths, including Hinduism with local traditional beliefs, while a minority follow Christianity.

The Paniyas bury their dead in formal funeral rites. Burial usually takes place close to the padi and is followed by a seven-day mourning period observed by family members.

## Language & religion

Paniya is a Dravidian language and one of the Malayalamoid languages spoken in India. It is also known as Pania, Paniyan, and Panyah. According to the 1981 Census, there were 63,827 speakers, including 56,952 in Kerala, 6,393 in Tamil Nadu, and 482 in Karnataka. The language is most closely related to Malayalam, Kadar, Ravula, and other Dravidian languages. Paniya is used at home and during religious ceremonies. Some Paniyas also use Malayalam, Tamil, or Kannada.

Writing systems vary by location: Paniya in Karnataka use the Kannada script, those in Kerala use the Malayalam script, and those in Tamil Nadu use the Tamil script. Religious practice includes Hinduism with local traditional beliefs and minority Christianity. Chief deities named in the sources include Kuttichathan, Kattu-bhagavathi or Kaali of the forest, Mariyamma, and Ayyappan. The worship of the banyan tree is significant, and banyan trees are considered sacred.
