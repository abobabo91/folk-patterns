## Overview

The Adi are an indigenous ethnic group primarily inhabiting the Indian state of Arunachal Pradesh, with smaller populations in neighboring regions. They identify the Adi as a people associated with Arunachal Pradesh and list several notable individuals, including Mamang Dai, Talom Rukbo, and Gegong Apang.

## Music & performance

They mention the BBC television program *Tribe*, which included an episode on the Adi and stated that explorer Bruce Parry lived among them for a month as an honorary tribesman, having been “adopted” by a village gam.

## Festivals & rituals

They identify Talom Rukbo as a spiritualist and founder of the Donyi Polo Movement, but give no further account of its practices or ceremonies.

## Oral tradition & literature

They list *Folk culture and oral literature from north-east India*, edited by Tamo Mibang and S. K. Chaudhuri, as a reference, and identify Mamang Dai as a poet. The supplied material does not provide details of her writing or of Adi oral-literary traditions.

## Language & religion

The supplied sources provide an article titled “Adi language” and list works concerning the Adi language, including *An introduction to Adi language* by Tamo Mibang and P. T. Abraham and a doctoral dissertation titled “Morphology of the Adi language of Arunachal Pradesh” by C. Lalrempuii. The sources identify Talom Rukbo as the founder of the Donyi Polo Movement.
