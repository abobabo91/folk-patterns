## Overview

The Kalbelia are a snake charming tribe from the Thar Desert in Rajasthan, India. They live a nomadic life and belong to the scheduled tribes, with the largest cluster in Pali district and significant groups in Ajmer, Chittorgarh and Udaipur districts. Kalbelias are divided into the Daliwal and Mewara groups. Their traditional occupation was catching snakes and trading snake venom, while dance and song became major sources of income after the Wildlife Act of 1972 pushed them out of traditional snake handling. Their dances and songs are markers of identity and creative adaptations to changing socio-economic conditions. They are also known as Sapera, Jogira, Gattiwala and Poogiwara.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Angrakhi* — An upper-body cloth worn by Kalbelia dancers. It forms part of a costume in mixed red and black hues with embroidery.

*Odhani* — A headcloth worn by Kalbelia dancers as part of their performance clothing.

*Lehenga* — The lower-body cloth worn by Kalbelia dancers. It is described as embroidered and made in mixed red and black hues.

**Mirrored and silver-thread embroidery** — Kalbelia garments are richly embroidered with small mirrors and silver threads.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no specific textile motifs.

### Clothing & dress

Kalbelia women performing the dance wear flowing black skirts and clothing in mixed red and black hues. Their costume includes an *angrakhi*, an upper-body cloth; an *odhani*, a headcloth; and a *lehenga*, the lower-body cloth. These garments are embroidered, and the clothing is described as resembling the movements and appearance of serpents through its relationship to Kalbelia dance. Dancers also wear jewelry and garments richly embroidered with small mirrors and silver threads.

### Architecture

Kalbelias traditionally live in makeshift camps called *deras*. Their deras are moved from one place to another along a circuitous route repeated over time.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do mention cane baskets, in which Kalbelia men traditionally carried cobras from door to door in villages.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Kalbelia dancers wear jewelry and garments richly embroidered with small mirrors and silver threads. The dancers are tattooed in traditional designs.

## Music & performance

Kalbelia men traditionally provide the musical accompaniment for the dance. The instruments named in the sources are the *pungi*, a woodwind instrument traditionally played to capture snakes; the *dufli*; the *been*; the *khanjari*, a percussion instrument; the *morchang*; the *khuralio*; and the *dholak*. These instruments create the rhythm on which the dancers perform, and the rhythm becomes faster as the performance progresses.

Kalbelia songs are based on stories from folklore and mythology. The Kalbelias are known for composing lyrics spontaneously and improvising songs during performances. The songs and dances belong to an oral tradition handed down through generations without texts or training manuals. Special dances are performed during Holi. Performance opportunities are sporadic and depend on tourism, which is season specific; performing arts are now a major source of income for the community.

## Dance & theatre

The **Kalbelia dance** is a folk dance of Rajasthan and an integral part of Kalbelia culture. It is performed by women and men, with women dancers wearing flowing black skirts and twirling in movements that replicate those of a serpent. The dance is a celebration, a source of pride, and a marker of Kalbelia identity. Male participants play instruments while the women dance.

The dance represents the creative adaptation of a community of snake charmers to changing socio-economic conditions and to their role in rural Rajasthani society. Special dances are performed during Holi.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources mention Holi as a context for special Kalbelia dances.

Kalbelias are Hindus dedicated to the Nath tradition, described as a Shaiva sub-tradition. Their spiritual life centers on Lord Shiva and the sacred Nāgas. They worship deities such as Manasa and observe Naga Panchami, described as a day dedicated to honoring the cobra as a divine entity. Kalbelias bury their dead rather than cremating them and place an idol of Lord Shiva’s Nandi Bull over the graves.

## Foodways

They state that Kalbelias traditionally rear snakes, dogs, hens, horses, donkeys, pigs and goats.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kalbelia songs are based on stories taken from folklore and mythology. Kalbelias have a reputation for composing lyrics spontaneously and improvising songs during performances. These songs and dances form an oral tradition passed down through generations, without texts or training manuals.

## Language & religion

They describe the Kalbelias as Hindus and dedicated followers of the Nath tradition, a Shaiva sub-tradition. Their spiritual life centers on Lord Shiva and the sacred Nāgas. They worship deities such as Manasa, observe Naga Panchami, revere the cobra, and advocate the non-killing of such reptiles. They bury their dead and place an idol of Lord Shiva’s Nandi Bull over the graves.
