## Overview

Meo Rajputs are a Muslim ethnic group originating from the Mewat region of north-western India. Mewat consists of the Nuh district of Haryana, adjoining parts of the Alwar and Bharatpur districts of Rajasthan, and parts of western Uttar Pradesh. The community is also associated with Pakistan, where the 2023 census recorded around 1.1 million Mewati speakers, virtually all in Punjab; the estimated population of Meos is over 2 million. Meos speak Mewati, an Indo-Aryan language, although Urdu and Hindi have become dominant in some areas. They matter to folk-culture studies because their Islamic religious identity is intertwined with cultural connections to neighbouring Hindu communities and with an ethnic structure rooted in Hindu caste society.

## Festivals & rituals

Meos did not migrate collectively to Pakistan during the Partition of India, although some gotras relocated individually. During 1947, Meos were displaced from Alwar and Bharatpur States, and there was significant loss of life in intercommunal violence. In 1947, Mahatma Gandhi visited Ghasera, a village in the present-day Nuh district, to urge Muslims there not to leave; the people of Ghasera still celebrate Mewat Day.

Meo marriage traditionally involved both *nikah* and *saptapadi*, although *saptapadi* has mostly been abandoned with the advent of Islam. Meos generally do not marry within their own gotras, despite Islam permitting marriage with cousins. They generally do not follow Muslim inheritance law; custom permits a younger cousin to marry the widow of the deceased through a simple *Nikah* ceremony.

## Language & religion

Mewati is an Indo-Aryan language spoken by Meos, although Meos in some areas have adopted Urdu and Hindi because of the dominance of those languages. The term Mewati can also function as a general demonym for someone from Mewat and is therefore not always an ethnic classification.

Meos profess Islam. The sources describe the roots of their ethnic structure as lying in Hindu caste society and state that Meos share much of their culture with Hindu counterparts in neighbouring areas of Haryana and Rajasthan. Meos embraced Islam primarily through the influence of the Sufi saint Ghazi Saiyyad Salar Masud, nephew of Mahmud Ghaznavi. Other Sufi figures named in the source include Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti, Hazrat Nizamuddin Aulia, and Miran Sayyed Husain Khang Sawar.
