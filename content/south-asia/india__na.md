## Overview

The Nah people are a small sub-tribe of the Tagin living in the Taksing area of Nacho circle, in Upper Subansiri District, Arunachal Pradesh. As of 2000, their tribal population stood at 1,500. They speak the Na dialect, classified under the Tagin language and belonging to the Sino-Tibetan language family; they also use Hindi or English. Their literacy rate is 30%. The Nah are associated with permanent stone houses, terraced cultivation, wool clothing, yak and sheep breeding, Tibetan Buddhism, and pre-Buddhist Shaman practices. They claim to be a separate group while acknowledging common ancestry with the Tagin.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources state that the Nah wear clothes made of spun wool. They do not distinguish men's and women's clothing, ceremonial dress, head coverings, belts, footwear, or named garments.

### Architecture

The Nah build permanent houses made of stone.

## Music & performance

The sources mention religious occasions involving Buddhist Lamas and traditional Shamans, known in the native tongue as Nyibu.

## Festivals & rituals

The Nah are adherents of Tibetan Buddhism and are also influenced by pre-Buddhist Shaman practices. Buddhist Lamas and traditional Shamans, known as Nyibu in the native tongue, are employed for religious occasions.

## Foodways

The sources state that Nah villages have terraced fields where maize, millet, and barley are grown. They also state that the Nah breed domestic animals including yaks and sheep. No named dishes, cooking methods, beverages, dietary rules, or ceremonial foods are documented.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that the Nah speak the Na dialect and describe historical relations with Tibetans, including migration, trade, mediation by the Nishi, and a 1906 incident involving a massacre.

## Language & religion

The Na dialect is described as a member of the Sino-Tibetan language family and is classified under the Tagin language. The Nah also use Hindi or English, and the population's literacy rate is 30%. The sources describe Tibetan Buddhism alongside pre-Buddhist Shaman practices. Traditional Shamans are called Nyibu in the native tongue, while Buddhist Lamas and Nyibu conduct religious occasions. A separate article describes Na, also called Narua or Mosuo, as a language of the Naish subbranch of the Naic group of the Sino-Tibetan languages; it records Yongning Na and Lataddi Narua as varieties and describes Narua's default agent–object–verb word order.
