## Overview

The Irula, also known as Iruliga, are a Dravidian ethnic group living in the Indian states of Tamil Nadu and parts of Kerala and Karnataka. Their population in this region is estimated at around 200,000 people, with 189,621 in Tamil Nadu, 23,721 in Kerala and 10,259 in Karnataka. They are mainly concentrated in northern Tamil Nadu, in an area extending from Krishnagiri and Dharmapuri districts in the west to Ariyalur and Cuddalore districts in the south and Tiruvallur district in the north. Smaller populations live in Coimbatore and Nilgiris districts. In Kerala they are found in Palakkad district, while in Karnataka they are concentrated in Ramanagara, Chamarajanagara and Bangalore districts. Their language belongs to the Dravidian language family, and their folk-cultural record includes oral riddles, religious traditions, snake and rat catching, honey collection and a traditional method of fumigating grain-destroying rats.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention a traditional earthen pot fumigation method used by Irula men to combat rats that destroy grain on Tamil Nadu-area farms. Smoke is blown through the mouth, and the method is associated in the source with severe respiratory and heart problems.

## Festivals & rituals

The Irula people practice a form of Animism and primarily believe in a goddess named Inga, who lives with her six sisters. The sisters are worshipped in the form of stones under trees. Irula tradition holds that these seven sisters created the first Irula man and woman, and this traditional concept of seven goddesses was Sanskritised as Sapta Matrika.

The Irula also believe in ancestral spirits. Kannipe, described as spirits of virgin girls, can possess anyone; these spirits are worshipped as guardian deities of villages by Irula tribals. Evil spirits known as Pe are believed to possess and haunt people. To keep these spirits away, a rooster is sacrificed and offered to them.

Many Irula people worship Hindu gods and take part in Hindu festivals. Some Irulas have been converted to Christianity.

## Foodways

They state that honey collection has traditionally been one of the main occupations of the Irulas. Fishing and cattle farm are also described as major occupations, while some Irulas work as labourers in fields during sowing and harvesting seasons or in rice mills.

## Oral tradition & literature

Although the majority of the Irula are described as illiterate, they possess an oral tradition, a significant part of which consists of riddles.

## Language & religion

The Irula speak Irula, a Dravidian language closely related to Tamil. People of Irula ethnicity are called Irular. Those living in Karnataka are named Iruligas. The name Irula is derived from iruḷ, meaning “black,” and -a/iga, meaning “people.”

Religious life combines the worship of Inga and her six sisters, belief in ancestral spirits, the village guardianship of Kannipe spirits and fear of Pe, described as evil spirits. Many Irula people also worship Hindu gods and participate in Hindu festivals, while some have converted to Christianity.
