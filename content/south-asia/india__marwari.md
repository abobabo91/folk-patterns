## Overview

The Marwari or Marwadi are an Indo-Aryan ethno-linguistic group originating in the Marwar region of Rajasthan, India. The term historically referred to the former princely state of Marwar, also called the Jodhpur region of southwest Rajasthan. Marwaris live throughout India and have sizeable populations in Pakistan and Nepal. The community has historically been associated with migration, trade, banking and finance. Marwari is a Western Indo-Aryan language within the Indo-Iranian subdivision of the Indo-European languages and belongs to the broader Rajasthani language family. The community’s historical importance is especially visible in its commercial networks, its role in financing Rajput kingdoms, and its later development of major business groups during the British period and after Indian independence.

## Oral tradition & literature

Marwari literature is taught as Rajasthani until secondary level, and Marwaris have traditionally referred to their language as *Marrubhasha*.

## Language & religion

Marwari is a Western Indo-Aryan language belonging to the Indo-Iranian subdivision of the Indo-European languages. It is closely related to Rajasthani and forms part of the broader Rajasthani language family alongside varieties such as Dhundhari, Shekhawati and Mewari. Marwari is spoken primarily in Rajasthan, including Jodhpur, Pali, Jaisalmer, Barmer, Nagaur and Bikaner, as well as in Gujarat, Haryana, eastern Pakistan, migrant communities in Nepal and among Indians in Kenya. The language has around 7.9 million speakers in India according to the 2001 census. It is generally written in Devanagari; it was historically written in Mahajani and is also written in Perso-Arabic script in Pakistan. Mahajani is a Laṇḍā script associated primarily with mercantile purposes, including financial ledgers, records and accounts.
