## Overview

The Juang are an Austroasiatic ethnic group found only in the Gonsaika hills of Keonjhar district of Odisha, India. Some Juangs migrated to neighbouring plains of Dhenkanal district during the Bhuiyan revolt in the late 19th century. Their language belongs to the Munda family of the Austroasiatic languages, and they are classified as a Scheduled Tribe by the Indian government. The 2011 census showed their population to be around 50,000. Juang tradition identifies the Gonasika Hills, near Keonjhar and at the source of the Baitarani River, as the tribe’s place of origin. Their documented folk-cultural practices include basket-weaving, leaf clothing, festivals, and vigorous dances that imitate birds and other animals.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Formerly, the Juang were also known as *Patuas*, literally “leaf-wearers.” Traditionally, Juang women wore girdles of leaves, while men wore a small loincloth.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Basket-weaving is the only documented Juang material practice in the sources. After the British declared their forests reserves, the Juangs were forced to seek alternative forms of sustenance. They became skilled at basket-weaving, and exchanged baskets with village traders for salt, oil, food, and money.

## Dance & theatre

Juang traditional folk dance included vigorous dances that mimicked birds and other animals.

## Festivals & rituals

The Juang celebrate festivals including *Pusha Purnima*, *Amba Nuakhia*, *Pirha Puja*, *Akhaya Trutiya*, *Asarhi*, and *Gahma*.

## Foodways

The Juang formerly exchanged baskets with village traders for salt, oil, and food.

## Oral tradition & literature

Juang tradition claims that the tribe originated in the Gonasika Hills, near Keonjhar, at the source of the Baitarani River.

## Language & religion

Juang is a Munda language of the Austroasiatic language family and is spoken primarily by the Juang people of Odisha state, eastern India. It is roughly divided into Hill and Plains varieties. Hill Juang is associated with the Gonasika Hills in Keonjhar district and the Pallara Hills; Plains Juang is spoken in about 147 villages in southern Keonjhar district and eastern Dhenkanal district. The language is currently endangered and considered vulnerable, with roughly under 20,000 speakers remaining. Its writing system is Odia.
