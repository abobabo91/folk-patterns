## Overview

The Lom people, also known by non-Loms as Bosha or Posha, are an ethnic group originating from the Indian subcontinent. Their Lomavren language combines an Indo-Aryan substrate with Armenian. The group is dispersed and often mostly assimilated, making its exact number difficult to determine. Estimates suggest that only a few thousand live across Armenia and Georgia, while the Armenian Government's census reports 50 living in the former. Concentrations are found in Yerevan and Gyumri, and communities also live in Tbilisi, Kutaisi, Akhalkalaki and Akhaltsikhe. In Turkey, Loms or Poshas mostly live in Artvin, Rize, Ardahan and Kars.

## Language & religion

The Lom language, or Lomavren, is a mixed language combining an Indo-Aryan substrate with Armenian. The sources also record the names Lomlar in Turkish, Poşa in Turkish, Բոշա in Armenian, ბოშა in Georgian and Боша in Russian. Some Bosha in Armenia have adopted Armenian and partially assimilated with the larger Armenian population. In Turkey, the Loms or Poshas adopted Islam in the 19th century and assimilated into Turkish culture; they identify themselves as Meskhetian Turks, hide their Lom origins and take Armenian words from contact with the Hemshin.
