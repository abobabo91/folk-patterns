## Overview

The Munda live mainly in eastern India, with communities elsewhere in South Asia. Their culture includes seasonal festivals, clan rules, dance, song, tales, and ritual stones. Mundari is their native language. Marriage includes bride price, feasting, drinks, and dance.

## Material culture

Recorded material culture includes cotton clothing, bamboo weapons, carved bone and stone objects, metal containers, and silver-alloy bowls made from rupee coins.

## Music & performance

Munda music and performance include folk songs, dances, tales, and the principal instrument called naqareh.

- **Principal musical instrument** (*naqareh*) — The naqareh is identified as a principal Munda musical instrument.
- **Dance** (*susun*) — Susun is the Munda term for dance.
- **Song** (*durang*) — Durang is the Munda term for song.
- **Winter Mage song** (*Mundari Mage*) — The winter Mundari Mage song has a rhythm similar to the Nagpuri Fagua song.

## Dance & theatre

Jadur, Karam Susun, and Mage Susun are named Munda folk dances performed at social events, festivals, and marriages.

- **Jadur** — Jadur is a named Munda folk dance.
- **Karam dance** (*Karam Susun*) — Karam Susun is a named Munda folk dance.
- **Mage dance** (*Mage Susun*) — Mage Susun is a named Munda folk dance.

## Festivals & rituals

Munda ritual life centers on seasonal festivals, life ceremonies, marriage rules, and the erection of inscribed stones.

- **Mage festival** (*Mage Parab*) — Mage Parab is a seasonal agricultural festival.
- **Karam festival** (*Karam*) — Karam is a seasonal agricultural festival.
- **Spring festival** (*Baha parab*) — Baha parab is a seasonal agricultural festival.
- **Stone erection** (*Patthalgari*) — Patthalgari is the tradition of erecting stones at graves or village entrances.
- **Marriage beginning** (*Sagai*) — A Munda marriage ceremony begins with Sagai.

## Oral tradition & literature

Munda oral culture includes abundant folk literature, songs, and tales, while written literature before the nineteenth century was lacking.

- **Munda encyclopedia** (*Encyclopaedia Mundarica*) — John-Baptist Hoffmann and Menas Orea published the 15-volume Encyclopaedia Mundarica.
- **Munda country study** (*The Mundas and Their Country*) — S. C. Roy published The Mundas and Their Country in 1912.
- **Rituals and customs book** (*Adidharam*) — Adidharam describes Munda rituals and customs in Mundari with a Hindi translation.

## Language & religion

Mundari is the Munda native language, and Singbonga is the Munda deity.

- **Mundari language** (*Mundari*) — Mundari belongs to the Munda subgroup of the Austroasiatic language family.
- **Munda deity** (*Singbonga*) — Singbonga is the Munda deity.
- **Marriage vermilion rite** (*Sindurdaan*) — Sindurdaan is described as a Hindu element borrowed into Munda marriage tradition.
- **North Munda branch** (*North Munda*) — One classification divides Munda languages into North Munda and South Munda branches.
- **South Munda branch** (*South Munda*) — One classification divides Munda languages into North Munda and South Munda branches.
