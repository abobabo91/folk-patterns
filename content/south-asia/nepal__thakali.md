## Overview

The Thakali are an ethnolinguistic Tibeto-Burman ethnic group of Nepal. Their traditional area, called Thak-sat-se or the Thak Khola region, lies in the Kali Gandaki River valley in Mustang District, Gandaki Province, western Nepal. The 2001 census recorded around 12,973 Thakali, while the 2011 Nepal census recorded 13,215. Thakali communities are also distributed across several districts, including Manang, Myagdi, Kaski, Baglung, Eastern Rukum, Kathmandu, Jumla, Lalitpur, Parbat, Pyuthan and Rupandehi. Four groups consider themselves Thakali: Teen Gauley, Marphali, Chaar Jaat or “four castes,” and Thakali. Their customs, culture, dress and festivals differ slightly among these groups.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources used mention Thakali dress only as one aspect of culture that differs slightly among the four groups who consider themselves Thakali.

## Music & performance

The madal, khaprang and thamken are identified as the main musical instruments of the Thakali.

The sources identify Dhnom as the title of the Thakali priest who performs the work of the local shaman. No further information is provided about the relationship between musical performance and the work of the Dhnom, or about particular song genres, dances, ensembles or performance contexts.

## Festivals & rituals

The Lhafewa, also called Barha Barse Kumbha Mela, is listed as a major Thakali festival. Toranlha is associated with ancestral worship, while Falo is identified as Kumar Yatra.

Dhnom is the title of the Thakali priest who performs the work of the local shaman.

The four groups who consider themselves Thakali are Teen Gauley, Marphali, Chaar Jaat or “four castes,” and Thakali. The sources state that the customs, culture, dress and festivals of each differ slightly. Teen Gauley refers to Thakali from Thini, Syang and Chimang villages. Marphali refers to Thakali from Marpha village and is further subclassified as Hirachan, Pannachan, Jwarchan and Lalchan. Chaar Jaat comprises Sherchan, Tulachan, Bhattachan and Gauchan. Thakali also refers to those from Southern Mustang, Tukuche and Jomsom.

## Language & religion

Thakali is a Sino-Tibetan language of Nepal spoken by the Thakali people, mainly in Myagdi and Mustang Districts. Its dialects have limited mutual intelligibility. The language is spoken in the middle of the Kali Gandaki River valley and in the upper part of the Kali Gandaki Gorge, also known as Thak Khola, in Mustang District, Gandaki Province. The area is bounded by Annapurna Himal on one side and Dhawalagiri Himal on the other, with Tatopani village in the south and Jomsom in the north.

The sources list Barhagaule, Marpha, Panchgaunle, Puntan Thakali, Syang, Tamhang Thakali, Thaksaatsaye, Thaksatsae, Thaksya, Tukuche and Yhulkasom as other names or dialect names. Ethnologue lists Tukuche, Marpha and Syang as dialects. Tukuche is also called Tamhang Thakali, Thaksaatsaye or Thaksatsae; Marpha is also called Puntan Thakali; and Syang is also called Yhulkasom.
