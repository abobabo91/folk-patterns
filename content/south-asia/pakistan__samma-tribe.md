## Overview

The Samma are a Sindhi tribe present in Sindh, Pakistan and Gujarat, India. Offshoots in Gujarat include the Sandhai Muslims, Jadejas and Chudasamas. The sources describe Samma history in relation to the Jats, Sumras, local Rajput tribes, Islamic conversion, and the Samma dynasty, which ruled over Sindh from 1336 to 1524.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Wikipedia source records several traditions about Samma origins. Some link the Sammas’ ancestry with Ikrima ibn Amr, a companion of Islamic prophet Muhammad; other accounts connect their arrival in Sindh with Muhammad ibn al-Qasim. The Sammas are also described as descendants of Sam ibn Umar ibn Hashim ibn Abu Lahab, or as deriving their lineage from the legendary Iranian king Jamshid.

## Language & religion

The Samma are identified as a Sindhi tribe. According to the Wikipedia source, Sarah Ansari described the Sammas and Sumras as local Rajput tribes whose chiefs converted to Islam and followed Suhrawardi Sufi saints with their base at Uch and Multan.
