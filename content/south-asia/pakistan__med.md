## Overview

The Med are an indigenous fishermen and historically seafaring community from the coastal areas of Makran in Balochistan, Pakistan. A majority of the coastal population of Makran consists of Meds. Their origins are described as ambiguous, but they are identified as non-Baloch and as predating Baloch migration into Makran. They speak medī, a dialect of the Balochi language. Historically, Meds were associated with seafaring, piracy, and, during some periods, pastoral life. After the 11th century, they came to live in their present homeland in Las Bela and Makran. In modern usage, *med* has largely become an occupational term associated with fishery rather than an exclusively ethnic designation.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They state that Meds tend to live in their own congested quarters known as *medānī pāṛa*.

## Music & performance

They state that the Kummāṛī, a well-known Med group in Gwadar, engages solely in seamanship.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that anyone from the coast who is engaged in fishing and follows Sunni Islam is called a *med*. Meds who are Zikris are identified as Baloch.

## Foodways

The historical discussion identifies the Med with the Ichthyophagi, literally “fish-eaters,” a name associated with inhabitants of the coast of Makran in the 4th century BCE.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Med appear in early Muslim historiography as seafarers. Muslim historians al-Idrisi and Ibn Hauqal described Meds as nomads living in a vast region between the Indus river and Makran.

## Language & religion

The Med speak medī, a dialect of the Balochi language. In modern usage, the term *med* is associated primarily with fishery: anyone from the coast engaged in fishing and following Sunni Islam is called a med. Meds who are Zikris are identified as Baloch. The sources also state that fishermen from other tribes, including Bizenjo and Mengal, are known as Meds.
