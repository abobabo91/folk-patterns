## Overview

The Boro, also called Bodo, are a Tibeto-Burman-speaking ethnolinguistic group native to Assam in India. They belong to the wider Bodo–Kachari family and are spread across northeastern India, with their main concentration in the Bodoland Territorial Region of Assam; they also inhabit other districts of Assam and Meghalaya. Around 1.45 million Bodos live in Assam, constituting 4.53% of the state population. They are mostly settled farmers and have traditional irrigation systems known as *dong*. The Boro language belongs to the Boro–Garo group of the Tibeto-Burman branch of the Sino-Tibetan family and is an official language of Assam and the Bodoland Territorial Region. Boro culture includes collective cultivation, Bathou religious practice, Hindu and Christian communities, clan-based social identities, and distinctive festivals and rituals.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They state that Boros historically lived near the forested regions of the lower Himalayan foothills and used forest lands for shifting cultivation.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention traditional irrigation, *dong*, and the use of tools in the general context of cultivation.

## Music & performance

The sources used provide very little information about Boro music. The clan list identifies **Bingiari** as a clan traditionally associated with musical instruments, but no instruments are named. The sources also state that **Proneeta Swargiary** won *Dance India Dance (season 5)*, without describing the performance tradition involved.

Religious ceremonies provide the clearest documented performance contexts. **Hoom Jaygya** is described as a Hindu worship ceremony conducted through fire. A clean surface near a home or courtyard is prepared, and offerings usually include a betel nut called *goi*, a betel leaf called *pathwi* or *bathwi*, rice, milk, and sugar. **Kherai Puja**, in which an altar is placed in a rice field, is identified as the most important festival of the Boros.

## Dance & theatre

They mention **Proneeta Swargiary** as the winner of *Dance India Dance (season 5)*, but give no ethnographic information about the dance she performed.

## Festivals & rituals

Boro religious life traditionally centred on **Bathouism**, the worship of a supreme God known as *Obonglaoree*. The *shijou* tree is taken as the symbol of Bathou and is worshiped; it is also described as the supreme god. The name Bathou is explained through the Boro terms *Ba*, meaning five, and *thou*, meaning deep. The number five is significant because Boros believe in five mighty elements of God: land, water, air, fire, and ether.

According to the Bathou account of creation, before the universe existed there was a great void in which the formless supreme being *Aham Guru*, also called *Anan Binan Gosai* or *Obonglaoree*, existed. Aham Guru later desired to live in flesh and blood, descended into the void with human characteristics, and created the universe.

**Kherai Puja** is identified as the most important Boro festival. Its ritual includes placing an altar in a rice field. The sources also describe **Hoom Jaygya**, a Hindu fire-worship ceremony performed on a clean surface near a home or courtyard. Offerings commonly include *goi*, *pathwi* or *bathwi*, rice, milk, and sugar.

Boros have also converted to Hinduism and Christianity. Most Boro Hindus do not practise caste and dowry customs and follow rules called **Brahma Dharma**. Christianity is followed by around 10% of Boros and is predominantly Baptist.

## Foodways

Rice is named among the offerings used in **Hoom Jaygya**, together with milk and sugar.

The sources do state that Boros are mostly settled farmers and historically practised shifting cultivation for self-sustenance. Landholding, sowing, harvesting, irrigation, and hunting were performed collectively. They also controlled forest products and participated as intermediaries in the trade of forest-based and other goods between the plains and hills.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that the history of the Boro people can be explained through folk traditions. According to **Suniti Kumar Chatterjee**, Boros are mythologically described as the offspring of the son of Vishnu, *Baraha*, and Mother-Earth, *Basumati*; during the Epic period they were termed *Kiratas*.

They mention **Boro Folktales**, a work by **Pompi Basumatary** published in *Indian Literature* in 2025. The sources also identify **Sidney Endle** as the author of *The Kacharis*, published in 1911, and note that his evangelical work formed part of a later stream of Boro articulation.

Boro identity formation involved organisations, publicists, and writers. **Kalicharan Brahma** established a monotheistic faith called **Brahma-ism** and promoted a new Bodo identity. The sources also mention **Rupnath Brahma**, **Jadunath Khakhlari**, and other Boro and Kachari public figures in debates about identity, language, religion, and historical political legacy.

## Language & religion

The Boro language is a member of the Sino-Tibetan language family and belongs to the Boro–Garo group of the Tibeto-Burman branch. It is an official language of Assam and the Bodoland Territorial Region of India, and one of the twenty-two languages listed in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution of India. Over two-thirds of the people are bilingual and speak Assamese as a second language.

*Boro* is the community’s self-designation or autonym. The source explains it through *Bara-fisa*, meaning “son of Bara,” with *Bara* meaning “man” or “male member” of the group. In the cognate language Kokborok, *Borok* means man. In the wider sense, *Boro* means a human being, though not specifically a female member of the family, in languages used by Bodo–Kachari peoples.

Bathouism remains the traditional religion and is often classified as Hindu in censuses because it has no official recognition under the Indian constitution. Hinduism, including **Hoom Jaygya**, **Kherai Puja**, and **Brahma Dharma**, is also practised. Christianity is followed by around 10% of Boros, mainly through the **Boro Baptist Convention** and **Boro Baptist Church Association**.
