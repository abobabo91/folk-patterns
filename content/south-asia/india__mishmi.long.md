## Overview

The Mishmi are an ethnic group of Tibet and Arunachal Pradesh, India, in the area known as the Mishmi Hills. They consist of four tribes: Idu Mishmi, also called Idu Lhoba; the Digaro tribe, also called Taraon or Darang Deng; Miju Mishmi, also called Kaman Deng; and the Deng Mishmi. In Arunachal Pradesh they are concentrated in Upper and Lower Dibang Valley, Lohit and Anjaw Districts, bordering southern Tibet. The sources state that there are around 30,000 of them in Arunachal Pradesh. Mishmi languages are several Sino-Tibetan languages that do not form a single branch or genetic grouping. The sources document the group through its regional distribution, folklore, clothing, tools and other museum objects.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum catalogue records objects made from iron, wood, rattan, silver, bamboo, cane, plaited basketry, steel, bone and metal. These include a dao, a wooden knife, a silver knife sheath with a Chinese seal attached by a cord, a sword with a straight single-edged blade, and a knife made of metal and bone. A bow is made of wood with cane bindings and a cane string; a crossbow has a bone trigger and animal hair string. Quivers are made of bamboo with cane caps, plaited basketry containers and cane straps. Three bamboo arrows have metal heads.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources used do not document an annual festival calendar, seasonal celebrations or life-cycle rites. They record a creation account in which, long ago, the world was a vast flood; Deraogao created the earth, and the gold-people Ajiani married the eagle. The Deng people are described as the offspring of this union, and Ajiani is identified as their ancestor in Deng folklore.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources document a Deng creation myth. In the account, Deraogao created the earth after a primordial flood, while Ajiani, one of the gold-people, married the eagle; their descendants became the Deng people. The sources also state that Ajiani is regarded as an ancestor in Deng folklore. No epic poetry, proverbs, riddles, contemporary literary revival or named storytelling performance is documented in the supplied material. *Mishmi Folk Tales of Lohit Valley*, edited by Obang Tayeng, appears in the Wikipedia article’s further reading.

## Language & religion

Mishmi languages are described as several Sino-Tibetan languages spoken by the Mishmi people of Tibet, China and Arunachal Pradesh, India. They are not a single branch or genetic grouping, but a cultural grouping of various Sino-Tibetan languages that are not closely related to each other. The sources name Idu Lhoba, Yidu Lhoba, Taraon, Darang Deng, Kaman Deng, Geman Deng, Taraung, Tayin and Tain in connection with Mishmi groups and languages. The sources used do not provide a script, a detailed religious system or a description of current religious practice.
