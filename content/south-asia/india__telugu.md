## Overview

Telugus, also known by the ethnonym Āndhras, are a Dravidian ethnic group native to Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Yanam district of Puducherry. They are also found in Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal and Maharashtra, as well as across the United States, Australia, Malaysia, Mauritius, Myanmar, South Africa and other countries. Telugu is a South-Central Dravidian language and the most widely spoken member of the Dravidian language family. The sources give different population figures, including 96 million speakers in India and 110 million worldwide, and describe Telugu as the fourth most spoken language in India and the fourteenth most spoken native language in the world. Telugu culture is represented by distinctive cuisine, literature, architecture, classical dance, shadow puppetry and cinema.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources name several forms of Telugu dress. Men’s clothing includes *Uttareeyam*, also called Uttariya or Pai Pancha, with Angvastram or veil; *Pancha*, identified as a dhoti; *Jubba*, identified as a kurta and described as the top portion; and *Lungi*, described as casual dress. Women’s clothing includes *Langa voni*, identified as a half sari, and *Cheera*, identified as a sari.

### Architecture

Telugu architectural history includes the Amaravati School of Art, an ancient Indian style that evolved in Amaravati, then known as Dhānyakaṭaka, from the 2nd century BCE to the end of the 3rd century CE. It is also called the Andhra School or Vengi School. Its sculpture influenced art in South India, Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia, and the Buddha image in its sculptures was later used as a prototype in different Buddhist countries. The Amaravati style is found at Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda and Chandavaram.

Kakatiya architecture developed a distinct style described as a fusion of Dravidian architecture and Nagara Bhumija styles. It used sandbox technology to construct Vimana, a horizontal stepped tower. Named examples include the Thousand Pillar Temple in Hanamkonda, Ramappa Temple in Palampet, Warangal Fort, Golconda Fort and Kota Gullu in Ghanpur. Ramappa Temple, also known as the Rudreswara temple, is located in Mulugu and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Music & performance

Telugu culture is associated with Carnatic music, one of the two main subgenres of Indian classical music. The sources state that Telugu predominates in the evolution of Carnatic music and that Telugu is widely taught in music colleges focusing on the Carnatic tradition.

Telugu performing arts include Kuchipudi, Perini Sivatandavam, Burra Katha, Oggu Katha, Andhra Natyam, Vilasini Natyam and Nava Janardhanam. The sources describe Kuchipudi as a dance-drama performance originating from the eponymous village in Krishna district. It is one of the eight major Indian classical dances and has roots in the Hindu Sanskrit text Natya Shastra. It developed as a religious art associated with traveling bards, temples and spiritual beliefs.

The sources also describe *Tholu Bommalata* as a Telugu shadow puppetry tradition dating to the 3rd century BCE. They identify it as the ancestor of Wayang, an Indonesian art form.

## Dance & theatre

*Kuchipudi* is a Telugu dance-drama performance originating from a village of the same name in Krishna district. It is one of the eight major Indian classical dances and developed as a religious art linked to traveling bards, temples and spiritual beliefs. Its roots are connected with the Sanskrit text Natya Shastra.

Other named Telugu performing arts are Andhra Natyam, Vilasini Natyam, Nava Janardhanam, Perini Sivatandavam, Oggu Katha and Burra Katha.

*Tholu Bommalata* is a Telugu shadow puppetry tradition. The sources date it to the 3rd century BCE and describe it as the ancestor of Wayang.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources list festivals celebrated by Telugu people throughout the year. Bhogi, Makara Sankranti and Kanuma are celebrated in January. Maha Sivaratri occurs in February or March. Ugadi, identified as the Telugu New Year, occurs in March or April, followed by Sri Rama Navami nine days later. Bonalu is celebrated in Ashada masam, given as July or August. Hanuman Jayanti is listed for March, May or June.

Vaikunta Ekadasi occurs in December or January. Varalakshmi Vratam, Krishna Janmashtami and Vinayaka Chaviti are celebrated in August. Bathukamma is celebrated for nine days during Durga Navaratri. Dasara occurs in September or October. Atla Tadde falls on the third day in the bright half of the Ashviyuja month and occurs in September or October in the Gregorian calendar. Deepavali is also listed, with its date varying according to the Hindu calendar. Nagula Chavithi occurs in October or November.

The sources also list Christmas, Easter, Ramzan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Muharram and Vesak among festivals observed by minorities.

## Foodways

Telugu cuisine is generally described as tangy, hot and spicy. Rice is the central staple and is mixed with or served alongside other preparations. Ragi is popular in Rayalaseema and Palnadu. Andhra Pradesh is identified as a leading producer of red chili and rice in India, and the concentration of red chili production has contributed to the liberal use of spices.

Major culinary categories include *koora*, a curry, and *iguru*, a curry in which much of the moisture evaporates. *Vepudu* refers to fried preparations, while *Gujju* is a gravy based on tomato or coriander seed. *Pulusu* is a sour curry-like stew usually made with tamarind paste, and *pachi pulusu* is an unheated version made from mangoes or tamarind. *Pappu* dishes include toor dal and moong dal, often cooked with vegetables. *Mudda pappu* is plain toor dal cooked with salt. *Charu*, also called rasam, is a sour and spicy soup.

Pickles and condiments include *pachadi*, *ooragaya* and *podi*. Pachadi is a fresh chutney made from vegetables, greens or lentils, while ooragaya is prepared in large quantities and has a longer shelf life. *Avakaya* is a mango pickle made with mustard powder, red pepper powder, salt and sesame oil. Mixed rice dishes include biriyani, pulaos and *pulihora*. A complete vegetarian meal may include rice, ghee, pappu, sambar, chaaru, curries, appadam, chutney, pachadi, avakaya, yoghurt and a sweet.

## Oral tradition & literature

Telugu has an unbroken and diverse literary tradition of over a thousand years. The earliest Telugu literature is dated to the 11th century CE and includes Nannaya’s Andhra Mahabharatam. During the Kakatiya period, Telugu emerged as a literary medium through the writings of Nannaya, Tikkana, Eranna and Pothana, who are described as translators and poets of Hindu epics including Ramayana, Mahabharatha and Bhagavatha.

The Telugu language page describes Avadhānaṃ as a literary performance requiring immense memory power and extensive knowledge of literature and prosody. It originated among Telugu poets and was specially cultivated for over five centuries. Telugu literature experienced a golden age under the patronage of the Vijayanagara king-poet Krishnadevaraya.

## Language & religion

Telugu is a Dravidian language, specifically part of the South-Central Dravidian subgroup. It is primarily spoken in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, where it is an official language, and in Yanam district of Puducherry. It is also spoken in Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Andaman and Nicobar Islands and by Telugu diaspora communities.

The sources describe several historical stages: Pre-historic Telugu, Old Telugu, Middle Telugu and Modern Telugu. Telugu inscriptions and Telugu words appear in records from the 4th century BCE onward. The earliest long inscription entirely in Telugu is dated to 575 CE. Old Telugu script inscriptions have been found as far away as Indonesia and Myanmar. Telugu became a court language for several dynasties, including the Vishnukundina, Telugu Chodas, Eastern Chalukyas, Kakatiyas, Vijayanagara Empire and Qutb Shahis.

The sources mention Hindu, Buddhist and Muslim historical contexts, including Hindu epics, Buddhist references to Andhras, and festivals observed by minorities.
