## Overview

The Kadar, also spelled Kadan or Kadir, are a tribal or indigenous community in India and a designated Scheduled Tribe in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Kerala. Their traditional way of life has been based on hunting and gathering. They formerly stayed in the Annamalai Hills in the Western Ghats and later moved to other locations, including parts of Wynaad taluk, Palghat, Trichur, and Palakkad District. A 2001 census located Kadar tribals in Chhitoor taluk. Kadar is a Dravidian language closely related to Malayalam and spoken in Kerala and Tamil Nadu. The group is significant in this atlas because its documented economy connects forest gathering, trade, basket making, map weaving, and changing relations with the forest.

### Architecture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do record the collection of umbrella sticks for trade, as well as basket making and map weaving among occupations pursued in addition to traditional work.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that the Kadars practice Animism and Hinduism, but provide no further account of ritual observance.

## Foodways

They do record the collection of honey, wax, sago, arrowroot, cardamom, and ginger for trade with merchants from the plains.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources describe the Kadar language as critically endangered and at risk of disappearing forever because speakers are shifting to regional languages such as Tamil or Malayalam.

## Language & religion

Kadar is a Dravidian language of Kerala and Tamil Nadu, closely related to Malayalam. It is spoken by the Kadar people and is also written in scholarly literature as Kada, Kadan, and Kadir. Scholars have placed it close to Malayalam in the classification of Dravidian languages; some view it as an independent language, while others regard it as a dialect of Malayalam. The language is critically endangered because speakers are shifting to regional languages such as Tamil or Malayalam. The Kadars practice Animism and Hinduism.
