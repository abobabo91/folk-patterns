## Overview

The Apatani are an ethnic group living in the Ziro valley of Arunachal Pradesh, speaking an endangered Tani language. They are known for integrating their material life with their land through wet rice farming, sustainable forestry, and traditional village governance. Their material culture emphasizes plaited cane and bamboo crafts alongside wool-and-cotton weaving, and their calendar centres on two major festivals celebrating harvest and inter-village friendship.

## Material culture

Apatani material culture combines plaited fibre work for everyday and outdoor use with woven wool-and-cotton garments for ritual and status.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Apatani are known for intricate handloom designs, with wool and cotton woven pieces surviving in museum collections.

- **Supplementary-weft banded cloak cloth** — A wool cloak in yellow, brown and red with white horizontal bands woven in supplementary weft technique using undyed and indigo-dyed cotton.
- **Priest's woollen cloth and cloak** — Ceremonial cloth and cloak made of wool and cotton, held as priest's garments for ritual use.
- **Embroidered man's jacket** — A white cotton jacket embroidered on the back in blue, yellow, black, white and brown.
- **Cane and bamboo craft** — Plaited rattan, cane and vegetable fibre appear in headgear, belts, rings, quivers, sheaths and rain gear.

### Clothing & dress

Apatani dress divides between plaited fibre gear for work and outdoor life and woven garments for ritual and status.

- **Cane helmet** — Plaited headgear with a neck-piece and twisted fibre loop at the front.
- **Composite rain-shield** — A bamboo framework filled with double open-work plaited work and leaf lining for rain protection, fitted with head cover, pouch and carrying slings.
- **Rattan waist belt** — Red rattan belts with a tail plaited in undyed cane.
- **Priest's head-dress** — Wool and cotton headgear serving ritual and martial roles, worn by priests and warriors.
- **Vegetable fibre rain cape** — Outdoor garment worn with a carrying basket for protection against the valley's heavy rain.

### Architecture

The Apatani landscape is shaped by systematic land-use practices, but domestic house forms are not recorded.

- **Bulyañ** — Traditional village council that organizes Apatani social and agricultural life.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Apatani metalwork focuses on edged tools and weapons, always combined with plaited fibre, while ceramics consist of unfired clay models.

- **Dao** — A single-edged steel blade with wooden hilt sheathed in plaited vegetable fibre, carried in a plaited fibre-bound sheath.
- **Bamboo and iron bow** — A blackened bamboo bow with node at the grip and string of twisted vegetable fibre, used with composite arrows.
- **Bamboo quiver** — A quiver with plaited vegetable fibre cap and rattan sling, mottled brown and black.
- **Unfired clay mithan model** (*mithan*) — Light grey clay model of a bullock with ridge down the back and impressed decoration on horns and face.
- **Bamboo pipe-bowl** — Tobacco pipe with charred residue, the only recorded consumption object.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Apatani ornament is largely plaited cane and fibre rather than metal, worn by both men and women.

- **Plaited waist-rings** — Rings made from three pieces of cane plaited to form patterns, or double rings of cane covered with black and buff vegetable fibre.
- **Knee rings** — Rings of plaited vegetable fibre in natural colour worn by men.
- **Ear-rings** — Both plaited vegetable fibre and coiled yellow metal versions worn by men and women.
- **Women's nose-plugs** — Cylindrical wooden nose-plugs, charred, the collection's sole recorded women's facial ornament.

## Music & performance

Apatani musical instruments and genres are not recorded, though festivals feature named performance pieces.

- **Paku-Itu** — A cultural programme performed at the Dree agricultural festival.
- **Daminda** — A cultural programme performed at the Dree festival alongside other performances.

## Dance & theatre

The Piree dance is the sole named Apatani dance in records, performed as part of the agricultural festival.

- **Piree dance** (*Piree*) — A dance performed as part of the cultural programme of the Dree agricultural festival in July.

## Festivals & rituals

Two major festivals structure the Apatani calendar: Dree celebrates the harvest, while Myoko renews inter-village friendship bonds.

- **Dree** — The agricultural festival celebrated in July with prayers for bumper harvest and human prosperity, featuring Paku-Itu, Daminda and Piree dance.
- **Myoko** — Festival in mid-March commemorating inter-village friendship, with rituals beginning in the preceding month and lasting into April; the host village serves and distributes large quantities of food and drink.

## Foodways

Apatani food culture centres on wet rice cultivation worked without farm animals or machinery, supported by centuries of ecological knowledge.

- **Wet rice cultivation system** — Extensive scale rice farming of extremely high productivity worked entirely by hand, recognized by UNESCO for sustainability and unique ecological preservation.
- **Tobacco use** — Documented by a bamboo pipe-bowl containing charred tobacco, the only recorded consumption practice outside farming.
- **Myoko feast** — A large celebration during the Myoko festival in which the host village serves and distributes substantial quantities of food and drink.

## Oral tradition & literature

Apatani oral traditions are not recorded, but the group has developed a modern writing system and active language documentation initiatives.

- **Roman-script orthography** — An alphabet based on Roman script adopted by the Apatani after debate and formal approval by the Apatani Apex Council to standardize the written language.
- **Apatani Language Development Committee (ALDC)** (*ALDC*) — A body formed by the Apatani Cultural and Literary Society to recommend a standard script for the Apatani language.
- **Talking Apatani Dictionary** — A current language documentation initiative by LivingDictionaries to preserve the Apatani language.
- **Popi Sarmin Society revision project** — An ongoing initiative to revise and improve documentation of the Apatani language.

## Language & religion

Apatani is an endangered Tani language spoken in seven Ziro valley villages, with spiritual beliefs tied to environmental stewardship.

- **Apatani language** (*Tanw*) — Also called Apa Tani or Tanii, a Tani language of the Sino-Tibetan family with possible non-Tani substratum elements.
- **Ritual specialists** — Priests wear dedicated dress including a jacket, cloth, cloak and head-dress, indicating a formal ritual office.

## Glossary

- *Apatani* — The language and ethnic group of the Ziro valley
- *Tanw* — Vernacular name for the Apatani language
- *Tani* — Branch of Sino-Tibetan languages to which Apatani belongs
- *bulyañ* — Traditional village council governing Apatani social and agricultural life
- *dao* — Single-edged steel blade with wooden hilt used as tool and weapon
- *mithan* — A bullock or cattle animal, represented in clay models
- *Dree* — Agricultural festival in July celebrating harvest and human prosperity
- *Myoko* — Festival in March celebrating inter-village friendship bonds
- *Paku-Itu* — Cultural programme performed at the Dree festival
- *Daminda* — Cultural programme performed at the Dree festival
- *Piree* — Dance performed at the Dree agricultural festival
- *Ziro* — Valley in Lower Subansiri district where Apatani live
- *ALDC* — Apatani Language Development Committee, formed to standardize the written language
- *Lower Subansiri* — District in Arunachal Pradesh where the Apatani valley is located
- *Sino-Tibetan* — Language family to which Apatani belongs
- *supplementary weft* — Weaving technique using extra weft threads to create pattern over solid ground
- *plaited fibre* — Interlaced vegetable fibres used for clothing, belts and carrying gear
- *rattan* — Palm stems used in Apatani plaiting and basketry
