## Overview

The Tipra, also known as Tripuri, Tripura, Twipras or Tipperahs, are a Tibeto-Burman-speaking ethnic group of the Indian state of Tripura and the independent nation of Bangladesh. They are descendants of inhabitants of the Twipra/Tripura Kingdom in North-East India and Bangladesh. The Tripuri people ruled the Kingdom of Tripura through the Manikya dynasty for over 600 years, beginning in 1400 and ending when the kingdom joined the Indian Union on 15 October 1949. Their cultural life includes Kokborok language traditions, festivals, ritual worship, foodways, marriage customs, dance, music, dress and community administration.

## Music & performance

Music appears in the account of the traditional marriage ceremony, where the groom’s procession to the bride’s home includes music and traditional water pots. The sources also describe Tripuri dance and music as elements of the community’s distinctive culture. Traditional sports are called *thwngmung* in Tripuri and are common in almost all the clans of Tripuri.

## Festivals & rituals

The Tripuri festival calendar includes Buisu, Goriya, Sena, Kharchi, Ker, Osa, Mamita, Hojagiri and Hangrai. Buisu is the traditional New Year’s Day of the Tripuri/Tripura people in India and Bangladesh and falls on 13 or 14 April. Its first day, Hari Buisu, involves cleaning and decorating houses with flowers and offering special prayers in houses and temples; the following day, Buisu, is marked by visits, socializing and traditional foods.

Goriya is a seven-day festival during which Lord Goriya is worshipped from Buisu to Sena. Sena is the last day of Goriya worship. Kharchi mwtai is the worship of the dynasty deity of the Tripuri people, the fourteen gods; it is performed in July/August on the eight day of new moon by the royal priest Chontai. Ker mwtai is celebrated 14 days after Kharchi mwtai and involves worship of all the gods of the Tripuri people at one time.

Osa is the worship of Tripuri Goddess Ama Osa. Mamita is a harvest festival during which Goddess Ama Mailuma is worshipped and which is held during Osa Mwtai. Hojagiri is a festival of lights associated with worship of goddess Ama Mailuma and Ama Khuluma. Hangrai is a harvest festival involving preparation beginning 4–5 days beforehand, the construction of *Nowshah* from bamboo and paddy husks, picnics, food, worship, holy places, individual sacrifices and rituals.

The traditional marriage system is known as *Hamjwk Tubui Kaimani*. It begins with negotiations between the bride’s and groom’s families through a marriage broker called a *Raibai*. The formal engagement, *Koksungma*, includes blessings with rice beer, offerings and a feast. Wedding rituals include worship of the Lampra Wathop deity and Tuisangramma, a procession, and the bride’s departure in a ceremonial palanquin.

## Foodways

Tripuri foodways include fresh vegetables from the hills, bamboo shoots called *Muya* in the Kokborok language, dry fish and sticky rice. Sticky rice is prepared in several forms, including *Awang Bangwi* or *Awang Bwthai*, *Awang Sokrang*, *Awang Phanswi*, *Phap ni Awang* and *Awang Belep*. The sources list eight traditional recipes or cuisines: *Chakhwi*, *Gudok*, *Bermabwtwi*, *Mosdeng*, *Awandru*, *Mwkhwikwtwi*, *Serma* and *Thokmui*.

*Awang Bangwi*, also called *Awang Bwthai*, is a cone-shaped rice roll steamed in *Lairu*, or banana leaves. It is prepared with sticky rice, butter or ghee, raisins, nuts, ginger and onion, and is described as the national food of Tripura State. Bamboo is also used to cook chicken, pork or fish. The food is marinated with different ingredients, placed inside bamboo with a little water and cooked for 40–50 min on charcoal.

During Hangrai, hot rice cakes and different foods are served at picnics. Elders share *Awangs*, *Moi* or curries, together with rice-based alcohol called *Arak* or *chuwak*. Marriage ceremonies include rice beer and a feast.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources name two notable historical literary works written by court scholars: the *Rajratnakar*, described as the royal chronicle of Tripuri kings, and the *Rajmala*, described as a chronicle of the Tripuri kings of Tripura. Historically, Tripuri was written in the native Tripuri script known as *Koloma*. The earliest known writing in Tripuri dates from the 1st century AD and was written in Koloma; the script was later replaced by an alphabet based on the Eastern Nagari script. The revival of the ancient Koloma script is in process.

## Language & religion

The Tripuri people speak Kokborok, also known as Tipra, a Tibeto-Burman language. Tripuri is the official language of Tripura, India. The sources describe three main dialects; the central dialect of the royal family, DebBarma, also called Puratan Tripur, is a prestige dialect understood by everyone and is used for teaching and literature. It is the medium of instruction up to class fifth and a subject up to graduate level in Tripura.

In the 2011 census, 93.6% of the Tripuri people followed an admixture of folk religions and Hinduism, while 6.4% were Christians, mostly Baptists. Tripuri Hinduism is described as a syncretic religion combining traditional folk religion with Hindu elements. A minority of the Uchoi clan are Buddhist. Ritual life includes worship of deities such as Lord Goriya, Goddess Ama Osa, Ama Mailuma, Ama Khuluma, the Lampra Wathop deity and Tuisangramma, as well as worship of the fourteen gods during Kharchi mwtai.
