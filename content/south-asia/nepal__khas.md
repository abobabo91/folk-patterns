## Overview

The Khas are an Indo-Aryan ethno-linguistic group native to the Himalayan region of South Asia, especially Nepal and the Indian states of Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, West Bengal, Assam and Sikkim. The sources describe Khas communities including Kshetri, Thakuri, Bahun and Sanyasis, as well as Gharti, Damai, Kami, Sarki, Hudka, Tamote, Gaine, Badi and others. Historically, Khas speakers used Khas language, while modern Khas people in Nepal are associated with Nepali. They are also known as Parbatiyas, Parbates, Paharis and Pahadis. Their cultural significance in the sources lies in their languages, regional histories, caste and community identities, music, dance, religious practices and links to Himalayan political formations.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

 They state only that Khas and Rajput were differentiated in Kumaon during Chand rule by the number of threads in the sacred thread: Khas were allowed to wear three threads, while Rajput used six.

### Architecture

 They mention village-level theocratic republics called *Gram-Rajya* and *Mandals*, and refer to castles and capitals associated with Khas-linked dynasties, including Joshimath, Baijnath and the castle at the foot of Banahal Pass.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They mention blacksmiths among the occupational Khas groups, identifying *Kāmi* as blacksmiths, *Damāi* as tailors and *Sārki* as shoemakers and leather workers.

### Jewelry & body adornment

 The only bodily marker described is the sacred thread distinction between Khas and Rajput in Kumaon during Chand rule.

## Music & performance

The sources identify *Deuda* as a song and folk dance performed during various festivals in the Sudurpashchim and Karnali provinces of Nepal. Khas music is described as originating in the practices of Nepali Khas society. The sources name Damai, Gandharva and Gaine as castes whose members play musical instruments and sing during festive occasions of the Khas people.

The sources also state that modernization has caused this practice to almost disappear, while mass electronic media, including television and the internet, increasingly provide entertainment, especially in the Kathmandu valley. The term “Khas Music” is itself described as being in decline because Khas has become obsolete as a social designation.

## Dance & theatre

The sources name *Deuda* as a folk dance performed on the occasion of various festivals in the Sudurpashchim and Karnali provinces of Nepal.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that *Deuda* song and folk dance are performed during various festivals in the Sudurpashchim and Karnali provinces of Nepal. They also state that the Khas people had a sect of Shaivism known as Masto religion, in which twelve Masto gods were worshipped; these gods were said to be sons of Shiva.

The majority of Khas are described as followers of Hinduism, while some follow Buddhism and some were converted to Christianity. Masto worship was prevalent throughout the Western Himalayan region.

## Foodways

 They state only that some Khas communities were identified as *Matwali*, meaning alcohol-drinker Khas, and that alcohol abstinence was treated as a high-caste taboo by Brahmin immigrants from the plains.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources connect the Khas with the Khasas mentioned in ancient Hindu literature and refer to the *Mahabharata*. They also mention the *Rajatarangini*, a twelfth-century text written by the Kashmiri Pandit historian Kalhana, which describes the rulers of Rajapuri as the “lord of the Khasas” and identifies the Lohara dynasty as belonging to the Khas tribe.

The Khas language is described as having been known historically as *Khas kurā*, meaning Khas speech, and the Newar people used names including *Khayan Bhaya*, *Parbatiya* and *Gorkhali* for it.

## Language & religion

Khasa, also called Khasa Prakrit, Khas Prakrit, Sanskrit Khasa, Himalayan Prakrit, Northern Prakrit and Khas Kura, is described as a Prakrit language of medieval South Asia and a common ancestor of Pahari languages, including Nepali, Kumaoni, Jaunsari, Mandeali, Garhwali and Mahasuvi. Khasa belongs to the Indo-European family and is described as descending from an early form of Vedic Sanskrit through Shauraseni Prakrit and Śauraseni Apabhraṃśa.

The Khas people of Nepal originally referred to their language as *Khas kurā*. It later became known as Nepali, which is described as a national language of Nepal and a lingua franca among much of the population of the northern region of West Bengal, Sikkim and Bhutan. The majority of Khas profess Hinduism; others follow Buddhism or Christianity. Their own Shaivite sect, Masto religion, worshipped twelve Masto gods said to be sons of Shiva.
