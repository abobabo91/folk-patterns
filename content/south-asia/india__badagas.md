## Overview

The Badagas are an ethno-linguistic community living in the Nilgiris district of Tamil Nadu, India. They live throughout the district in nearly 400 villages called *Hattis* and speak Badaga, a language closely related to Kannada. Their history is preserved through oral traditions concerning migration from the plains of Mysore, seven founding siblings, and the ancestor Hethappa. Badaga cultural life includes community marriage traditions, the festival Devva Habba, worship of seven founding ancestors under the name Hethappa or Hetha, life-cycle rituals, and reverence for Sri Mariamman. The community also maintains distinctive women’s clothing, oral traditions, songs, and stories. Several Badaga have held positions in the Indian Government.

## Festivals & rituals

The important Badaga festival named in the sources is Devva Habba, which provides insights into the community’s origin. The Badagas worship their seven founding ancestors under the name Hethappa or Hetha.

Badaga cultural rules and rituals extend through the life cycle, beginning with the birth of a child and continuing through puberty, marriage, naming ceremony, seventh-month pregnancy, housewarming, and death.

The sources also record reverence for Sri Mariamman. In April, the Badagas celebrate a car festival at the Sri Mariamman temple in Ooty, pulling the car with the image of Sri Mariamman to their music and dance. During the early 1900s, they were described as swearing solemn oaths by the Sri Mariamman temple: they bathed, brought coconut and fruit, killed an animal, placed the head on the shrine step, and walked from seven feet away to put out the light in front of the idol. The sources state that Britisher judges sometimes followed this practice in court and sent witnesses to perform the ritual with a Court official.

## Foodways

The sources mention coconut and fruit as offerings used in the oath ritual at the Sri Mariamman temple, as well as animal sacrifice.

## Oral tradition & literature

Badaga oral tradition tells of ancestors presumed to have been Vokkaligas who migrated from the plains of Mysore to avoid Muslim persecution. Another tradition says that seven siblings lived in the Thalaimalai Hills and later settled in different parts of the Nilgiris after fleeing from a Muslim ruler who tried to rape their sister.

The account concerning Hethappa describes the second brother’s conflict with two Todas, the aid of two Bayaluru, and the claim that inhabitants of the village descended from the Bayalurus and Badaga’s daughters. The sources also state that LACITO in Paris houses numerous varieties of Badaga stories and songs collected over the past two decades. Paul Hockings is identified as an American anthropologist whose research on the Badagas spans nearly six decades.

## Language & religion

Badaga is the language spoken by the Badaga community and is closely related to Kannada. LACITO in Paris houses collected varieties of Badaga stories and songs.

The Badagas worship their seven founding ancestors under the name Hethappa or Hetha. They also show great reverence for Sri Mariamman, including the April car festival at the Sri Mariamman temple in Ooty and the oath ritual described in the sources.
