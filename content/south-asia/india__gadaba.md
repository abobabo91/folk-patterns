## Overview

The Gadaba, also called the Gutob people, are an ethnic group of eastern India. They are classified as a Scheduled Tribe in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha. The 2011 Indian census recorded 84,689 Gadabas in Odisha and 38,081 in Andhra Pradesh. Their subgroups are Bada Gadaba, Sana Gadaba, Gutab Gadaba, Farenga Gadaba and Allar Gadaba. Their socioeconomic life is based on farming and daily labour, including slash-and-burn and plow cultivation. They live in permanent villages. The Gadabas speak Gutob and Ollari, and are known for the tribal dance Dhemsa. Since the early 1980s, hydro-electric dams and the resulting lakes have largely displaced them from their villages.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Two-piece dress** — Gadaba women traditionally weave a colorful two-piece dress themselves. The clothing is often striped in red, blue and white.

### Clothing & dress

The sources describe women’s traditional clothing. A Gadaba woman traditionally wears a two-piece dress that is very colorful and often striped in red, blue and white. The dress is woven by the women themselves.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They mention blacksmiths only in connection with the removal of women’s neck rings.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Gadaba women traditionally wear neck rings weighing about 500–700 grams each. The rings cannot be removed without the help of a blacksmith and, as part of the tradition, are removed only after the wearer’s death. The sources say that Gadaba ornaments are not very different from those of other tribes.

## Music & performance

The sources identify the Dhemsa as a tribal dance associated with the Gadabas. No instrument names or song genres are given.

## Dance & theatre

**Dhemsa** is identified as the Gadabas’ tribal dance.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state only that neck rings are traditionally removed after a woman’s death.

## Language & religion

The term Gadaba language refers to several languages associated with the Gadaba people. Gutob language, also called Bodo or Boi Gadaba, is an Austro-Asiatic language. Ollari language, also called Ollar or Pottangi Gadaba, is a Dravidian language. Kondekor language, also called Mudhili or Gol Gadaba, is closely related to Ollari.
