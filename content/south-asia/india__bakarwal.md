## Overview

The Bakarwal, also spelled Bakkarwal or Bakerwal, are a nomadic Muslim ethnic group and a subgroup of the larger Gujjar community. They primarily inhabit the Indian Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, and are also found in Himachal Pradesh and Azad Jammu and Kashmir. Their territory extends from the Pir Panjal Range to Zanskar in the Himalayan mountains of India, including Suru Valley and Kargil in Ladakh. Bakarwals are traditionally pastoral nomads who seasonally migrate with sheep and goats between high-altitude summer meadows and lower-altitude winter areas. Their annual transhumance crosses the Pir Panjal and Banihal passes. The 2001 Indian census recorded 60,724 Bakarwals in Jammu and Kashmir, while the 2011 census recorded 113,198.

## Language & religion

Bakarwals and Gujjars speak dialects of Gojri and practice Islam. The Bakarwals are described as completely Muslim and adherents of the Sunni sect of Islam, similar to other Muslim Gujjar communities.
