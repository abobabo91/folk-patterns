## Overview

The Dogras, or Dogra people, are an Indo-Aryan ethno-linguistic group living primarily in the Indian union territory of Jammu and Kashmir. They live predominantly in the Duggar region of the Jammu Division, in adjoining areas of Punjab and Himachal Pradesh, and in northeastern Pakistan. The Jammu Dogras traditionally inhabited the area between the slopes of the Shivalik range of mountains and Surinsar Lake and Mansar Lake, later spreading throughout the Jammu region. They speak Dogri and other dialects similar to Dogri or western Pahadi-influenced languages. Dogra culture is represented in the sources through regional performance traditions, ceremonial cooking, historical kingdoms, military institutions, and the religious diversity of Jammu and Kashmir.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Babor, also called Babbapura, is described as a former chief state of the Dogras. Lying 45 km east of Jammu, it contains the ruins of six magnificent temples, which the source presents as evidence of a “thriving artistic activity.”

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They mention a copper-plate inscription in Chamba in connection with the etymology of the term Dogra.

## Music & performance

Dogra performance traditions include vocal, instrumental, theatrical, and narrative forms. **Paakh**, also called **Gwatri**, **Kark**, or **Masade**, is a chorus narrative sung by a group of 10 singers without musical instruments. **Gwatri** combines song and dance: singers narrate text that is acted by Gwatari dancers. **Karak** is a narrative ballet sung by a community called “Jogies.” Its performers narrate a popular folk tale in their dance style, with three members accompanied by a folk instrument called a *rubab*.

**Benthe** is a chorus-singing tradition performed by a specific community of tribal people called Gujjar and Bakerwal; its dance is performed by 5–7 members. **Fumenie** and **Jagarana** are sung by women on the eve of a groom’s departure to his in-laws’ house, with each song performed by a group of 15–20 members.

## Dance & theatre

**Kud** is a ritual dance performed in honour of Lok Devatas. It is performed mostly at night, is spontaneous, and involves people of all ages and genders. The instruments used during Kud are *narshingha*, *chhaina*, flute, and drums. The rhythm controls the participants’ movements, the dance continues all night, and the number of participants ranges from 20 to 30.

**Heren** is a traditional theatre form performed during the Lohri festival by 10–15 people. It is mostly performed in the hills of Jammu, Udhampur, and ramnagar. **Fumenie** and **Jagarana** are women’s dance traditions associated with the departure of a groom to his in-laws’ house. **Gwatri** combines song and dance, while **Karak** presents a popular folk tale as a narrative ballet. **Benthe** includes both chorus singing and dance.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources identify Lohri as the festival during which **Heren** is performed. They also describe **Kud** as a ritual dance honouring Lok Devatas, performed mostly at night and continuing all night. **Fumenie** and **Jagarana** are associated with the eve of a groom’s departure to his in-laws’ house, and therefore connect performance with marriage-related movement and family relationships. They state that Dogra society includes followers of Hinduism and people of other religions; in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, some Dogras embraced Islam and Sikhism. No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription is supplied for the group.

## Foodways

Wheat, maize, and bajra are staple foods, alongside rice and cereals. A tangy preparation of mango or tamarind is known as *ambal* or *maani*; combined with dal patt, it forms *dal patt maani*. *Mittha madra* is made with milk, dry fruit, and semolina.

Ceremonial cooking includes preparations of *rajmash*, a special variety of red kidney beans; *mash da madra*, a yogurt-based gravy for black lentils; *auryia*, a curd dish fermented by rye; *kulth di daal*, made from horse gram; and *ambal* made from pumpkin, jaggery, and tamarind. In *Dogra Dhaam*, community feasts and banquets, cooking is supervised by expert family chefs from the Dogra Brahmin community known as *Siyans*, assisted by Dogra *jheers*.

*Kalari* is milk preserved through the coagulation of proteins and then fried in a pan. It is also prepared by mixing flour, cottage cheese, and milk cream with water in a small cup-shaped pot, and is served with milk. *Khatta meat* is mutton cooked with sour pomegranate seeds or lime juice and flavoured with fumes from burning charcoal soaked in mustard oil. *Keyur* is made by frying flour or maida batter and served with sugar and curd; it is often served to bridegrooms by their in-laws.

*Babbru* and *pathoru* are prepared with flour and fried in oil. *Babbru* is served with *maani*, potato dish, *kheer*, or curd. *Kheer* is made from milk with rice and dry fruit and is served on special occasions and festivals. *Guchiyyan*, dried black morel, is added to pulao and served with mountain potatoes. Saffron, or *kesar*, flavours sweet dishes, while pickles are made from Kasrod, mango, tyaoo, lasoode, and girgle.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources name several narrative forms. **Karak** is a narrative ballet in which Jogies narrate a popular folk tale, and **Paakh**, **Gwatri**, **Kark**, and **Masade** are described as chorus-narrative or song-and-dance forms. The sources also mention the *Rajatarangini* in connection with the history of Babbapura and Raja Vajradhara.

## Language & religion

Dogra is an Indo-Aryan ethno-linguistic identity associated with the Dogri language. The Dogras generally speak Dogri and other dialects similar to Dogri or western Pahadi-influenced languages. In modern times, the term Dogra refers to all who speak Dogri, irrespective of religion.

The majority of Dogras are followers of Hinduism, while many people in Jammu and Kashmir follow other religions. In the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, some Dogras embraced Islam and Sikhism. The sources therefore describe the Dogra population of Jammu and Kashmir as including members of all three religions.
