## Overview

The source identifies the Ewan as the Awan community, a group hailing from and mainly based in northern Punjab and neighbouring areas of Pakistan. “Awan” is also used as a surname by the community. Awans believe themselves to be of Arab origin and descended from Ali ibn Abu Talib; this claimed ancestry is described as giving them high status in the Indian Muslim environment. Historical accounts describe them as farmers and warriors who established settlements in the Salt Range and along the Indus to Sind, with a densely populated centre near Lahore. The community has a strong presence in the Pakistan Army and a notable martial tradition.

## Music & performance

The source names Mir Sultan Khan as a chess master, Ahmad Nadeem Qasmi as an Urdu poet, journalist, literary critic, dramatist, and short story author, and Sultan Bahu as a Sufi mystic and poet.

## Foodways

The source describes the Awans historically as farmers.

## Oral tradition & literature

The source records an Awan origin tradition linking the community to Ali ibn Abu Talib and his second wife, Hanafiya. It also states that Awans believe themselves to be of Arab origin and descended from Ali ibn Abu Talib. Christophe Jaffrelot describes this as a legend and notes that historians describe the Awans as valiant warriors and farmers.

Several named Awan-associated figures are identified in literary or religious roles. Ahmad Nadeem Qasmi is described as an Urdu poet, journalist, literary critic, dramatist, and short story author. Sultan Bahu is described as a Sufi mystic and poet. Abdul Mannan Wazirabadi is identified as an Islamic scholar, faqīh, and muhaddith.

## Language & religion

The source identifies Urdu in the spelling of the name Awan and refers to the Indian Muslim environment. It identifies several Islamic scholars and spiritual figures associated with the Awans, including Khadim Hussain Rizvi, founder of Tehreek-e-Labbaik Pakistan; Ameer Muhammad Akram Awan, spiritual leader of Naqshbandia Owaisiah; Abdul Mannan Wazirabadi, an Islamic scholar, faqīh, and muhaddith; and Sultan Bahu, founder of Sarwari Qadiri.
