## Overview

The Raute are a nomadic ethnic group officially recognized by the Government of Nepal. They live in small settlements in western Nepal, while about 150 people, as late as 2016, still chose to live a nomadic life. Their population is estimated at 650, including 618 people recorded in Nepal’s 2011 census. Raute move from place to place, usually staying no more than 4 to 5 months in one location and often only a few days. Their language is called Raute and is a small Sino-Tibetan language associated with Dadeldhura District and Sudurpashchim Province. Their folk-cultural importance in the sources lies in their continuing combination of hunting, gathering, mobility, language, and wood-carving exchange practices.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The Raute erect tents using poles cut from small trees in state-run forests.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Raute carve wooden bowls and boxes, which they trade to local farmers for grain, including rice, iron, cloth, and jewelry.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention jewelry as one of the goods Raute obtain through exchange.

## Festivals & rituals

Raute do not share their worship practices with sedentary villagers, in order to maintain their cultural purity.

## Foodways

Raute are known for subsistence hunting of langur and macaque monkeys. They regularly gather wild forest tubers, fruits, and greens, and obtain grain, including rice, by trading wooden bowls and boxes to local farmers. They move between places in search of better water sources and villages where they can sell wood products for food staples. The sources state that they do not sell bushmeat or forest medicinal plants.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that Raute maintain their own language and education and do not share their language with sedentary villagers.

## Language & religion

Raute is a small Sino-Tibetan language of Dadeldhura District and Sudurpashchim Province in Nepal. It is called “Raute” in most studies and sometimes “Khamci,” meaning “our talk,” in a few other studies. Alternate names listed for the language include Boto boli, Khamchi, Raji, Rajwar, Ra’te, Rautya, and Rautye. Raute is closely related to the languages spoken by the Ban Raji and Raji, described in the sources as “Little Rulers of the Forest” and “Little Rulers.” Raute do not share their worship practices with sedentary villagers.
