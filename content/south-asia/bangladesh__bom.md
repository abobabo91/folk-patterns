## Overview

One source describes the Bawm or Bawmzo as an ethnic community inhabiting the Chittagong Hill Tracts of Bangladesh, while another describes Bom as an endangered language of Sierra Leone. The Bawm population in Bangladesh was recorded as 13,193 in the 2022 Bangladeshi census. Around 3,000 Bawm inhabited India in 2025, and 2,500 Bawm reside in Myanmar. In Bangladesh, 11,854 Bawm live in Bandarban District, where they constitute 2.46% of the district’s population; they also live in Belaichhari Upazila of Rangamati District and surrounding areas. The Bawm language belongs to the Kuki-Chin language branch.

## Language & religion

The Bawm language is described as belonging to the Kuki-Chin language branch. The separate Bom language is described as an endangered language of Sierra Leone belonging to the Mel branch of the Niger–Congo language family and particularly closely related to Bullom So. Most Bom-language speakers are bilingual in Mende, and use of the language is declining among members of the ethnic group.
