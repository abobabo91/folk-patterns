## Overview

The Nicobarese are an Austroasiatic-speaking people of the Nicobar Islands, a chain in the Bay of Bengal north of Sumatra and part of the union territory of Andaman and Nicobar Islands, India. The archipelago consists of 19 islands, of which 12 are inhabited, including Car Nicobar, Chowra, Teressa, Nancowry, Great Nicobar and Little Nicobar. Great Nicobar is the largest and main island. The inhabitants of individual islands have specific names but are collectively known as the Nicobarese; they call themselves *Holchu*, meaning “friend.” They are a designated Scheduled Tribe in India. Their culture is closely connected with nature, horticulture, fishing, hunting, domestic architecture, painted sculptures and animistic beliefs.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no textile motifs.

### Architecture

Nicobarese villages consist of sporadically placed huts in designated areas. The huts are normally round and have dome-shaped roofs. They are typically raised above the ground, with ladders that residents pull up after climbing into the huts at night.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Nicobarese make pottery and canoes.

## Festivals & rituals

 They describe religious practices associated with the traditional religion of the islands, which is animistic in nature. Followers believe in spirits, ghosts and the existence of the soul. After death, a person becomes a ghost when the soul leaves the body, and the ghosts of the Nicobarese are believed to be present throughout the islands. Spirits are held responsible for unfortunate occurrences, after which shamans are called upon to deal with bad spirits.

The sources also describe social and family arrangements. The Nicobarese are headed by a matriarchal chief. Men and women have approximately equal status; women can choose their husbands and, after marriage, can live with either partner’s parents.

## Foodways

The Nicobarese traditionally have a horticultural economy based on coconuts, pandanus, areca nut palms, bananas, mangoes and other fruits. They also hunt, fish and raise pigs.

## Oral tradition & literature

Nicobarese painted sculptures are related to oral tradition and to the myth of Nicobarese origins. In the conception of the world described by the source, the Nicobarese inhabit the kingdoms of the sea, the earth and the sky, which they cross through a magic-religious sphere. Anthropomorphic creations represent the spirits of living beings, natural elements and everyday objects. These beings live with humans or visit them from another world.

In houses, sculptures are inhabited by the spirits of family ancestors and form part of the domestic environment. The inhabitants of the present are described as living in harmony with people from other worlds, represented by these images.

## Language & religion

The Nicobarese languages form an isolated group of about half a dozen closely related Austroasiatic languages spoken by most inhabitants of the Nicobar Islands. They have about 30,000 speakers, including 22,100 native speakers. Most Nicobarese speakers speak Car. The languages have verb-initial word orders, elaborate paradigmatic agreement systems and suffixing.

Different islands speak different dialects. The principal groups are Car, Chaura–Teressa, Central and Southern. The named forms include Car (*Pū*), Chaura (*Tutet/Sanënyö*), Teressa (*Taih-Long/Lurö*), Nancowry (*Nang-kauri/Mūöt*), Katchal (*Tehnu*) and Southern Nicobarese (*Sambelong*). Most people understand the Car dialect. The source also lists Shompen (*Shom Peng*) among the Nicobaric languages in one classification.

Most island inhabitants are Christian. Christianity was taught by John Richardson, who translated the New Testament into Nicobarese. Other Nicobarese follow the islands’ traditional animistic religion, involving spirits, ghosts, souls and shamans.
