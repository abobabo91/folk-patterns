## Overview

The Chenchus are a Dravidian aboriginal tribe designated as a Scheduled Tribe in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka and Odisha. Their traditional way of life is based on hunting and gathering, and many live in the sparse, deciduous Nallamala forest of Telangana and Andhra Pradesh. Chenchu is a Dravidian language belonging to the Telugu branch of the South-Central family. The language was spoken by about 280,764 people according to the 1981 census. Chenchus have maintained relationships with non-tribal people that have often been described as symbiotic, including the collection of forest products for sale and the sharing of agricultural knowledge. Their importance in folk-cultural terms lies in the continued presence of forest-based subsistence, a distinct language, and religious traditions connected with the Chenchu Kannappa.

## Festivals & rituals

They do document the religious importance of Kannappa, described as a legendary Shaiva saint and a Chenchu revered in Hindu Shaivic traditions for his devotion to Lord Shiva. His story is closely connected with the Srikalahasteeswara Temple in Andhra Pradesh. Kannappa is identified as one of the 63 Nayanmars in the Saiva Siddhanta tradition and is believed to be a reincarnation of Arjuna from the Mahabharata times.

## Foodways

They state that Chenchu subsistence has traditionally depended on hunting and gathering, and that the Chenchus do not cultivate land but hunt for a living. The sources also mention that non-tribal people living among them rent land from the Chenchus and pay a portion of the harvest.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do preserve the account of Kannappa’s devotion to Lord Shiva and his place in Hindu Shaivic traditions, including the association of his story with the Srikalahasteeswara Temple in Andhra Pradesh.

## Language & religion

Chenchu is a Dravidian language belonging to the Telugu branch of the South-Central family. It is spoken mostly in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana in India and is also called *Chenchukulam*, *Chenchwar*, *Chenswar* or *Choncharu*. The Chenchus are primarily Hindus. The sources describe Chenchu as the language of an Aboriginal forest hunter-gatherer tribe and state that it is vulnerable. They also report that the use of Telugu has increased among Chenchu children, contributing to the endangerment of the Chenchu language.
