## Overview

The Marathi people, or Marathis, are an Indo-Aryan ethnolinguistic group native to Maharashtra in western India. Marathi is their native language and belongs to the Indo-Aryan language family. Marathi-speaking populations also occur in Goa, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, and overseas. The Marathi community became politically prominent in the 17th century with the establishment of the Maratha Empire by Shivaji in 1674. Maharashtra became a Marathi-speaking state of India on 1 May 1960. Marathi culture includes regional variations in dialect, folk song, food, dress, religion, literature, theatre, cinema, and festivals.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources name garments and jewelry.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Maharashtra attire includes the *dhoti* and *pheta* for men. Women wear a *choli* and a nine-yard saree locally known as *Navwri saree*. Traditional clothing is increasingly replaced by trousers and shirts for men and five-yard sarees or *salwar kameez* for women. Traditional attire is increasingly worn during special occasions and festivals. Marathi women also wear a *gajra* or *veni* made from flowers with their sarees.

### Architecture

They do document temples, forts, and historical monuments in Maharashtra. Temples combine architectural styles borrowed from North and South India and blend themes from Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain cultures. Maharashtra has many hill, land, and sea forts, including *Shivneri*, *Raigad*, *Vijaydurg*, *Pratapgad*, and *Sinhagad*. The sources state that forts played an important role in Maharashtra’s history, especially from the time of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj. Historical monuments named in the sources include the Gateway of India, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, Shaniwar Wada, Aga Khan Palace, and Deekshabhoomi.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention *Twashta Kasar* as an artisan caste that traditionally worked with brass, and *Kumbhar* as the potter or pottery-related village-servant caste, but provide no catalogue of objects or techniques.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Maharashtra jewelry includes *tanmani*, *bormal*, *Ranihar*, *kolhapuri saaj*, and *thushi*, worn on the neck. *Bajuband* is worn on the arm above the elbow, and *painjan* on the ankles. Ear ornaments include *kudi*, *bugdi*, *vajra kutka*, *vel*, and *Bali*. The *Marathi nath* is worn on the nose and *jodave* on the toes. The sources also describe *gajra* and *veni*, floral adornments worn by women with their sarees. Tattoos, henna, and other body-marking practices are not covered.

## Music & performance

Lavani, Powada, and Tamasha are identified as popular folk forms in Maharashtra. *Lavani* is a form of folk song, and its songs can address romance, tragedy, politics, and society. *Powada* is associated with the achievements of Shivaji and is also described as a ballad sung in honour of warriors. *Tamasha* combines folk dance and performance.

Other community-entertainment forms based on folk song include *Bhajan*, *Bharud*, *Gondhal*, *Kirtan*, *Lalita*, *Abhangas*, and *Tumbadi* singing. *Bhaleri* is sung by farmers. Village women sing *Owi*, which describes the husband’s home and is generally performed while grinding grain on a *jaata*. *Suvasinis* sing folk songs during the *halad* ceremonies of a marriage. *Palane* or *Angai geet* is a lullaby.

The *Vasudev* is a folk artist who walks from house to house in the morning, sings different *Abhangas* in villages, begs for food, and gives blessings. The tradition of the Vasudeva in Marathi culture is estimated in the source at around one thousand-twelve hundred years.

## Dance & theatre

Named folk dances include *Koli*, *Powada*, *Banjara Holi*, and *Lavani*. *Koli* dance originated with the fishermen community of Maharashtra. *Lavani* dance presents subjects including romance, tragedy, politics, and society. *Powada* dance depicts the achievements of Shivaji. Other dances named in the sources are *tamasha*, *dindi*, *kala*, *dhangari gaja*, and *lezim*.

Marathi theatre began in the middle of the 19th century and flourished in the 1950s and 1960s. It includes *Sangeet Natak*, or musical drama, and *Tamasha*, described as folk dance. Its repertoire includes humorous social plays, farces, historical plays, musicals, experimental plays, and serious drama. The sources name Vijay Tendulkar, P. L. Deshpande, Mahesh Elkunchwar, Satish Alekar, Mama Varerkar, and Kirloskar in connection with Marathi theatre.

## Festivals & rituals

*Gudhi Padwa* is celebrated as the first day of the Hindu year. A *Gudhi* is characterized by a bamboo stick with coloured silk cloth, flowers, and a sweet placed at the top. *Ganesh Chaturthi* is described as the most important festival in Maharashtra and is celebrated for ten days with large festivities. In Diwali, children build a mud-and-rock replica of a fort known locally as *Killa*, decorated with mustard shoots, *mavalas* or toy soldiers, toy cannons, and miniature animals, as a tribute to Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj.

Other festivals named in the sources include Banganga Festival, *Bhaubeej*, Elephanta Festival, Ellora Festival, Ganga Dashahara, *Jivti Puja*, Kalidas Festival, *Kojagiri Pournima*, Maharashtra Day, *Naga Panchami*, *Narali Pournima*, *Pola*, *Shiv Jayanti*, and *Vat Pournima*. *Halad* ceremonies are associated with marriage, and *Suvasinis* sing folk songs during them.

## Foodways

Maharashtrian cuisine includes wheat, rice, *jowar*, *bajri*, vegetables, lentils, and fruit. Peanuts and cashews are frequently served with vegetables. The traditional staple of the Deccan Plateau is usually *bhakri*, spiced cooked vegetables, *dal*, and rice. *Bhakri* is an unleavened flatbread made from grains such as sorghum, *ragi*, millet, *bajra*, *bajri*, or *jwari*. In coastal Konkan, rice is the traditional staple, and wet coconut and coconut milk are used in many dishes.

A typical meal may include boiled rice, *bhakri* or *poli*, *varan*, *aamti*, cooked lentils, or a spiced vegetable. Distinctive dishes named in the sources include *ukdiche modak*, *aluchi patal bhaji*, *kanda pohe*, *thalipeeth*, *batata vada*, *vada pav*, *puran poli*, *sabudana khichdi*, *pav-bhaji*, *masala bhat*, *pitla*, *bharleli vangi*, *shrikhand*, *basundi*, and *misal pav*. Regional foods include Malvani cuisine, *saoji mutton*, *pandhra rassa*, and *taambda rassa*.

Cooking methods include *phodani*, simmering, deep frying, pan frying, steaming, roasting, pressure cooking, baking, sun drying, and fermentation. *Modak* is prepared during the Ganpati Festival, and *puran poli* is associated with festivals. Many Marathi communities follow a lacto-vegetarian diet, while meat, fish, and eggs are also eaten. Seafood is especially important in Konkan coastal communities. Tea or coffee is commonly served at breakfast.

## Oral tradition & literature

Marathi devotional and literary traditions include the works of Dnyaneshwar, Namdev, Eknath, Bahinabai, Tukaram, and other Bhakti poets. Dnyaneshwar wrote *Dnyaneshwari* and *Amrutanubhava* in Marathi. Eknath composed *Eknāthī Bhāgavat* and devotional songs called *Bharud*. Tukaram wrote more than 3000 *abhangs*, or devotional songs. Mukteshwar translated the *Mahabharata* into Marathi, while other works named in the sources include *Yatharthadeepika*, *Naladamayanti Swayamvara*, *Pandava Pratap*, *Harivijay*, and *Ramvijay*.

Marathi historical narratives include *Bakhars*, journals or narratives of historical events written in Marathi and Modi script during the Maratha period. *Powada* developed as a ballad form honouring warriors, while *Lavani* became associated with romantic songs presented with dance and instruments such as tabla. Marathi Dalit literature emerged as a named movement in the 20th century. In 1958, the term “Dalit literature” was used at the first conference of the Maharashtra Dalit Sahitya Sangha in Mumbai. The sources name Baburao Bagul, Namdeo Dhasal, Daya Pawar, Annabhau Sathe, Urmila Pawar, and other Marathi Dalit writers.

## Language & religion

Marathi is a classical Indo-Aryan language predominantly spoken in Maharashtra. It is an official language of Maharashtra and an additional official language in Goa. The language is derived from Maharashtri Prakrit and is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India. The sources give 83 million speakers according to the 2011 census and state that Marathi was designated a classical language by the Government of India in October 2024.

Major dialects include Standard Marathi and Varhadi Marathi. Other named varieties include *Zaadi Boli* or *Zhaadiboli*, Thanjavur Marathi, Namadeva Shimpi Marathi, Arey Marathi, Kasaragod, Bhavsar Marathi, *Judæo-Marathi*, East Indian Marathi, Maharashtrian Konkani, Malvani, Sangameshwari, Agri, Andh, Warli, Vadvali, and Samavedi. Marathi has historically been written in the Modi script for administration and business and in Devanagari for literature. Since 1950 it has been written in the Balbodh style of Devanagari.

Around 80% of Maharashtrians are Hindu, with Muslim, Christian, and Buddhist minorities. The 2011 census figures cited in the sources give Hinduism 79.83%, Islam 11.54%, Buddhism 6%, Jainism 1.2%, Christianity 1.0%, and Sikhism 0.2%. The Varkari religious movement, devotion to Lord Vitthal, and the worship of deities including Lord Ganesha, Maruti, Mahadeo, Khandoba, Kalubai devi, and Lord Vitthal are central religious subjects in the sources.
