## Overview

The Khasi live mainly in Meghalaya, especially the Khasi Hills and eastern Meghalaya. They are known for matrilineal society, weaving, distinctive dress, oral mythology, and living root bridges. Khasi is an Austroasiatic language written in the Latin script. Most Khasi people are Christian, while a smaller group follows Ka Niam Khasi.

## Material culture

Khasi material culture includes woven fabrics, ceremonial clothing, living root bridges, and tools and baskets recorded in museum collections.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Khasi textile traditions include Ryndia and Eri silk, with women central to Eri silk production.

- **Hand-woven fabric** (*Ryndia*) — Ryndia is a hand-spun, hand-woven fabric worn by men and women in undyed off-white or maroon and mustard plaid.
- **Peace silk** (*Eri silk*) — Eri silk is called “peace silk” because its production process is considered non-violent, and women do the weaving and production in Meghalaya.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Khasi dress uses distinctive male and female garments, with more elaborate clothing worn on ceremonial occasions.

- **Sleeveless coat** (*Jymphong*) — The Jymphong is a longish sleeveless coat without a collar, fastened at the front with thongs.
- **Female garment** (*Jainsem*) — The Jainsem is an elaborate garment made from two pieces of material fastened at each shoulder.
- **Female garment** (*Dhara*) — The Dhara is an elaborate garment made from a single piece fastened at each shoulder.

### Architecture

The War inhabitants of the Khasi community built living root bridges in the War region.

- **Living root bridges** — Living root bridges were designed and built by the War inhabitants of the Khasi community in the War region.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded Khasi objects include iron tools, bows, bamboo baskets, and a cane water-pipe.

- **Curved-blade bill** — A bill has a wide, curved iron blade and a carved wooden shaft.
- **Carrying basket** — A miniature bamboo carrying basket has a lid, wickerwork construction, a plaited bamboo strap, and three brown bamboo stripes.
- **Bow** — A bow is made of wood with a cane string and a metal sleeve.
- **Bill-hook** — A bill-hook has an iron blade, carved wooden haft, and plaited rattan.
- **Cane water-pipe** — The collection includes part of a cane water-pipe.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ceremonial Khasi adornment includes crowns and ornamental waist-bands.

- **Ceremonial crown** — Women may wear ceremonial crowns of silver or gold with a spike or peak fixed to the back.
- **Ornamental waist-band** — Men may wear an ornamental waist-band with the Jymphong and sarong on ceremonial occasions.

## Festivals & rituals

Khasi mythology tells of seven families remaining on earth after the human race was divided into 16 heavenly families.

- **The Seven Huts** (*Ki Hynñiewtrep*) — Ki Hynñiewtrep is the name for the Khasi tribe’s original abode, meaning “The Seven Huts.”
- **God, the Lord Master** (*U Blei Trai Kynrad*) — U Blei Trai Kynrad distributed the human race into 16 heavenly families.
- **Sixteen heavenly families** (*Khadhynriew Trep*) — Khadhynriew Trep is the name for the 16 heavenly families, with seven families remaining on earth and nine in heaven.
- **Heavenly peak** (*Lum Sohpetbneng Peak*) — A heavenly ladder resting on Lum Sohpetbneng Peak once enabled people to travel freely to heaven.
- **Divine tree peak** (*Lum Diengiei Peak*) — Access to heaven ended after people were tricked into cutting a divine tree at Lum Diengiei Peak.

## Language & religion

Khasi is a matrilineal society’s Austroasiatic language, and its indigenous religion is monotheistic.

- **Indigenous Khasi religion** (*Ka Niam Khasi*) — Ka Niam Khasi is the indigenous Khasi religion.
- **Supreme creator** (*U Blei Nongthaw*) — U Blei Nongthaw is the one, formless supreme creator in Ka Niam Khasi.
- **Shillong Plateau language group** (*Khasic group*) — Pnar, Lyngngam, and War are identified as Khasi’s closest relatives in the Khasic group of the Shillong Plateau.
- **First Khasi publication** (*First Khasi Reader*) — The First Khasi Reader became one of the first Khasi publications in 1842.
- **Catechism** (*Rhodd Mam*) — Rhodd Mam became one of the first Khasi publications in 1842.
