## Overview

The Garhwali people are an Indo-Aryan ethnolinguistic group native to the Garhwal division of Uttarakhand in the Indian Himalayas. They speak Garhwali, a Central Pahari language of the Northern Zone of Indo-Aryan languages. Garhwali is spoken primarily in Tehri Garhwal, Pauri Garhwal, Uttarkashi, Chamoli and Rudraprayag districts, and also by migrants in other parts of India. The 2011 Census of Languages in India estimated 2,482,089 speakers. Garhwali cultural life includes rural deities, ancestor worship, ritual performance, oral folklore, folk songs, religious literature, regional newspapers and community language workshops. The region’s history includes the Garhwal Kingdom, the British Garhwal district and the continuing use of Garhwali in home and local contexts.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They mention temple buildings and small stone shrines, including the temple of Chandrabadni Devi, the temple of Lord Umra Narayan, Koteshwar Mahadev temple, Dhari Devi temple and the temple of the goddess Kali at Kalimath.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention small rocks placed inside a stone-made shrine known as a *Thaan*, where Bhumiyal are represented.

## Music & performance

Garhwali folk music is represented in the sources by named singers and composers, including Basanti Bisht, Chander Singh Rahi, Jeet Singh Negi, Meena Rana, Narendra Singh Negi and Pritam Bhartwan. The sources identify Basanti Bisht, Narendra Singh Negi and Pritam Bhartwan as Padma Shri award recipients; Narendra Singh Negi also received the Sangeet Natak Akademi Award. Garhwali singers are described as having renewed interest in the language through popular songs and videos.

They do, however, identify several oral and literary forms connected with Garhwal, including ancient folk songs, *Mangal*, *Bhadiyali* and *Panwara*. The first Internet radio of Kumaon, Garhwal and Jaunsar was launched in 2008 by non-resident Uttarakhandi people in New York to give exposure to Uttarakhand folk music. Its name was *Bedu Pako Baro Masa*, described as a famous melody of the hills of Himalaya.

## Dance & theatre

They state that the Akhil Garhwal Sabha organised cultural programmes called *Kautig Uttarakhand Mahotsava* from 1998 onwards to promote traditional folk dances and traditions of Uttarakhand. Garhwali literature includes plays and drama, and named dramatic works include *Pralhad*, *Chaunphal Ramayan*, *Matho Singh Bhandari Nirtya Natika*, *Jeetu Bagdwal* and *Dr. Asaram*.

## Festivals & rituals

Garhwali rural religious life includes a pantheon of local deities worshipped through *Jagar*, described as a ritual. These deities include Narsingh, Bhairav, Nagaraja, Pandav and Draupadi. The pantheon is headed by Durga and Mahadev, who are also referred to as Bhagwati, Surkanda, Kali, Bhavani and Nanda. Garhwali people also worship ancestors known as Bhumiyal, understood in the sources as guardians of the land. Each Garhwali village has its own Bhumiyal, represented by small rocks in a *Thaan*. Crops were offered to Bhumiyal as thanks for protecting the village.

The sources describe annual events at Chandrabadni Devi Temple, including cultural and religious seminars. Kalimath is regarded as a divine place and Shakta pitha, and the temple of the goddess Kali there is visited by devotees throughout the year, especially during the *Navratras*. The upper part of Goddess Kali is worshipped at Dhari Devi.

## Foodways

They state that grain was one of the major crops of the Garhwal area, while cloth, salt, borax, livestock and wool were imported from Tibet.

## Oral tradition & literature

Most Garhwali literature is described as being preserved in folk form and passed down orally. The sources mention ancient folk songs, *Mangal*, *Bhadiyali* and *Panwara*, as well as proverbs and folklore collected and published by Pandit Ganga Datt Upreti in 1894. Garhwali literature includes poetry, novels, short stories and plays. The oldest manuscript in Garhwali identified in the sources is the poem *Ranch Judya Judige Ghimsaan Ji*, written by Pt. Jayadev Bahuguna in the 16th century.

Garhwali literary history also includes *Sabhaasaar*, written by Maharaja Sudarshan Shah in 1828 AD; the New Testament, published in Garhwali by American missionaries in 1830 AD; and the Gospel of St. Matthew, printed at Lucknow in 1876. The sources name Garhwali translations of the *Mahabharata* and *Ramayana*. Contemporary preservation efforts include the Akhil Garhwal Sabha’s annual Garhwali language workshop, its newspaper *Rant Raibar*, the *Chakhul Garhwali Dictionary* app launched in 2015, and the community-driven encyclopedia Garhpedia, launched in 2025.

## Language & religion

Garhwali is an Indo-Aryan language of the Central Pahari subgroup. It is primarily spoken in the Garhwal division of Uttarakhand and has regional dialects including Srinagari, Tehri or Gangapariya, Badhani, Dessaulya, Lohbya, Majh-Kumaiya, Bhattiani, Nagpuriya, Rathi, Salani, Gangadi and Chandpuri. Srinagari is described as the literary standard, while Pauri is generally regarded as the prominent dialect. The sources state that Garhwali is designated as vulnerable in UNESCO’s Atlas of the World’s Languages in Danger.

Garhwali religious life includes worship of rural deities through *Jagar*, devotion to Durga, Mahadev, Kali, Vishnu and Shiva, and ancestor worship directed toward Bhumiyal. Garhwali literature has included religious matters, poetics, astronomy, astrology and ayurveda.
