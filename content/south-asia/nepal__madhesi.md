## Overview

Madheshi people are several ethnic groups living in the Terai region of Nepal. The term is used for Hindu caste groups, Muslims, Marwaris, Brahmin and Dalit people, Maithils, Bhojpuri, Awadhi and Bajjika speaking people, and indigenous people of the Terai. Many share cultural traditions, educational ties and family ties with people south of the international border in Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal. Tharu people and Pahari people living in the Terai do not consider themselves Madheshi. The term became widely recognised for Nepali citizens with an Indian cultural background only after 1990.

## Festivals & rituals

Madheshi religious practices are described as a mixture of orthodox Hinduism and animism. Muslim Madheshis practise the traditional *nikah* marriage, which is recognised by law. Many Muslim Madheshis practise endogamy.

## Foodways

A 1989 study of food consumption patterns among 108 people in a village in Chitwan district found that people consumed seven food items on average. Rice constituted almost half of their daily food intake and was supplemented by vegetables, potatoes, milk and dairy products. Meat, fish, eggs and fruit were consumed less frequently. About 13.7% of men’s total food intake was alcohol, while females consumed far less alcohol. Fruit commonly grown in the Terai includes mango, lychee, papaya, guava, banana and jackfruit.

## Language & religion

Madheshi people speak Maithili, Bhojpuri, Bajjika, Urdu and Awadhi. The National Population and Housing Census of 2011 listed 3,092,530 Maithili speaking people, of whom 3,004,245 lived in the Terai; 1,584,958 Bhojpuri speaking people, of whom 1,542,333 lived in the Terai; 793,418 Bajjika speaking people, including 791,737 in the Terai; and 691,546 Urdu-speaking people, including 671,851 in the Terai. Muslim Madheshis speak Urdu primarily, as well as Awadhi, Bhojpuri, Bajjika and Maithili, depending on whether they live in the western, central or eastern Terai. Religions practised in the Terai include Hinduism, Islam, Buddhism, Kirat, Christianity, Prakriti, Jainism and Bon, as well as fewer than 900 followers of the Baháʼí Faith and fewer than 500 Sikhs.
