## Overview

The Nyishi are a dominant ethnic group in Arunachal Pradesh in northeastern India. They speak Nishi, also known as Nyishi, a Sino-Tibetan language of the Tani branch. Their population is around 300,000, making them the most populous tribe of Arunachal Pradesh according to the 2001 census; the Nishi-speaking population was approximately 280,000 according to the 2011 census. They are concentrated in Arunachal Pradesh, especially around the Dafla Hill range in the northern part of Papum Pare district, and also live in districts of Assam. Nyishi society is organized through patrilineal descent and clans. Polygyny has traditionally indicated social status and economic stability, while festivals, priestly mediation, hornbill conservation, and the indigenous Donyi-Polo tradition are among the cultural subjects documented in the sources.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The Nyishi traditionally wear cane helmets surmounted by the crest of a hornbill beak, known as *pudum* or *padam*. The sources describe this headgear as a distinctive element of Nyishi dress.

## Music & performance

They state that the priest, or *Nyubh*, performs rituals and acts as a translator, mediator, and negotiator between humans and spirits.

## Festivals & rituals

Nyokum, Boori-Boot, and Longte are festivals celebrated by the Nyishi people in commemoration of their ancestors. Within the Donyi-Polo tradition, the Nyishi believe that everything in nature has an owner, or spirit, called *uyu* or *wiyu*. The *Nyubh* serves as a translator, mediator, and negotiator between humans and spirits, performing rituals and animal sacrifices to please them. Spirits may be benevolent or malevolent.

Polygyny is prevalent among the Nyishi and signifies social status and economic stability. It has also been considered useful during clan wars, social hunting, and other social activities, although the practice is diminishing with modernization and the spread of Christianity.

## Foodways

They mention animal sacrifices performed by the *Nyubh* as part of ritual practice but provide no further information about foodways.

## Oral tradition & literature

Nyishi mythology contains many versions of Abo Tanyi in the form of spirits and other beings. Nyiha, also called Niya, is described as one of the sons of Abo Tanyi, the first perfect human being, and the Nyishi are described as his descendants. The sources also state that the Nyishi trace their descent patrilineally and are divided into several clans.

## Language & religion

Nishi, also known as Nyishi, Nisi, Nishang, Nissi, Nyising, Leil, Aya, Akang, Bangni-Bangru, and Solung, is a Sino-Tibetan language of the Tani branch. It is spoken in districts of Arunachal Pradesh and in Darrang District of Assam. Nishi is a subject–object–verb language and uses three tones: rising, neutral, and falling. Its dialects include Akang, Aya, Nyishi, and Tagin; Bangni-Bangru and Solung are described as particularly different from the others. The language has also been called Dafla and North Assam group in historical descriptions.

Nyishi religious life includes Donyi-Polo, Christianity, and Hinduism. According to a 2011 survey, 31% of Nyishi people were Christian and 29% Hindu, while many of the remainder continued to follow the indigenous Donyi-Polo tradition. Christian missionaries began operating in Arunachal Pradesh in the 1950s, although many proselytizing activities were limited by the government until the 1970s. A tiny amount of Nyishi people have become Muslim.
