## Overview

The Zou are an ethnic group indigenous to the India–Myanmar frontier and a subgroup of the Kuki-Chin-Mizo peoples. In India, they live principally in Churachandpur and Chandel districts of Manipur, where they share cultural and linguistic similarities with the Thadou and Paites. In Myanmar, also known as Burma, they are counted among the Chin peoples and are associated with Chin State, Tiddim, and the Chin Hills. The 2001 census placed the Zou population in Manipur at around 20,000, less than 3% of the population. Zou belongs to the Kuki-Chin language family. The sources document music, hunting, marriage, religion, oral traditions, language, and Christian conversion.

They state that most Zou hunters use a gun known as *Zouthau*.

## Music & performance

*Khuang*, a traditional drum made of wood and animal skin, plays an integral part in Zou church music. The sources also mention a Zou folk song composed during the Kuki Uprising.

Music is also associated with hunting rituals. When dangerous animals such as tigers are killed, the entire village gathers to perform traditional victory songs and the *sa-ai* ritual. This ritual involves the ceremonial killing of tamed animals, usually pigs, accompanied by drinking traditional wine called *zu*. The animal is then skinned and its carcass buried. The source states that this practice is now very rare.

They do identify church music as an important context for *khuang* and indicate that missionaries used elements of Sakhua vocabulary in Bible translations and hymnals.

## Festivals & rituals

They do record several historical, religious, hunting, and life-cycle practices.

Zou society traditionally practises patrilocal and monogamous marriage, typically but not exclusively exogamous. Patrilineal cross-cousin marriage and the payment of a bride price are traditional features. In cases of divorce, the spouse considered at fault must pay fines in money and customary services. The source states that marriage practices are now governed by the church.

Zou society is patriarchal. Within the family, the husband is the head of the household, daughters leave their natal family upon marriage, and inheritance follows a patrilineal system. The eldest son is entitled to inherit his father’s properties after his death, although the father may distribute property among other sons before his passing.

Historically, Zou people followed Sakhua, also known as Lawki in the Chin Hills. This religion included belief in natural spirits and ancestor worship. Hunting rituals included the *sa-ai* ceremony, traditional victory songs, the drinking of *zu*, and the ceremonial killing of tamed animals.

## Foodways

They identify *zu* as a traditional wine consumed during the *sa-ai* hunting ritual and state that tamed animals, usually pigs, were ceremonially killed in that context.

## Oral tradition & literature

Zou oral traditions trace the group’s origin to a cave known as *Khul*, believed to be located in “the extreme north.” They also trace descent to three ancestral brothers: *Songthu*, also known as *Chongthu*, *Songza*, and *Zahong*. According to Zou folklore, the name “Zou” was inherited from an eponymous ancestor of the same name, who is believed to be the progenitor of the wider Kuki-Chin-Mizo peoples.

The sources state that a Zou folk song was composed during the Kuki Uprising. They also identify several Zou dialects, including *Haidawi*, *Khuangnung*, *Thangkhal*, *Khodai*, and *Tungkua*. *Thangkhal* influenced traditional folk songs, while *Haidawi* became the dominant literary dialect through its use in vernacular Bibles and hymnals.

## Language & religion

Zou is a Northeastern Kuki-Chin language originating in western Burma and also spoken in Mizoram and Manipur in northeastern India. It is part of the Kuki-Chin language family and is similar to the Paite language. Zou is primarily written in Roman script. In 1952, M. Siahzathang of Churachandpur created an alternative script known as *Zolai* or *Zoulai*, an alphabetic system with some alphasyllabic characteristics. Cultural, political, and literary organizations began adopting it from the 1970s.

The Zou language includes the dialects *Haidawi*, *Khuangnung*, *Thangkhal*, *Khodai*, and *Tungkua*. Zou is one of the prescribed Major Indian Languages in Manipur high schools. The sources report 20,900 speakers in India based on the 2001 Indian census and around 61,000 speakers in Myanmar in 2012.

Historically, Zou people followed Sakhua, also called Lawki, which included natural-spirit belief and ancestor worship and was similar to Mizo Sakhua. In the 20th century, Zou people began converting to Christianity, which now constitutes the majority faith. The first documented Christian convert among the Zou was Pi Lam Za Vung of Phaitu Village, Chin State, Myanmar, who converted in early 1920. On 20 February 1954, Zou youth organised the first Jou Christian Association Conference at Daizang village, Manipur. Missionaries also introduced formal education.
