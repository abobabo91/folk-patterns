## Overview

The Wancho, also known as the Wancho Naga, are a Tibeto-Burmese indigenous ethnic group inhabiting the Patkai hills of Longding district in the Northeast Indian state of Arunachal Pradesh. Wancho is spoken in 36 villages of southeastern Longding district, Tirap district, Arunachal Pradesh, as well as in Assam and Nagaland. The language belongs to the Tibeto-Burman family under Northern Naga languages and is a Konyak language. The Wancho people had a population of 56,866 according to a 2011 census. They are ethnically related to the Nocte of Arunachal Pradesh and Konyak of Nagaland. Their documented folk-culture includes tattooing, ceremonial dress, songs, dances, prayer, the Oriah festival, rice beer, village feasts, and governance by elderly chieftains.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Wancho culture includes ceremonial costumes worn by boys and girls during Oriah.

### Architecture

The sources mention the *murung*, glossed as a dormitory, where feasts are arranged during Oriah.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They state that villagers exchange bamboo tubes filled with rice beer during Oriah.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Tattooing plays an important part in Wancho culture. According to tradition, a man is tattooed on his four limbs and his entire face, except for certain regions around the eyes and lips. Women wear necklaces and bangles and also have some light tattooing.

## Music & performance

Prayer, songs, and dances form part of Oriah, the prime festival of the Wancho. Boys and girls wearing ceremonial costumes sing and dance during the festival.

## Dance & theatre

Dance is part of Oriah. People dance around a *Jangban*, a long ceremonial pole planted during the festival. Boys and girls in ceremonial costumes sing and dance during Oriah.

## Festivals & rituals

Oriah is the prime festival of the Wancho. It is a spring festival held in March or April for six to twelve days, with prayer, songs, and dances. Villagers exchange bamboo tubes filled with rice beer as a mark of greeting and goodwill, and pork skin is offered to the village chief as a mark of respect. During the festival, jhum paddies are sown, pigs, buffaloes, and gayals are sacrificed, and feasts are arranged in each *murung*. Boys and girls wearing ceremonial costumes sing and dance, while people dance around a *Jangban*. A common day for celebrating Oriah began in 1975, and the government of Arunachal Pradesh eventually fixed the day as 16 February.

## Foodways

The sources document rice beer, pork skin, pigs, buffaloes, and gayals in connection with Oriah. Villagers exchange bamboo tubes filled with rice beer as a greeting and goodwill gesture. Pork skin is offered to the village chief as a mark of respect. Pigs, buffaloes, and gayals are sacrificed, and feasts are arranged in each *murung*.

## Language & religion

Wancho belongs to the Tibeto-Burman family under Northern Naga languages and is a Konyak language of north-eastern India. Alternate names include *Banpara Naga*, *Joboka*, and *Jokoba*. The language is spoken in 36 villages of southeastern Longding district, Tirap district, Arunachal Pradesh, as well as in Assam and Nagaland. The documented dialects are Changnoi, Bor Muthun, also called Bor Mutonia, Horu Muthun, and Kulung Muthun, also called Mithan. Wancho is generally written in Devanagari or Latin script. Between 2001 and 2012, Banwang Losu devised a unique alphabetic Wancho script taught in some schools; in 2019, it was officially published into Unicode 12.0.

Animist Wancho believe in two powerful deities, Rang and Baurang. Christianity has gained followers among the Wancho, including people belonging to Baptist or Catholic denominations. As per the 2001 census, 10% claimed to be Hindu and 16% Animist. By the 2011 census, 2.55% claimed to be Hindu and 0.55% Animist. The Wancho are traditionally governed by a council of elderly chieftains known as *Wangham* or *Wangsa*.
