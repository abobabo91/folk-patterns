## Overview

The Aos are a major Naga ethnic group native to Mokokchung District of Nagaland in Northeast India. Their territory extends from the Tsüla (Dikhu) Valley in the east to the Tsürang (Disai) Valley in the west. Approximately 227,000 Ao Nagas lived in Nagaland according to the 2011 census; they are concentrated mainly in Mokokchung District, with smaller populations in Assam and other parts of Nagaland. The Aos call themselves *Aoer*, meaning “those who came” from across the Dikhu river. Ao is a dialect cluster of Naga languages, including Chungli, Mongsen, Changki, Mongsen Khari, Dordar (Yacham), and Longla. Ao culture includes distinctive textiles, bamboo and cane work, wood carving, festivals, cuisine, and a history of Christian conversion beginning in 1872.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Shawl* — Weaving is traditionally done by women, who design and weave patterns in shawls.

*Sling bag* — Women also weave patterned sling bags.

*Headgear* — Patterned headgears are included among the woven items made by women.

*Wraparound garment* — Wraparound garments, commonly called *Mekhala*, are woven by women and carry designed patterns.

### Clothing & dress

Ao women traditionally weave shawls, sling bags, headgears, and wraparound garments commonly called *Mekhala*. They mention headgears, but provide no further information about their construction or use.

### Architecture

They state that wood carvings of human beings, hornbill, mithun head, elephants, tiger, and other animals are displayed at village-gate entrances, front doors, or house porches.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ao cane and bamboo products include mats, basketry, mugs, plates, sofas, head bands, leg guards, bangles, neck bands, necklaces, armlets, leggings, fish traps, and fish baskets. Pottery is used for cooking, storing water, and holding other items, and is made from a special type of soil or clay. Metalwork includes agricultural implements, hunting and war weapons, ornaments, spears, and the *Dao*, described as a Naga sword. Wood carving produces figures of human beings and animals, including hornbill, mithun head, elephants, and tiger.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Traditional ornaments are worn by both men and women, especially during festive occasions. The sources name necklaces, earrings, armlets, bracelets, and headgears. These objects are made from beads, brass, bones and horns of animals, boar’s teeth, ivory, shells, precious stones, and metals.

## Music & performance

The sources mention singing, dancing, and feasting as activities associated with the Tsüngremmong Festival, which is celebrated after the harvest.

## Dance & theatre

Dancing is identified as one of the activities marking the Tsüngremmong Festival, the community’s harvest festival.

## Festivals & rituals

The *Moatsü Festival*, identified as the Sowing Festival, is observed in the first week of May every year after sowing has been completed. It provides recreation and entertainment following the work of clearing fields, burning jungles, and sowing seeds.

The *Tsüngremmong Festival*, identified as the Harvest Festival, is the community’s biggest festival. It is celebrated immediately after the harvest, typically in August, and includes offering prayers to God, singing, dancing, and feasting.

Traditional Ao religion is described as animist, with benevolent and malicious spirits appealed to and placated through ceremony and sacrifice. Lichaba, the creator, is revered most highly among the Ao deities. Since the 19th century, the majority of Ao have converted to Christianity, although many continue to practise traditional animist festivals and rituals outside their religious contexts.

## Foodways

Traditional Ao cuisine is characteristically non-vegetarian. Food is preferably cooked by boiling rather than frying. Pork is the most popular meat and is mostly cooked with fermented bamboo shoots. Local herbal ingredients and spices enhance food flavours, and Ao cuisine tends to be spicy.

*Anishi* is identified as a signature Ao food. It is a dried paste made from taro leaves and gives dishes a distinct smoky flavour and deep black colour.

## Language & religion

Ao is a dialect cluster of Naga languages spoken in Nagaland by the Ao Naga. Its varieties include Mongsen Khari, Changki, Chungli, Dordar (Yacham), and Longla. Chungli Ao is written in Latin script. Chungli and Mongsen are nearly mutually unintelligible; Changki is spoken only in Changki, Japu, and Longjemdang, and is poorly documented. Many villages contain both Chungli and Mongsen speakers, while each Ao village has distinctive speech characteristics.

The traditional religion is animist, involving ceremonies and sacrifices intended to appeal to and placate spirits. Lichaba, the creator, is the most highly revered Ao deity. Christianity first entered Ao territory in 1872 through the American Baptist missionary Edward Winter Clark. The first family to convert was the “Aier” family, and the majority of Ao have converted to Christianity since the 19th century.
