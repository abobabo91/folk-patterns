## Overview

Bengalis are an Indo-Aryan ethnolinguistic group originating from and culturally affiliated with the Bengal region of South Asia. Their population is divided principally between Bangladesh and the Indian regions of West Bengal, Tripura, Assam’s Barak Valley, the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, and parts of Meghalaya, Manipur and Jharkhand. Bengali speakers worldwide are generally estimated at 300–310 million, while Bengali has about 242 million native and about 284 million total speakers. Bengalis are the largest ethnic group in South Asia and the third-largest ethnic group in the world. Their cultural contributions include arts and architecture, language, folklore, literature, politics, military, business, science and technology. Most speak Bengali, an Indo-Aryan language.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

 They state that eastern Bengal was prominent in textile manufacturing and was a major exporter of silk and cotton textiles during the Mughal era.

### Architecture

 They mention arts and architecture among the fields to which Bengalis have contributed, and they record monasteries, Islamic institutions, temples and bricked homes in historical contexts.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They mention silver coins, iron weaponry and bricked homes in connection with the Wari-Bateshwar civilisation, and describe a temple of Lord Manibhadra referenced in an inscription from present-day Cumilla.

## Music & performance

 They state that Bengalis have contributed to the arts, folklore and literature, and mention Charyapada as a work containing a song composed approximately in the 9th–10th century by the poet Bhusuku Pa.

## Festivals & rituals

 They do describe religious affiliations and practices: approximately 70% of Bengalis are adherents of Islam, with a large Hindu minority and sizeable communities of Christians and Buddhists. Bengali Muslims primarily belong to the Sunni denomination. Bengali Hindus generally follow Shaktism or Vaishnavism in addition to worshipping regional deities.

The sources also mention the Hajj pilgrimage, which Muslims are required to undertake once in their lifetime, and describe the historical funding of Islamic institutions in the Hejaz by Bengali sultans. Islamic institutions in the Hejaz became known by Arabs as Bengali madrasas.

## Foodways

 They state that eastern Bengal exported agricultural and industrial produce during the Mughal era.

## Oral tradition & literature

Bengalis have contributed to folklore and literature. They mention Charyapada, a work containing a song composed approximately in the 9th–10th century by the poet Bhusuku Pa, who refers to himself as “Bangāli.” The sources also describe Bengali literary development under Shamsuddin Ilyas Shah, when the Bengali language gained state patronage.

The sources name several historical writers and intellectual figures, including Abu'l-Fazl ibn Mubarak, Ghulam Husain Salim, Ram Mohan Roy, Swami Vivekananda and Atiśa. Ram Mohan Roy wrote the Persian book *Tuḥfat al-Muwaḥḥidīn* in 1804. The supplied material does not describe contemporary literary revivals or preservation projects.

## Language & religion

Bengali belongs to the Indo-Aryan language family and is the principal unifying characteristic of Bengalis. It is generally written using the Bengali script and evolved circa 1000–1200 CE from Magadhi Prakrit. Bengali has historical vocabulary borrowed from Persian and Sanskrit, while modern borrowings primarily come from English. Its closest modern relatives include Assamese, Odia and the Bihari languages.

The sources distinguish Classical Bengali, Standard Bengali and Colloquial Bengali. Classical Bengali, *সাধু ভাষা Śadhu Bhaśa*, was a historical literary form. Standard Bengali, *চলিত ভাষা Čôlitô Bhaśa* or *শুদ্ধ ভাষা Śuddho Bhaśa*, is the modern literary form based upon dialects of the divided Nadia region. Colloquial Bengali, *আঞ্চলিক ভাষা Añčôlik Bhaśa* or *কথ্য ভাষা Kôththô Bhaśa*, refers to informal spoken language varying by region.

Bengalis include Muslims, Hindus, Christians and Buddhists. Islam first appeared in Bengal during Pala rule through increased trade with the Middle East and later spread through Sufi missionaries and Muslim conquests. Bengali Hindus generally follow Shaktism or Vaishnavism and worship regional deities.
