## Overview

The Mughal people, or Mughals, are a Muslim ethnic group of Turco-Mongol origin in South Asia, primarily associated with Bangladesh, India and Pakistan. They are descended from the Turkic-Mongol Barlas tribe and historically settled in the Indian subcontinent during the Mughal Empire, where they mixed with the native Indian population. In Pakistan, they are mostly settled in Azad Kashmir and in the provinces of Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. In India, some Mughals use Mirza as a surname or honorific and are sometimes referred to as Chughtais or Chagatai Turks. The sources describe regional identities connected with the Mughal, Mirza and Tarkhan names.

## Language & religion

The sources identify the Mughals as Muslim and describe them as a Turco-Mongol ethnic group. They state that the Barlas and other Central Asian tribes spoke Chagatai Turkic language, and that Mughals are sometimes called Chagatai Turks. The sources also mention regional social and historical terms including Mughal, Mirza, Tarkhan, Biradari, Sardar and Jamat.
