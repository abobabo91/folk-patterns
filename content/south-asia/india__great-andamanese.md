## Overview

The Great Andamanese are an Indigenous people of the Great Andaman archipelago in the Andaman Islands. Historically, they lived throughout the archipelago and were divided into ten major tribes whose distinct but closely related languages formed the Great Andamanese language family. Their population, once estimated between 2,000 and 6,600, declined through disease, alcohol, colonial warfare, and loss of hunting territory. Only 52 remained in February 2010, while reports gave 59 by August 2020. Today, only the Jeru and Bo tribes remain in significant number, while the Cari tribe is moving toward extinction. The group is important to folk-culture research because its languages, social divisions, subsistence practices, and cultural identities have been radically transformed by colonial contact, resettlement, intermarriage, and population decline.

## Music & performance

The Great Andamanese language article records a poem in Akabea written by a chief, Jambu, after he was freed from a six-month jail term for manslaughter. The poem is presented with a chorus and is described as having abbreviated or inverted words and sentence structure to obtain a desired rhythmical effect. Its imagery includes the sky’s surface, a ripple, and a bamboo spear.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do record major changes in the group’s history: a British colonial presence was established in 1789; a permanent settlement and penal colony followed in the 1860s; surviving Great Andamanese were relocated to a reservation on Bluff Island in 1949 and to Strait Island in 1969.

## Foodways

The Great Andamanese on Strait Island still obtain part of their diet from hunting, fishing, and gathering. They also consume rice and other Indian food, practice some agriculture, and have established poultry farms.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Great Andamanese language article records a poem in Akabea written by the chief Jambu. It also gives part of a creation myth in Oko-Juwoi, described as reminiscent of Prometheus. The Akabea poem is presented in its original form, followed by a literal rendering and a translation. Its rhythm is associated with abbreviated or inverted words and sentence structure.

## Language & religion

The Great Andamanese languages are a nearly extinct language family once spoken by the Great Andamanese peoples of the northern and central Andaman Islands. They are one of two identified Andamanese language families; the other is Ongan. The Great Andaman languages fall into three clear clusters, and several varieties traditionally listed as languages are dialects. Their grammar is agglutinative, with extensive prefix and suffix systems and a distinctive noun-class system based largely on body parts. Body parts are inalienably possessed, requiring a possessive adjective prefix. The sources state that the available languages had only two cardinal numbers—one and two. Hindustani serves as the primary language of surviving speakers, while a mixed Great Andamanese creole is also spoken.
