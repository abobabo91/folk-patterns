## Overview

The Minaro, also called Brokpa, are an Indo-Aryan ethnolinguistic group found mainly in the Indian-administered union territory of Ladakh, around the villages of Dha and Hanu in the lower Indus valley. Communities also live across the Line of Control in Pakistan-administered Baltistan, around Ganokh. Their language, Brokskat, is an Indo-Aryan language and a variant of Shina. The number of Brokskat speakers was estimated as 3,000 in 1996. The Brokpa are mostly Vajrayana Buddhist, with some Muslims. Their folk-culture profile in the sources is especially documented through language, settlement history, foodways, high-altitude grazing, agriculture, and the festival *Bono-na*.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention remnants of forts associated with Brokpa history. Their chiefs are believed to have ruled at Khalatse until the 12th century, and remnants of their forts can still be found there. Another group of Brokpa settled in the Turtuk region in the lower Shyok river valley, where remnants of their fort can also be found.

## Festivals & rituals

The Minaro celebrate ***Bono-na***, a festival of thanksgiving to deities for good crops and prosperity.

They state that mutton is especially accessible during festivals and rituals.

## Foodways

The traditional Brogpa diet is based on locally grown foods, including barley and hardy wheat. These are most often prepared as *tsampa* or *sattu*, described as roasted flour. Other important foods are potatoes, radishes, turnips, and *Gur-Gur Cha*, a brewed tea made from black tea, butter, and salt.

Dairy and poultry sources are not eaten because of religious taboos. Brogpa eat three meals a day: *Choalu Unis* for breakfast, *Beali* for lunch, and *Rata Unis* for dinner. Meat consumption varies, with mutton as the main meat. A household’s economic position affects how much meat is consumed, while festivals and rituals provide greater access to mutton.

## Language & religion

The Minaro speak Brokskat, an Indo-Aryan language and a variant of the Shina language spoken in the Gilgit region. Brokskat converged with Purgi to the extent of being mutually intelligible. The group uses the endonym *Minaro*. The name Brokpa is associated with the meaning “highlander,” while Ladakhis pronounce the same name as Drokpa or Dokpa.

The Brokpa are mostly Vajrayana Buddhist, although some are Muslim. The sources also state that dairy and poultry are avoided because of religious taboos.
