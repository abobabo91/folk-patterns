## Overview

The Haryanvis are an Indo-Aryan ethnolinguistic group native to Haryana in northern India. The sources describe Haryanvi as an Indo-Aryan language spoken primarily in Haryana and the territory of Delhi. Haryanvis also speak Rajasthani languages such as Ahirwati, Mewati, and Bagri. The group has a diaspora in Pakistan and in Australia, Canada, Singapore, New Zealand, Saudi Arabia, UAE, UK, and US. The sources emphasize Haryanvi folk music, dance, oral culture, cuisine, clothing, and cinema. They identify folk songs, tales, oral history, seasonal performances, ceremonial songs, named dances, instruments, and regional dishes as important parts of Haryanvi cultural life.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Traditional attire for men consists of a turban, shirt, dhoti, jutti, and a cotton or woollen shawl. Women typically wear an *Orhna*, described as a veil, together with a shirt or *Angia*, a short blouse, a *Ghagri*, a heavy long skirt, and *Jitti*. Saris are also worn. The sources state that Khaddar, a coarse cotton weave cloth, is frequently used as fabric.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do mention instruments made from or incorporating everyday materials: the *Gharha* is a pitcher with a rubber cover added on top, and the *Thali* is beaten with a stick to make music.

## Music & performance

Folk music is described as integral to Haryanvi culture. Folk music in Haryana has two main forms: classical folk music and desi folk music, or country music. These forms include ballads and songs about parting lovers, valor and bravery, harvest, happiness, seasonal life, ceremonies, and religious legendary tales.

Classical folk music is associated with Indian classical music. Hindustani classical ragas are used for singing *Alha-Khand* about the bravery of Alha and Udal, *Jaimal Fatta*, *Brahmas*, *Teej* festive songs, *Phaag* songs of the Phalgun month of Holi, and Holi songs. The Mewati gharana is described as a musical apprenticeship tradition of Hindustani classical music in the Mewat region and as the musical lineage of Pandit Jasraj.

Desi Haryanvi music uses Raag Bhairvi, Raag Bhairav, Raag Kafi, Raag Jaijaivanti, Raag Jhinjhoti, and Raag Pahadi. Ahirs also use melodic Raag Pilu. Folk performers sing seasonal songs, ballads, ceremonial songs, and religious tales. Folk singers are invited to events, ceremonies, and special occasions. The sources name the Bhats, Saangis, and Jogis as transmitters of Haryana’s folk music.

Named instruments include *Sarangi*, *Harmonium*, *Chimta*, *Dhadd*, *Dholak*, *Manjeera*, *Khartal*, *Damaru*, *Duggi*, *Daf*, *Bansuri*, *Been*, *Ghungroo*, *Dhak*, *Gharha*, *Thali*, and *Shankha*. The *Been* consists of two bamboo pipes fixed in a gourd and is associated with snake charmers. The *Iktara* is a one-stringed instrument made from bamboo with a gourd at one end and is associated with the Jogis. The *Sarangi* is a bow instrument used in Haryana’s folk and classical music, while the *Shankh* is a sacred wind instrument associated with Vishnu.

## Dance & theatre

The sources name Khoriya, Chaupaiya, Loor, Been, Ghoomar, Dhamal, Phaag, Sawan, and Gugga as Haryanvi dances. They describe Haryanvi dances as having fast, energetic movements.

*Loor* is performed by girls in traditional Haryanvi attire during the month of phalguna, in connection with the Holi festival. It uses a question-and-answer format and marks the arrival of the pleasant spring season and the sowing of rabi crops. The sources also describe *Rasa lila* and *Ragini* as folk theatrical performances in Haryana. The *Ragini* form of theatre was popularised by Lakhmi Chand.

## Festivals & rituals

Folk songs are sung on occasions including child birth, weddings, festivals, and *Satsang*, described as the singing of religious songs. The sources mention Holi, Teej, and seasonal observances connected with Phalgun, spring, and the sowing of rabi crops.

Young girls and women sing seasonal, love, relationship, and friendship songs, including *Phagan*, *Katak*, *Samman*, *Bande-bandi*, and *Sathne*. Older women sing devotional *Mangal Geet* and ceremonial songs such as *Bhajan*, *Bhat*, *Sagai*, *Ban*, *Kuan-Poojan*, *Sanjhi*, and Holi songs. *Bhat* is described as a wedding gift to the mother of the bride or groom from her brother. *Ban* is a Hindu wedding ritual in which pre-wedding festivities begin. *Kuan-Poojan* welcomes the birth of a male child by worshiping a well or source of drinking water.

The sources state that these songs are inter-caste and are sung collectively by women from different strata, castes, and dialects.

## Foodways

Haryana is described as an agricultural state producing wheat, barley, pearl millet, maize, rice, and high-quality dairy. A daily village meal consists of a simple *Thali* of *Roti*, paired with leafy stir-fry, including *Saag* dishes such as *Gajar methi* or *Aloo palak*. Condiments include *Chaas*, chutney, and pickles.

Named Haryanvi dishes include green *Choliya*, described as green chickpeas; *Bathua yogurt*; *Bajre ki roti*; *Sangri ki sabzi*, described as beans; *Kachri ki chutney*, made with wild cucumber; and *Bajre ki khichdi*. Sweets include *Panjiri* and *Pinni*, prepared with unrefined sugars such as *Bura* and *Shakkar*. *Malpua* is popular during festivals.

## Oral tradition & literature

Haryanvi has a substantial oral culture including folk songs, tales, and oral history. These are practiced by Haryanvi speakers during special occasions and in everyday life. The folk music sources name *Kissa* folklores of bravery and love, including *Nihalde Sultan*, *Sati Manorama*, *Jai Singh ki Mrityu*, and *Saran de*. They also mention religious legendary tales such as *Puran Bhagat*.

Songs address day-to-day themes and use earthy humor. Women’s songs concern seasonal life, love, relationships, friendship, devotion, weddings, birth, and festivals.

## Language & religion

Haryanvi is an Indo-Aryan language and is considered part of the Western Hindi dialect group, which also includes Khariboli and Braj. It is written in the Devanagari script; another source describes the script as a modified form of Devanagari. The Rangri dialect of Haryanvi is written in the Nastaliq variant of the Perso-Arabic script.

The sources describe Haryanvi-speaking communities in Pakistan, including Ranghar and Muley Jat Muslims. Most Ranghar are described as bilingual or multilingual, speaking Urdu as a national language, Punjabi, Saraiki, and Sindhi as regional languages, and Rāngrri as a first, mother, village, or community language. Haryanvi folk culture includes devotional songs, *Satsang*, *Bhajan*, Holi songs, and the *Shankh*, which is associated with Vishnu.
