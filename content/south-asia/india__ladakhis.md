## Overview

Ladakhis, also called Ladakhi people or Ladakspa, are an ethnic group and first-language speakers of the Ladakhi language. They live in Ladakh in India, with a small minority in Tibet in China. They describe Ladakh as a crossroad of high Asia whose contacts with neighboring cultures, empires, and technologies contributed to a distinctive regional tradition and culture.

## Music & performance

They state that the music of the Ladakh region is similar to Tibet but provide no further description.

## Dance & theatre

The sources name *khatok chenmo* and *cham* as popular dances in Ladakh.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that people in Ladakh celebrate several festivals throughout the year, including *Hemis Tsechu* and *Losar*.

## Language & religion

Ladakhi is identified as the traditional language of the region, and Ladakhis are described as first-language speakers of Ladakhi. The sources used do not classify the language by family, describe dialects, or identify historical scripts.

The religious landscape is divided between Buddhism and Islam. In the 2011 census, 76.87% of the population of Kargil was Muslim, mostly Shia, while 66.40% of the population of Leh was Buddhist. The cited totals were 140,802 for Kargil and 133,487 for Leh. Buddhism is described as the majority faith in Leh, Changthang, Zanskar, Sham, and Nubra, while Islam is the majority faith in Kargil and Drass. The sources state that the majority of Ladakhis, Changpa, and Brokpa follow Buddhism, while most Purigpa and Shina follow Islam. Brokpa, Drokpa, Dard, and Shinu tribes and Shina people predominantly follow Islam, with small minorities following Tibetan Buddhism.
