## Overview

The Tanchangya people, also called Tonchonga, Tanchangyas, or Tonchongyas, are an ethnic group living in the Chittagong Hill Tracts of Bangladesh, the Indian states of Tripura and Mizoram, and Rakhine state of Myanmar. According to the 2022 census, Bangladesh has 45,972 Tanchangyas, mainly in Rangamati District, Bandarban District, Chattogram District, and Cox's Bazar District. Their principal language is Tanchangya, described in the sources as an Indo-Aryan language, although one source notes debate about its classification and features associated with Tibeto-Burman. Agriculture, jhum cultivation, Buddhist practice, distinctive clothing, handmade musical instruments, and the festival of Bishu are central subjects in the available documentation.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Paiet Kapor** — The full dress of a Tanchangya woman is collectively known as *Paiet Kapor*, meaning “five parts.” The sources identify its components as Pinuin, Paduri, Mada-khobong, Khadi, and Chalum.

**Pinuin** — *Pinuin* is one part of Paiet Kapor and is described as having seven colors with stripes.

**Paduri** — *Paduri* is used as a belt within the five-part women’s dress.

**Mada-khobong** — *Mada-khobong* is worn on the head as part of Paiet Kapor.

**Khadi** — *Khadi* is used as a scarf within the women’s five-part dress.

**Chalum** — *Chalum* is a blouse and forms one of the five parts of Paiet Kapor.

### Clothing & dress

Traditionally, Tanchangya women wear colorful dresses and ornaments. Their full dress is called *Paiet Kapor* and consists of Pinuin, Paduri, Mada-khobong, Khadi, and Chalum. Pinuin has seven colors with stripes; Paduri serves as a belt; Mada-khobong is worn on the head; Khadi is used as a scarf; and Chalum is a blouse. Tanchangya men traditionally wear a loincloth and long-sleeved shirts.

### Architecture

They state only that Tanchangya people have at least one Buddhist vihara in their localities.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They state that Tanchangya ornaments are made mainly of silver and that, after a death, burnt bones are collected in a pot and covered with a piece of cloth.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Tanchangya ornaments include *Rajjur* and *Jhanga* for the ears; *Baghor* and *Kuchikharu* for the wrists; *Tajjur* for the arms; and *Chandrahar*, *Hochuli*, and *Sikchara* for the neck. These ornaments are made mainly of silver.

## Music & performance

Tanchangya musical instruments are described as rich in variety and handmade. The named traditional instruments are *Bansi*, *Khengkhong*, *Dhuruk*, *Singa*, and *Suma*. Tanchangya people enjoy these instruments during leisure time, particularly after harvesting paddy from shifting cultivation.

## Dance & theatre

The sources name *Ghila kala*, *Nahdeng kala*, and *Gudhu kala* as Tanchangya traditional sports.

## Festivals & rituals

Tanchangyas celebrate *Bishu* as a main enjoyable festival at the end and beginning of the new year. *Pachon*, a mixed vegetable preparation with dried fish and other ingredients, is described as a special item for Bishu. *Bishu mela* is organized in Tanchangya localities.

Tanchangya people are Buddhists and, on specific occasions, perform Buddhist ceremonies including *kathino Civara Dana*, *Buddha Purnima*, and *maghi purnima*. They have at least one Buddhist vihara in their localities.

The sources also describe funeral practices. After a death, the body is bathed and covered with a white cloth. People pray for the departed soul in the presence of monks. The eldest son or a close relative moves the body to the funeral pyre. On the next day, burnt bones are collected in a pot, covered with a piece of cloth, and thrown into a river.

## Foodways

Agriculture is the main occupation of the Tanchangyas, who practice *jhum*, a traditional slash-and-burn farming method. They cultivate paddy, ginger, garlic, and *bagurpada*, identified in the source as including coriander, on hill slopes.

*Pachon* is a special food associated with Bishu. It is described as a mixed vegetable preparation with dried fish and other ingredients.

## Language & religion

Tanchangya is primarily spoken by the Tanchangyas. One source categorizes it as an Indo-Aryan language and describes it as closely related to Chakma, Chittagonian, Noakhali, and Rohingya. Another source says that its classification is debatable and that it retains several features of Tibeto-Burman. Tanchangya contains words from Indo-Aryan languages, Burmanised words, Buddhist terminology, and words derived from Arabic, Portuguese, and English. It also has its own script.

Tanchangya people are Buddhists of the Theravadin tradition and venerate Buddha. Their documented Buddhist ceremonies include kathino Civara Dana, Buddha Purnima, and maghi purnima.
