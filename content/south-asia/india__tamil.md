## Overview

The Tamils are a Dravidian people with more than 3,800 years of continuous human occupation recorded in Tamil Nadu. They speak Tamil, one of the world's longest-surviving classical languages. Tamil culture is known for literature, temples, sculpture, music, dance, food and religious practice. Tamil-speaking communities also live across South Asia, Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Africa, the Caribbean and the Western World.

## Material culture

Tamil material culture includes silk and cotton clothing, painted textiles, grand temples, bronze images, practical wooden and metal tools, and gold jewelry.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Tamil textile traditions include decorated paintings, silk saris and regional cotton saris.

- **Thanjavur painting** — This 16th-century art uses a cloth base, dyes, semi-precious stones and silver or gold threads.
- **Kanchipuram silk sari** — This silk sari from Kanchipuram is worn by many South Indian women for weddings and other special occasions.
- **Kovai Cora cotton** — This is a cotton sari made in the Coimbatore region.
- **Flower hair pattern** (*veni*) — Veni is a pattern of flowers placed in the hair.

### Clothing & dress

Tamil dress includes draped saris, wrapped men's garments and colorful rural lungis.

- **Sari** — Women wrap this long garment around the waist and over one shoulder, usually leaving the midriff uncovered.
- **Long skirt** (*pavaadai*) — Young girls wear this long skirt with a shorter sari.
- **Short sari** (*dhavani*) — Young girls wear this shorter sari with a pavaadai.
- **Men's wrap garment** (*veshti*) — Men wrap this rectangular, often white cloth around the legs and knot it at the waist.
- **Rural wrap garment** (*lungi*) — This tubular garment has stitched ends and colorful batik patterns and is common in the countryside.

### Architecture

Dravidian temples are large complexes built around a sanctum, towers, halls and water features.

- **Sanctum** (*garbhagriha*) — The central shrine room is topped by a pyramidal tower.
- **Pyramidal tower** (*vimana*) — This tower rises above the temple sanctum.
- **Porch** (*mantapa*) — These porches lead toward the temple sanctum.
- **Gate-pyramid** (*gopuram*) — These ornate entrance towers stand around temple enclosures and are topped by finials.
- **Mahabalipuram monuments** — This Pallava complex from the 7th and 8th centuries contains more than forty rock-cut temples, monoliths and rock reliefs.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Tamil objects include temple sculpture, bronze images, ritual vessels and practical tools.

- **Chola bronze statue** — These statues were made by the lost-wax process using beeswax, sal tree resin, clay and molten metal.
- **Throwing blade** (*valari*) — This wooden object has a flattened round handle and a curved blade widening toward its flat tip.
- **Water vessel** — A brass vessel was used to carry water.
- **Betel box** — A wooden box was used for betel.
- **Trident** (*triśūla*) — This metal trident is a recorded Tamil object.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Tamil adornment includes gold jewelry, bell anklets and flowers woven into the hair.

- **Jasmine bud necklace** (*Malligai Arumbu Malai*) — This 19th-century necklace is made of gold and rubies strung on black thread.
- **Gold ear pendant** (*Kathija*) — Kathija is a gold ear pendant.
- **Bell anklets** (*salangai*) — These special anklets contain small bells and are worn with ceremonial dress.
- **Flower hair arrangement** (*veni*) — Women plait their hair in a particular way and decorate it with flowers in a veni pattern.

## Music & performance

Tamil music includes ancient systems, temple hymns, folk storytelling, percussion groups and Carnatic concerts.

- **Tamil musical system** (*Tamil Pannisai*) — This was the musical system of ancient Tamil country.
- **Temple reed instrument** (*nadaswaram*) — This reed instrument is often played with the thavil in temples and weddings.
- **Temple drum** (*thavil*) — This drum commonly accompanies the nadaswaram.
- **Percussion ensemble** (*melam*) — Melam is a group of percussion instruments played at events and functions.
- **Bow-song storytelling** (*Villu Paatu*) — This form mixes spoken storytelling with music from a string bow and other instruments.

## Dance & theatre

Tamil performance includes classical dance, folk dances and open-air theatre and puppetry.

- **Classical dance** (*Bharatanatyam*) — This dance from Tamil Nadu uses bent legs, footwork, hand gestures, mudras and expressive eyes and faces.
- **Pot-balancing dance** (*Karakattam*) — Dancers balance decorated pots on their heads.
- **Stick dance** (*Kolattam*) — Dancers perform this dance with crossed sticks.
- **Peacock dance** (*Mayilattam*) — This dance imitates a peacock.
- **Street theatre** (*Koothu*) — Koothu combines dance, music, narration and singing in open public places during festivals.

## Festivals & rituals

Tamil festivals honor harvests, water, gods, cattle and the Tamil calendar, while temples hold village-deity rituals.

- **Harvest festival** (*Pongal*) — This multi-day festival in Thai honors Surya and takes its name from rice boiled with milk and jaggery.
- **Cattle festival** (*Mattu Pongal*) — Cattle are bathed, decorated, garlanded and taken in processions.
- **Bull-holding event** (*Jallikattu*) — A bull is released into a crowd and participants try to hold its hump.
- **Tamil New Year** (*Puthandu*) — Puthandu marks the first day of the Tamil calendar in April.
- **Festival of lights** (*Karthikai Deepam*) — This festival is observed on the full moon of the Kartika month in November or December.

## Foodways

Rice is the staple, meals are often served on banana leaves, and hospitality is a major duty.

- **Lentil and vegetable sauce** (*sambar*) — Sambar is commonly served with rice.
- **Spiced broth** (*rasam*) — Rasam is commonly served with rice.
- **Vegetable dish** (*poriyal*) — Poriyal is commonly served with rice.
- **Steamed rice cake** (*idli*) — Idli is a popular Tamil dish.
- **Rice pancake** (*dosa*) — Dosa is a popular Tamil dish.

## Oral tradition & literature

Tamil literature is ancient and includes poetry, epics, ethics, religious hymns and storytelling.

- **Grammar treatise** (*Tolkāppiyam*) — This is the oldest surviving book of Tamil literature and discusses Tamil grammar.
- **Eight Anthologies** (*Ettuttokai*) — This is a collection of early Tamil literature.
- **Ten Idylls** (*Pattuppattu*) — This is a collection of early Tamil poems.
- **Ethics book** (*Tirukkural*) — This is a book on ethics by Thiruvalluvar.
- **Epic of Kannagi** (*Cilappatikaram*) — This epic tells how Kannagi took revenge after her husband Kovalan was wrongly executed.

## Language & religion

Tamil is an ancient Dravidian language, and Tamil religious life combines Hindu traditions with village worship and other faiths.

- **Tamil language** (*Tamil*) — Tamil belongs to the southern Dravidian branch and is an official language in Tamil Nadu, Puducherry, Singapore and Sri Lanka.
- **Tamil Brahmi script** (*Tamil Brahmi*) — This is one of the historical writing systems of Tamil.
- **Rounded script** (*vaṭṭeḻuttu*) — This is one of the historical writing systems of Tamil.
- **Village guardian deity** (*Ayyanar*) — Ayyanar is one of the named deities in Tamil religious life.
- **Healing system** (*Siddha medicine*) — Siddha medicine is a traditional system associated with Tamils and Siddhars.
