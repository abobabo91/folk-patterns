## Overview

The Apatani are an ethnic group of the Ziro valley, in the Lower Subansiri region of Arunachal Pradesh in northeast India. They are one of the major ethnic groups of the eastern Himalayas, and their language, *Apatani* — also called Apa Tani or *Tanw* — belongs to the Tani branch of the Sino-Tibetan family; Ethnologue records it as spoken in seven Ziro villages: Hong, Hari, Biilla, Dutta, Hija, Mudang-Tage and Bamin Michi. What distinguishes the Apatani in folk-culture terms is the integration of their material life with their land: a wet rice cultivation system worked without farm animals or machinery, a sustainable social forestry system, and village councils called *bulyañ*. UNESCO has proposed the Apatani valley for World Heritage listing for its "extremely high productivity" and its "unique" way of preserving the ecology.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources name no Apatani weaving traditions by vernacular name. What they document is that the group is known for intricate handloom designs, and that specific woven pieces survive in museum collections, described only by material and technique:

**Supplementary-weft banded cloak cloth** — A cloak of wool in yellow, brown and red, with white horizontal bands of design woven in supplementary weft technique from undyed cotton, and some indigo-dyed cotton at the edges. This wool-ground, cotton-patterned construction is the only Apatani weave structure the catalogue records describe in technical terms.

**Priest's woollen cloth and cloak** — Ceremonial cloth and cloak made of wool and cotton, held as priest's garments rather than everyday wear.

**Embroidered man's jacket** — A white cotton cloth jacket embroidered on the back in blue, yellow, black, white and brown.

Alongside the loom, the sources emphasise **cane and bamboo craft** as an Apatani skill, and the museum objects bear this out: plaited rattan, cane and vegetable fibre appear in headgear, belts, rings, quivers, sheaths and rain gear more often than textile does.

### Clothing & dress

Apatani dress in the collection divides between plaited fibre gear for outdoor and working life and woven wool-and-cotton garments for ritual and status. For men the catalogued pieces include a cotton cloth jacket, white, embroidered across the back in blue, yellow, black, white and brown, and an old man's cloak of wool and cotton. Priestly dress is a distinct category: a priest's jacket of wool and cotton, a priest's cloth and cloak of the same materials, and a priest's head-dress identified in the register as a warrior's head-dress, also of wool and cotton — the same garment type serving ritual and martial roles.

Headgear is plaited rather than woven. A cane helmet carries a neck-piece and a twisted fibre loop at the front; a composite hat is built of rattan basketry covered with black palm fibre and decorated with bamboo slivers, triangles of plaited rattan and leaves. Belts are rattan waist belts, red, with a tail plaited in undyed cane. Against the valley's rain the Apatani made a vegetable fibre rain cape worn with a carrying basket, and a rain-shield on a bamboo framework filled with a double open-work plait holding a lining of leaves — the leaves being the rain-proofing agent — fitted with an adjustable head cover, a pouch for carrying goods, head and shoulder slings, and in one example a bunch of plaited cords at the back for tying loose objects.

### Architecture

The sources describe Ziro as a living cultural landscape shaped by systematic land-use practices and a social forestry system, but they give no account of Apatani house form, building materials, roof construction or architectural ornament. The one built form they name is institutional rather than domestic: the *bulyañ*, the traditional village council, which the sources present as an organisation rather than a structure.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Apatani metalwork in the collection is confined to edged tools and weapon fittings, always composite with plaited fibre. The *dao* is a single-edged steel blade with a wooden hilt sheathed in plaited vegetable fibre strands, carried in a wooden sheath bound with plaited vegetable fibre and fitted with a sling; a related composite knife has a single-edged steel blade and a wood hilt covered with plaited vegetable fibre. There is an iron axe-head with the broken end of its wooden haft still in the socket. Archery equipment is bamboo and iron: a blackened bamboo bow with a node at the grip and a string of twisted vegetable fibre; composite arrows with barbed and tanged iron heads on wooden shafts "feathered" with leaf and nicked below the head; and a bamboo quiver with a plaited vegetable fibre cap smeared with a gummy substance and a rattan sling, mottled brown and black. Domestic objects include a bamboo pipe-bowl still holding charred tobacco and a leopard skin pouch with a sling.

Unfired clay modelling is the group's one recorded ceramic practice: light grey models of a *mithan* or bullock, each with a ridge down the back and impressed decoration on the horns and face, and a clay model of a human figure with one hand to the face and the other stretched forward, its ankles strengthened with wooden pins.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Apatani ornament is largely plaited cane and fibre rather than metal. Waist-rings come in two constructions: rings made from three pieces of cane plaited to form a pattern, and double rings of cane covered with black and buff vegetable fibre plaited to form a pattern — collected in quantity, seventeen in a single register entry. Men wore knee rings of plaited vegetable fibre in its natural colour. Ear-rings appear in both idioms: plaited vegetable fibre ear-rings, and coiled yellow metal ones, eleven in one lot. Women's cylindrical nose-plugs of wood, charred, are the collection's one item of facial ornament and the one piece marked explicitly as women's.

## Music & performance

The sources name no Apatani instruments, song genres or musical performance contexts. The only performed material they record belongs to the festival calendar: *Paku-Itu*, *Daminda* and the *Piree* dance are named as the main cultural programmes performed at the Dree festival.

## Dance & theatre

The one named Apatani dance in the sources is the *Piree* dance, performed as part of the cultural programme of the agricultural festival Dree, alongside Paku-Itu and Daminda.

## Festivals & rituals

The Apatani have two major festivals, *Dree* and *Myoko*.

**Dree** is the agricultural festival, celebrated in July with prayers for a bumper harvest and for the prosperity of all humankind. Its main cultural programmes are Paku-Itu, Daminda and the Piree dance.

**Myoko** commemorates the inter-village friendship handed down by the forefathers through the generations to the present, a bond each generation of members carries forward. The main celebration falls in mid-March, but the festival's rituals begin much earlier, in the preceding month, and end in April. Through this period the host village serves and distributes a large quantity of food and drink — the hosting village bearing the cost of the bond being renewed.

The sources describe Apatani spiritual belief systems as one of the forces, with traditional customs, that has nurtured the interdependence of people and environment in the Ziro valley. The catalogue's priest's jacket, priest's cloth and cloak and priest's head-dress indicate a ritual office with its own dress, and the unfired clay models of mithan and of a human figure sit within the same collected material, but the records do not state their use.

## Foodways

What the sources establish is the agricultural base: a wet rice cultivation system of extensive scale worked without farm animals or machines, which UNESCO has singled out for its extremely high productivity, supported by traditional ecological knowledge of natural resource management built up over centuries through informal experimentation. The one recorded consumption practice outside farming is tobacco, evidenced by a bamboo pipe-bowl containing charred tobacco. During the Myoko festival period a huge amount of food and drink is served and distributed by the host village.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources record no Apatani folktales, epics, proverbs or storytelling contexts. They document instead an active literacy movement. Apatani, like many endangered languages, had no standardised orthography until recently, and Apatanis debated which script should be used. Tanw Supuñ Dukuñ, the apex Tanii organisation, recommended constituting an Apatani Language Development Committee (ALDC), which the Apatani Cultural and Literary Society (ACLS) then formed; after a year of discussion and study the ALDC recommended an alphabet based on Roman script, the ACLS accepted it, and Tanw Supuñ Dukuñ — the Apatani Apex Council — gave administrative approval for its use. Apatanis had already been writing their language in the Roman alphabet through an English-inspired orthography, which began as a practical means of written communication and has grown into a writing system used for intergroup communication. Current initiatives include a revision project by the Popi Sarmin Society and LivingDictionaries' Talking Apatani Dictionary.

## Language & religion

Apatani — also Apa Tani or *Tanw*, and *Tanii* in the self-designation used by its language organisations — is a Tani language within the Sino-Tibetan family, spoken in seven villages of the Ziro valley in Lower Subansiri district: Hong, Hari, Biilla, Dutta, Hija, Mudang-Tage and Bamin Michi. It carries a notable classification puzzle: Post & Kanno (2013) and Macario (2015) observe that Apatani has various words that do not reconstruct to Proto-Tani, which points to a possible non-Tani substratum, and Macario lists the divergent forms. The language is on the endangered list. On religion, the sources say only that Apatani spiritual belief systems, together with traditional customs, nurtured the interdependent coexistence of people and environment in Ziro; they name no sect or deity. The priest's jacket, cloth, cloak and head-dress in the British Museum indicate a ritual specialist with dedicated dress.
