## Overview

The Chakma, or Changhma, are an ethnic group and nation native to the Indian subcontinent and western Myanmar. They are the largest indigenous group and the second largest ethnic group of the Chittagong Hill Tracts in southeastern Bangladesh. Significant populations also live in Arunachal Pradesh, Tripura, Assam and Rakhine State of Myanmar, while the Chakma form the majority in the Chakma Autonomous District Council of Mizoram. The Chakma language is an Eastern Indo-Aryan language within the Indo-European family and has nearly 1 million speakers. Chakma identity is associated with Theravada Buddhism, the Chakma script, historical political institutions headed by the Chakma Raja, and a long record of movement through Myanmar, Arakan and the Chittagong Hill Tracts.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Historical passages do record capitals and settlements associated with Chakma political authority, including Shukbilash, Rajanagar and Rangamati, as well as ruins of a royal palace and other historic buildings at Shukbilash.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The language article does record manuscripts written on handmade tree-bark paper, with ink sourced from China via Myanmar.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The historical material mentions a Kadu tradition of wearing heavy earrings and stretching the lobes, but it presents this as a practice associated with the Kadu rather than as a documented Chakma custom.

## Music & performance

Chakma manuscripts displayed at a seminar in Pecharthal, Tripura, are said to cover history, culture, music, herbal medicine and Buddhism. More than 3,000 such manuscripts are reported, with some conserved by Tripura University.

They state that Chakma culture includes literary materials preserved by the community and that the manuscripts were written in Chakma script on handmade tree-bark paper. No catalogue record or museum object supplied with the sources documents a Chakma musical instrument.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that most modern Chakma people practice Theravada Buddhism, attributing this religious predominance to reforms and institutionalisation associated with Queen regnant Rani Kalindi in the 19th century.

The sources also connect Chakma historical identity with Buddhist traditions. The Rājnāmā presents a lineage claim linking Chakma chiefs with the Śākya clan of the Buddha, and describes Sudhanya as joining the Sangha. These accounts are presented as part of a claimed historical tradition rather than as a documented calendar of contemporary ritual practice.

## Foodways

The historical sources mention yam and cotton crops in the Chittagong Hill Tracts.

One Chakma migration narrative explains dispersed populations through a story in which one camp took too long to cook its fish and was left behind. The sources present this as folklore and do not provide a recipe, named preparation or broader food tradition.

## Oral tradition & literature

Chakma historical traditions include narratives of migration, kingship and political descent. Chakma-Kormi wrote the Rājnāmā, or Chronicle of Chakma Kings, in the Bengali language in 1941. Chakma-Korni claimed that the document had been written in the 19th century by a pandit named Śaṅkarācārya. The Rājnāmā traces Chakma chiefs to mythical times, including the Lunar Dynasty, and names figures such as Abhiratha, Sudhanya, Langaldhana, Shyamala and Champakali.

Chakma traditions also preserve accounts of Arakan, Macchagiri, Mangthi, Yengcho and the movement of groups toward the Chittagong Hill Tracts. A migration story explains dispersed populations by describing a camp that cooked fish too slowly and was left behind. The sources state that such folklore is used among the Saks of Manipur and the Daingnaks of Sittwe.

The Chakma community has preserved nearly century-old manuscripts written in Chakma script on handmade tree-bark paper. These texts cover history, culture, music, herbal medicine and Buddhism, and some are conserved by Tripura University.

## Language & religion

Chakma is an Eastern Indo-Aryan language in the Bengali-Gauda subgroup of the Indo-Iranian branch of the Indo-European family. It has nearly 1 million speakers: 60% reside in the Chittagong Hill Tracts, 35% are spread across Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Mizoram and Tripura, and the remaining 5% live in Myanmar. Chakma is mutually intelligible with Chittagonian, Tanchangya, Noakhali and Rohingya.

The language has its own script, the Chakma script or *ajhapat*, an abugida belonging to the Brahmic family. The sources state that Chakma evolved from the Burmese script, which was ultimately derived from Pallava. Chakma is notably tonal, and this feature is described as a substrate influence from an earlier Tibeto-Burman language. The sources state that the Chakmas historically underwent a language shift from a Tibeto-Burman language toward their current Indo-Aryan language.

Most modern Chakma people practice Theravada Buddhism. The sources associate this religious landscape with reforms and institutionalisation by Queen regnant Rani Kalindi. They also record a Chakma tradition connecting the group with the Śākya clan of the Buddha.
