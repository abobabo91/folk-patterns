## Overview

The Asur are a very small Austroasiatic ethnic group living primarily in the Indian state of Jharkhand, especially in the Gumla, Lohardaga, Palamu, and Latehar districts. Their language, Asur or Asuri, belongs to the Munda branch of the Austroasiatic family. Asuri is also spoken by smaller groups in Chhattisgarh, West Bengal, and Odisha. The Asur are associated particularly with iron-smelting, an indigenous technology that forms an important part of their identity. They were once hunter-gatherers and also practiced shifting agriculture, although the majority later moved toward agriculture. Their language is considered “definitely endangered,” and the community faces difficulties connected with limited access to health services, education, transportation, and drinking water, as well as the decline of iron-smelting and the effects of bauxite mining.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Traditional male clothing is the *dhoti*. The sources describe women’s tattoo marks as ornaments depicting totemic objects. Women also wear metal and non-metal ornaments and glass bangles.

### Architecture

The Asur live in *pats*, described as clearing areas surrounded by forest. Their houses have mud walls supported by wooden poles, with roofs covered by paddy straw and self-baked *khapras*, or tiles. Houses include spaces for cattle and poultry and a separate area for the worship of ancestors.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Iron-smelting is a central Asur occupation and an important expression of their identity. The sources describe iron, aluminium, and earthenware utensils used for cooking and storing water drawn from wells. Asurs use common agricultural implements for cultivation and sometimes bows and arrows for hunting game in the forest.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Asur women wear tattoo marks depicting totemic objects, using them as ornaments. They also wear metal and non-metal ornaments and glass bangles.

## Music & performance

During iron-smelting, Asur women sing a song that relates the furnace to an expectant mother. The song encourages the furnace to give birth to a healthy “baby,” meaning good-quality and plentiful iron from the ore. The source associates this practice with a fertility cult. No instrument, song genre, performance venue, or additional musical tradition is documented in the sources used.

## Festivals & rituals

The Asur celebrate Sarhul, Karma, Dhanbuni, Kadelta, Rajj karma, and Dasahara Karam. Marriage is described as an important ritual that comes essentially in the life of every individual. The Asur follow monogamy, while bigamy or polygamy may be practiced in cases of barrenness, widowhood, or widowerhood. Widow remarriage is permitted, and marriage follows the rule of tribe endogamy. Those who disobey these rules may be expelled from the community but can be readmitted after paying a feast seven times over to community members.

Asur religion includes ancestral worship, and houses contain a separate area for the worship of ancestors. Except in emergency cases, Asurs use traditional herbal medicines. Disputes are settled by a community council called the *jati panch*.

## Foodways

 They state that the Asur accept food from Rajputs, Oraon, Kharwar, Thakur, Ghasi, and a few others.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They do describe the song sung by Asur women during iron-smelting, in which the furnace is addressed as an expectant mother and encouraged to produce good-quality and plentiful iron. The sources also state that a young Asur woman called Sushma Asur is working to preserve the art, culture, and existence of her community.

## Language & religion

Asuri is an Austroasiatic language of the Munda branch, spoken by the Asur people. It has many Dravidian loanwords through contact with Kurukh. The majority of Asuri speakers reside in the Gumla district of Chota Nagpur; smaller groups are found in Chhattisgarh, West Bengal, and Odisha. Asuri is considered “definitely endangered,” partly because it has no written form and exists only as a spoken language. It has 31 phonemes: twenty-six segmental and five suprasegmental phonemes, including twenty-one consonants and five vowels. The language is listed in the UNESCO Interactive Atlas of the World’s Languages in Danger. Mobile radio is used to spread the language through broadcasts of news and entertainment programmes in public places.

Asur religion combines animism, animatism, naturalism, and ancestral worship. It also includes beliefs in *bhut-pret*, or spirits, and witchcraft. The chief deity is Singbonga; other named deities are Dharati Mata, Duari, Patdaraha, and Turi Husid. Asurs identify Mahishasura of Durga Maa as a benevolent ancestor and mourn during the Durga Puja period, which they understand as the unjust killing of their ancestor.
