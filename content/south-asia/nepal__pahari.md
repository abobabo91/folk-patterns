## Overview

Pahari communities live in the hills and Himalayan foothills of Nepal. Their culture includes terrace farming, wool work, painting, music, dance, and caste-based social organization. Most speak Eastern Pahari languages, including Nepali. Religious life includes Hindu, shamanistic, oracular, animistic, Muslim, and Buddhist practices.

## Material culture

Pahari material culture centers on wool textiles, painting, pottery, woodwork, mountain clothing, and food made from local ingredients.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Pahari textile and painting traditions include wool weaving, natural dyes, wall paintings, and miniature art.

- **Kullu shawls** — These woolen shawls are known for their patterns and natural dyes.
- **Chamba rumals** — These handkerchiefs are a named Pahari textile tradition.
- **Woolen carpets and blankets** — Handlooms produce woolen carpets and blankets, with most Paharis spinning wool and lower castes weaving fabrics.
- **Bangadwari** — These traditional nuptial wall paintings retain features of 16th-century Northern Indian art.
- **Pahari painting** — This miniature-painting tradition developed from the 17th through the 19th centuries in lower Himalayan hill kingdoms.

### Clothing & dress

Pahari clothing is made for the cold mountain climate and includes woolen garments, cloaks, shawls, scarves, and caps.

- **Long cloaks** (*chogas*) — Men wear chogas, or long cloaks.
- **Shawls and pashmina scarves** — Women drape shawls or pashmina scarves.
- **Himachali caps** — These regional caps are marked by visible colors and geometric patterns.
- **Loose winter gown** (*pheran*) — In Kashmir, the pheran is a loose winter gown worn by men and women.

### Architecture

The profile gives little information about Pahari houses but names painted buildings, temples, and fortresses.

- **Double-storey painted buildings** — Basohli paintings show double-storey building structures in square-format backgrounds.
- **Decorative temple towers** (*shikharas*) — Basohli compositions include elaborate shikharas.
- **Krimchi temples** — The temples of Krimchi are mentioned among buildings decorated with mural paintings under the Dogras.
- **Babbor temples** — The temples of Babbor are mentioned among buildings decorated with mural paintings under the Dogras.
- **Bahu fort** — The fort at Bahu is mentioned as a building decorated with mural paintings under the Dogras.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pahari crafts include pottery, wood carving, and woodwork, while some lower-caste Paharis work as goldsmiths and leather workers.

- **Pottery** — Pottery is named as a Pahari craft, but no specific forms are described.
- **Wood carving** — Wood carving is named as a Pahari craft.
- **Woodwork** — Woodwork is named as a Pahari craft.
- **Goldsmithing** — Lower-caste Paharis are associated with goldsmithing.
- **Leather working** — Lower-caste Paharis are associated with leather working.

- **Painted jewelry** — Pahari paintings include jewelry rendered with fine brushstrokes, especially in Kangra compositions.

## Music & performance

Music has a central role in Pahari celebrations, festivals, and rituals, with songs about nature, love, separation, and devotion.

- **Double-sided drum** (*dhol*) — The dhol is a double-sided drum used for folk songs.
- **Kettledrum** (*nagara*) — The nagara is a kettledrum used for folk songs.
- **Flute** — The flute is used for Pahari folk songs.
- **Jingling percussion instrument** (*chimta*) — The chimta is a percussion instrument with jingling discs.

## Dance & theatre

Nati is a slow, rhythmic Pahari dance performed in lines during festivals and celebrations.

- **Nati dance** (*Nati*) — Nati features dancers moving in unison to traditional music.

## Festivals & rituals

Pahari rituals include harvest festivals, Muslim festivals, varied funeral customs, and diverse marriage practices.

- **Vaisakhi** — Paharis in districts such as Rajouri and Poonch observe Vaisakhi as a harvest festival.
- **Eid** — Paharis in Jammu and Kashmir observe Muslim religious festivals such as Eid.
- **Spirit-world burial** (*Jhankri*) — Indigenous healers called Jhankri are buried with their fontanelle pierced so their spirit can rise to the spirit world.
- **Paramour marriage practice** (*Jari*) — Jari involves a woman taking a paramour and leaving her first husband.
- **Compensation payment** (*Jar dine*) — The second husband pays the first husband Jar dine for the loss of his wife.

## Foodways

Pahari food uses local ingredients and includes lentils, rice, vegetables, dairy products, meat, fish, and regional spices.

- **Chicken in milk and yogurt** (*pahari murga*) — Pahari murga is chicken cooked with milk and yogurt and seasoned with coriander, ginger, garlic, cardamom, cinnamon, and fennel.
- **Black gram dish** (*chainsoo*) — Chainsoo is a lentil dish from Garhwal made from black gram.
- **Stuffed fried bread** (*Babru*) — Babru is a poori filled with cooked black gram dal and deep-fried.
- **Chickpeas in yogurt gravy** (*Chana madra*) — Chana madra is chickpeas slow-cooked in yogurt-based gravy with cardamom, cloves, turmeric, and cumin.
- **Steamed stuffed bread** (*Siddu*) — Siddu is steamed wheat bread stuffed with lentils or vegetables.

## Oral tradition & literature

Pahari ballads, folk tales, and dialect traditions have been passed on orally but are at risk of loss.

- **Ballads** — Pahari ballads have traditionally been transmitted orally.
- **Folk tales** — Pahari folk tales have traditionally been transmitted orally.
- **Radha and Krishna love stories** (*Radha and Krishna*) — The eternal love of Radha and Krishna is the central theme of Pahari painting.
- **Ramayana** — The Ramayana is one of the literary and religious subjects used in Pahari painting.
- **Gita Govinda** — The Gita Govinda is one of the literary and religious subjects used in Pahari painting.

## Language & religion

Pahari refers to languages and communities in the lower Himalayas, with Nepalese Paharis mainly speaking Eastern Pahari languages and following varied religious traditions.

- **Nepali** — Nepali is an Indo-Aryan language spoken by Pahari people in Nepal.
- **Matwali Khasa Chhetris** (*Matwali*) — Matwali Khasa Chhetris are described as shamanistic and oracular.
- **Dvija castes** (*Dvija*) — Dvija are the higher clean Hindu castes, including Bahun and Chhetri.
- **Dalit castes** (*Dalit*) — Dalit are the lower unclean castes described in the profile.
- **Bahun and Chhetri** — Bahun and Chhetri are included among the Dvija castes.
