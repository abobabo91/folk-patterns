## Overview

The Yakkha, also called Dewan, are an indigenous ethnic group of the Indian subcontinent, mainly associated with Nepal and India. In Nepal they inhabit the lower Arun valley in the east, particularly the southern part of Sankhuwasabha district and the northern part of Dhankuta district. The Yakkha homeland lies within the historic Kirat region east of the Kathmandu valley. The 2021 Nepal census recorded 17,460 Yakkhas, while the 2011 census recorded 24,336. Their language belongs to the Eastern Kiranti languages within the Tibeto-Burman language family and is described as nearly extinct in the group article. The Yakkhas are subsistence farmers who practice the Kirati religion of nature worship and maintain a distinct language, culture and tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that the Yakkhas practice the Kirati religion of nature worship. They also state that marriages do not occur between families sharing the same *Sameychong*, a sub-group within a Yakkha family name, but provide no further account of wedding or marriage ritual.

## Foodways

The group article identifies the Yakkhas as subsistence farmers.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention Yakkha historical and cultural publications, including *Kirat Yakkha Ko Itihas Ek Chhalphal*, described as a discussion on the history of the Kirat Yakkha, and *Yakkha Jatiko Samskar ra Samskriti*, described as Yakkha Indigenous People’s Tradition and Culture.

## Language & religion

Yakkha is a Tibeto-Burman language belonging genealogically to the Eastern Kiranti languages. It is grouped with several Limbu languages, including Belhare, Athpare, Chintang and Chulung, while Yakkha people perceive themselves as distinct from other Kiranti groups such as Limbu. The language is spoken in parts of Nepal, Darjeeling district and Sikkim. Its villages include areas east of the Arun River, in southern Sankhuwasabha district and northern Dhankuta district. The sources identify three named varieties or forms of speech: *Mugali*, spoken around Mugakhola and Sinuwakhola; *Phangduwali*, spoken above the Mugakhola headwaters; and *Lumba-Yakkha*, spoken in Arkhaule Jitpur and Marek Katahare. Yakkha has rich nominal and verbal morphology, including case and number marking, and marks non-singular common nouns with the suffixing clitic *=ci*. The Yakkhas practice the Kirati religion of nature worship.
