## Overview

The Dangaria Kandha are members of the Kondhs who live in the Niyamgiri hills of Odisha, India. They sustain themselves from forest resources through horticulture and shifting cultivation. The Dongria Kondh community numbers approximately 8,000 people and inhabits about 100 villages. Niyamgiri is a densely forested hill range spread over 250 km2, with deep gorges and cascading streams; its highest point is Niyamgiri, or Niyam Dongar, at 1,306m. The community uses Kui, a spoken language that is not written. Their folk-culture significance in the sources lies in their relationship with the forests, hills, streams, clan territories, customary institutions, beliefs, and opposition to mining development.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name triangular designs found on village shrines, reflecting mountains.

### Architecture

They state that the community has more than 300 settlements or hamlets within its clan territories and that these settlements are not necessarily permanent, because communities may abandon houses in search of new locations within their habitat region.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention agricultural land, horticulture, shifting cultivation, and the forest resources on which the community sustains itself.

## Music & performance

They describe village shrines dedicated to the many gods of the village, farm, and forests, and state that art reflects the mountains through triangular designs.

## Festivals & rituals

The Dangaria Kandha worship Niyam Raja, also called Niyamraja, described as the supreme god of the Niyamgiri jungle and the source of their essential resources. Respect for their gods, hills, and streams pervades their lives. The sources mention village shrines to the gods of the village, farm, and forests.

The community’s social and political organization includes the Kutumba, divided between the Kuda Kutumba at clan level and the Nayu Kutumba at settlement level. Four functional groups, or punjas, address religious and political matters: jani, pujari, bismajhi, and mandal. The sources also describe youth dormitories used to impart cultural and traditional values to adolescents and youths. They state that women have equal status in matters including widow remarriage and possession of property without interference from husbands and sons.

## Foodways

They state only that the community sustains itself from the resources of the Niyamgiri forests and practices horticulture and shifting cultivation, also called podu.

## Oral tradition & literature

They identify Kui as the language used by the people of Niyamgiri and state that it is spoken but not written.

## Language & religion

The people of Niyamgiri use Kui language.

The Dangaria Kandha worship Niyam Raja, or Niyamraja, the supreme god of the Niyamgiri jungle. They regard Niyam Raja as the source of their essential resources and show deep reverence for their gods, hills, and streams. Their religious and political affairs are addressed through the punjas—jani, pujari, bismajhi, and mandal—within the Kutumba system.
