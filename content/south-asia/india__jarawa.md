## Overview

The Jarawa are one of four surviving Andamanese tribes, numbering around 270 to 400. They live in the Andaman Islands and have historically kept to themselves, so much of their culture remains poorly known. What is documented comes mainly from museum collections of hunting tools, baskets, and ornaments. They live by hunting wild pigs and fishing with bows and arrows, gathering forest foods, and now receive government support.

## Material culture

Jarawa material culture centers on hunting and gathering tools made from wood, bone, iron, plant fiber, cane, and feathers, with little evidence of pottery or woven textiles.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Jarawa make no woven textiles but work extensively with plaited and twined plant fiber, cane, leaf, and bark cordage.

- **String and cordage** — Bags, fishing-nets, and arrow bindings are made from vegetable fiber cord and bark twine.
- **Cane and leaf plaiting** — Baskets, buckets, and work containers combine cane, bark, rattan, orchid stem, and leaf.

### Clothing & dress

Little is documented about everyday dress, but hunting gear and decorated head-bands are known.

- **Hunting chest guard** (*kekad*) — Worn by Jarawa men when hunting or on raids.
- **Feather head-band** — Made of cane bark and bark twine set with king crow feathers.
- **Drongo tiara** — A string head-band decorated with feathers of the racket-tailed drongo, sometimes substituting orchid stem for feathers.
- **Pandanus leaf belt** — A woven belt held in the British Museum collection.

### Architecture

No Jarawa house forms or settlement layouts are documented; knowledge comes only from indirect observation of reserve settlements and nearby Indian facilities.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The documented object repertoire is almost entirely hunting, fishing, and household tools made from wood, iron, cane, and fiber.

- **Bow** (*aao*) — Made from chuiood wood (Sageraea elliptica), used for hunting and fishing.
- **Arrow** (*patho*) — A wooden arrow with Areca wood head, bound with wax, cord, or bark.
- **Iron-headed arrow** (*aetaho*) — Combines an iron head with Areca wood or bamboo shaft, bound with wax and bark cord.
- **Adze** — An iron blade lashed to a wooden handle with rattan or bark cord for woodworking.
- **String fishing-net** — A woman's tool made of vegetable fiber cord for catching fish in shallow water.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ornaments are made from cord, twine, string, and feathers rather than metal or stone; feather head-dresses are the most elaborate pieces.

- **Cord arm ornament** — A simple arm band made of cord.
- **Twine and feather head ornament** — Decorative head piece combining twine with feathers.
- **String garter** — A neck or leg ornament of string.
- **King crow feather head-band** — An elaborately decorated head-band using king crow feathers with twine.

## Music & performance

No Jarawa instruments, songs, or musical traditions are documented.

## Dance & theatre

No named dance or dramatic traditions are documented; a 2012 incident of coerced tourist dancing does not represent tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

No festival calendar or life-cycle rites are documented for the Jarawa.

## Foodways

The Jarawa are hunter-forager-fishermen drawing from forest and sea, with a diet of wild pigs, fish, molluscs, turtles, eggs, honey, and gathered fruits and tubers.

- **Bow and arrow fishing** — Men fish in shallow water using bows and arrows; women catch fish with baskets.
- **Pig hunting** — Jarawa hunt endemic wild pigs with bows and arrows, recently aided by dogs.
- **Honey gathering** — Jarawa collect wild honey from nests, using a plant extract to calm the bees.
- **Marine diet** — Molluscs, dugongs, turtles, and turtle eggs are major food sources.
- **Forest gathering** — Jarawa collect fruit, tubers, yams, larvae, jackfruit, wild citrus, and wild berries from the forest.

## Oral tradition & literature

No Jarawa folktales, epic poetry, or proverbs are documented, but tribal memory recalls the extinct Jangil as a parent tribe.

- **Jangil ancestry memory** — The Jarawa regard the now-extinct Jangil as a parent tribe from which they split centuries or millennia ago.

## Language & religion

Jarawa is an Ongan (Andamanese) language; no spiritual or religious practices are documented.

- **Jarawa language** (*Ongan or Jarawa-Onge*) — An Andamanese language typologically distinct from the Greater Andamanese and unrelated to the Jarawa Bantu language of Nigeria.

## Glossary

- *aao* — Jarawa bow, made from chuiood wood
- *patho* — Jarawa arrow with wooden head
- *aetaho* — Iron-headed arrow
- *kekad* — Chest guard worn for hunting or raids
- *chuiood* — Sageraea elliptica, the wood used for bows
- *Aong* — The Jarawa name for themselves
- *Ongan* — The subfamily of Andamanese languages spoken by the Jarawa
- *Jangil* — An extinct Andamanese tribe regarded by Jarawa as ancestral
- *pandanus* — A plant whose leaf is woven into belts
- *racket-tailed drongo* — A bird whose feathers decorate head-bands and tiaras
- *orchid stem* — Plant material used in plaiting and head-dress decoration
- *rattan* — Climbing palm fiber used in binding and plaiting
- *Areca wood* — Wood used for arrow heads
