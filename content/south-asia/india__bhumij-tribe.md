## Overview

The Bhumij, also transliterated as Bhumuj, are a Munda ethnic group of India. They primarily live in West Bengal, Odisha, Assam and Jharkhand, especially in the old Singhbhum district, and are also found in Bihar and Bangladesh. In India they are concentrated in East Singhbhum and Saraikela Kharsawan in Jharkhand; Purulia, Bankura, Midnapore and 24 Parganas in West Bengal; and Mayurbhanj, Sundargarh, Keonjhar and Balasore in Odisha. In Bangladesh they live in Sylhet, Rajshahi, Khulna, Srimangal, Dhaka and Chittagong. Their language belongs to the Munda subfamily of the Austroasiatic languages, and they use Ol Onal script. Bhumij culture includes the martial art Firkal, village-based social organization, Sarnaism and historical participation in the Chuar revolt.

## Music & performance

They state that the Bhumij practice the martial art Firkal, although it has been reduced to a single village among the Bhumij who perform it.

## Dance & theatre

The sources identify Gambhir Singh Mura as a tribal Chhau dancer.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that followers of Sarnaism among the Bhumij have organized protests and petitions seeking recognition of their religion by the government of India in census forms. The sources also describe Bhumij succession and inheritance as following Hindu practices.

## Oral tradition & literature

The historical material records the Chuar revolt, a series of peasant rebellions that began between 1766 and 1816 in the countryside surrounding the West Bengali settlements of Midnapore, Bankura and Manbhum. Bhumij zamindars who supported the rebels included Jagannath Singh of Dhalbhum, Durjan Singh of Raipur, Baidyanath Singh of Dhalbhum, Mangal Singh of Panchet, Lakshman Singh of Barabhum, Raghunath Singh of Dhalbhum, Ganga Narayan of Barabhum, Rani Shiromani of Karnagarh, Raja Madhu Singh of Manbhum, Subal Singh of Kuilapal, Shyam Ganjam Singh of Dhadka, Raja Mohan Singh of Juriah, Lakshman Singh of Dulma, Sunder Narayana Singh and Phateh Singh.

## Language & religion

Bhumij is a Munda language within the Austroasiatic language family and is related to Ho, Mundari and Santali. It is spoken mainly in Jharkhand, Odisha and West Bengal, by around 100,000 people in India. Many Bhumij in West Bengal and Orissa speak Bengali and Odia respectively as their first language, while those living nearer to the Chota Nagpur Plateau retain linguistic links with Mundari. In Dhalbhum they are described as completely Hinduised. Bhumij uses Ol Onal script, and in January 2019 it was accorded the status of a second language in Jharkhand. The Bhumij follow Hindu practices of succession and inheritance, while followers of Sarnaism seek recognition of their religion in government census forms.
