## Overview

The Chaks are a community inhabiting the Chittagong Hill Tracts of Bangladesh and the northeastern states of India. In Myanmar they are known as Thet people and are classified under the Rakhine subgroup. Their history in the Chittagong Hill Tracts is connected with migration after their kingdom was overrun by the Arakanese in the 14th century; Chaks also live in Arakan. By the mid-13th century, the Saks had diverged from the Kadu people, who now reside in northwestern Myanmar’s Sagaing Region. The Chak language, also called Sak, Cak, or Tsak, is a Sino-Tibetan language of the Sal branch.

## Language & religion

Sak, also known as Cak, Chak, or Tsak, is a Sino-Tibetan language of the Sal branch spoken in Bangladesh and Myanmar by the Chak people. It is spoken in Bangladesh by about 3,000 people and in Rakhine State by about 1,000 people according to Ethnologue. In Bangladesh, it is spoken in Baishari, Naikhyongchari, and Dochari; in Rakhine State it is spoken in Maungdaw, Buthidaung, Rathedaung, and Mrauk U townships. The Baishari dialect is described as the most conservative. Sak uses a decimal-based numeral system, with an indigenous set of numerals and another set borrowed from Arakanese. A new script was devised by Mong Mong Cak and disseminated in 2013; previously, Bengali and Burmese scripts were used on an ad hoc basis.
