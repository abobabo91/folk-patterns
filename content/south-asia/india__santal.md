## Overview

The Santal are the largest tribal community in Jharkhand and West Bengal. They speak Santali and live across eastern India, northern Bangladesh, and Nepal. Their culture includes village councils, sacred groves, festivals, music, dance, puppetry, carved houses, ritual objects, and painted scrolls. Marriage, ancestor spirits, and village spirits are important parts of social and religious life.

## Material culture

Santal material culture includes carved houses, palanquins, drums, agricultural tools, metal ornaments, ritual objects, puppets, and painted scrolls.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not document named Santal textile traditions.

### Clothing & dress

The sources give little information about everyday or ceremonial clothing.

- **Ritual chest thread** (*poeta*) — Poeta are people who wear a thread on the chest during worship.
- **Ritual friendship** (*phul*) — Phul is a ritual friendship with members of other ethnic groups.

### Architecture

Santal homes and village spaces combine carved decoration with sacred places for spirits.

- **Sacred grove** (*Jaher*) — Jaher is a sacred grove at the edge of a village where spirits live and annual festivals take place.
- **Ancestor mound** (*Majhi Than*) — Majhi Than is a raised earth mound with a thatched roof outside the headman’s house, linked to the spirits of his ancestors.
- **Bridal palanquin** — Santal bridal palanquins are finely carved.
- **Carved house walls** — Traditional Santal house walls show carved animals, hunting scenes, dancing scenes, and geometric patterns.
- **Village council** (*manjhi*) — Local affairs are handled by a village council led by a manjhi.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources describe a few tools, weapons, and ritual objects but do not give a broad account of Santal craftwork.

- **Battle-axe** — A museum catalogue records a battle-axe with a dark wood handle and an iron head.
- **Arrow** — A museum catalogue records an arrow with a pale wood head and bamboo shaft.
- **Oil press** — A model wooden oil-press is made from horizontal and vertical wooden pieces.
- **Painted scroll** (*pata*) — A painted pata scroll shows a tiger’s god, a shrine, dancers, warriors, and musicians.
- **Rice-beer vessel** (*handi*) — A handi is used to hold rice-beer during marriage negotiations.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Recorded Santal-associated ornaments include metal anklets, armlets, necklets, and decorated ear-plugs.

- **White-metal anklet** — A woman’s anklet is made of white metal.
- **Bronze anklets** — Bronze anklets have interlocking sections and incised geometric or floral patterns.
- **Bronze armlets** — Bronze armlets have ridges, punched patterns, and square rosettes.
- **Bronze necklet** — A broad bronze necklet has geometric patterns.
- **Decorated ear-plug** — A palm-leaf ear-plug is rolled into a cylinder and covered with gold and green foil and small red seeds.

## Music & performance

Santal music remains closely tied to dance and festivals, with drums, flutes, lutes, and other instruments.

- **Two-headed drum** (*Tumdak’*) — The Tumdak’ is a hand-struck, two-headed drum with a clay body and leather straps.
- **Kettle drum** (*Tamak’*) — The Tamak’ is a single-headed kettle drum struck with two sticks and has special spiritual meaning and power.
- **Seven-hole flute** (*Tirio*) — The Tirio is a bamboo instrument with seven holes.
- **One-string bowed lute** (*Dhodro banam*) — The Dhodro banam is a one-stringed bowed lute often carved with human-like heads.
- **Plucked substitute instrument** (*kabkubi*) — The kabkubi is a one-stringed plucked instrument used by Christian Santal musicians instead of the Tamak’.

## Dance & theatre

Santal dance is linked to festival music, while puppetry and painted scrolls provide forms of folk performance.

- **Santal puppetry** (*Chadar Badar*) — Chadar Badar is a folk show with wooden puppets placed in a small cage that serves as the stage.
- **Festival dance accompaniment** — Santal dances are traditionally accompanied by the Tamak’ and Tumdak’.
- **Painted performance scenes** (*pata*) — Pata scrolls show dancers, musicians, worshippers, warriors, and a tiger’s god.

## Festivals & rituals

Sohrai is the principal Santal festival, and festivals take place around sacred groves, spirits, music, dance, and marriage rites.

- **Principal festival** (*Sohrai*) — Sohrai is the principal festival of the Santal community.
- **Harvest festival tunes** (*Sohrae*) — Tunes used for the Sohrae harvest festival are called Sohrae.
- **Spring festival** (*Baha*) — Baha is one of the important Santal festivals named in the sources.
- **Marriage rite** (*kesimek’*) — In kesimek’, families investigate the couple’s intentions, consult the bride and groom, and negotiate a bride price.
- **Ancestor’s marriage** (*hapramko bapla*) — Hapramko bapla is the most elaborate of the seven recognised kinds of Santal marriage.

## Foodways

The sources mention rice-beer in marriage customs but do not describe wider Santal foodways.

- **Rice-beer** (*handi*) — Handi is rice-beer given to guests during kesimek’ marriage negotiations.
- **Shared rice-beer** (*pera hor handi*) — Pera hor handi is shared when the bride returns to her natal village.

## Oral tradition & literature

Santal oral tradition tells of migrations, creation, ancestral figures, and the beginnings of the clans.

- **Place of origin** (*Hihiri*) — Santal folklore says that the people came from Hihiri, identified by scholars as Ahuri in Hazaribagh district.
- **Creator figure** (*Marang Buru*) — Marang Buru appears in the Santal creation story.
- **Creation figure** (*Malan Budhi*) — Malan Budhi appears in the Santal creation story.
- **First ancestor** (*Pilchu Haram*) — Pilchu Haram appears in the Santal creation story.
- **First ancestress** (*Pilchu Budhi*) — Pilchu Budhi appears in the Santal creation story.

## Language & religion

Santali is a Munda language, and Santal religious life centers on spirits that connect the visible and invisible worlds.

- **Village and household spirits** (*bonga*) — Bonga are spirits linked to villages, households, ancestors, and sub-clans.
- **Visible world** (*noa puri*) — Noa puri is the visible world.
- **Invisible reality** (*hana puri*) — Hana puri is the invisible reality.
- **Creator** (*Thakur Jiu*) — Thakur Jiu is another name for the creator Marang Buru.
- **Religion based on truths** (*Sari Dharam*) — Sari Dharam is described as a religion based on truths.
