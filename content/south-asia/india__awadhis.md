## Overview

The Awadhi people, or Awadhis, are an Indo-Aryan ethno-linguistic group who speak the Awadhi language and reside in the Awadh region of Uttar Pradesh. Many Awadhis migrated to Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand, Gujarat, Maharashtra, and Odisha in India, as well as adjoining regions of the Terai in Nepal. Awadhi people are also found throughout the world, notably in Fiji, Guyana, Mauritius, South Africa, Suriname, Trinidad and Tobago, the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, the Netherlands, Australia, and New Zealand. As of 2011, Awadhi was spoken by just over 4 million people. Historically, Indo-Aryans dominated the North Indian Gangetic Planes, and the Awadhi language continuously evolved over the centuries in the Awadh region of Uttar Pradesh.

## Foodways

Awadhi cuisine consists of vegetarian and non-vegetarian dishes and has influences linked to the Mughal Empire. It is similar to much of North Indian cuisine and Pakistani cuisine. The cuisine is known for aromatics and spices such as cardamom and saffron, used in a slow-fire cooking process. Ingredients include paneer and mutton, and Awadhi cuisine also includes rice, curry, dessert, and chaat preparations specific to the cuisine.

## Language & religion

Awadhi is an Indo-Aryan language spoken by the Awadhis. As of 2011, it was spoken by just over 4 million people; the population speaking Awadhi in India was approximately 3.85 million, while native speakers in Nepal were estimated at 500,000. Linguistically, Awadhi has its own grammar and is a distinct language, although the Indian government considers it a dialect under Hindi for political reasons. Awadhi is not formally taught in any institution.
