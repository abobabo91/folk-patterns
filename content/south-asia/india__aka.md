## Overview

The Aka, also known as Hrusso, are an ethnic group native to Arunachal Pradesh in India. They live in areas including Thrizino Bhalukpong, Buragaon, Jamiri, Palizi and Khuppi in West Kameng. Their language belongs to the Tibeto-Burman family. The Aka practise shifting cultivation, rear domestic animals such as the Mithun, and grow maize, millet, leaves, pulses, potato and rice. Their material culture includes bamboo, wood and cane-leaf houses, basket weaving, wood carving, indigenous clothing, silver ornaments and haversacks decorated with the Jana flower design. Village leadership, festivals, Animist beliefs, and contacts with Vaishnava, Hindu, Buddhist and Tibetan traditions also shape Aka cultural life.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Jana flower design** — The Jana flower is a Chinese design found on indigenous Aka haversacks. Aka tradition connects the design with an ancient Tibetan king who was believed to have lived through daily reincarnations.

**Motif vocabulary.** Jana flower — a design used on indigenous haversacks.

### Clothing & dress

Aka men and women keep long hair, but their clothing differs. Most men generally wear a silky Assamese toga. Women wear a long, dark-red garment covering the entire body. Aristocrats wear Assamese silk and the Tibetan knobbed hat. A cane cap reaching three inches high and decorated with two bird feathers is described as a notable feature of Aka costume. The sources also mention silver ornaments as part of women’s dress, including vase-shaped earrings and a fillet of silver chain worn around the head by richer women. Jade beads are made into necklaces. Indigenous lingchong cream, manufactured from pine resin, is used as a cosmetic by Aka women.

### Architecture

The Aka live in elongated houses made from bamboo, wood and cane leaves. These houses are raised on platforms about 6 feet above the ground and subdivided into three sections. A granary is built away from the main house.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention bamboo, wood and cane leaves as house materials, basket weaving and wood carving as principal arts, indigenous haversacks bearing the Jana flower design, and domestic animals including the Mithun.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Aka women wear silver ornaments, including vase-shaped earrings and, among richer women, a fillet or filet of silver chain around the head. Jade beads are made into necklaces. Women use lingchong cream, manufactured from pine resin, as a cosmetic. Face tattooing is documented among some Aka, especially women, who tattoo a straight line from the forehead to the chin.

## Music & performance

A separate source about Aka music concerns the Aka Pygmies of Central Africa and therefore does not describe this ethnic group.

## Festivals & rituals

Indigenous festivals are held under the guidance of a village Chieftain and involve affiliation with the natural world and community. The sources specifically mention the four-day Nechido festival, held in November.

The Aka are mainly Animists and are described as following a variant of the Nyezi-No religion, meaning Sky and Earth. Hindu and Buddhist influences entered Aka beliefs and culture through contact with Assamese, neighbouring Buddhist tribes and Tibetans. Veneration of the Assamese Hindu God Hori Deo is connected in the source to the imprisonment of a local Aka king, Tagi Raja. Shizhou is described as the most popular form of magic ritual. Its reported procedure involves slaughtering a dog, draining blood from its head and sprinkling, throwing or burning the blood in connection with an enemy; the source states that, if the ritual succeeds, the enemy is supposed to lose their life.

## Foodways

The Aka practise shifting cultivation and grow maize and millet as staple foods. They also plant leaves, pulses, potato and rice. The sources name no particular dishes or cooking techniques. Drinks made locally from fermented maize and millet include Lao pani, Mingri and the Aarah.

## Oral tradition & literature

They mention a legend concerning the Jana flower: it was believed to commemorate an ancient Tibetan king who led his entire life through daily reincarnations and lived in an open, giant palace where Jana flowers grew whenever the sun set.

## Language & religion

The Aka language belongs to the Tibeto-Burman family. A second group living among the Aka, the Koro, has its own separate and only distantly related Tibeto-Burman language. Koro is described as having more similarities with the Tani language group in easternmost Tibet. The source estimates 800 to 1200 remaining speakers of Koro and 4,000 to 6,000 speakers of Aka.

The Aka are mainly Animists and follow a variant of the Nyezi-No religion, meaning Sky and Earth. Hindu and Buddhist influences have shaped religious rites and culture. The sources also describe Tibetan influence from the Sherdukpen and occasional contacts with Assamese and neighbouring Buddhist groups. Superstitions and magic are important in the belief system, with Shizhou identified as a prominent ritual.
