## Overview

The Rabha people are a Tibeto-Burmese ethnic group living mostly in the Northeast Indian state of Assam, with a smaller population in the adjacent state of West Bengal. They primarily inhabit the plains of Lower Assam and the Dooars, while some live in the Garo Hills. Outside India, Rabha communities are present in Bhutan, where they live in nine districts. The Rabha population was recorded as 296,189 in the 2011 census, representing 7.63% of Assam’s Scheduled Tribe population. Rabha culture has been associated with agriculture, forest-based activities, shifting cultivation, weaving, matrilineal social organization, and several dialectal groups within the Bodo-Kachari ethnolinguistic family.

## Language & religion

Rabha belongs to the Sino-Tibetan, Tibeto-Burman, Jingpho-Konyak-Bodo, Konyak-Rabha, Rabha classification. The sources identify eleven dialectal groups: Rongdani, Maituri, Pati, Dahori, Dotla, Halua, Betolia, Hanna, Sunga, Modahi, and Kocha. Rongdani, Maituri, and Kocha remain actively spoken, while the other groups have become endangered or completely died out. Except for Rongdani, Maituri, and Kocha, the other Rabha groups have largely abandoned their mother tongue for Assamese. Daily speech can combine Assamese with Rabha words and expressions, a form called “Rabhamese” by some researchers.
