## Overview

The Mishmi live in the Mishmi Hills of Tibet and Arunachal Pradesh. They include the Idu, Digaro, Miju and Deng tribes. Their material culture includes skin clothing, basketry hats, knives, swords, bows, crossbows and bamboo arrows. Deng folklore tells of Deraogao, Ajiani and the eagle.

## Material culture

Mishmi material culture is known in the sources mainly through museum objects made from wood, bamboo, cane, rattan, basketry, iron, silver, steel, bone and animal materials.

### Textile & pattern traditions

### Clothing & dress

The sources record skin clothing and hats but do not describe dress traditions in detail.

- **Red panda skin coat and hat** — A British Museum catalogue records a coat made of skin and a hat made from red panda skin, bound with thin leather.
- **Basketry hat** — The catalogue records a basketry hat possibly filled with palm leaves.

### Architecture

The supplied sources do not describe Mishmi houses or architecture.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Mishmi museum objects include edged weapons, hunting gear and containers made from many natural materials.

- **Dao** — A dao is recorded among the Mishmi objects.
- **Silver knife sheath** — A silver knife sheath has a Chinese seal attached by a cord.
- **Straight-bladed sword** — The catalogue records a sword with a straight, single-edged blade.
- **Bow and crossbow** — A bow has cane bindings and a cane string, while a crossbow has a bone trigger and animal-hair string.
- **Bamboo arrow set** — Three bamboo arrows have metal heads.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources record a Deng creation account but no festival calendar or life-cycle rites.

- **Deng creation account** (*Deraogao*) — In Deng folklore, Deraogao created the earth after the world was a vast flood.
- **Ajiani and the eagle** (*Ajiani*) — Ajiani, one of the gold-people, married the eagle, and their descendants became the Deng people.

## Foodways

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources document a Deng creation myth and mention a collection of Mishmi folk tales.

- **Deng creation myth** (*Deng*) — The myth says that Deraogao created the earth and that Ajiani's descendants became the Deng people.
- **Mishmi Folk Tales of Lohit Valley** — This folk-tale collection was edited by Obang Tayeng and appears in the further reading.

## Language & religion

Mishmi languages form a cultural grouping of several unrelated Sino-Tibetan languages.

- **Idu Lhoba** — Idu Mishmi is also called Idu Lhoba or Yidu Lhoba.
- **Taraon** — The Digaro tribe is also called Taraon, Darang Deng or Taraung.
- **Kaman Deng** — The Miju Mishmi are also called Kaman Deng or Geman Deng.
- **Tayin** — Tayin and Tain are names connected with Mishmi groups and languages.

- *Taraon* — Name for the Digaro tribe

- *Taraung* — Another name connected with Digaro
- *Kaman Deng* — Name for the Miju Mishmi
- *Geman Deng* — Another name for Miju Mishmi
- *Tayin* — Name connected with Mishmi languages
- *Tain* — Name connected with Mishmi languages
- *Deraogao* — Creator in the Deng creation account
- *Ajiani* — Gold-person and Deng ancestor
- *Deng* — Mishmi tribe and people in the creation myth
- *dao* — Mishmi edged tool or weapon
