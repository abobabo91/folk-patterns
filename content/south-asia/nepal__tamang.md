## Overview

The Tamang people (རྟ་དམག་; Devanagari: तामाङ; *tāmāṅ*) are an ethnic group living in Nepal, Northeast India and southern Bhutan. In Nepal they are concentrated in the central hilly and Himalayan regions and numbered over 1.6 million people in the 2021 census. Tamang communities in India live in Sikkim, the Darjeeling and Kalimpong districts of West Bengal and Assam. In Bhutan they live foremost in the southern foothills, including Tsirang District, Dagana District, Samtse District, Chukha District, Sarpang District and Samdrup Jongkhar District. Tamang belongs to the Sino-Tibetan language family and is closely related to Gurung. Tamang culture includes a distinct language, dress, social structure, song and dance style, ritual songs, Buddhist practices and a system of more than 100 sub-clans.

## Music & performance

The typical Tamang song and dance style is known as **Tamang Selo**. It includes songs representing humor, satire, joy and sorrow, and has a brisk movement and rhythmic beat specific to the Tamangs. The *damphu* is a distinctive musical instrument, described as a small, round drum covered with goatskin.

Traditional Tamang songs are known as *Hwai*. They are sung by Tamang genealogists called *Tamba*. The sources describe *Hwai* songs as ritualistic and as holding tremendous importance in Tamang rituals.

The sources also identify singing as an important contemporary field for Tamang people. Named singers include Aruna Lama, called the “Nightingale of the Hills,” Buddha Lama, Raju Lama, Nima Rumba, Phiroj Shyangden, Prashant Tamang, Robin Tamang, Hira Devi Waiba and Navneet Aditya Waiba. Hira Devi Waiba is identified as an Indian singer in Nepali-language Tamang Selo, while Raju Lama is identified as the lead vocalist of Mongolian Heart band.

## Festivals & rituals

**Sonam Lhosar** is described as the main festival of the Tamangs. It is celebrated in the month of Magh, identified in the sources as February–March, to welcome the Tamang new year.

**Saga Dawa** is another significant festival. The sources describe it as a religious festival based on the birthday of Gautam Buddha.

Tamang ritual life also includes *Hwai* songs, which are sung by Tamang genealogists called *Tamba*. The sources describe these songs as ritualistic and as holding tremendous importance in Tamang rituals.

## Oral tradition & literature

Tamang oral history is described in connection with conflict and displacement. It says that the local chief Rinjen Dorje was killed by the Gorkhas after Prithvi Narayan Shah attacked the Tamangs in Temal in 1762. It also states that Gorkhali forces hid their weapons in the sand on the Sunkoshi riverbank before attacking Tamang forces, and that the Gorkhas later washed their weapons in springs at Dapcha Kuwapani. According to the source, this is why modern-day Tamangs do not drink there. Similar stories appear in oral histories throughout the region.

The sources also state that Tamang genealogists called *Tamba* sing *Hwai*, ritualistic songs that are important in Tamang rituals. No named Tamang epic, folktale cycle, proverb collection, riddle tradition, literary canon or contemporary literary revival is documented in the sources used.

## Language & religion

Tamang is a Sino-Tibetan language cluster spoken mainly in Nepal, Sikkim, West Bengal, including Darjeeling, and North-Eastern India. It comprises Eastern Tamang, Northwestern Tamang, Southwestern Tamang, Eastern Gorkha Tamang and Western Tamang. Eastern and Western Tamang are mutually unintelligible, and lexical similarity among Tamang languages varies between 81% and 63%. Tamang is tonal, uses a canonical subject-object-verb order and employs postpositions. The language is described as using the Tamyig script, similar to Tibetan, and Devanagari; another source states that it is written in prakriti.

About 87% of Tamang people are Buddhist, around 3% are Christian and 8% Hindu. They follow the Chinese lunar calendar of the 12-year cycle.
