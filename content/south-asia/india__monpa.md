## Overview

The Monpa are a major people of Arunachal Pradesh. Their culture includes weaving, wood and bamboo work, paper making, Tibetan Buddhist practice, farming, and pastoral life. Most live in Tawang and West Kameng. Monpa languages belong to the Tibeto-Burman family and use the Tibetan script.

## Material culture

Monpa material culture combines wood, bamboo, paper, stone, wool, silver, coral, and turquoise.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Monpa weaving includes carpets, bamboo work, paper making, and religious woodblock printing.

- **Carpet making** — The Monpa are known for making carpets and weaving.
- **Herringbone bamboo box** — A rectangular box is woven from split bamboo strips in a mostly yellow herringbone pattern with green counterpoint designs.
- **Sukso paper** (*sukso*) — Paper is made from the pulp of the local sukso tree.
- **Prayer-flag print** (*wind horse*) — A woodblock print on handmade paper shows a wind horse, protective animals, Tibetan text, and auspicious symbols.
- **Amulet print** — Woodblock amulets show royal figures, arrows, and a possible distaff with a ball of wool.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Monpa dress is based on the Tibetan chuba and includes yak-hair headwear.

- **Tibetan-style robe** (*chuba*) — Men and women wear traditional dress based on the Tibetan chuba.
- **Yak-hair headwear** — Men and women wear headwear made from yak hair with long tassels.
- **Warm jacket** — Women commonly wear a warm jacket.
- **Calf-length chemise** — Women commonly wear a sleeveless chemise reaching to the calves, tied at the waist with a long narrow cloth.
- **Peacock-feather cap** — A cap with one peacock feather in a felt hat is also mentioned.

### Architecture

Monpa houses use stone, wood, bamboo matting, carved details, and warm interior features.

- **Stone-and-wood house** — Monpa houses are built from stone and wood with plank floors.
- **Carved doors and windows** — House doors and window frames are often carved.
- **Bamboo-mat roof** — Roofs are made with bamboo matting to help keep houses warm in winter.
- **Sitting platform** — Living rooms contain sitting platforms.
- **Hearth** — Living rooms also contain hearths.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Monpa everyday objects include wooden bowls, bamboo boxes, and votive clay stupas.

- **Wooden bowl** — The Monpa are known for wooden bowls.
- **Lidded bamboo box** — A rectangular bamboo box with a lid was made for Torgya.
- **Clay votive stupa** (*stupa*) — Votive stupas are made from moulded, unfired clay.
- **108-stupa model** — One votive model has a central and upper miniature stupa surrounded by rings of smaller stupas, totaling 108.
- **Lotus-base stupa model** — Another model has a central dome and eight round-domed stupas on a lotus-petal base.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Monpa ornaments use silver, coral, and turquoise.

- **Silver ornaments** — Monpa ornaments are made of silver.
- **Coral ornaments** — Coral is used in Monpa ornaments.
- **Turquoise ornaments** — Turquoise is used in Monpa ornaments.
- **Yak-hair tassels** — Yak-hair headwear is worn with long tassels.
- **Peacock feather** — Some headwear includes a single peacock feather.

## Music & performance

The sources do not name Monpa instruments or song traditions, but they describe religious and festival performances.

- **Ajilamu pantomime** (*Ajilamu*) — Pantomime dances are the main feature of the Ajilamu festival.
- **Choskar scripture reading** (*Choskar*) — During Choskar, Buddhist lamas read religious scriptures in gompas for several days.
- **Field procession** — After the readings, villagers walk around cultivated fields carrying sutras on their backs.

## Dance & theatre

Monpa dance is represented by pantomime performances at Ajilamu and a religious field procession at Choskar.

- **Pantomime dance** (*Ajilamu*) — Pantomime dances are the principal feature of the Ajilamu festival.
- **Choskar field walk** (*Choskar*) — During Choskar, villagers walk around cultivated fields with sutras on their backs.

## Festivals & rituals

Monpa religious life centers on Choskar, Losar, Torgya, Buddhist monasteries, and printed sacred objects.

- **Choskar harvest festival** (*Choskar*) — Choskar combines scripture reading and field processions to support cultivation, prosperity, and crop protection.
- **Losar** — During Losar, people generally pray at Tawang Monastery for the Tibetan New Year.
- **Torgya** — Torgya is a festival held a month before the Tibetan New Year.
- **Prayer-wheel mantra sheet** (*Om mani padme hum*) — A handmade paper sheet bearing the mantra Om mani padme hum is placed inside a prayer wheel.
- **Eight Auspicious Symbols print** — Another mantra print is surrounded by the Eight Auspicious Symbols and signs of offerings.

## Foodways

Monpa foodways combine cold-weather foods, local crops, livestock, alcohol, and changing youth diets.

- **Butter tea** — Butter tea is part of Monpa foodways.
- **Distilled grain liquors** (*Chang*) — Locally made liquors from maize, millet, barley, buckwheat, or rice are called Chang, Baang-Chang, and Sin-Chang.
- **Cold-weather diet** — People consume substantial amounts of cheese, salt, and meat with alcohol to cope with extreme cold.
- **Cultivated crops** — Crops include paddy, maize, wheat, barley, millet, buckwheat, peppers, pumpkin, and beans.
- **Domestic animals** — Cattle, yaks, cows, pigs, sheep, and fowl are kept.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not record Monpa folktales or literary traditions, but they describe religious printing at Tawang Monastery.

- **Monastery printing press** — A printing press at Tawang Monastery produces religious texts on local paper with wooden blocks.
- **Printed amulets** — Museum records describe printed amulets on handmade paper.
- **Prayer-flag prints** — Woodblock prayer-flag prints include Tibetan texts, sacred figures, and symbols.
- **Tibetan mantra sheets** — Printed sheets bear Tibetan mantras for religious use.

## Language & religion

Monpa languages are varied Tibeto-Burman languages written in Tibetan script, and most Monpas follow Tibetan Buddhism.

- **Monpa languages** — The term covers several different languages rather than one single genealogical language.
- **Kho-Bwa** — Kho-Bwa is associated with the Monpa language area.
- **East Bodish** — East Bodish is associated with Monpa languages, including Tawang.
- **Tawang language** (*Tawang*) — Tawang is an East Bodish language and a variety of Dakpa.
- **Gelug Buddhism** (*Gelug*) — Most Monpas generally follow the Gelug sect of Tibetan Buddhism.
