## Overview

The Kusunda, also known as the Ban Raja (“people of the forest”), are a tribe of former hunter-gatherers from the forests of western Nepal. They are known to themselves as the *Mihaq*, *Myahq*, or *Myahak*. Kusundas now live in settled villages and are intermarried with neighboring peoples. In 1968, Johan Reinhard located Kusundas near Gorkha in Central Nepal; in 1969 and 1975 he found further members in the Dang and Surkhet valleys of Western Nepal. The 2021 Nepal census recorded 253 ethnic Kusunda, while the 2001 census recorded 164. Their language, Kusunda, is a language isolate, and Nepali is now their language of everyday communication. Their former hunting practices and endangered language make them important to the study of Nepalese folk culture.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources do describe bows and exceptionally long arrows used for hunting birds: the arrows were approximately 160 cm long, unfeathered, and poorly suited to hunting land animals.

## Festivals & rituals

The Kusunda are followers of animism, although Hindu overtones may be seen in their religious rituals. The sources describe a former practice of silent trade with Nepali farmers: Kusundas were said to leave a recently hunted deer for a farm household, expecting the farmers to provide farm goods without spoken negotiation.

## Foodways

The Kusunda custom of eating only the meat of wild animals continued until recent times. Their former subsistence included hunting birds resting in trees at night with bows and exceptionally long arrows.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do document modern language-preservation work. Uday Raj Aaley and Timotheus A. Bodt published “New Kusunda data: A list of 250 concepts” in 2020. Aaley also wrote *Kusunda Tribe and Dictionary*, which contains more than 1000 words from the Kusunda language. Gyani Maiya Sen and Kamala Sen-Khatri, together with Uday Raj Aaley, have contributed to language training and revival by teaching Kusunda to interested children and adults.

## Language & religion

Kusunda is a language isolate spoken by a few people in western and central Nepal. The language was formerly classified with its neighbors as Tibeto-Burman, but David E. Watters argued that it is distinct lexically, grammatically, and phonologically from neighboring languages. The language is moribund, with no children learning it because Kusunda speakers have married outside their ethnicity. The Kusunda language article states that, as of 2023, Kamala Sen-Khatri was its single fluent speaker, while the 2021 Nepal census recorded 23 native speakers. Nepali is now the everyday language of communication. The Kusunda follow animism, with Hindu overtones in religious rituals.
