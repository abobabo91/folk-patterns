## Overview

The Kutchi people traditionally hail from the Kutch district of the western Indian state of Gujarat and the Sindh province of Pakistan. Kutchi is an Indo-Aryan language spoken in the Kutch region, some parts of the neighbouring state of Rajasthan, and Sindh, Pakistan. Kutchi communities have also lived in southern Sindh for decades and have settled in parts of East Africa, North America, the United Kingdom, and Oman.

## Music & performance

The sources identify several Kutchi people associated with music and performance, including Anandji Virji Shah and Kalyanji Virji Shah, described as Bollywood music directors; Laxmichand “Babla” Virji Shah, described as a musician and percussionist; Salim Merchant; Sulaiman Merchant; Viju Shah, described as a music director; and Shekhar Ravjiani, identified as being from the music duo Vishal–Shekhar.

The Kutchi-language article also records that Kutchi-speaking communities in East Africa often integrated Swahili words and phrases into their language, producing a creole language called Kutchi-Swahili. This documents a linguistic result of migration and contact.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe a Swaminarayan procession held by Kutchi Hindus in Nairobi in 1928, attended by 1200 people. They also state that Kutchi Memons converted from Hinduism to Islam in the 15th century A.D. under the influence of Sunni Pirs such as Saiyid Abdullah. From the 14th century onwards, many Kutchis were influenced by Isma'ili Nizari Pirs such as Pir Sadrudin and converted from Hinduism to Isma'ilism.

These references document religious history and one public procession.

## Foodways

The foodways described in the sources are those of Gujarati cuisine, including foods associated with Gujarat and its regions. A typical Gujarati thali consists of rotli, dal or curry, rice, and shaak. It can also include pulse or bean preparations called kathor, farsaan such as dhokla, pathra, samosa, or fafda, and sweets such as mohanthal, jalebi, or sevaiya.

Staple foods include khichdi, chaas, and pickles. Main dishes commonly use steam-cooked vegetables, spices, and dals added to a vaghar, a mixture of spices heated in oil. Salt, sugar, lemon, lime, and tomatoes are used frequently. Sugar or jaggery is also added to some vegetable dishes and dal. Seasonal foodways include Keri no Ras, made from fresh mango pulp, and winter dishes such as rotla made from bajra flour and bhakri made from wheat flour. Gujarati meals may also include garlic chutney, onion, and chaas.

The sources list numerous named dishes, including undhiyu, dabeli, bhajiya, dal vada, locho, chaat, dahi vada, handvo, kachori, khaman, khandvi, muthia, pani puri, sev khamani, samosa, khichdo, batata vada, Gujarati daal, moong dal, Gujarati kadhi, adadiya, sutarfeni, basundi, ghari, ghughra, jalebi, shrikhand, doodhpak, and kulfi. Gujarati cuisine is described as predominantly vegetarian, although some communities, including Muslim communities and Parsis, include seafood, chicken, and mutton. Regular fasting with diets limited to milk, dried fruits, and nuts is also mentioned.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They identify Mamai Dev as the first and earliest writer of Kutchi literature. The Kutchi-language article also mentions Vadhod, meaning “Inquiry,” as a book or publication in the modified Gujarati script, and states that books and magazines are published in Kutchi using that script.

The sources further mention the archived Kutchi Language Online website, which maintained a list of Kutchi vocabulary words.

## Language & religion

Kutchi, also called Kachhi, is an Indo-Aryan language spoken in the Kutch region of Gujarat, parts of Rajasthan, and Sindh, Pakistan. Some scholars have considered it a dialect of Sindhi, although the sources state that Kutchi and Sindhi are distinct. Kutchi has borrowed vocabulary from Gujarati, Marwari, Hindi-Urdu, and, in East Africa, Swahili. The variety spoken in Sindh and the Banni region of Kutch is described as more similar to the Lari dialect of Sindhi, while eastern Kutchi has more Gujarati influence.

Kutchi is normally written in a modified Gujarati script. In parts of Pakistan, a modified Perso-Arabic script is used. Earlier scripts included Khudabadi and Khojki, with Khojki described as now extinct. The sources also mention the possibility of a separate Kutchi script.

Kutchi-speaking communities include Charans, Jadeja, Bhanushalis, Lohanas, Brahmins, Meghwals, Visa Oswal and Dasa Osval Jains, Ismaili Khojas, Bhatias, Rabaris, Siddis, Muslim Kutchi Khatris, the Muslim Royma, and Kutchi Memons. Religious history in the sources includes Hindu, Sunni Muslim, Isma'ili, and Satpanth affiliations.
