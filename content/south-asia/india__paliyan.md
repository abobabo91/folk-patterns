## Overview

The Paliyan, also called Pulliyar, Palaiyar or Pazhaiyarare, are a group of around 9,500 formerly nomadic Dravidian tribals living in the South Western Ghats montane rain forests of South India, especially in Tamil Nadu and Kerala. They are traditional nomadic hunter-gatherers, honey hunters and foragers. Paliyan communities are also recorded in the Palni hills, Varushanad, Sathuragiri, the Manjampatti Valley and Idikki District. Their language, Paliyan, is a Dravidian language closely related to Tamil. Their folk-cultural importance in the sources lies in their hunting, gathering, honey collecting, forest-product knowledge, ethnomedicine and ways of learning subsistence skills.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

In the early part of the 20th century, Paliyans dressed scantily.

### Architecture

In the early part of the 20th century, Paliyans lived in rock crevices and caves. The sources also state that several Paliyan are without proper houses, while the Tamil Nadu government built houses for a few Paliyan families at Kurinji Nagar.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They describe forest-product collection, hunting, fishing, yam and sago foraging, honey collecting and cultivation.

## Music & performance

They describe Paliyan ways of learning subsistence skills with minimal formal verbal instruction, empirical testing of theories and a prescription for a good life involving residence in a natural setting, cultivation of a mature and independent self, respect for others and a peaceful society.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources mention Paliyan theories of illness, shamanistic diagnosis and 75 measures for treatment of illness and injury, but provide no further account of associated ceremonies.

## Foodways

Yams are described as the Paliyans’ major food source. Traditional gathering includes yams and sago, while hunting, fishing and honey collecting are also documented. Paliyan dietary practices included local, natural resources and unconventional foods. Studies of these practices were carried out at Vadakaraparai, Moolayar, Valaigiri, Velankulam and Boy's town area of Dindigul District.

## Oral tradition & literature

They report that Paliyans learn subsistence skills with minimal formal verbal instruction and ascertain for themselves what others denote by terms amid substantial interpersonal knowledge variation. Peter M. Gardner’s work describes Paliyan knowledge and ways of life, while Christer Norström’s fieldwork during 1991–2001 examined the combination of hunting and gathering with work in a plantation economy.

## Language & religion

Paliyan is a Dravidian language of Kerala closely related to Tamil, and it is spoken by the Paliyans. The sources identify Paliyan as a language. They document traditional botanical preparations as a primary resource for health care, tribal herbal practitioners, Paliyan theories of illness and shamanistic diagnosis.
