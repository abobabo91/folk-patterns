## Overview

The Dendi are an ethnic group located in Benin, Niger, Nigeria and northern Togo, mainly in the plains of the Niger River. They are part of the Songhai people and were an integral part of the Songhai Empire as the Dendi province, or Dendiganda. The name “Dendi,” derived from the Songhay language, translates as “down the river.” The community consists of 195,633 people, including 4,505 in Nigeria. In Niger, Dendi communities live around the city of Gaya. Their mother tongue is Dendi, a Songhay language used as a trade language across northern Benin and spoken also in neighbouring countries.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Many Dendi houses have rectangular forms, mud construction and corrugated tin roofs.

## Music & performance

They do describe religious activity involving Islamic prayers and ceremonies of spirit possession. Some communities have imams who teach Islamic philosophy, while Islamic marabouts, described as holy men, perform the main prayers and also act as healers.

## Dance & theatre

The Dendi religious ceremony called *yenaandi* is described as a rain dance. It is one of the main Dendi religious ceremonies and is celebrated in the dry season.

## Festivals & rituals

The main Dendi religious ceremonies are *genji bi hori* and *yenaandi*. *Genji bi hori* is a festival involving offerings to the “black spirits” that control the plague. *Yenaandi* is a rain dance. Both ceremonies are celebrated in the dry season. Ceremonies of spirit possession are also celebrated and have characteristics that vary according to place; in some places, this type of ceremony can be held weekly or more often.

Dendi religious life combines Islam with ancestral cultural traits. Islam first appeared among the Dendi around the late 15th century, and most Dendi were Muslim by the 19th century. The sources also describe magic, spirit possession, ancestor worship and witchcraft as important cultural traits. Magician-healers and witches are present throughout the country and live in most villages.

## Foodways

Dendi subsistence farming includes crops such as millet, maize, plantains and manioc in Niger. In Benin, Dendi farmers grow rice, cowpeas, groundnuts, cassava, carrots, tomatoes, peppers, cabbage, millet and several types of squash. Dendi people also keep cattle, camels, sheep, goats and chickens. In Niger, Dendi drink the milk of cows and goats.

The sources describe gendered economic activities connected with food production. Only men work the fields. In Benin, women are dedicated to fruit production, tending gardens with mango, guava, citrus, papayas, bananas and dates, as well as meal preparation. In Niger, women grow vegetables and herbs in their gardens.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention Islamic philosophy as a subject taught by some imams.

## Language & religion

Dendi is a Songhay language used as a trade language across northern Benin along the Niger River. It forms a dialect cluster with Zarma and Koyraboro Senni and is heavily influenced by Bariba. Dendi has been described as a four-tone language. It is mainly spoken in northern Benin, as well as in other parts of Benin and neighbouring countries. Dendi speakers are the main group in the Departments of Alibori, Borgou, Donga and Atakora. In Nigeria, they are found in Kebbi, Kwara, Niger and Sokoto and are usually referred to by the Hausa name Dendawa.

The Dendi are almost all Muslim. The sources mention Islamic sects including Ibadhi, Ahmadi, Alevi, Yazidi, Druze and Khariji, alongside ancestral practices involving magic, spirit possession, ancestor worship and witchcraft. The grave accent, acute accent and macron are used on vowels to indicate tones.
