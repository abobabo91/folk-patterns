## Overview

The Kono are a Mande-speaking people living in diamond-rich eastern Sierra Leone. Most work as diamond miners and farmers and rarely leave the region. Their tradition says they came from Mali-Guinea as hunters in the mid-1500s, though archaeological evidence shows people in the area as far back as 2200 B.C. Colonial borders split the group between Sierra Leone and Guinea.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Body marking and adornment are known mainly from carved sculpture rather than living practice.

- **Scarification marks** — Three parallel marks on each cheek and three radiating lines from the outer corner of each eye appear on the Sowei helmet mask.
- **Neck rings** — The Sowei mask shows a figure wearing three neck rings, two of which curve to the front.
- **Lobed coiffure** — The Sowei mask displays an elaborate three-lobed hairstyle framed by carved panels imitating basketry.

## Festivals & rituals

Kono ritual life centers on a women's society and ancestral veneration.

- **Bondo society** (*Bondo*) — A women's association that gradually and firmly establishes adult attitudes in girls through instruction in fertility, morality, and sexual comportment, and maintains interest in members' welfare throughout life.
- **Ancestral invocation** — Ancestors are invoked and prayed to for protection, health, guidance, and good fortune, and are believed present during all activities.
- **Protective charms and magic** — Curses, omens, charms, and magic are used in daily life by some Kono alongside both Muslim and Christian practice.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kono historical tradition preserves memories of migration and conflict rather than folktales or proverbs.

- **Migration history** — Kono tradition says the people were once powerful in Mali and Guinea, migrated south as hunters, and were pushed north by Mende attacks before returning east to their homeland.

## Language & religion

Kono speak a Mande language and practice Islam, Christianity, and traditional religion together.

- **Kono language** (*Kono*) — A Mande language (ISO code kno) spoken as a first language by most Kono; distinct from Kono languages in Guinea and Nigeria.
- **Krio** — A creole language used by many younger Kono when dealing with other Sierra Leonean groups.

## Glossary

- *Kono* — A Mande-speaking people of eastern Sierra Leone
- *Mande* — A language group of West Africa
- *Krio* — A creole language used across Sierra Leone
- *Kono District* — Eastern Sierra Leone, diamond-bearing homeland of the Kono
- *Bondo* — A women's society for educating girls into adulthood
- *Sowei* — A carved wooden helmet mask
- *Mende* — A neighboring people who drove early Kono migrations
- *Mali* — Former West African empire from which Kono claim descent
- *Guinea* — Neighboring country where some Kono also live
- *Alatangana* — A reference to related Kono cultural or historical material
- *Scarification* — Marks or lines cut into the skin for decoration or identification
- *Charms* — Objects or practices used for protection or magical effect
- *Omens* — Signs believed to foretell future events
