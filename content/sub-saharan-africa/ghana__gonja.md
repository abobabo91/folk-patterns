## Overview

The Gonja, also known as Ghanjawiyyu and by the endonym Ngbanya, are a subgroup of the Guan ethnic group living in Ghana. They established the Gonja kingdom in northern Ghana, founded in 1675 by Sumaila Ndewura Jakpa. Following the fall of the Songhai Empire around 1600, the Mande Ngbanya clan moved south, crossed the Black Volta, and founded its capital at Yagbum under Naba'a. The Gonja language, properly called Ngbanya or Ngbanyito, is a North Guang language spoken by an estimated 230,000 people, almost all of whom belong to the Gonja ethnic group of northern Ghana. The sources document Gonja history, social organization, language, trade, and religious change.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They identify Yagbum as the capital founded by the Mande Ngbanya clan under the leadership of Naba'a.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that most Gonja are Muslims while incorporating traditional practices and beliefs, and that conversion to Islam occurred around the 18th century through the influence of Muslim missionaries and merchants.

## Foodways

They state that the Gonja economy depended largely on trade in slaves from Central Africa and kola nuts, particularly through the market town of Salaga, which was sometimes called the “Timbuktu of the South.”

## Language & religion

Gonja, properly called Ngbanya or Ngbanyito, is a North Guang language within the Guang languages and is related to the Kwa languages family. It is closely related to Akan languages and is spoken in northern Ghana, including by about a third of the population in the northern region. Its dialects are Gonja and Choruba. Vowels listed for the language are a, e, i, o, ɔ, u, and ɛ; consonants include ch, ŋm, ny, gb, kp, and sh. Most Gonja are Muslims but incorporate traditional practices and beliefs. The sources attribute conversion to Islam around the 18th century to Muslim missionaries and merchants, as well as to the perceived healing powers of Muslims, the strength of Islamic prayer, and trade.
