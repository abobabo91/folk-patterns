## Overview

The Saho are a Cushitic ethnic group and one of the nine official ethnic groups of Eritrea. Most Saho live in Eritrea, primarily in the Southern and Northern Red Sea regions, while a few also live in Ethiopia, mainly in the Tigray Region. Their traditional speech area in Eritrea extends from the bay of Erafayle in the east to the Laacasi Gade valleys in the south and the Eritrean Highlands to the west. A 2015 estimate placed their total population between 250,000 and 650,000; a 2016 estimate gave approximately 206,000 Saho in Eritrea, while a 2012 estimate placed the Ethiopian population at 37,000. The Saho language belongs to the Lowland East Cushitic languages within the Cushitic branch of the Afro-Asiatic family. The sources describe pastoralism, agriculture, customary law, clan organization, language, and religious affiliation, but provide no museum catalogue records or detailed documentation of Saho folk objects, textiles, music, or foodways.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Wikipedia account states that an “Eritrean mission” led by A. Mochi and L. Loria collected Saho cultural objects in Italian Eritrea and that many were displayed at the Museo di Storia Naturale di Firenze.

## Music & performance

The sources mention “Saho videos” as an external link and refer to *rahbe* meetings, in which Saho people discuss matters including water, pasture, land, clan disputes, and ways to alleviate these problems. These meetings are described as customary-law conferences rather than musical performances.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that the Saho are predominantly Sunni Muslim and that the majority had adopted Islam by the 13th century, following the growing influence of religious mystics and traders from the Arabian peninsula. Many Saho belong to the Khatmiyya Sufi order, which became dominant in the region around the 19th century. A few Christians, also known as the Irob, live in the Tigray region of Ethiopia and the Debub Region of Eritrea. No further ritual details are supplied.

## Foodways

They state that most Saho are pastoralists who also engage in some agriculture, while a few groups are settled farmers.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources describe the memorization and narration of family descent, laws, and customs. Family descent is memorized going back at least 30 or 40 generations, and laws and customs are also memorized and narrated. The sources further state that a skilled representative called a *madarre* presents arguments during a *rahbe* meeting and attempts to persuade the participating sub-clans or tribes. Clan or tribal wise men or elders are called *ukal*, while *shimagale* are appointed as mediators in smaller disputes between two individuals.

## Language & religion

Saho is a Cushitic language of the Afro-Asiatic family, spoken in Eritrea and Ethiopia. It is closely related to Afar and belongs to the Saho-Afar dialect cluster of the Lowland East Cushitic languages. The language has Northern, Central, and Southern dialects. The sources name Casawurta, Tharuuca, Casabat Care, Faqhat Xarak, Minifere, Minifire, Xazo, Dabrti-meela, Irob, and Sancafe in connection with dialect distribution. Saho has written versions in the Latin alphabet, official in Eritrea; the Ge'ez script, official in Ethiopia; and the Ajami script, which has no official recognition. The language is also used in cyberspace, and the sources state that Arabic, described as “special now Latin letters,” has been used to document Saho history and render information. Most Saho are Sunni Muslim, and many belong to the Khatmiyya Sufi order. A few Saho are Christian.
