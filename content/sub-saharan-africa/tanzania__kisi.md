## Overview

The Kisi are a Bantu ethnolinguistic group from Makete District of Njombe Region, Tanzania, on the northwestern shore of Lake Malawi. They are also found alongside Lake Nyasa in Ludewa District in Iringa Region, especially around Lupingu. In 2001, their population was estimated at 18,000, of whom 10,200 spoke the Kisi language. The sources describe fishing, pottery, canoe transport, barter, agriculture, and trade as important aspects of Kisi life. They report that the Kisi began using Nyakyusa as a second language only in 1900.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pot-making is documented as an important Kisi activity. Kisi women bartered homemade pots, while Kisi fishermen exchanged these pots inland with their catch, including hippopotamus, for cattle from the Sangu.

## Foodways

Fishing is described as the main economic activity in the Kisi settlements around Lake Nyasa. The sources state that fishermen exchanged pots inland with their catch, which included hippopotamus, for cattle from the Sangu. They also report agriculture, including the cultivation of cassava.

## Language & religion

The Kisi are identified as a Bantu ethnolinguistic group. In 2001, 10,200 of an estimated population of 18,000 spoke the Kisi language, and the sources state that Nyakyusa began to be used as a second language only in 1900. The separate language article supplied in the sources concerns Kissi, also called Kisi or Kisiei, a Mel language of West Africa, and does not provide information about the language of the Kisi of Tanzania.
