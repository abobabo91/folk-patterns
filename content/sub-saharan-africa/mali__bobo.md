## Overview

The Bobo live mainly in Burkina Faso, with some communities in Mali. Their major southern community is Bobo-Dioulasso, with Fô and Kouka farther north and Boura in northern Mali. Farming is central to daily life. Bobo culture is also known for masks, woodwork, drums, and elders and lineages guiding social life.

## Material culture

Bobo material culture is chiefly made of wood, with masks, stools, palls, drums, and sculptures, plus a few metal figures.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not document Bobo textile traditions, but they name several carved and painted motifs.

- **Bobo masks** (*Bolo*) — These wooden masks rise above the main body, use red, black, and silver or white paint, and are repainted each season.
- **Geometric motifs** — Named motifs include triangles, rhombs, crosses, checker patterns, and other incised geometric designs.

### Clothing & dress

- **Wooden pall carried over the shoulder** (*Bobo Ule*) — A wooden pall from Bomborokuy was carried hooked over one shoulder, but the source does not call it clothing.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Documented everyday objects are chiefly wooden, with two recorded metal objects.

- **Wooden stools** — These stools have human-head handles, three or four legs, and sometimes incised geometric patterns.
- **Wooden pall** (*Bobo Ule*) — A one-piece pall from Bomborokuy was owned by one family for at least three generations.
- **Small metal human figure** (*Bobo Fing-folket*) — This copper or possibly bronze figure has a round pointed hat and a bent implement over one shoulder.
- **Crocodile figure** (*Bobo Fing-folket*) — This reddish figure was probably made of copper.

## Music & performance

Bobo performance records include wooden and skin drums from Bomborokuy, but no named musical genres or ensembles.

- **Drums** (*Bobo Ule*) — Recorded drums range from 24 to 91 centimeters tall and are played with a stick or with the hands.
- **Tambourine drum** (*Bobo Ule*) — One recorded drum is called a tambourine and is played with the hands.

## Dance & theatre

Masks are danced by men for entertainment and can also protect communities during important rites.

- **Bolo mask dance** (*Bolo*) — Bolo masks are danced by male performers during funerals, male initiations, and harvest celebrations.
- **Two-faced bird-head mask** (*Bobo Fing*) — A tall mask with two faces and nine carved bird heads is said to keep evil spirits away.
- **Horned helmet mask** (*Do*) — The mask bearer represents the protective spirit Do and drives away people who threaten death rites or fertility ceremonies.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe communal celebrations, death rites, fertility ceremonies, and religious beliefs rather than a yearly festival calendar.

- **Funeral mask dances** (*Bolo*) — Bolo masks are danced during funerals.
- **Male initiation mask dances** (*Bolo*) — Bolo masks are danced during male initiations.
- **Harvest celebrations** (*Bolo*) — Bolo masks are danced during harvest celebrations.
- **Protective mask rites** (*Do*) — The horned helmet mask is used around death rites and fertility ceremonies.
- **Dwo worship** (*Dwo*) — Blacksmiths traditionally worship Dwo, Wuro’s son in the contemporary epoch.

## Foodways

Farming is essential to daily Bobo life and centers on several food and cash crops.

- **Red sorghum** — Red sorghum is a major Bobo food crop.
- **Pearl millet** — Pearl millet is a major Bobo food crop.
- **Yams** — Yams are a major Bobo food crop.
- **Maize** — Maize is a major Bobo food crop.
- **Cotton and peanuts** — Cotton and peanuts are primary cash crops, and cotton is sold to textile mills in Koudougou.

## Oral tradition & literature

Bobo oral tradition in the sources centers on origins, creation, and the first blacksmith and mask.

- **Wuro’s creation of the world** (*Wuro*) — Bobo cosmogony says Wuro created the world and arranged creations in opposing pairs.
- **The first blacksmith** (*Wuro*) — Legend says Wuro created the first man, who was a blacksmith and received the first mask.
- **Opposing pairs** — The pairs include humans and spirits, male and female, village and bush, and culture and nature.

## Language & religion

Bobo is a Mande language with two strongly differing dialects, and Bobo religion centers on Wuro and Dwo.

- **Bobo language** (*Bobo*) — Bobo has Northern and Southern dialects, which share only 20%–30% intelligibility by one standard.
- **Northern dialect** (*Konabéré*) — The Northern Bobo dialect is also known as Konabéré.
- **Black Bobo** (*Bobo Fing*) — Bobo Fing means “Black Bobo.”
- **White and Red Bobo** (*Bobo Gbe; Bobo Oule*) — Bobo Gbe means “White Bobo,” while Bobo Oule means “Red Bobo.”
- **Creator god** (*Wuro*) — Wuro created the earth from a ball of clay and is not represented by sculptures.
