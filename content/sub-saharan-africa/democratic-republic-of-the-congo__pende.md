## Overview

The Pende live in west-central Democratic Republic of the Congo. Eastern and Western Pende have different artistic and ceremonial traditions but identify as one people. Extended families and maternal descent have been important in social organization. Their art includes masks, performances, sculpture, textiles, vessels and musical instruments.

## Material culture

Pende material culture includes masks, raffia costumes, carved figures, pottery, baskets, tools, instruments and small ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Raffia fiber is central to Pende masquerade dress, which also uses woven cloth, dyed cloth and patterned bands.

- **Minganji fiber ensemble** (*minganji*) — This ensemble includes a knotted fiber mask, body covering, fringed skirt and limb coverings.
- **Raffia mask hair** — Raffia hair attached to a gambanda mask helps create the look of a performed character.

### Clothing & dress

Pende ceremonial dress often covers the body with raffia and combines masks with hair, fringe and head ornaments.

- **Minganji fiber suit** (*minganji*) — A minganji suit can cover the body from head to ankle.
- **Striped fiber costume** — British Museum pieces include striped fiber costume parts, skirts, hats and head ornaments.
- **Small mask pendant** (*gikhokho*) — Small mask pendants were worn as personal display and markers of Pende identity.

### Architecture

The sources do not establish a distinctive Pende building type, house plan or architectural tradition.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pende makers produced pottery, carved vessels, baskets, tools, weapons and other objects in clay, wood, fiber, iron and ivory.

- **Lidded pottery vessel** — British Museum records attribute lidded pottery vessels to Pende makers.
- **Carved cup or bowl** — Collected wooden cups and bowls include human-shaped vessels and decorated pedestal forms.
- **Fish trap** — Fish traps are among the everyday objects attributed to Pende makers.
- **Divination instrument** (*galukoji*) — A galukoji joins a small mask-like head to an expanding bamboo mechanism used by a diviner during consultations.
- **Carved stool** — A carved chair or stool can be a substantial work of sculpture, though its exact use varies by object.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Pende adornment includes ivory and wooden miniature masks, pendants, hairpins, feathers and fiber rings.

- **Ivory pendant mask** (*gikhokho*) — A gikhokho was carved by a professional sculptor and worn as decoration.
- **Wooden miniature mask** — Wooden mask pendants could be prescribed by healers, unlike the ivory ornamental examples.
- **Face-shaped pendant or badge** — British Museum collections include a small face-shaped pendant or badge.
- **Decorated hairpin** — Decorated hairpins are among the recorded Pende ornaments.
- **Fiber ring** — A fiber ring is one of the selected Pende adornments.

## Music & performance

Pende musical instruments support performances, but the sources rarely identify their tunes, performers or occasions.

- **Thumb piano** (*sanza*) — Pende records identify thumb pianos, or sanza, among their instruments.
- **Panpipes** — Panpipes appear in the selected Pende museum records.
- **Drum** — A drum is among the instruments collected under Pende attribution.
- **Rattle** — A rattle is among the recorded Pende instruments.
- **Whistle** — Whistles appear in the selected Pende instrument records.

## Dance & theatre

Pende masquerade joins a mask, costume, movement, music and an audience, often in initiation and public ceremonies.

- **Mbuya mask performance** (*Mbuya*) — Mbuya wooden masks are danced with raffia costumes in performances linked to mukanda initiation.
- **Minganji masquerade** (*minganji*) — Minganji is a fiber mask and full costume used in Pende performances.
- **Giwoyo cap mask** (*giwoyo*) — A giwoyo cap mask sits on top of the head and has a long extension below the face.
- **Gambanda mask performance** (*gambanda*) — A gambanda mask represents the wife of a chief and uses restrained choreography and elaborate raffia hair.

## Festivals & rituals

Mukanda initiation and masquerade are important documented settings for Pende public and ritual life.

- **Male initiation** (*mukanda*) — Mukanda is a male initiation setting documented for masked performances.
- **Funeral masquerade** — Pende masks and costumes were used at funerals.
- **Leadership ceremony** — Pende masks and costumes were also used in ceremonies concerning leadership.
- **Divination** (*galukoji*) — A diviner used the galukoji instrument during consultations.
- **Ancestors** (*Mvumbi*) — Mvumbi is given as a name for ancestors.

## Foodways

The sources do not document a distinct Pende dish, recipe or food festival.

- **First harvest share** — An older summary says artists could receive a first share of the harvest because of their social standing.

## Oral tradition & literature

Pende historical memory is carried through mask characters, performance and objects, but the profile gives no song, story cycle or epic.

- **Giwoyo origin tradition** (*giwoyo*) — Pende people connect the giwoyo masking tradition with an Angolan origin.
- **Revolt memory** — Masks and pendants took on new political meaning after the 1931 revolt against Belgian colonial rule.

## Language & religion

Pende communities speak related Bantu language forms and combine Christianity with ancestral practices, with local variation.

- **Pende language** (*Giphende*) — Giphende is one name used for the Pende language.
- **Pende language** (*Kipende*) — Kipende is another name used for the Pende language.
- **Christianity** — Christianity is listed among Pende religious affiliations.
- **Traditional religion** — Traditional religion and ancestral practices are also part of Pende belief.
- **Maternal descent** — Historical summaries describe maternal descent as important in social organization.
