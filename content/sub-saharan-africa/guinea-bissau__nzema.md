## Overview

The Nzema are an ethnic group numbering about 328,700 people, including 262,000 in southwestern Ghana and 66,700 in the southeast of Côte d'Ivoire. In Ghana, their area is divided into Nzema East Municipal, also known as Evalue Gwira; Ellembele; and Nzema West, also known as Jomoro. Their language is known as Nzema, Nzima, or Appolo. Nzema is an Akan language and one of several Bia languages. The sources describe the Nzema primarily as farmers and document a matrilineal kinship system in which descent and property pass through maternal lines. Their annual Kundum Festival combines ritual drumming, singing, dancing, harvest timing, and satirical song performance.

## Music & performance

Music and performance are prominently associated in the sources with the Kundum Festival, a religious festival held annually throughout the Ahanta and Nzema areas. Its beginning is timed to the harvest period, with local communities determining when it starts. The festival begins in the easternmost part of Ahanta and advances southwestward together with the harvest period. Ritual drumming, singing, and dancing continue for four weeks. These activities are described as the means by which the community expels devils and protects its good fortune. The festival is also the principal occasion for the performance of satirical *avudewene* songs by young men.

## Dance & theatre

The sources used document dancing as part of the four-week Kundum Festival.

## Festivals & rituals

The principal festival described in the sources is the annual religious Kundum Festival, held across the Ahanta and Nzema areas. Its timing is linked to the harvest period rather than to a fixed date: local communities determine when it begins. The festival starts in the easternmost part of Ahanta and moves southwestward in parallel with the harvest period. Ritual drumming, singing, and dancing take place for four weeks. These practices are understood as expelling devils and protecting the community’s good fortune. The festival is the main setting for young men’s satirical *avudewene* songs.

## Foodways

The sources state that the Nzema are mostly farmers and that the Kundum Festival is coordinated with the harvest period.

## Language & religion

Nzema, also known as Nzima or Appolo, is an Akan language spoken by the Nzema people of southwestern Ghana and southeastern Ivory Coast. It is one of several Bia languages, has had considerable influence from the Twi-Fante language, is partially intelligible with Jwira-Pepesa, and is closely related to Baoulé. The language has ten vowels, eight of which may be nasalized. The sources describe the Kundum Festival as religious and state that its ritual practices expel devils and protect good fortune. They also document a matrilineal kinship system in which descent and property pass through maternal lines.
