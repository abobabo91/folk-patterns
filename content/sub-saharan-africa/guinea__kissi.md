## Overview

The Kissi live in Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone. Their material culture includes weaving, basket-making, iron tools, pottery, musical instruments, masks and society objects. Kissi women spin yarn for men’s looms. Traditional religion includes ancestor worship and prayer to deceased relatives.

## Material culture

Kissi material culture is known for woven goods, plant fibres, ironwork, pottery, instruments and objects linked to Bundu or Bandu societies.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Kissi weaving uses vertical looms, plant fibres and hand-spun cotton.

- **Vertical loom** — Kissi weavers are well known for using vertical looms.
- **Weaving implement** (*kpaba*) — The kpaba has a wooden frame with parallel heddles, while its other sides and heddles are made of rotang.
- **Cotton holder** (*temuo*) — The temuo is a round wooden stick that branches into four parts and holds cotton.
- **Spindle** (*kbendeleng*) — The kbendeleng is a spindle with a long heavy shaft and a conical soapstone weight called dindo.
- **Head covering** (*Fujó*) — The Fujó is made from pisang or Urèna fibre and decorated with cauris.

### Clothing & dress

The documented clothing is linked mainly to Bundu or Bandu societies, ceremonies and hunting.

- **Dance skirt** (*Jo*) — The Jo is a plant-fibre dance skirt worn over imported cotton cloth.
- **Net shirt** (*Kondoyila*) — The Kondoyila is a grey cotton net shirt with large knotted meshes and cauris around the neck and sleeves.
- **Hunter’s tunic** — A hunter’s or warrior’s tunic was made from hand-spun and hand-woven cotton strips, with cotton-wrapped amulets and small horns sewn to the back of the neck.
- **Girl’s head covering** (*Fujó*) — After a Bundu ceremony, an older woman braided the Fujó into a girl’s hair, and it was later removed.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Kissi smiths made iron tools and weapons, while households used pottery, baskets and farm objects.

- **Palm-fruit knife** (*Kissi penny*) — One knife was forged from a Kissi penny and was mainly used to harvest palm fruits.
- **Fighting dagger** — A curved iron dagger from Liberia was used in fighting.
- **Black earthen vessel** — Pottery records include a round black earthen vessel with an incised and fired pattern.
- **Palm-oil sieve** — A sieve was used to produce palm oil.
- **Bird sling** (*fanda*) — The fanda is a sling used from rice-field platforms to drive rice birds away.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Cauris decorate Bundu or Bandu clothing and head coverings.

- **Cauris on a head covering** (*Fujó*) — Cauris decorate the Fujó head covering worn by girls after Bundu or Bandu ceremonies.
- **Cauris on a net shirt** (*Kondoyila*) — Cauris decorate the neck opening and sleeves of the Kondoyila net shirt.

## Music & performance

Kissi instruments include drums, rattles, a lamellophone, a scraper and an ivory horn.

- **Goblet drum** (*sangbaa*) — The sangbaa is a goblet-shaped drum with a skin, a wooden stick, tension cords and a tubular foot ending in a tripod.
- **Seven-tongue instrument** (*sansa*) — The sansa has seven metal tongues attached to a wooden board with a plaited design.
- **Seed rattle** (*tjekó*) — The tjekó is a rattan-vine rattle with a calabash base filled with Canna indica seeds.
- **Calabash rattle** (*seláng*) — The seláng is a calabash rattle with an attached bead rattle and belongs to an ensemble including the sangbáa.
- **Grooved scraper** — A scraper made from Raphia vinifera has fifty-one grooves that are rubbed with a stick or palm-nut in time with dance or song.

## Dance & theatre

Dance objects are especially connected with Bundu or Bandu society events.

- **Ankle rattle** (*Jajei*) — The Jajei is an ankle rattle made for girls of the Bundu society.
- **Women’s dance rattle** — Women used a hollow bottle-like fruit covered with seed beads at Bundu parties when a masked Bundu “devil” appeared.
- **Horned mask** — A wooden mask is recorded with two horns, four probable chimpanzee teeth and animal fur.
- **Dance skirt** (*Jo*) — The Jo formed part of a Bundu or Bandu dance costume.
- **Net shirt** (*Kondoyila*) — The Kondoyila formed part of a Bundu or Bandu dance costume.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe Bundu or Bandu ceremonies for girls but no annual festival calendar.

- **Bundu bush ceremony** (*Bundu*) — After returning from the Bundu bush, girls wore Fujó head coverings braided into their hair, which were later removed.
- **Bandu society ceremony** (*Bandu*) — The Bandu society is linked to the Fujó, Jajei, Jo and Kondoyila.
- **Circumcision razor** — A razor is recorded as an instrument for circumcision, but the sources give no wider details about the rite.

## Foodways

The documented food-related objects concern rice fields, palm oil and palm fruits.

- **Rice-field sling** (*fanda*) — The fanda was used to drive rice birds away from platforms in rice fields.
- **Palm-oil sieve** — A sieve was used for producing palm oil.
- **Palm-fruit knife** (*Kissi penny*) — A knife made from a Kissi penny was chiefly used for harvesting palm fruits.

## Language & religion

Kissi is a tonal Mel language, and traditional religion centers on ancestors and deceased relatives.

- **Kissi language** (*Kisi, Kisiei*) — Kissi, also called Kisi or Kisiei, is a Mel language in the Niger–Congo family.
- **Northern dialect** (*kisiduei*) — The northern dialect, kisiduei, is spoken in Guinea and Sierra Leone.
- **Southern dialect** (*kpekeduei*) — The southern dialect, kpekeduei, is spoken in Liberia and Sierra Leone.
- **Ancestor worship** — Ancestor worship and prayer to deceased relatives are common.
- **Soapstone figures** — Carved soapstone figures and heads were made before colonial contact, but their purpose is uncertain.
