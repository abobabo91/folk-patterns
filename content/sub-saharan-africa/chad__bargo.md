## Overview

The Bargo, also called Maba or Wadai people, are a Sunni Muslim ethnic group found primarily in the mountains of the Wadai region in eastern Chad and southern Sudan. Their population is estimated at about 542,000, while another estimate places the total number of Bargo people in Sudan at about 28,000. They are traditionally pastoral and farmers, organized around clans. Their homelands lie along caravan routes connecting the Sahel and West Africa with the Middle East. They speak Maba, a Nilo-Saharan language of the Maban branch; the language is locally called Bura Mabang. The Bargo language was the state language of the Islamic Wadai Empire and remained important after the Islamic Bornu Empire conquered the region. ## Festivals & rituals

The sources identify the Bargo as primarily adherents of Islam, following the Maliki school of Sunni Islam. The sources state that the Bargo helped expel the Christian Tunjur dynasty and install an Islamic dynasty in their region in the early 17th century, but provide no ritual description of that historical event.

## Language & religion

The Bargo speak Maba, a Nilo-Saharan language of the Maban branch, locally called Bura Mabang. The first ten numerals are given as “tek, bar, kungal, asal, tor, settal, mindri, rya, adoi, atuk,” and the language is described as very distant from other Nilo-Saharan languages. Many Bargo also speak Arabic, their traditional trade language. The Bargo primarily adhere to Islam and follow the Maliki school of Sunni Islam. Historically, their language served as the state language of the Islamic Wadai Empire and remained important under the Islamic Bornu Empire.
