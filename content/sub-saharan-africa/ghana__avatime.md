## Overview

The Avatime are a Guan people who live in the Volta region of Ghana. History has it that they are Ahanta people who migrated to the Volta region. The Avatime language describes the group as the Avatime, with the self-designation *Kedone* (m.sg.). Avatime is a Kwa language spoken primarily in the seven towns and villages of Amedzofe, Vane, Gbadzeme, Dzokpe, Biakpa, Dzogbefeme, and Fume.

## Language & religion

*Avatime*, also known as *Afatime*, *Sideme*, or *Sia*, is a Kwa language of the Avatime people of eastern Ghana. The language has three tones and vowel harmony. It has nine vowels: /i ɪ e ɛ a ɔ o ʊ u/. Vowels may be long or short, and records from 1910 showed that all vowels could be nasalized, although this was disappearing and few words with nasal vowels remained by the end of the century. Avatime syllables include V, CV, CGV, and N, and the language allows consonant–approximant clusters.
