## Overview

The Venḓa are a Bantu people of Southern Africa, centered in northern Limpopo. Their history is linked with Mapungubwe, Great Zimbabwe, the Soutpansberg and the Limpopo River. Museum records show pottery, gourds, metalwork, wooden tools, musical instruments, clothing, jewelry and domestic life. The profile also documents musangwe, a traditional bare-knuckle fist-fighting practice.

## Material culture

Venda material culture includes decorated pottery and gourds, copper and iron objects, wooden tools, drums, xylophones, beadwork and household vessels.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not document named Venda textile traditions or motifs.

### Clothing & dress

Museum photographs show girls wearing aprons and a girl wearing a striped wrapper.

- **Striped wrapper** — A museum photograph shows a girl wearing a striped wrapper while carrying a basket on her head.
- **Aprons** — Several photographs show dancing girls wearing aprons, including a group lying on the ground.

### Architecture

Venda settlement history is linked with royal kraals and decorated compounds.

- **D’zata royal kraal** (*D’zata*) — The legendary chief Thoho-ya-Ndou is said to have founded a royal kraal called D’zata, whose remains are a National Monument.
- **Decorated walled compound** — A photograph shows women and children inside a decorated walled compound beside a thatched building at Tshakhuma.
- **Circular wall** — Another photograph shows a woman and two children beside a low circular wall containing a palm tree.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The profile records decorated pottery and gourds, copper and iron objects, and wooden household tools.

- **Decorated earthenware bowls** — The catalogue records earthenware bowls decorated with red and burnished graphite.
- **Gourd porridge bowl** — The catalogue records gourd porridge bowls, including one with burnt ornament.
- **Poker-work gourd vessel** — A gourd vessel is decorated with figures outlined in poker work and marked “MAKILIEBIN.”
- **Copper currency bar** (*lerale*) — A cylindrical copper currency bar was called lerale.
- **Composite weapons** — The catalogue records composite axes and composite spears.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The catalogue records bracelets, wristlets, charms and an ostrich-eggshell head ornament.

- **Bronze wire and hair bracelet** — One bracelet is made from bronze wire and animal hair.
- **Beaded hair wristlet** — A composite wristlet uses animal hairs with two groups of beads attached.
- **Iron charms on bead string** — Iron charms are attached to a fibre string of glass beads.
- **Ostrich-eggshell head ornament** — A head ornament consists of one row of ostrich-eggshell beads on a fibre string.

## Music & performance

Venda musical instruments in the catalogue include several drums and a gourd-resonator xylophone.

- **Composite drum** — One composite drum has a wooden body, a hide membrane and a loop handle.
- **Handled wooden drum** — Another drum has a wooden body, a hide membrane and a handle.
- **Four-handled drum** — A wood and hide drum has a solid wooden resonator, four handles, a base hole and a rattle inside.
- **Gourd-resonator xylophone** — A composite xylophone has wooden slats joined by strings and suspended over gourd resonators.
- **Drum playing** — Photographs from Sibasa show two young girls playing drums held between their legs.

## Dance & theatre

Photographs show lines and groups of dancing girls wearing aprons, but the dance is not named.

- **Line dance** — One photograph shows a line of dancing girls wearing aprons.
- **Long dance line** — Another photograph shows a long line of dancing girls.
- **Ground dance pose** — A photograph shows a group of girls wearing aprons lying on the ground.

## Festivals & rituals

The profile documents musangwe, a supervised bare-knuckle fight used for entertainment and respect.

- **Bare-knuckle fist fighting** (*musangwe*) — Musangwe is a traditional Venda bare-knuckle practice that offers entertainment and a way to earn respect.
- **Village-supervised match** — Village elders supervise musangwe, forbid biting and kicking, and punish rule breaking.
- **Chief’s prize** (*Khosi; Vhamusanda*) — Victors receive prizes chosen by the Khosi, meaning chief, or Vhamusanda, meaning headman.

## Foodways

The profile records millet, sorghum, livestock, cooking over a fire and several food vessels.

- **Millet and sorghum farming** — Mapungubwe records show people growing millet and sorghum.
- **Livestock keeping** — People at Mapungubwe tended cattle, goats and sheep.
- **Fire cooking** — A photograph shows a woman preparing food in a cooking pot over a fire at Sibasa.
- **Gourd porridge spoon** — The catalogue records a wooden porridge spoon and a ladle alongside gourd porridge bowls.
- **Head-carried basket** — A photograph shows a girl carrying a basket on her head beside pots, calabashes and a mortar.

## Oral tradition & literature

Oral tradition names Shiriyadenga as the first king of Venda and Mapungubwe.

- **King Shiriyadenga** (*Shiriyadenga*) — Oral tradition holds Shiriyadenga to have been the first king of Venda and Mapungubwe.
- **Kingdom of Mapungubwe** (*Mapungubwe*) — Mapungubwe was a trade center from about 1220 to 1300 AD and later lost power as people moved toward Great Zimbabwe.
- **Head of the Elephant** (*Thoho-ya-Ndou*) — Thoho-ya-Ndou is a legendary chief whose name is translated as “Head of the Elephant.”

## Language & religion

Venḓa is an official Bantu language of South Africa and Zimbabwe, but the profile does not describe current religion.

- **Venḓa language** (*Venḓa; Tshivenḓa*) — Venḓa, or Tshivenḓa, is mainly spoken by the Venda people in northern Limpopo and by some Lemba people in South Africa.
- **Sintu writing system** (*Isibheqe Sohlamvu; Ditema tsa Dinoko*) — Venda uses the sintu writing system called Isibheqe Sohlamvu or Ditema tsa Dinoko.
- **Luṱhofunḓeraru lwa Mibvumo** — Luṱhofunḓeraru lwa Mibvumo is the technical Venda name for this writing system.
- **Additional accented letters** (*ḓ; ḽ; ṋ; ṱ; ṅ*) — The Latin alphabet used for Venḓa adds the accented letters ḓ, ḽ, ṋ, ṱ and ṅ.
