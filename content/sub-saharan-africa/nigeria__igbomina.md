## Overview

The Ìgbómìnà are an ethnic subgroup of the Yoruba people originating from north central and southwest Nigeria. They speak Ìgbómìnà, also called Igbonna, a dialect classified among the Central Yoruba dialectical areas. Their region, Igbominaland, extends across southern Kwara State and northern Osun State. Traditional Igbominaland includes local government areas in both states, with major cities including Oke-Ila, Ora, Ila Orangun, Omu-Aran, Ajasse Ipo, Oro, and Igbaja. In folk-culture terms, the sources identify long-distance trading, agriculture, hunting, woodcarving, leather art, fortified settlements, carved stones, and the Elewe masquerade.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Igbomina defensive architecture included earthen ramparts, stone walls, mud walls, deep ditches, rock piles, boulders, and stone embankments. These structures were intended to slow attackers and provide advantageous positions for defenders. Gbagede was a fortified settlement with a mud-wall enclosure and existed from 1795 to the pre-19th century. Iyara and Ila-Yara had massive rampart walls with deep ditches, built in the late 16th or early 17th century. In northeastern Igbomina, stone piles and boulders were especially associated with hilltop sites, while some areas combined stone embankments with mud walls. These embankments could extend for up to 1 km, and construction began from the 18th century. A circular stone foundation was identified as a guards house or sentry where armed guards watched for enemy soldiers.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do record more than 800 carved stones, mostly representing human figures, found around Esie in western Igbomina, Iji-Isin, Ijara, and Ofaro villages. The sculptures appear to have been made around 1100 AD, although the sources state that it is not known who crafted them.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources mention the Elewe masquerade as an Egungun representing the ancestors during special festivals. No festival name, calendar date, season, or ritual sequence is provided.

The sources do record oral traditions concerning royal and non-royal migrations from Oduduwa’s Ile-Ife. These migrations encountered existing dynasties, which were displaced, subsumed, or subjugated. Igbominaland is described as predating the Oduduwa era.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources refer to oral traditions of royal and non-royal migrations from Oduduwa’s Ile-Ife. These traditions describe the arrival of dynasties that encountered existing political formations in Igbominaland and displaced, subsumed, or subjugated them. The sources also preserve historical information about conflicts involving the Oyo, Ijesha, Ekiti, Nupe, YagbaEkiti, Fulani Ilorin armies, and Ibadan.

## Language & religion

The Ìgbómìnà speak a dialect called Ìgbómìnà or Igbonna. It is classified among the Central Yoruba of the three major Yoruba dialectical areas. Peripheral parts of the dialectical region share similarities with the Ekiti, Ijesha, Yagba, and Oyo dialects. The sources identify the Ìgbómìnà as an ethnic subgroup of the Yoruba people.
