## Overview

The Mambwe live in northeastern Zambia and western Tanzania. They speak Mambwe, also called Cimambwe, a Bantu language. Their traditional culture includes music, dance, harvest ceremonies, clan history and ancestor worship. Most Mambwe people today are Christian.

## Dance & theatre

Traditional dance appears in both Mambwe music and the annual harvest ceremony.

- **Nsimba dance** (*Nsimba*) — Nsimba is identified as a traditional Mambwe dance.
- **Harvest dances** — Traditional dances are performed during the ceremony celebrating a successful harvest and praying for rain.

## Festivals & rituals

The main named ceremony celebrates the harvest, gives thanks to ancestors and asks for rain.

- **Annual harvest ceremony** — The Mambwe and Lungu of Tafuna hold this ceremony every last Saturday of June on the shores of Lake Chila.
- **Chiefs’ food blessing** — Chiefs bless food samples presented by their wives, give thanks to ancestral spirits, taste the food and then allow people to eat.
- **Shrine thanksgiving** — People leave part of each crop at a shrine and thank the ancestral spirits after the harvest.
- **Appeal during hardship** — Chiefs and their people appeal to shrines during drought, crop failure, locust invasions, disease and invasion.
- **Land shrines** (*mayao; maleza*) — Hills, caves, large rocks and permanent pools of water are treated as sacred places associated with ancestral spirits.

## Foodways

The sources mention harvest food offerings and wartime starvation, but do not name ordinary dishes or crops.

- **Chiefdom food samples** — At the annual harvest ceremony, chiefs’ wives present samples of food grown in their chiefdoms.
- **Harvest thanksgiving food** — Part of each crop is left at a shrine during thanks to the ancestral spirits.
- **Wartime starvation foods** — During conflict with the Bemba, some Mambwe were forced to live on rats, dogs and baby-carrying bark cloth.

## Oral tradition & literature

Mambwe origin accounts trace the ruling line from Changala through Mwimbe and Nsokolo.

- **Changala** — Changala is said to have come from Kola, been a Mulua, and founded the ruling line.
- **Chindo** — Chindo was the head of the Simwinga clan and held a commoner title.
- **Nsokolo** — Nsokolo was the senior royal title in the origin account.
- **Mambwe clans** — Named clans include Sichula, Sichilima, Simpemba, Simwinga and Simpasa.
- **Mambwe Proverbs** — Mambwe Proverbs by A. Werner and A. N. Tucker is listed among the relevant literature.

## Language & religion

Mambwe is a Bantu language, while traditional religion centers on Leza, chiefly ancestors and sacred natural places.

- **Mambwe language** (*Cimambwe*) — Mambwe, also called Cimambwe, is a Bantu language and a dialect of Mambwe-Lungu.
- **Supreme creator** (*Leza*) — Leza created the earth and everything on it but is described as remote and not worshiped.
- **Chiefly ancestral spirits** (*imipasi yamwene yafwe*) — Worship is directed toward the spirits of chiefly ancestors.
- **Ancestral spirits** (*imipasi*) — Imipasi are ancestral spirits associated with land shrines.
- **Village ancestor shrines** (*kavua*) — Kavua shrines were erected in villages for local chiefly ancestors.
