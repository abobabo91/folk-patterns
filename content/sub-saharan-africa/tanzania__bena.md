## Overview

The Bena are a Bantu group of south-central Tanzania with a population around 670,000. Their language has several dialects that differ in how consonants sound. What we know of Bena culture comes mainly from pottery, baskets, ironwork and musical instruments held at the British Museum, plus photographs of women firing pots outdoors.

## Material culture

Bena crafts are practical objects made from natural materials—wood, plant fiber, iron, clay and skin—built by the same people who use them.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Bena decorative work appears in plant fiber baskets and pottery rather than cloth.

- **Twined grass bottle** — A bottle-shaped vessel made by twining grass with bands of pale and dark yellow fibers that form the pattern.
- **Plaited bamboo dish** — A shallow basketry dish made from bamboo in a twill pattern with a hoop rim.
- **Graphite-burnished pottery** — Pottery decorated with graphite, which gives the surface its finish rather than paint or carving.

### Clothing & dress

Almost nothing is known about Bena dress except that photographs show women wearing cloths while working outdoors.

### Architecture

Bena buildings and settlement are not documented, but evidence suggests pottery is fired outdoors at open hearths and smithing happens at fixed working places.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pottery and ironwork are the strongest evidence of Bena craft skill and daily life.

- **Pottery vessels** — Bowls, pedestal bowls, vases, lids and other earthenware pots, mostly plain, fired outdoors by women working in groups around a hearth.
- **Hoe blade** — An iron tool that also served as currency in bridewealth and exchange.
- **Hippopotamus harpoon-head** — An iron hunting weapon that shows the Bena lived near water large enough for hippos.
- **Smith's bellows** (*tuyère*) — A forked wooden nozzle carved in one piece with box-shaped hollows, showing the smith had a fixed working place.
- **Wooden calendar** — A string of wooden cylindrical pendants used to track time, though what interval each pendant marks is unknown.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Jewelry and body decoration are not documented in available sources.

- **Sansa beads** — Beads attached to a lamellophone as a jangle to add a buzzing rattle to the sound.

## Music & performance

Three Bena instruments survive: plucked, struck and blown types, all made for household use rather than court music.

- **Sansa** — A lamellophone with metal keys plucked by the thumbs, hung with beads that jangle against it to create a buzzing sound.
- **Drum** — A wooden drum with a skin head tuned by wax weighting, used in music-making.
- **Combined flute and rattle** — A wooden instrument that both blows and shakes, with decoration scorched into the wood rather than carved or painted.

## Dance & theatre

No Bena dances or theatrical traditions are documented in available sources.

## Festivals & rituals

No Bena festival calendar, birth rites, initiations, weddings or funerals are described in the sources.

- **Wooden calendar** — A string of wooden pendants used to track time, showing that time was marked on a portable object, though its ritual use is unknown.

## Foodways

Bena food and cooking methods are unknown, but equipment shows they farmed, hunted and cooked in pots over fire.

- **Iron hoe blade** — A farming and clearance tool that also functioned as currency.
- **Iron bill-hook** — A tanged iron tool made by the smith for farming and land clearance.
- **Hippopotamus harpoon-head** — A hunting weapon showing the Bena had access to water large enough to hold hippos.
- **Wooden spoons and ladles** — Utensils for cooking and serving food.
- **Pottery cooking vessels** — Pots and lids used for cooking and storage over fire rather than baking or grilling.

## Oral tradition & literature

No Bena folktales, epics or proverbs are documented, though the language itself has been studied.

- **Bena language grammar** — Michelle Elizabeth Morrison's doctoral thesis describes the sound system and grammar of Bena, including dialect differences.

## Language & religion

Bena is a Bantu language with dialect differences in how consonants sound, while religion is not documented.

- **Maswamu dialect** (*Maswamu*) — A Bena dialect that can pronounce /k/ and /h/ as a velar fricative and /j/ as a palatal stop.
- **Twangabita dialect** (*Twangabita*) — A Bena dialect that can pronounce /ts/ as a different kind of affricate.
- **Njombe highlands Bena** — The main Bena population group, distinguished by place rather than speech differences.
- **Ulanga offshoot** — A Bena group that split off from the Njombe highlands Bena, distinguished by place rather than speech differences.

## Glossary

- *Wabena* — Another name for the Bena people
- *sansa* — A lamellophone with plucked metal keys hung with beads
- *tuyère* — A forked wooden nozzle of a bellows used in ironworking
- *Maswamu* — A Bena dialect spoken in some areas of Njombe and Iringa
- *Twangabita* — A Bena dialect spoken in some areas of Njombe and Iringa
- *Bantu* — A language family and group of related peoples of Africa
- *Njombe* — A highland region of south-central Tanzania where the main Bena population lives
- *Ulanga* — A region where an offshoot Bena population lives, descended from the Njombe Bena
- *twill pattern* — A weaving or basketry pattern made by interlacing threads or fibers diagonally
