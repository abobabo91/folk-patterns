## Overview

The Tswana are a Bantu people of Southern Africa. Their society has been shaped by chiefdoms, clans, cattle, customary law, craft work, public assemblies, and initiation systems. Music, food, clothing, and rituals are tied to weddings, funerals, seasons, and community life. Setswana is spoken mainly in Botswana and South Africa, and also in Namibia and Zimbabwe.

## Material culture

Tswana material culture includes textiles, leatherwork, pottery, woodwork, basketry, metalwork, beadwork, and objects made from ostrich eggshell, horn, ivory, and fibre.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Tswana textile traditions include fabrics and blankets worn for weddings, initiation ceremonies, baby-showers, and funerals.

- **Cotton celebration fabric** (*Leteisi*) — Leteisi, also called Shweshwe, is a cotton fabric worn at weddings and other traditional celebrations.
- **Checkered small blanket** (*Mogagolwane*) — Mogagolwane is worn by mothers at traditional baby-showers and by married women at weddings, initiation ceremonies, and funerals.
- **Triangular patterns** — Museum descriptions name triangular patterns among Tswana motifs.
- **Incised oval motifs** — Museum descriptions name incised oval motifs among Tswana decorations.

### Clothing & dress

Tswana dress combines blankets, cloth, leather, beads, fibre, wire, and decorated ornaments.

- **Checkered small blanket** (*Mogagolwane*) — Women wear mogagolwane at baby-showers, weddings, initiation ceremonies, and funerals.
- **Cotton celebration fabric** (*Leteisi*) — Leteisi, also called Shweshwe, is used for weddings and other traditional celebrations.
- **Women’s village clothing** — A museum photograph shows adult women wearing skirts, head-cloths, and blouses in a Tswana village.
- **Ox-hide cloak** — Museum objects include a cloak made of ox hide.
- **Dance head-dress** — A dance head-dress is made from porcupine bristles fixed to a leather band.

### Architecture

Traditional Tswana settlements used circular compounds, stone walls, thatched buildings, and houses surrounded by traditional walls.

- **Circular stone-walled compound** — Historic Tswana settlements included circular stone-walled family compounds.
- **Kweneng’ Ruins** — Kweneng’ Ruins were large settlements occupied from the 15th to the 19th century.
- **Circular thatched building** — A British Museum photograph shows a Tswana village with a circular building and a thatched roof.
- **Model thatched house** — A museum model house is made of thatch, paper, board, and straw on a marked base.
- **Traditional wall** — A modern house in Seronga is shown surrounded by a traditional wall linked to older building traditions.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Tswana crafts include pottery, metalwork, woodwork, basket weaving, and leatherwork.

- **Milk pot** — An earthenware milk pot has a narrow neck, black and red triangular patterns, and incised dots.
- **Red bowl** — A wide red earthenware bowl has faint black decoration.
- **Tripod vessel** — A wooden tripod vessel is decorated with chevrons.
- **Wooden bowl** — A wooden bowl has a short handle.
- **Iron weapons** — Metalwork includes iron-bladed daggers, knives, and battle-axes.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Tswana adornment uses beads, brass, copper, wire, ostrich eggshell, leather, fibre, horn, ivory, and buttons.

- **Seed-and-bead necklace** — Some necklaces are made from alternating seeds and beads.
- **Ostrich-eggshell necklace** — Some necklaces use ostrich eggshell pieces strung on twisted fibre.
- **Brass armlets and bangles** — Armlets and bangles are made from brass coils, brass wire, copper beads, and bright iron wire.
- **Cane charm** — A charm is made from cane, string, gourd ends, buttons, and beads.
- **Horn snuff-box** — A snuff-flask or box is made from springbok horn.

## Music & performance

Tswana music is mostly vocal and is performed at feasts, weddings, beer gatherings, initiation ceremonies, and seasonal work.

- **Initiation songs** (*Moamo*) — Moamo is music made during initiation ceremonies.
- **Folklore songs** (*Mainaane a segologolo*) — Mainaane a segologolo are folklore songs described as being of great importance.
- **Unison singing** (*Pina e e kodunngwe*) — Pina e e kodunngwe means songs sung in unison.
- **Call and response** (*Segalodimo / segalo tlase*) — In multipart songs, segalodimo is the call and segalo tlase is the response.
- **Tswana rap** (*Motswako*) — Motswako is modern Tswana rap.

## Dance & theatre

Tswana dance is linked to music, feasts, beer gatherings, weddings, initiation, farming seasons, and group activity.

- **Beer-dance** — Beer-dances are associated with Selemo, the summer season.
- **Group dancing** — Group dancing may use drums, antelope horns, and leg rattles.
- **Drum dance accompaniment** (*Moropa*) — The moropa drum may accompany group dancing.
- **Antelope-horn accompaniment** (*Lepapata*) — The lepapata antelope horn may accompany group dancing.
- **Leg-rattle accompaniment** (*Mathlo*) — Mathlo leg rattles may accompany group dancing.

## Festivals & rituals

Tswana communal life follows seasonal activities and includes female and male initiation, marriage negotiations, weddings, and funerals.

- **Autumn work season** (*Letlhafula*) — Letlhafula is linked with hoeing and work-party songs.
- **Winter fireside season** (*Mariga*) — Mariga is linked with children’s fireside story songs.
- **Female initiation** (*Bojale*) — Bojale incorporates girls into female mephato and teaches songs, responsibilities, morality, marriage, and motherhood.
- **Male initiation** (*Bogwane*) — Bogwane is a preparatory phase before boys enter initiation camps, are circumcised, trained, named, and given weapons.
- **Marriage payment** (*Bogadi*) — Bogadi formalises marriage and usually consists of cattle.

## Foodways

Tswana foodways centre on sorghum, meat, milk, and foods prepared for everyday life and celebrations.

- **Sorghum staple** (*Bogobe*) — Bogobe is sorghum meal eaten with meat or vegetables.
- **Sorghum porridge** (*Ting*) — Ting is the most popular sorghum porridge.
- **Milk porridge** (*Bogobe jwa Logala / Sengana*) — Bogobe jwa Logala, or Sengana, is sorghum porridge mixed or cooked with milk.
- **Pounded meat** (*Seswaa*) — Seswaa is pounded or shredded meat often served with bogobe at weddings, funerals, and other celebrations.
- **Sour cultured milk** (*Madila*) — Madila is sour cultured cow or goat milk eaten as relish with pap and used in motogo.

## Oral tradition & literature

Tswana oral tradition includes proverbs about public speech and creation stories about spiritual beings and ancestral figures.

- **Council proverb** (*Mmualebe o a bo a bua la gagwe*) — This proverb says that even someone who speaks badly must be allowed to speak.
- **Public assembly** (*Kgotla*) — The kgotla is a place where people voice opinions and the Kgosi brings views together.
- **Supreme creator** (*Modimo*) — Creation myths include Modimo.
- **Ancestral spirits** (*Badimo*) — Creation myths include Badimo.
- **Matsieng footprints** (*Matsieng*) — Matsieng is said to have left giant footprints in sandstone.

## Language & religion

Setswana is a Bantu lingua franca, while traditional belief centres on Modimo, ancestors, rain, and rituals alongside Christianity.

- **Tswana language** (*Setswana*) — Setswana is a Bantu language spoken in Botswana, South Africa, Zimbabwe, and Namibia.
- **Urban variety** (*Sepitori*) — Sepitori is an urbanised variety associated with Pretoria.
- **Supreme spiritual force** (*Modimo*) — Modimo is a supreme spiritual force linked with creation and cosmic order.
- **Ancestral spirits** (*Badimo*) — Badimo are ancestral spirits who act as intermediaries between living people and Modimo.
- **Rain** (*Pula*) — Ancestors are believed to influence weather and the arrival of rain, called Pula.
