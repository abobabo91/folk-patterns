## Overview

The Anyi, also known as Agni, Anyin, Aowin, and other names, are an Akan people living mainly in southeastern Côte d'Ivoire and southwestern Ghana. Their historical origins are associated with the ancient Akan state of Bonoman, from which groups migrated during the 17th and early 18th centuries. Anyi polities included Aowin, Indénié, Sanwi, Moronou, Bettié, and Diabé. Contemporary communities are concentrated in southeastern Côte d'Ivoire, including Abengourou, the Zanzan District, the N'zi-Comoé Region, and areas linked to Sanwi. A related population lives in Ghana's Aowin Municipal District, which recorded 157,159 people in 2021. Anyi culture includes matrilineal kinship, chieftaincy, ancestor veneration, funerary art, agriculture, markets, and ritual specialists known as Kômians.

Anyi settlements are typically composed of dispersed extended-family compounds.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They state that funerary art includes elaborate monuments displaying familial wealth, status, and reverence for the dead.

### Jewelry & body adornment

 The sources mention the adornment of the deceased during funeral ceremonies without describing the objects used.

## Music & performance

 They state that Kômians are ritual specialists organized into secret societies and that they perform advisory, divinatory, and ceremonial functions. Through trance states, Kômians mediate between living people and spiritual forces to obtain guidance.

## Dance & theatre

 They mention ceremonial functions among Kômians but provide no description of dance or theatre.

## Festivals & rituals

Traditional Akan religion continues to underpin Anyi spiritual life through ancestor veneration, cosmological order, and ritual propriety. Funeral ceremonies are central cultural events and include ritual cleansing, adornment of the deceased, public mourning, and symbolic transition into the spiritual realm. Kômians serve as ritual specialists and conduct advisory, divinatory, and ceremonial activities through trance states. Marriage customs include the exchange of *O-Bla-Kale*, a financial provision for the bride's upbringing and education; *Adyia-Tila*, trousseau or dowry preparation; and *Be-Ti-Sika*, a symbolic bond between the bride and her natal family. Adultery was traditionally subject to sanctions including social exclusion or, in severe cases, capital punishment. Female pre-marital disclosures were regarded as necessary for lineage legitimacy and communal honor. The sources mention festivals in Ghana commemorating ancestral ties to the historic Aowin state but provide no festival names or dates.

## Foodways

Anyi livelihoods are predominantly agrarian. Staple crops include banana, taro, and yam, while maize, cassava, peppers, peanuts, tomatoes, squash, and sweet potatoes are also cultivated. Livestock husbandry includes sheep, goats, poultry, and dogs. Periodic markets are held every four days and are primarily operated by women; they exchange agricultural produce, artisanal goods, and imported commodities. Forestry and palm oil production also contribute to the economy, with palm oil used for domestic consumption and international trade. In the Aowin Municipal District, cocoa cultivation, oil palm processing, timber extraction, and localized commerce are important economic activities.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that Anyi origins and migrations are described through oral tradition and historical accounts. They connect the Agni movement from present-day Ghana during the 17th and early 18th centuries with the expansionist pressures of Denkyira and the Asante Empire.

## Language & religion

Anyin, also known as Agni, Agny, and Anyi, is a Niger-Congo language of the Kwa branch of the Akan family. It is spoken mainly in Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana and forms a dialect continuum with Baoulé. It is closely related to Nzema and Sehwi. Its dialect areas include Sannvin, Abé, Ano, Bona, Bini, and Barabo in the Northern area, and Ndenye and Juablin in the Central area. In Côte d'Ivoire there are approximately 1.45 million native speakers and 10,000 second-language users; Ghana has approximately 66,400 speakers. Morofo, spoken by 300,000 people in southeastern Côte d'Ivoire, is classified in the sources either as an Anyin dialect or as a separate language. Traditional Akan religion emphasizes ancestors, cosmological order, and ritual propriety, while Kômians mediate with spiritual forces through trance.
