## Overview

The Gbandi, also known as Bandi, Bande, Gbande, Gbunde, or Gbande, are an ethnic group of Liberia. They live chiefly in Lofa County in northern Liberia, where Kolahun is their major demographic city and district headquarters. The Gbandi are bordered by the Kissi, Mende, Gola, Kuwaa, and Loma peoples. Their population is estimated at 107,000 people, although many fled to Guinea and Sierra Leone during the Liberian Civil War. Gbandi is a Southwestern Mande language within the Niger-Congo language family. Agriculture, rice cultivation, family organization, marriage customs, ancestor beliefs, and traditional religion are central subjects in the available description of Gbandi life.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe religious beliefs and life-cycle customs. Gbandi people practice Islam and Christianity as well as traditional beliefs; 10% identify as Christian, including 1% Evangelical, 10% as Islamic, and 80% as practicing traditional religions. Many believe in animism.

Ancestors are held in the highest regard, and the Gbandi believe in reincarnation. Bringing disgrace to ancestors through malicious behavior or falsehoods is considered incredibly serious. The sources state that the Gbandi view death as occurring for one of three reasons: the ancestors called the person, the person was killed by a witch, or the person was killed by “the witch within himself.”

Girls are placed into arranged marriages from childhood, usually infancy. Grooms are expected to pay a dowry before the wedding. Men may have more than one wife, and widows typically become married to their brother-in-law. Parallel or familial marriage, including cousin-marriage, is prohibited.

## Foodways

Agriculture is central to the Gbandi economy, and the people are primarily farmers. Rice is grown in swamps and on hillsides. At least eight months of the calendar year are dedicated to planting, nurturing, and harvesting subsistence and cash crops. Planting rice is undertaken by all able-bodied members of the family, while women are usually in charge of subsistence farming.

Cabbage, potatoes, and okra are grown behind the home. Young men and boys are typically responsible for the family’s cattle, sheep, and goats.

## Language & religion

Gbandi, also called Bandi, Bande, Gbande, or Gbunde, is a Mande language spoken primarily in Lofa County in northern Liberia by the Gbandi people. It belongs to the Niger-Congo language family. The language has six dialects: Tahamba, Wawana, Wulukoha, Hasala, Lukasa, and Hembeh. The dialects have 96% lexical similarity among one another, and 83% similarity with the most similar dialect of the Mende language. Tahamba is used mainly for literature, and the language is written in the Latin style.

Gbandi religious life includes Islam, Christianity, and traditional beliefs. The Gbandi are described as believing in a polytheistic religious structure permeating everyday life. Ancestor spirits have the highest regard, and reincarnation is an important belief. Many Gbandi believe in animism.
