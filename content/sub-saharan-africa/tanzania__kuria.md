## Overview

The Kuria live in Tanzania’s Mara Region and southern Kenya. They farm, keep cattle, and grow crops such as maize, beans, cassava, coffee, and sweet potatoes. Their clan traditions trace their ancestry to Mokurya and a migration from Misiri. Museum records show ornaments, a leather belt, finger-knives, and men playing drums.

## Material culture

The documented material culture centers on leather, metal, glass beads, cowrie shells, silver chains, soapstone, and iron objects.

### Clothing & dress

Museum records show decorated belts, cloths, and body ornaments.

- **Man’s chained and beaded leather belt** — A museum record describes a man’s leather belt decorated with chains and glass beads.
- **Cloths and body ornaments** — A photograph shows two adult males wearing cloths, neck-ornaments, arm-rings, and ear-ornaments.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum catalogue records four iron finger-knives, but their use and style are not explained.

- **Iron finger-knives** — The museum catalogue records four finger-knives made of iron.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Kuria adornment in the museum records uses metal, glass beads, leather, cowrie shells, silver chains, and soapstone.

- **Beaded metal ear ornament** — A man’s metal ear ornament has glass beads suspended on leather thongs.
- **Coiled metal ear ornament** — Another man’s ear ornament uses coiled metal, metal chains, white glass beads, and two small hide thongs.
- **Beaded and shell necklace** — One necklace has glass beads, a leather pendant, cowrie shells, and silver chains.
- **Silver-chain pendant necklace** — Another necklace has glass and silver beads, chains, and a leather pendant covered with silver chains, beads, and a cowrie shell.
- **Soapstone armlet** — The museum records include a man’s soapstone armlet.

## Music & performance

A photograph shows two adult males playing drums, but the drums, songs, and performance setting are not named.

- **Drum playing** — A photographic print shows one man sitting and another squatting while playing drums.

## Festivals & rituals

The Kuria practice circumcision for both males and females, although modern practices also exist.

- **Male and female circumcision** — The Kuria practice circumcision for both males and females until today.

## Foodways

Kuria food crops include maize, beans, and cassava, while coffee and sweet potatoes are described as cash crops.

- **Maize** — The Kuria grow maize as a food crop and it is also listed among cash crops.
- **Beans** — The Kuria grow beans as a food crop.
- **Cassava** — The Kuria grow cassava as a food crop.
- **Coffee** — Coffee is described as a cash crop.
- **Sweet potatoes and yams** — Sweet potatoes and yams are listed among the cash crops.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kuria clan traditions trace the people to Mokurya, whose descendants migrated from Misiri and reached Bukurya.

- **Mokurya** — Major clan traditions name Mokurya as the ancestor of the Kuria.
- **Migration from Misiri** (*Misiri*) — The traditions say Mokurya’s descendants migrated from Misiri, or Egypt.
- **Journey to Bukurya** (*Bukurya*) — The traditions say the migrants eventually reached Bukurya after wandering along Lake Victoria.
- **The Abasai** (*Abasai*) — The Abasai are described as descendants of Mokurya’s elder wife.
- **The Abachuma** (*Abachuma*) — The Abachuma are described as descendants of Mokurya’s younger wife.

## Language & religion

Kuria is a Bantu language spoken mainly in Northern Tanzania and also in Kenya; the profile does not describe Kuria religion.

- **Kuria language** (*Kuria*) — Kuria is a Bantu language spoken by the Kuria people of Northern Tanzania, with some speakers in Kenya.
- **Simbiti variety** (*Simbiti*) — Maho treats Simbiti as a distinct language.
- **Hacha variety** (*Hacha*) — Maho treats Hacha as a distinct language.
- **Surwa variety** (*Surwa*) — Maho treats Surwa as a distinct language.
- **Sweta variety** (*Sweta*) — Maho treats Sweta as a distinct language.
