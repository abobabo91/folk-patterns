## Overview

The Kuba are a confederation of nineteen ethnic groups living in the savanna and gallery forest of the Kasai region in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Their court title system — in which half of all Bushoong men held titles in the 1880s — turned ordinary household objects into competitive displays of skill and creativity. Kuba geometric design is one of the most elaborate non-representational ornamental systems documented anywhere, appearing on cloth, carved wood, walls, and even the human body through scarification.

## Material culture

Kuba material culture centers on geometric patterning applied across raffia textiles, carved wood, woven walls, and body adornment as a unified design system.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Kuba cloth — raffia textiles woven and embroidered with geometric designs — is the foundation of their material culture.

- **Kuba cloth** — Raffia textiles woven by men and then embroidered, appliquéd and dyed by women, with a heavy, matte, slightly stiff finish.
- **Shoowa cut-pile embroidery** (*Kasai velvet*) — Dyed raffia threaded through ground weave, pulled up as loops, and sheared flush to create a plush surface where the embroiderer deliberately breaks symmetry as the design advances.
- **Ntshak** — A wrapped dance skirt made from multiple woven raffia lengths joined end to end, worn by both sexes at ceremonies and funerals.
- **Mbal** — Appliqué and patchwork cloth where cut shapes of contrasting raffia are stitched over the ground, often over deliberately opened holes, associated with women's mourning and dance skirts.
- **Tukula-dyed cloth** (*tukula*) — Raffia colored with red powder ground from camwood, central to the Kuba idea of beauty for dance and burial.

### Clothing & dress

Everyday dress is simple wrapped cloth, while ceremonial dress is among the most encumbered in Central Africa, with regalia announcing title and status.

- **Cache-sexe apron** (*cache-sexe*) — A small embroidered apron worn suspended from the waist cord, densely patterned with concentrated geometric design.
- **Mukyeem headdress** (*mukyeem*) — A beaded, cowrie-covered helmet with a swelling forehead and trunk-like projection referencing the elephant, reserved for the highest title ranks.
- **Beaded and cowrie-covered belt** (*belts*) — Built by netting and stitching shells and beads into dense polychrome geometry, each configuration licensed by title.
- **Tukula face and body reddening** (*tukula*) — Red powder applied to faces, chests and hair before performance as cosmetic and ritual color.

### Architecture

Villages are laid out on rectangular plans with houses whose woven and plaited raffia walls display the same geometric patterns as cloth.

- **Woven wall panels** (*walls*) — Whole panels woven and plaited from raffia palm ribs and split canes into geometric lattices of chevrons, lozenges, and interlace at architectural scale.
- **Patterned plaited palisades** (*palisades*) — Tall screened enclosures around royal compounds, patterned and placed to control sightlines and mark the graded approach to the court.
- **Central plaza** (*plaza*) — The ceremonial center of villages where the king's compound and men's meeting houses are located.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Kuba carved wood objects for court competition display the same geometric vocabulary as cloth, making them the signature objects of the culture.

- **Palm-wine cups** (*mbwoongntey*) — Carved wooden cups in cylinder, horn, head or half-figure forms with incised interlace surfaces, owned and drunk from by titled men.
- **Kuba boxes** (*ngedi mu ntey*) — Carved wooden containers for tukula powder, razors, hairpins and ritual items, in square, rectangular, mask, and crescent shapes.
- **Ndop figures** (*ndop*) — Seated commemorative portraits of individual kings introduced after 1700, each identified by a personal emblem carved on the plinth.
- **Iron and copper ceremonial blades** (*blades*) — Ceremonial knives and blades with openwork designs, signaling wealth and rank.
- **Wooden drinking horns** (*horns*) — Carved with ridges and incisions, used for consuming palm wine at ceremonies.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment is built from cowrie shells, imported glass beads, and copper worked onto fiber, with each configuration licensed by title.

- **Cowrie and bead belts** (*belts*) — Built by netting and stitching shells and beads into dense polychrome geometric patterns, each configuration marking a specific title.
- **Copper and brass rings and collars** (*rings*) — Neck collars, arm bands and rings that signal wealth and status across the titled hierarchy.
- **Leopard-tooth and claw pendants** (*pendants*) — Chiefly and royal ornaments made from predator remains.
- **Keloid scarification** (*scarification*) — Raised patterns of lozenges and interlace on belly, chest and temples that repeat the textile vocabulary on the body itself.
- **Carved hairpins and combs** (*pins*) — Decorative and functional objects used to dress and style the hair.

## Music & performance

Kuba music is court music first, combining percussion and melodic instruments to accompany ranked performance tied to the title system.

- **Royal drum** (*drum*) — The largest drum in the royal ensemble, surfaced with beads and cowries in the manner of regalia.
- **Slit drums** (*drums*) — Percussion instruments that encode proverbs and names so the instrument speaks as well as sounds.
- **Xylophones** — Raffia-and-gourd resonated melodic instruments played in royal ensembles.
- **Ivory and antelope-horn trumpets** (*trumpets*) — Side-blown melodic instruments whose calls announce the king's presence.
- **Praise songs** — Performed before the throne by titled singers recounting royal genealogy and the deeds of Shyaam a-Mbul a Ngoong.

## Dance & theatre

Kuba masquerade centers on a triad of royal masks danced together as a mythic drama of founding and succession.

- **Mwaash aMbooy** (*mwaash amboy*) — The king mask made of leopard skin, cowries and beads, representing Woot the first human and founder.
- **Bwoom** — A broad helmet mask with bulging forehead and copper sheet over wood, representing the commoner, subversive prince, or Twa who contests with the king mask.
- **Ngady aMwaash** — The female mask painted in triangles and stripes with beaded lines signaling tears, danced by a man as Woot's sister and wife.
- **Mukyeem** — An elephant-trunked beaded helmet appearing at royal funerals and accessions.
- **Nkaan masks** (*nkaan*) — Non-danced masks used in men's initiation to police and instruct novices in a forest enclosure.

## Festivals & rituals

The ceremonial year is keyed to the court and agricultural cycle, with major events centered on royal accession, funerals, and title-taking.

- **Royal accession rites** (*accession*) — Ceremonies involving seclusion, investiture with regalia, commissioning of a new ndop, and masked dancers.
- **Royal funerals** (*funeral*) — Events where the king is buried with court treasures, the whole kingdom's title hierarchy assembles, and masked dancers perform.
- **Nkaan initiation** (*nkaan*) — Men's initiation in which novices are secluded in a bush enclosure, undergo ordeals, encounter masked beings, and emerge reddened with tukula.
- **Title-taking ceremonies** (*title-taking*) — Occasions marking adult life when a man commissions a carved cup or embroidered skirt to display his new rank.
- **First-fruits observances** (*first-fruits*) — Harvest celebrations marking maize and cassava crops that underwrote the kingdom's eighteenth-century wealth.

## Foodways

The staples are cassava and maize, both American crops adopted early, prepared in sauces flavored with palm oil, groundnut paste, and fish.

- **Fufu** — Stiff porridge made from cassava flour stirred and eaten by hand in balls dipped into sauce, the everyday center of the meal.
- **Cassava leaves relish** (*pondu*) — Cassava leaves pounded and simmered long with palm oil into a dense dark relish.
- **Palm wine** — Tapped from raffia and oil palm and drunk lightly fermented, the ceremonial drink and reason for the carved cup as signature Kuba object.
- **Protein sources** (*protein*) — River fish, game from the gallery forest, goat, chicken, caterpillars and termites in season.
- **Kola nut and tobacco** (*kola*) — Offered in hospitality, exchange, and ceremonial contexts alongside palm wine.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kuba oral tradition is unusually historical, centered on remembered king lists and narratives of state founding transmitted by titled specialists.

- **Woot** — The first human who named the animals and brought civilization, whose incest with his sister underlies the royal masquerade.
- **Shyaam a-Mbul a Ngoong** — The culture hero who around 1625 travelled west, learned from other kingdoms, returned, usurped a local ruler and founded the confederated state.
- **Bomazi** — A founding ancestor figure appearing in the origin traditions.
- **Animal trickster tales** (*trickster tales*) — Oral narratives featuring animals, transmitted as evening entertainment.
- **Proverbs** — Sayings encoded in drum language and carved as emblems on cups and ndop plinths so they can be told, drummed, or displayed.

## Language & religion

The Kuba speak Bantu languages with Bushong as prestige tongue; cosmology centers on Bumba the sky father and Woot the civilizing first human.

- **Bushong language** (*Bushong*) — The court and prestige language spoken among the Kuba confederation.
- **Regional lingua francas** (*Kituba*) — Kikongo-based Kituba and Lingala serving as regional trade languages across the broader Congo region.
- **Bumba** — The distant sky father who produced sun, moon, stars and planets and, with the earth mother, life.
- **Woot veneration** (*Woot*) — The first human and bringer of civilization receives immediate religious attention and is the focus of ritual life.
- **Divination and charm-making** (*divination*) — Active spiritual practices centered on ancestral intervention and nature spirits that coexist with Christianity.

- *ntshak* — A wrapped dance skirt made from multiple woven raffia lengths

- *mbwoongntey* — Carved wooden palm-wine cups in various forms with incised geometric designs
- *ngedi mu ntey* — Kuba carved wooden boxes for holding tukula powder, razors, and ritual items
- *ndop* — Seated commemorative portrait of a Kuba king, identified by a personal emblem on the plinth
- *mwaash amboy* — The king mask representing Woot, made of leopard skin, cowries and beads
- *bwoom* — A broad helmet mask representing the commoner or subversive prince in the royal masquerade
- *ngady amwaash* — The female mask in the royal masquerade, representing Woot's sister and wife

- *fufu* — Stiff porridge made from cassava flour, eaten in balls dipped into sauce
