## Overview

The Kuba are a confederation of Bantu peoples in central Congo, organized since the seventeenth century under a paramount ruler called the nyim. They are renowned for their embroidered raphia textiles, royal portrait statues, and masked theatrical traditions. Their visual culture—from cloth to carved cups to palace matwork—is saturated with named geometric patterns that function as both decoration and language. The confederation traces its cultural flowering to king Shyaam a-Mbul a Ngoong around 1625, who introduced maize, cassava, and the raphia weaving techniques that made them famous.

## Material culture

Kuba material culture centers on decorated small objects and textiles, with geometric interlace patterns repeated across cloth, wood, matwork and metalwork as a conscious formal system.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Kuba textile art is built on raphia cloth embroidered with named geometric motifs that Bushoong artists discuss as a discursive vocabulary.

- **Raphia cloth** (*mbal, mbala*) — The base fabric, woven by men on a single-heddle loom from young raphia palm leaves, roughly one metre square and pounded soft before decoration.
- **Ntshak** — A long overskirt wrapper made of sewn raphia panels with appliqué, embroidery and tufted borders, worn for dance and funerals by both sexes.
- **Cut-pile embroidery** (*bushoong*) — Women pull dyed raphia threads through the woven ground and cut them flush with a knife to build a dense plush pile in geometric fields, creating the 'Kasai velvet' known worldwide.
- **Shoowa panels** (*Shoowa*) — Smaller prestige squares (50–60 cm) worked entirely in cut-pile and stem stitch, produced by Shoowa women as gifts, tribute and grave goods.
- **Geometric motif system** (*imbol, woot, bikwaam, nnaam, muyingu*) — Bushoong weavers name and combine dozens of interlace and lozenge patterns, deliberately breaking and shifting them mid-panel in the celebrated 'syncopation' that draws modern design interest.

### Clothing & dress

Ceremonial dress is made entirely of raphia and beadwork, with the king's regalia reaching extraordinary density of ornament and the colors red tukula, white kaolin and natural fiber marking rank and ritual states.

- **Men's court dress** (*ntshak, laket*) — A long ntshak wrapper, beaded belt, bare chest with shoulder cords of cowrie and glass beads, and for titled elders a conical cap with feathers.
- **King's regalia** (*bwaantshy, mukyeem*) — An astonishing ensemble of embroidered raphia skirt, beaded and cowrie-encrusted tunic, beaded belt with leopard-tooth apron, and the mukyeem helmet-mask sheathed in cowries and crowned with beadwork elephant tusks.
- **Women's dress** (*ntshak*) — A shorter ntshak overskirt above a plain raphia underskirt, with broad woven or beaded belt and sometimes a small beaded cache-sexe apron for initiation dances.
- **Body painting** (*tukula, kaolin*) — Red tukula camwood powder mixed with palm oil and white kaolin mark initiation and funerary states on the body.

### Architecture

Kuba villages follow a rigid grid layout attributed to king Shyaam, with houses of woven raphia mats and the palace matwork patterned with the same geometric motifs as the textiles.

- **Village grid** (*Shyaam*) — Villages are laid out in an unusual rectilinear grid pattern for the forest belt, a layout Kuba oral history attributes to king Shyaam.
- **House construction** — Rectangular houses have walls of woven raphia mats or split-palm laths lashed to timber frames and steep pitched roofs thatched in palm leaves.
- **Palace matwork** — The walls of the nyim's principal reception houses at Nsheng are covered with woven geometric matwork in dyed and natural palm strips, echoing the imbol and muyingu textile motifs.
- **Doorway frames** — Entrances of chiefly houses are framed by carved posts, and raphia wall panels are renewed periodically as a tribute obligation from villages.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Kuba daily life is dense with carved wooden objects—cups, boxes, headrests, pipes—all incised with the same interlace motifs as the cloth, while practical ceramics remain plain.

- **Palm-wine cups** (*mbwoongntey*) — Carved wooden cups shaped as human heads, cupped hands or janus figures, with surfaces incised in interlace motifs, central to the palm-wine drinking culture.
- **Cosmetic boxes** (*ngedi mu ntey*) — Crescent- or box-shaped containers with sliding lids that hold red tukula paste, carved with the formal pattern vocabulary.
- **Headrests** (*mutshaam*) — Carved with caryatid supports in the same geometric idiom as cups and boxes, used for sleeping.
- **Throwing knife** (*shongo*) — The multi-bladed knife forged by specialist smiths whose silhouette gave the confederation its name Bakuba, along with ceremonial swords and hoes.
- **Divination oracle** (*itoom, itombwa*) — A small carved animal rubbed with a disc to give yes/no answers for divination purposes.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Kuba adornment is dominated by beadwork, cowries and scarification rather than metal, with the density of ornament visibly marking rank and status.

- **Cowrie and bead regalia** (*bwaantshy*) — The king's bwaantshy is estimated to include tens of thousands of cowrie shells and glass beads sewn onto raphia and leather; titled dignitaries wear scaled-down versions.
- **Scarification** (*ngingi*) — Raised keloid patterns on the temples, chest and back echoing textile motifs, common into the mid-twentieth century and now attenuated.
- **Hair dressing** (*laket*) — Elaborate coiffures held with carved combs and mounted over fiber armature for women of rank to produce a high crest; men wear the laket conical cap.
- **Metal ornaments** — Copper and brass wristlets and anklets worn by titled women, and iron neck-rings by some age grades.

## Music & performance

Kuba music centers on drums, slit-gongs and voice, with the royal drum ensemble at the palace and griots chanting the king-list and royal genealogy.

- **Royal drum ensemble** (*pelambish, ngoma, lokombe*) — The great signal drum pelambish, double-headed ngoma, and wooden slit-gong lokombe whose tonal language carries formal announcements between villages.
- **Slit-gong** (*lokombe, nkumvi*) — A wooden tonal instrument that accompanies praise-singing of the nyim and recitation of the king-list.
- **Xylophones** (*madimba*) — Wooden xylophones with gourd resonators played by professional entertainers.
- **Praise-singing and griots** — Court historians chant eighty-odd names in the royal genealogy from Woot down to the reigning nyim, and senior women lead funerary laments.

## Dance & theatre

The great theatrical form is a masked drama enacting the royal origin myth, staged at investitures, funerals and boys' initiation, with three dominant masks each representing a figure in the founding story.

- **Mwaash a mbooy** — The founding ancestor and first king Woot, represented by a mask sheathed in leopard skin, cowries and beads with a raphia beard and trunk-like projection.
- **Ngady a mwaash** — Woot's sister-consort, painted with alternating black, white and red triangles across the face with diagonal cheek lines read as tears of the founding incest.
- **Bwoom** — A broad, bulging-browed helmet mask identified as a commoner, pygmy or rival prince whose interventions stage the tension between king and people.
- **Boys' initiation** (*nkaan*) — A ritual involving seclusion in a bush camp, tuition in patterns and proverbs, and emergence with new adult names and scarification.

## Festivals & rituals

The Kuba ritual calendar centers on the biography of the nyim and age-grade progressions, with royal installations and funerals as the great pan-Kuba occasions.

- **Royal installation** (*nyim*) — The installation of a new king at Nsheng is the great pan-Kuba occasion, drawing tribute delegations and requiring commissioning of fresh regalia, mats and masks.
- **Royal funeral** — The king is buried with a substantial part of the palace textile store; villages are levied for replacement ntshak panels as grave furniture.
- **Ndop commissioning** (*ndop, ibol*) — A seated portrait statue of a reigning nyim carved during his lifetime with an identifying emblem at the base, constituting one of Africa's few royal portrait sculpture traditions.
- **Boys' initiation** (*nkaan*) — Recurs at village level every few years, involving seclusion and tuition in ritual knowledge.
- **Divination and healing** (*ngesh*) — Specialist practitioners outside the court structure handle divination, healing and propitiation of nature spirits.

## Foodways

Kuba foodways reflect the savanna-forest edge, with cassava and maize as staples, palm oil as the principal fat, and palm wine as the ritual social drink.

- **Cassava preparation** (*mangbwadi, bidia*) — Cassava is prepared as a stiff fufu-type paste called bidia, the main staple alongside maize, plantain and cocoyam.
- **Palm wine** (*malafu*) — The ritual and social drink par excellence, central to Kuba life and responsible for the extraordinary elaboration of carved drinking cups.
- **Proteins** — Bushmeat from the forest edge, river fish, snails and caterpillars provide protein; goats and chickens are reserved for ceremonies.
- **Tobacco smoking** — Imported by Shyaam according to tradition, tobacco is smoked in long-stemmed carved pipes as a social and ritual act.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kuba oral literature is dominated by the royal chronicle—the memorised genealogy of the nyim from Woot to the present—with patterns themselves functioning as mnemonic and rhetorical system.

- **Royal chronicle** — The recited genealogy of the nyim from Woot through Shyaam a-Mbul a Ngoong to the present, memorised by court historians and performed at investitures and funerals.
- **Origin myths** (*Woot, Shyaam a-Mbul a Ngoong*) — Stories of the founding of the confederation and each subordinate chiefdom, centered on the culture-hero Shyaam credited around 1625 with importing key crops and weaving techniques.
- **Proverbs and tales** (*miningo*) — A rich body of proverbs, riddles and moral tales featuring tricksters and forest animals, with pattern itself functioning as visual proverb.
- **Pattern as language** (*imbol, ibol*) — Named interlace motifs on cloth, mat and cup are read as visual proverbs and discussed as a discursive vocabulary by Kuba artists and speakers.

## Language & religion

The Kuba speak closely related Bantu C-zone languages with Bushoong as the prestige dialect, and practice a syncretic religion combining ancestral veneration and nature-spirit propitiation with Christianity.

- **Bushoong language** (*Bushoong*) — The prestige court language and lingua franca of the confederation, with Ngeende, Kete and Shoowa mutually intelligible to varying degrees.
- **Creator god** (*Nyeeem, Mboom*) — The distant creator god, worshipped alongside the ancestors and nature spirits in traditional Kuba religion.
- **Ancestral veneration** (*Woot*) — The founding ancestor Woot and the line of nyim are central to Kuba religion and royal ritual, continuing in parallel with Christian practice.
- **Nature spirits** (*ngesh*) — Spirits associated with rivers, forest groves and particular villages, propitiated by specialist mediums.
- **Christianity** — Catholic and Protestant missions from the late nineteenth century made Christianity the majority affiliation, though ancestral ritual continues alongside it.

## Glossary

- *bakuba* — People of the throwing knife; the exonym for the confederation
- *bikwaam* — Crocodile back; a named pattern motif
- *bwoom* — Broad helmet mask representing a commoner or rival in the founding myth drama
- *bwaantshy* — Ceremonial regalia of the king, densely encrusted with cowries and beads
- *bushoong* — Cut-pile embroidery technique creating plush velvet-like surface
- *imbol* — The interlace knot; a named geometric pattern motif
- *ibol* — Identifying emblem carved at the base of an ndop portrait statue
- *itoom* — Friction oracle or divination instrument, a small carved animal
- *kaolin* — White earth or chalk used for body painting
- *laket* — Conical fiber or beaded cap worn by titled men, bristling with feathers
- *lokombe* — Wooden slit-gong instrument with tonal language for announcements
- *malafu* — Palm wine, the ritual and social drink
- *mbal* — Raphia cloth base fabric
- *mbwoongntey* — Carved wooden palm-wine cup
- *mukyeem* — Helmet-mask reserved to the king, sheathed in cowries and beads
- *muyingu* — Diamond-lattice grid pattern motif
- *ngady a mwaash* — Mask of Woot's sister-consort, painted with alternating colored triangles
- *ngingi* — Raised keloid scarification pattern
- *ngesh* — Nature spirits associated with rivers, forests and villages
- *nnaam* — Lightning zigzag pattern motif
- *ntshak* — Long overskirt wrapper made of sewn raphia panels with embroidery and appliqué
- *nyim* — The paramount ruler or king of the Kuba confederation
- *tukula* — Camwood powder mixed with palm oil, used for red body painting
