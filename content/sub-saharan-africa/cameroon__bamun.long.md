## Overview

The Bamun are a people of Cameroon’s highlands. Their language is known as Bamum, *Shü Pamom*, *Shümom*, Shupamem, Bamun, or Bamoun. Bamum is an Eastern Grassfields language with approximately 600,000–700,000 speakers in 2025. The language is associated with an original script developed by King Njoya and his palace circle in the Kingdom of Bamum around 1895. Museum catalogue material identifies Bamun objects including brass figures, smoking-pipes, masks, a staff finial, a palm-wine bottle, a woven bag, and an arm-dagger with sheath.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Palm-wine bottle** — A museum catalogue records a palm-wine bottle with bead mosaic.

**Woven bag** — A British Museum catalogue records a bag woven of raffia or sisal. It is made from a rectangular piece of beige fibre folded and sewn at the sides, with striped fringe, green X-shaped decoration, and a braided brown-fibre handle with tassels.

**Knife sheath** — The sheath of a small knife is made of leather. Its tanned-leather body has an undyed central strip embroidered with a thin leather thong and a small leather belt loop.

**Pipe-stem decoration** — One smoking-pipe has a wooden core and a bowl in the form of an elephant head with its trunk raised. Its stem is decorated with coiled brass wire.

**Mask** — A cast brass hip mask is shaped as a face with a beard of mudfish.

**Dance mask** — A museum register records a wooden dance mask.

**Brass figures** — The catalogues record brass figures of a woman, a male figure, a man with a vessel, a man with a xylophone, a horseman, a man smoking a pipe and carrying a spear, a man with a dead body, a woman with a vessel, and a woman with a rattle.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum catalogues document metalwork and household objects. These include cast brass smoking-pipes, brass pipe-bowls, a brass staff finial depicting six intertwined snake representations, and brass figures. One pipe-bowl represents a European figure with a hat seated on a bearded head with two branch heads. Another is a carved pottery bowl made to simulate a tree trunk ending in a face with bulging cheeks. The catalogues also record a palm-wine bottle with bead mosaic, a woven fibre bag, and a small knife with a steel blade, wooden handle, and leather sheath.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources record a cast brass hip mask and a pendant-mask.

## Music & performance

Bamum has four or five tones and distinguishes between lexical and grammatical tone. Cameroonian musician Claude Ndam was a native speaker of Bamum and sang in the language. A museum catalogue records a brass figure of a man with a xylophone and several smoking-pipes, including one with a bowl shaped like an elephant head and another with a bowl representing two human heads flanked by two crocodiles.

## Language & religion

Bamum, also called *Shü Pamom*, *Shümom*, Shupamem, Bamun, or Bamoun, is an Eastern Grassfields language of Cameroon. It has tone, vowel length, diphthongs, and coda consonants. The language has four or five tones; one analysis includes a mid tone, while another includes downstep. Bamum distinguishes between lexical and grammatical tone. Its original script was developed by King Njoya and his palace circle in the Kingdom of Bamum around 1895.
