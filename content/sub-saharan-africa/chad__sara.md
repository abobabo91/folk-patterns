## Overview

The Sara live mainly in southern Chad and nearby border areas. They form about 30% of Chad’s population and are the country’s largest ethnic group. Their social order includes several patrilineal clans. Their history includes French colonial rule, forced labor, conscription, and political dominance in Chad after 1960.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Sara material culture includes iron throwing-knives, cane shields, hoe-blade currency, and wooden lip plugs.

- **Iron throwing-knives** — Museum records include throwing-knives with leather-bound handles, punched decoration, and reptile-skin wrapping.
- **Hide sheaths** — Some throwing-knives were kept in hide or composite leather sheaths, including one reinforced with bark and decorated with stitching and triangular holes.
- **Cane shield** — A cane shield has a wooden frame, painted cane slats, a leather-wrapped edge, and a hide handle tied at the back.
- **Crescent hoe-blade currency** (*sakania*) — An iron crescent moon-shaped hoe-blade was used as currency.
- **Wooden lip plugs** — The catalogue records wooden lip plugs, including a smaller plug with engraved decoration.

## Festivals & rituals

Many Sara people retain an ethnic religion described as animism, while others follow Christianity or Islam.

- **Ethnic religion** — Many Sara people have retained their ethnic religion, described in the source as animism.

## Foodways

Sara subsistence centers on hoe farming, with root crops as main staples and several kinds of livestock.

- **Hoe farming** — Sara subsistence is primarily based on hoe farming.
- **Root-crop staples** — Taro, yams, and sweet potatoes are the main staples.
- **Livestock raising** — Sara people raise cattle, sheep, goats, chickens, and small horses.
