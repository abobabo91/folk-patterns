## Overview

The Kanuri live mainly in the lands of the former Kanem and Bornu Empires. They have traditionally farmed, fished in the Chad Basin, traded, and processed salt. Their culture includes leatherwork, quilted clothing, proverbs, and written and oral literature. They are predominantly Sunni Muslim and have used Ajami Arabic writing for at least four hundred years.

## Material culture

Kanuri material culture includes quilted cotton clothing, detailed leatherwork, iron tools and weapons, pottery, calabash objects, and jewelry.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Named textile traditions are not documented, but museum records show quilted cotton and patterned leather objects.

- **Quilted coat** — A white cotton coat has concentric-circle quilting, red edging, a wide collar, leather buttons, and plaited leather loops.
- **Quilted cap** — A coloured cloth cap is quilted and finished with tassels.
- **Leatherwork belt** — A belt has 12 pouches with geometric decoration and hanging leather straps.
- **Leatherwork cushion** — A round leather cushion has geometric patchwork on top and fringe around the edge.
- **Plaited-leather container** — A cylindrical tin container is covered with plaited leatherwork and has a cover.

### Clothing & dress

The records show quilted clothing, leather belts, neck and ear ornaments, plaited hair, and facial scarification.

- **White quilted coat** — The coat has red edging, a deep back split, a wide turned-down collar, leather buttons, and plaited leather loops.
- **Quilted cap** — The coloured cloth cap is quilted and has tassels.

Kanuri objects include iron weapons, riding gear, pottery, calabash vessels, a chess set, and leather bags.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment includes stone hair ornaments, neck and ear ornaments, styled hair, and facial scarification.

- **Stone hair ornaments** — Women’s hair ornaments are made of cornelian and agate.
- **Neck ornament** — Photographs show women wearing neck ornaments.
- **Circular ear ornaments** — Women wear circular ear ornaments and other ear ornaments.
- **Ear piercing** — One portrait shows holes in the ears for ear ornaments.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not describe a festival calendar or life-cycle rites, but they connect Kanuri religious life with Sunni Islam and proverbs.

- **Sunni Islam** — The Kanuri are predominantly Sunni Muslim and became Muslims in the 11th century.
- **Proverb teaching** — Proverbs are used in Islam to explain social happenings and teach meanings.

## Foodways

The sources do not name Kanuri dishes or cooking methods, but they describe farming, fishing, trade, and salt processing.

- **Chad Basin fishing** — Kanuri groups have traditionally fished in the Chad Basin.
- **Salt processing** — Kanuri groups have traditionally processed salt.
- **Bilma salt trade** — The Bilma subgroup is described as the dominant ethnic group in Bilma’s salt evaporation and trade industry.

## Oral tradition & literature

Proverbs and stories carry social lessons, religious ideas, and memories of Kanuri history.

- **Kanuri proverbs** — Wise old men create proverbs that teach beliefs, experiences, social attitudes, and lessons from daily life.
- **Trickster ground-squirrel** (*kə̀nyérì*) — Koelle recorded the trickster ground-squirrel, but mistakenly identified it as a weasel.
- **Kanuri oral literature collection** — Koelle’s 1854 book contains Kanuri proverbs, stories, fables, and historical fragments with English translations.
- **Sef origin tradition** — Kanuri tradition says that Sef, son of Dhu Ifazan of Yemen, arrived in Kanem in the ninth century and founded the Sayfawa dynasty.

## Language & religion

Kanuri is a tonal Nilo-Saharan language with several dialects, Arabic and Latin writing systems, and a mainly Sunni Muslim population.

- **Manga Kanuri** — Manga Kanuri is one of the main Kanuri dialects.
- **Yerwa Kanuri** — Yerwa Kanuri is one of the main Kanuri dialects.
- **Ajami Arabic script** (*Ajami*) — Kanuri has been written in the Ajami Arabic script for at least four hundred years, especially in religious and court settings.
- **Modified Latin script** — Kanuri is also written in a modified Latin script.
- **Standardized romanized orthography** — A standardized romanized orthography was approved in Maiduguri in 1975.
