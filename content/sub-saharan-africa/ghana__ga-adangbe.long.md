## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources used do not document Ga-Adangbe textile or pattern traditions.

### Clothing & dress

The catalogue records one cap made from cotton cloth that folds flat in four panels. It is embroidered with yellow and pink thread on purple background cloth, has a white lower border and a red and white button at the top, and its materials appear to be of European manufacture. The sources do not identify this cap as everyday, ceremonial, men’s, or women’s dress, and do not document footwear, belts, or other garments.

### Architecture

The sources used do not document Ga-Adangbe architecture, house forms, building materials, roof types, decoration, or urban traditions.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum records describe wooden objects including coffins and model coffins in the forms of an eagle, elephant, catfish, camera, cocoa pod, Coca-Cola bottle, and lager bottle marked “Premium Club.” The eagle coffin has painted feather markings, hinged wings, carrying handles, and a cavity lined with textiles; the elephant coffin has a cavity lined with green silk and red textile. The catfish, cocoa pod, and lager-bottle coffins are painted and have hinged wooden lids over cavities. The sources do not identify these objects as specifically Ga-Adangbe forms or explain their everyday use.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources used do not document Ga-Adangbe jewelry, body adornment, tattooing, hair practices, or ritual ornament. One catalogue record describes a wooden phallic fertility figurine of a standing male with a blue bead girdle and necklace, another necklace, and straw around the neck; it is partly covered with white paint.
