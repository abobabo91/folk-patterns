## Overview

The Chewa are a Bantu ethnic group primarily found in Malawi and Zambia, with smaller populations in Mozambique. Their language is Chewa, also called Chichewa, and belongs to Guthrie Zone N alongside Tumbuka, Sena and Nsenga. The Chewa are associated with masks and the Nyau secret societies, whose members conduct initiation ceremonies and perform Gule Wamkulu. The group is described as a remnant of the Maravi people. One source gives a population of 1.5 million people, while another gives 7.9 million as a 2024 estimate. Chewa society is matrilineal, with lineage, property and land rights organized through the mother.

### Architecture

 They mention village headmen, regional chiefs and Paramount Chiefs.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 The language source gives lexical examples including *katúndu*, meaning “possessions” or “luggage, furniture,”.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources document special tattoo marks called *mphini*. No further information is given about their designs, application, materials, social meanings, or relationship to other forms of body adornment.

## Music & performance

The Chewa are known for masks and secret societies called Nyau. Members of the Nyau brotherhood are responsible for initiating young men into adulthood and for performing Gule Wamkulu at the end of the initiation procedure. The performance celebrates the young men’s integration into adult society. The language source mentions *ngoma* as a lexical example meaning “kudu,” but does not present it as an instrument or musical tradition. No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription is supplied for the Chewa or for Malawi.

## Dance & theatre

Gule Wamkulu is the named Chewa performance tradition in the sources. It is performed by members of the Nyau brotherhood at the end of young men’s initiation procedure and marks their integration into adult society. The Chewa are also described as being mainly known for masks and their secret societies.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe initiation into adulthood as the principal named Chewa ritual context. Members of the Nyau brotherhood conduct the initiation of young men, followed by the performance of Gule Wamkulu. This performance celebrates the initiates’ integration into adult society.

## Foodways

 The language source includes *chímanga* for “maize” and *mowa* for “beer” as lexical examples.

## Oral tradition & literature

Chewa oral records are described as referring to origins in Malambo, a region in the Luba area of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. These accounts describe migration into northern Zambia and then south and east into the highlands of Malawi. Oral history also states that the first Chewa kingdom was established some time before or after 1480. By the 16th century, two systems of government are described: one maintained by the Banda clan at Mankhamba, near Nthakataka, and the other by the Phiri clan at Manthimba.

## Language & religion

Chewa is a Bantu language spoken in Malawi, Eastern Province of Zambia, and Mozambique’s province of Niassa. In Malawi, its name was officially changed from Chinyanja to Chichewa in 1968. Chewa belongs to Guthrie Zone N, the same language group as Tumbuka, Sena and Nsenga. Chewa and Tumbuka were historically official languages of Malawi alongside English. The sources describe a religious system based on the Nyau secret societies and state that British and Portuguese missions converted many Chewa to Christianity. At least one fifth, or 20%, of Chewa are Muslims today, while a good number continue to hold their ancestral belief system. Women are associated with lineage reproduction, and the society is matrilineal.
