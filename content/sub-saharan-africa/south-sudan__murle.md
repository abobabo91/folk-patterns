## Overview

The Murle live mainly in Greater Pibor Administrative Area in South Sudan and in parts of southwestern Ethiopia. Their traditions tell of an origin at Jen and migrations along the Omo River. Cattle are central to marriage and social life. The supplied sources document some objects, ornaments, songs, myths, and religious ideas, but many parts of daily culture are not described.

## Material culture

The documented material culture includes hide ornaments, cowrie-shell belts, wooden weapons, tobacco pouches, and lower-lip ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The supplied sources do not document named Murle textile or pattern traditions.

### Clothing & dress

The sources record a woman’s hide apron ornament and a hide belt with cowrie shells, but do not describe general Murle dress.

- **Seed and shell apron ornament** — A woman’s hide apron ornament is decorated with short strings of black seeds, brown berries, and small white shells.
- **Cowrie-shell waist belt** — A waist belt is made from a strip of hide with white cowrie shells sewn on one side.

### Architecture

The supplied sources do not describe Murle houses, shelters, building materials, or settlement plans.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The documented wooden objects include clubs and a tobacco pouch, while metalwork, pottery, and household goods are not described.

- **Wooden club** — A wooden club has a thin round handle and a flat head.
- **Throwing club** — A wooden throwing club is described as a missile weapon.
- **Tobacco pouch** — A tobacco pouch is a wooden cylinder with a skin cover and a strap fixed to a hole in the bottom.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Women’s lower-lip ornaments are made from earthenware, wood, ivory, or rhinoceros horn.

- **Wooden lip ornament** — The catalogue records a wooden lower-lip ornament.
- **Ivory lip ornament** — The catalogue records a lower-lip ornament made of ivory.
- **Rhinoceros-horn lip ornament** — Several lower-lip ornaments are made of rhinoceros horn or possibly rhinoceros horn.

## Music & performance

Murle songs preserve creation stories and memories of Jen, the ancestors, cattle, and sweet tobacco.

- **Jen creation song** — One song recalls the creation of Murle humanity at Jen.
- **Murimaan origin song** — Another song connects the first union between spirit and woman with the birth of Murimaan.

## Dance & theatre

The supplied sources do not describe named Murle dances, theatre, masks, or entertainment genres.

## Festivals & rituals

Murle ritual life includes the sacred origin place Jen, funerary ties to the rat, cattle bride wealth, and a blend of animism and Christianity.

- **Jen sacred origin** (*Jen*) — Jen is venerated as a sacred point of origin and symbolizes the East, life, rainfall, and ancestral memory.
- **Rat funerary association** — The rat has a ceremonial association with funerary rites for Kelenya chiefs and their senior wives.
- **Cattle bride wealth** — Men can marry only after paying several dozens of cows.

## Foodways

The sources do not document Murle dishes or cooking traditions, but they connect cattle and sweet tobacco with Murle life and tradition.

- **Sweet tobacco at Jen** — A creation verse describes tobacco at Jen as sweet like cattle.
- **Cattle pastoralism** — The sources describe pastoral dependence on cattle and shortages of rain, drinking water, bush fruits, and cattle grass.

## Oral tradition & literature

Murle oral traditions describe origins, ancestors, migrations, spirits, and the discovery of animals.

- **Murimaan origin story** (*Murimaan*) — A woman encountered a spirit hidden among grass, and a second woman later gave birth to Murimaan, the founding ancestor of the Murle.
- **Abei ancestry story** (*Abei*) — Another account names Abei as the grandmother of all mankind and Murimaan as the progenitor of Murle chiefs.
- **Jok spirit tradition** (*Jok*) — Murle traditions refer to Jok as a great spirit.
- **Tammu sky tradition** (*Tammu*) — Tammu denotes God, rain, and the sky.
- **Etiwur animal stories** (*Etiwur*) — Etiwur stories tell of the discovery of animals.

## Language & religion

Murle is a Surmic language with complex noun number marking, distinctive vowel length, and a New Testament translation.

- **Murle language** (*Murle*) — Murle is spoken in southeastern South Sudan near the Ethiopian border and has basic verb–subject–object order.
- **Ajibba** — Ajibba is another name for the Murle language.
- **Beir** — Beir is another name for the Murle language.
- **Merule** — Merule is another name for the Murle language.
- **Mourle** — Mourle is another name for the Murle language.
