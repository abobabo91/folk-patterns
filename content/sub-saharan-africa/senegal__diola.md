## Overview

The Diola are linked with the Casamance region of southern Senegal and neighboring countries. They are also called Jola, Joola, Jola-Fonyi, Diola-Fogny, and Kujamataak. The profile records many practical objects for hunting, fishing, cooking, storage, and domestic use. It also describes a rice-based dish called Thiebou Diola.

## Language & religion

Jola is spoken in Casamance and nearby countries, with an official Senegalese writing system that marks long vowels and vowel harmony.

- **Jola language** (*Jola*) — Jola, also called Diola, Joola, Jola-Fonyi, Diola-Fogny, and Kujamataak, is spoken by 475,000 people in the region and neighboring countries.
- **Jola-Fonyi language** (*Jola-Fonyi*) — Jola-Fonyi is one of several closely related Jola languages spoken in the area.
- **Official orthography** — Senegal created an official orthography for the language by decree No. 2005-981.
- **Long-vowel spelling** (*aa, ee, ii, oo, uu*) — Long vowels are written by doubling the letter.
- **Vowel-harmony accent** (*acute accent*) — An acute accent over a vowel marks the advanced and retracted tongue root for that vowel and the other vowels in the word.
