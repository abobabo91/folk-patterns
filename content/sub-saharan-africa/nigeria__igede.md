## Overview

The Igede are a Nigerian people mainly from Oju and Obi in Benue State. They are farmers who grow maize, cassava, groundnut, and yams. Their culture includes the Igede-Agba yam-harvest festival, striped clothes, pottery, arrows, drums, songs, drama, and wooden figures. Their origin story is told in song and drama, though archival records give a different migration account.

## Material culture

Igede material culture includes striped clothes, pottery vessels, poisoned arrows, a pottery drum, and wooden figures.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Igede traditional clothes use blue, black, and white stripes.

- **Striped traditional clothes** (*Igede*) — The clothes are described as having blue, black, and white stripes.

### Clothing & dress

The profile only identifies blue, black, and white striped traditional clothes.

- **Traditional striped clothing** (*Igede*) — Igede traditional clothes are blue, black, and white striped.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Igede objects include pottery vessels, poisoned arrows, a pottery drum, and wooden figures.

- **Beer-pot** — This pottery vessel has vegetable varnish, a medial ridge, and impressed and incised patterns.
- **Carinated water- or beer-pot** — This vessel has a long slender neck, with hatched designs alternating with plain bands.
- **Three-spouted water- or beer-pot** — This two-handled pottery vessel has three spouts, grooved decoration, and vegetable varnish.
- **Poisoned arrows** — These arrows have iron heads and reed shafts.

## Music & performance

Igede history is recounted in song and drama, and a pottery pot was used as a drum.

- **Ego ny'Igede** — This record and drama piece recounts the migration of the Igede to present-day Benue State.
- **Musical pottery pot** — A globular pottery pot with one side hole was used as a drum.

## Dance & theatre

The Igede origin story is performed in song and drama.

- **Ego ny'Igede** — This drama piece tells the story of Igede migration from Sabon Gida Ora through Nsukka to present-day Benue State.

## Festivals & rituals

Igede-Agba is a colourful annual festival held during the yam harvest season in September.

- **Yam-harvest festival** (*Igede-Agba*) — Igede-Agba marks the yam harvest season in September.

## Foodways

The Igede are mainly farmers who grow maize, cassava, groundnut, and yams.

- **Yam farming** (*Igede-Agba*) — The Igede-Agba festival is linked to the yam harvest season.
- **Maize, cassava, and groundnut farming** — Igede farmers also cultivate maize, cassava, and groundnut.

## Oral tradition & literature

Igede oral tradition traces the group’s origin to Sabon Gida Ora and the chief Agba.

- **Igede origin story** — The story says the Igede descended from Agba, a high chief, and migrated after a skirmish with the natives of Ora.
- **Migration account** (*Ego ny'Igede*) — The story describes migration through Nsukka in Enugu State to present-day Benue State.
- **Archival migration account** (*Idoma*) — Archival records instead describe the Igede as migrants from Ogoja province who were later grouped under the name Idoma.

## Language & religion

Igede is spoken in Lower Benue State and Cross River State and belongs to the Benue-Congo subgroup of Niger-Congo.

- **Igede language** (*Igede*) — Igede refers to the language, the people, and the land they occupy.
- **Benue-Congo language group** (*Benue-Congo*) — Igede belongs to the Benue-Congo subgroup of the Niger-Congo language family.
