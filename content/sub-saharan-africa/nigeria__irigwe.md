## Overview

The Irigwe live mainly in Plateau State, Nigeria, with communities in southern Kaduna. Their headquarters is Miango, west of Jos. They speak Rigwe, a Central Plateau language. Their culture includes agricultural dances, occupational dances, a distinctive marriage system, priestly elders, ethnic religion, shrines, ironwork, carved wood, and strong traditions of clothing and body adornment.

## Material culture

Material culture includes fringed and tasseled cloth, thatched buildings, iron knives, carved wood, pipes, spindles, and varied body adornment.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources record hand-made and fringed cloth but no named textile patterns.

- **Fringed cloth** — Women wore fringed cloth tied around the waist.
- **Tasseled waist cloth** — Women wore waist cloth or ornaments with tassels.
- **Leaf-backed waist cloth** — Some women wore waist cloth with bunches of leaves at the back and front.
- **Hand-made waist cloth** — A carved wooden male figure has hand-made cloth around the waist.

### Clothing & dress

Dress included waist cloths, head coverings, ornaments, and animal-hide straps.

- **Head-cloth** — Women wore head-cloths.
- **Head-band** — Women and men wore head-bands.
- **Animal-hide shoulder strap** — One adult man wore an animal-hide strap across the shoulder.
- **Leaf belt** — Children wore belts with bunches of leaves covering the pubic area.

### Architecture

Buildings were low, round, and usually covered with thatch.

- **Thatched granary** — Several small, circular, thatched buildings were identified as granaries.
- **Animal-relief shrine** — A shrine was a low, circular, thatched building with animals shown in relief on its walls.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded objects include iron knives, a pipe, a spindle, and carved wood.

- **Loop-handle iron dagger** — One iron dagger had a loop handle and incised ornament.
- **Sheathed iron knife** — One iron knife had a wooden handle and a stamped leather sheath ending in a tassel.
- **Ornate pipe** — The catalogue records a long, ornate pipe.
- **Spindle** — The catalogue records a spindle.
- **Carved male figure** — A carved wooden standing male figure wore cloth around the waist.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment included ornaments, straps, scarification, styled hair, and fibre twine.

- **Neck ornament** — Women wore neck-ornaments.
- **Wrist ornament** — Women and men wore wrist-ornaments.
- **Arm, chest, and shoulder scarification** — Photographic records show scarification on the arms, chest, and shoulders.
- **Crested hair** — The carved male figure has hair dressed in a crest.
- **Neck twine** — Fibre twine is shown around the carved figure’s neck.

## Music & performance

The sources do not document Irigwe music, instruments, songs, or musical styles.

## Dance & theatre

Dances linked farming and occupational groups, and hunters used animal movements in ritual.

- **Crop-growth leaps** — Young farmers usually leaped at agricultural festivals to encourage crop growth.
- **Occupational expressive dances** — Blacksmiths, hunters, wood-carvers, and other occupational groups had their own expressive dances.
- **Hunter animal-mime dance** — Hunters mimed animal movements to control wild beasts and reduce their fears.

## Festivals & rituals

Ritual life included crop festivals, a group-wide rite, and spirit possession cults.

- **Agricultural-cycle festivals** — Young farmers leaped at these festivals to encourage the growth of crops.
- **Group well-being ritual** — An all-important ritual for the well-being of the whole group was led by priestly elders.
- **Spirit possession cults** — Spirit possession cults involved nearly all mature women and supported emotional release and social integration.
- **Priestly elder authority** — Priestly elders from several tribal subdivisions traditionally held authority over the group-wide ritual.

## Foodways

The sources do not document Irigwe foods, cooking, dietary rules, or food ceremonies.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not document Irigwe folktales, poetry, proverbs, riddles, or storytelling practices.

## Language & religion

Rigwe is a complex Central Plateau language, and most Irigwe people were reported to practice ethnic religion.

- **Rigwe** — Rigwe is a Central Plateau language spoken mainly in Bassa Local Government Area.
- **Nkarigwe** — Nkarigwe is another name for Rigwe.
- **Lateral fricative** (*/ɬ/*) — Rigwe has the unusual lateral fricative /ɬ/.
- **Ethnic religion** — About 62% of the total population was reported to practice ethnic religion.
- **Christianity** — Christian adherents were reported as 28% of the population.
