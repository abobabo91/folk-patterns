## Overview

The Idoma live in central Nigeria, especially the Benue region. This collection is based on objects that museums explicitly attribute to Idoma makers. It includes painted wooden face masks, a sculptural headdress, a seated mother-and-child figure, a barkcloth dress, carved stools, a brass bell, and ironwork. The range matters: masks are prominent, but they are only part of the material record.

## Material culture

The British Museum's early twentieth-century holdings show several distinct face masks. Some are white or black-and-white; others have horns, beards, carved facial markings, or metal teeth. One headdress carries two human figures and a patterned animal. The Smithsonian notes that related masks moved across ethnic boundaries and associations, and that the function of a particular mask cannot always be established from its appearance. These objects should therefore not all be assigned one ceremony.

The same British Museum collection records a painted, fringed barkcloth dress and a vegetable-fibre apron with seeds. Two carved wooden stools have different support structures. A cast brass bell, decorated arm ring, and iron arrowhead show additional uses of metal. These are documented individual objects, not a complete account of Idoma dress or daily life.

The Commons photographs selected for this page show museum objects and contemporary cultural performances. Their captions identify Idoma people or makers, but a photograph alone cannot establish the age or customary use of every form shown.
