## Overview

The Wala, also called Waala, live in the Upper West Region of Ghana. They are a predominantly Muslim people associated with the city of Wa and the Kingdom of Wala, which they founded. They speak Wali, a language belonging to the Gur group; the language had 84,800 speakers as of 2013. Their neighbors are the Birifor, Dagaaba, and Vagla peoples. Wala culture is described as similar to that of other Gur-speaking, Senoufo, and Mande groups in northern Côte d'Ivoire, Burkina Faso, and Mali. They are known for Sudano-Sahelian-style mosques and palaces, and are ruled by the Wa-Naa.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Wala architecture is represented in the sources by Sudano-Sahelian-style mosques and palaces. The Wa-Naa's traditional residence, Wa-Naa's palace, is a mud-brick-built palace in Wa.
