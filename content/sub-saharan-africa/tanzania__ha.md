## Overview

The Ha are a Bantu-speaking people of northwestern Tanzania who live in dispersed homesteads organized around lineage. Their material culture is documented mainly through museum collections of beadwork, hide and woven dress, metalwork, and carved wood. They cultivate sorghum, millet, corn, cassava, and yams, with livestock keeping varying by region depending on the tsetse fly's presence. Animist beliefs ground their ritual life, overlaid by Islamic and Christian influence from colonial and pre-colonial contact.

## Material culture

Ha material culture emphasizes geometric pattern in textiles and beadwork, refined ironwork, carved wooden vessels, and a distinctive single-string zither.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Ha woven dress combines structural pattern with dyed color, using raised cords and ribs to create grids of red and black.

- **Ribbed and squared women's dress** — A two-piece dress of coarse woven cloth with red and black squares created by raised cords, finished with fringe and beads.
- **Hide dress** — A dress made of cow skin hung with tassels, the hide counterpart to woven dress.
- **Beaded belts** — Women's belts of glass beads on fibre or fibre core covered with bands of black and white glass beads.
- **Geometric motifs** — Patterns include alternating natural and blackened triangles, four-pointed stars, black rings, and red-and-black squares.

### Clothing & dress

Ha women wore two-piece dresses of woven cloth or hide, belts of glass beads and copper wire, and distinctive breast ornaments.

- **Two-piece dress** — Front of cotton cloth and back of coarse woven cloth, both red and black squares with raised ribs, both fringed.
- **Kitindi** — Coiled bracelets of copper wire worn near the elbow by women, shared with neighbouring peoples.

### Architecture

Ha live in dispersed homesteads organized around lineage rather than nucleated villages.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ha ironworking is represented by weapons and smithing tools; woodworking includes vessels, head-rests and a decorated zither.

- **Arm-dagger** — A weapon made of iron and wood.
- **Barbed iron arrows** — Arrows with feathered shafts and barbed iron points for hunting.
- **Wooden bow** — A bow strung with cord.
- **Smith's bellows** — Built of wood, cane and skin, the equipment of ironworking.
- **Milk vessel** — A wooden container with a separate lid for storing milk.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ha ornament is worked in copper, brass and beads, with the penannular bracelet and coiled copper-wire Kitindi recurring as signature forms.

- **Copper and brass armlets** — Arm ornaments in copper and brass, some with a flattened side and projecting axe or ridge.
- **Penannular bracelets** — Open-ended brass bracelets worn by both adults and children.
- **Kitindi** — Coiled copper-wire bracelets worn near the elbow by women.
- **Maiden's breast ornaments** — Ornaments combining vegetable fibre, glass beads, shell beads and wood, worn by unmarried women.

## Music & performance

The Ha trough zither is a single-string seven-course instrument carved from one piece of wood with geometric decoration.

- **Trough zither** — Built from solid wood with hollowed resonator, strung with a single fibre cord looped around serrations to form seven strings, decorated with stars and triangles.
- **Wooden whistle** — A tube with crescent mouth, two side ears and expanded end, holding a feather inside.
- **Ivory hunters' whistles** — Two signalling instruments of ivory used in hunting.

## Festivals & rituals

Ha ritual life centers on animist reverence for ancestors and nature spirits, with livestock playing a central role in marriage and bride-wealth.

- **Bride-wealth** — Livestock including cattle and goats are highly valued and given as gifts at marriage.
- **Imana** — The supreme being and creator in Ha animist religion.
- **Medicine container** — A charm container made of horn used in ritual practice.

## Foodways

Ha cultivation focuses on sorghum, millet, corn, cassava, yams and peanuts, with hunting and honey-gathering supplementing livestock in tsetse-infested zones.

- **Milk vessel** — A wooden container with separate lid for milk from livestock.
- **Wooden bowl** — Cut from solid wood and colored light red on the outside for serving food.
- **Hunting whistles** — Wooden and ivory whistles used to coordinate hunting in tsetse-ridden northern zones.

## Oral tradition & literature

Ha oral tradition is not documented, but the language has been recorded in grammar, texts and Bible recordings.

- **Ha Language Grammar and Texts** — Lotta Harjula's 2004 reference work includes Ha language grammar, texts and vocabulary.
- **Bible recordings** — The Bible has been recorded in the Ha language.

## Language & religion

Ha is a Bantu language closely related to Kirundi and Kinyarwanda, with phonetic variation in /ɾ/ and /b/ across dialects.

- **Ha language names** (*Kiha, Ikiha, Giha, Igiha*) — The prefixed forms of the Ha language name.
- **Imana** — The supreme being and creator in Ha animist belief.
- **Animism** — Ha religious practice centered on revering ancestors and nature spirits.

## Glossary

- *Abaha* — The Ha's name for themselves.
- *Waha* — The Swahili name for the Ha people.
- *Buha* — The Ha's name for their country of grasslands and open woodland.
- *Kiha, Ikiha, Giha* — Names for the Ha language, Bantu branch of Niger-Congo family.
- *Imana* — The supreme being and creator in Ha animist religion.
- *Kitindi* — Coiled copper-wire bracelets worn near the elbow by Ha women.
- *Trough zither* — A single-string seven-course instrument carved from solid wood with geometric decoration.
- *Penannular bracelet* — An open-ended brass bracelet worn by adults and children.
- *Bride-wealth* — Livestock given as gifts at marriage in Ha society.
- *Kigoma Region* — The region in northwestern Tanzania where the Ha live.
- *Lake Tanganyika* — The lake along the eastern shore of which the Ha settle.
- *Bantu* — A branch of the Niger-Congo language family to which Ha belongs.
- *Kirundi* — A language closely related to Ha, spoken in Burundi.
- *Kinyarwanda* — A language closely related to Ha, spoken in Rwanda.
- *Lineage* — The kinship system through which male family members relate and organize homesteads.
