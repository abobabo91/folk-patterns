## Overview

The Avukaya are an ethnic group of South Sudan, with approximately 50,000 people residing in the country. They traditionally live in a rainforest area of Equatoria, close to the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Historically, they are associated with Maridi town, Mambe, Bahr-Olo and Amaki, and they are also found in Tore Payam, Yei town, Bangolo Payam in Mundri West and other towns of Sudan. Avukaya speakers occupy a contiguous area along both sides of the South Sudan–Congo international boundary, with Maridi and Faradje as the main language centres. Avokaya is a Central Sudanic language. The sources identify Mamburuku dance, multilingualism, foodways and regional historical movements as aspects of Avukaya cultural life.

## Music & performance

The sources identify a dance called *Mamburuku* and mention socialisation with other tribes.

## Dance & theatre

*Mamburuku* is identified as an Avukaya dance.

## Foodways

The Avukaya commonly enjoy *Pirinda* with *Nyasa*, described as food made from sorghum, millet or cassava flour. They also like the wild fruits *Omi* and *Kaniki*, as well as yams.

## Language & religion

Avokaya, also spelled Abukeia or Avukaya, is a Central Sudanic language spoken in southern South Sudan and parts of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The main dialects are Ajugu and Ojila; Northern Ogambi and Avokaya Pur are smaller dialects spoken only in the Faradje region of the Congo. Avokaya is closest to Logo, and a high degree of code switching exists among Avokaya and Logo speakers in the Faradje region. In Maridi, there is bilingualism and intermarriage with speakers of Baka and Mündü. Juba, Sudanese Arabic and English are used for wider communication in Sudan, while speakers in the Congo tend to use Swahili, Lingala and French. Many Avukaya belong to the Christian minority of South Sudan.
