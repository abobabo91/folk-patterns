| Where | Northern Namibia and southern Angola, especially the flat plains of Ovamboland. |
| How many | About 2 million people in the early 21st century; about 1 million Oshiwambo speakers were reported in Namibia and Angola. |

## Overview

The Ovambo live mainly in northern Namibia and southern Angola. Their settled life centers on farming, animal husbandry, and seasonal watercourses called oshanas. Their culture includes matrilineal family ties, fenced homesteads, ritual fires, music, dance, clothing, and many specialized objects. Most Ovambo are Christian, while older religious practices continue alongside Christian beliefs.

## Material culture

Ovambo material culture uses plant fibre, wood, iron, bamboo, shells, ivory, horn, bone, hide, and other natural materials for homes, tools, weapons, clothing, adornment, and ritual objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Ovambo men made mats for village fences, while other fibre objects and basketry are recorded without named patterns or techniques.

- **Village fence mats** — Men wove or braided mats for the outside of a village.
- **Fibre arrows** — Fibre was used in arrows and poisoned arrows.
- **Lidded basket** — A basket with a lid was made from basketry.

### Clothing & dress

Contemporary Ovambo women commonly wear a head scarf and a loose, full-length maxi.

- **Head scarf** — A head scarf is part of the typical dress described for contemporary Ovambo women.
- **Full-length maxi** — Women’s loose, full-length maxis come from the dress of 19th-century Finnish missionaries.
- **Lower-body cloth** — Adults in Angola were photographed wearing cloths around their lower bodies.
- **Head-gear** — One woman was described as wearing head-gear with a lower-body cloth and ornaments.

### Architecture

A traditional Ovambo homestead is a maze of huts enclosed by a pole fence with two gates.

- **Traditional homestead** — The homestead is a complex of huts enclosed by a fence made from large vertical poles and horizontal poles.
- **Woman’s hut** (*Ondjugo*) — Ondjugo is the hut of the woman of the homestead.
- **Kitchen area** (*Epata*) — Epata is a kitchen area.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ovambo everyday objects include farming tools, weapons, baskets, vessels, and ritual objects made from many natural materials.

- **Hoe** — A hoe has a long wooden shaft, a short fork, and a slightly convex iron head.
- **Bamboo bow** — A bamboo bow has a string made from hide.
- **Spears with counter-poises** — British Museum records include spears with counter-poises.
- **Divination object** — A divination object was made of ivory.
- **Bottle or vessel** — A bottle or vessel associated with the Ovambo dates to 1875.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ovambo adornment includes shell, ivory, horn, bone, fibre, neck, and wrist ornaments.

- **Shell necklace** — Shell necklaces are recorded among Ovambo adornments.
- **Amulet** — An amulet has a horn, a bone covered with snake skin, and broken wood tied to a fibre cord.
- **Ivory button or ornament** (*ekipa*) — Ekipa is an ivory button or ornament.
- **Neck ornament** — Photographic records describe neck ornaments worn with other personal ornaments.
- **Wrist ornament** — Photographic records describe wrist ornaments.

## Music & performance

Traditional Ovambo dancing is performed with drumming and is linked to social and religious life.

- **Oshiwambo folk music** (*Oshiwambo*) — Traditional dancing with drumming is described as Oshiwambo folk music.
- **Wedding performance** — Most weddings combine Christian beliefs with Ovambo traditions, including traditional dancing and drumming.

## Dance & theatre

The sources name traditional dancing with drumming and a rain-making dance, but no theatre traditions.

- **Rain-making dance** — A rain-making dance is part of traditional religious practice.
- **Traditional dancing** — Traditional dancing is accompanied by drumming.

## Festivals & rituals

Ovambo traditional religion centers on Kalunga, sacred fire, rites, and rituals that continue alongside Christianity.

- **Kalunga** — Kalunga is the supreme being in Ovambo traditional religion.
- **Sacred-fire ceremonies** — Rituals include elaborate ceremonies for making and keeping sacred fire.
- **Smoke and herb rite** — Some rites involve throwing herbs into a fire and inhaling the rising smoke.
- **Traditional head priest** — The traditional head priest was the tribal king and attended to spirits and represented the tribe to the deities.
- **Christian-Ovambo weddings** — Weddings combine Christian beliefs with Ovambo traditions.

## Foodways

Ovambo foodways are based on millet, sorghum, beans, animal husbandry, hunting, fishing, gathering, and fruit liquor.

- **Millet and sorghum** (*iilyavala*) — Millet and sorghum called iilyavala are staple crops.
- **Beans** (*omakunde*) — Beans called omakunde are another popular crop.
- **Milk** (*omashini*) — Animal husbandry mainly provides milk called omashini rather than meat.
- **Traditional liquor** (*ombike*) — Ombike is distilled from fermented fruit mash and is especially popular in rural areas.
- **Urban liquor** (*omangelengele*) — Omangelengele is a stronger and sometimes poisonous form of the liquor brewed in urban areas.

## Oral tradition & literature

Ovambo oral tradition is represented in the sources mainly by the story of Kalunga and the origin of the people through the daughter’s lineage.

- **Origin story** (*Kalunga*) — The cosmology says Kalunga created the first man and woman, whose daughter’s lineage created the Ovambo people.
- **Proverb collection** — A cited proverb collection is listed, but its contents are not described.

## Language & religion

Oshiwambo is the main local language of the Ovambo, while most people today identify as Christian.

- **Oshiwambo** — Oshiwambo is a Bantu language cluster spoken in southern Angola and northern Namibia.
- **Kwanyama** — Kwanyama is one of the written standards of Oshiwambo and is also an Ovambo-speaking group.
- **Ndonga** — Ndonga is one of the written standards of Oshiwambo and is also an Ovambo-speaking group.
- **Oshi- prefix** (*Oshi-*) — The Oshi- prefix developed in Ovambo from the Proto-Bantu prefix ki- through a form found in Herero.
- **Traditional religion** — Less than 3% hold the traditional religion, while traditional practices continue in a syncretic form of Christianity.
