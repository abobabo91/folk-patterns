## Overview

The Kusasi, also called Kusaasi or the Kusaal people, are an ethnic group primarily located in the Bawku Traditional Area, known as Kusaug, in Ghana’s Upper East Region. They also have a significant presence in southern Burkina Faso. Their estimated population is around 723,000, and they are the second-largest ethnic group in Ghana’s Northern sector. The Kusasi speak Kusaal, a Gur language, and are considered indigenous to the eastern part of the Upper East Region. They occupy Bawku Central, Bawku West, Pusiga, Garu, Binduri, and Tempane, with Bawku as the capital. The sources identify the Samanpiid Festival and the Kusaal language as documented aspects of Kusasi cultural life.

## Festivals & rituals

The Kusasi celebrate the **Samanpiid Festival**, which serves as a time to give thanks to God for a bountiful harvest during the farming season. The festival was first celebrated in 1987.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Kusaal language article states that literacy materials, collections of folk stories, and other materials have been produced by the Ghana Institute of Linguistics, Literacy and Bible Translation, or GILLBT, in Tamale, Ghana. It also states that David and Nancy Spratt compiled a simple dictionary from the same source.

## Language & religion

Kusaal, also called Kusaasi, is a Gur language spoken primarily in northern eastern Ghana and Burkina Faso. It is spoken by about 121,000 people and takes its name from the Kusaal people, or Kusasi. The language has a dialect division between Agole, to the east of the Volta River, and Toende, to the west; Agole has more speakers. Bawku West has Zebilla as its capital and is mainly inhabited by speakers of Atoende Kusaal, while Binduri, Bawku, Tempane, Garu, and Pusiga districts mostly have Agole dialect speakers.

The language is described as part of the Western Oti–Volta low-level grouping within Gur. Its word order is strictly SVO, and it has a tonal system with two tones and emic downsteps. Since 2013, a unified orthography has been in use. The sources state that the complete Bible translation is in the Agole dialect and that translators succeeded in revising the New Testament in 2015 and translating the complete Old Testament into Kusaal.

Religiously, the Kusasis are predominantly Christians, followed by Islam and African traditional religion.
