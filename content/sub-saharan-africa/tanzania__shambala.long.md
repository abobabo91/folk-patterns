## Overview

Sambaa, also called Shambaa or Shambala, is a Bantu language of Tanzania. The Shambaa speak Sambaa, Kisambaa, (ki)Shambaa, or (ki)Shambala in the Usambara mountains, in Lushoto District and Muheza District, Tanga Region, of northern Tanzania. The available sources describe the group primarily through its language and associated museum objects. These objects include weapons, household vessels, a hat, a smoking pipe, and a wind musical instrument.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

A Museum of World Culture catalogue record describes a straw hat, identified with the terms *Stråhatt*, *hatt*, *hat*, and *Kofia*. The record associates the object with Tanzania, Washambala, Shamba, Washambara, and Shambala People. It is dated 1929.06.0022 and includes the description “Kofia.” The catalogue record identifies the material as plant parts and connects the object with the Tangajikaområdet and Östafrika.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum catalogue records several everyday and material objects. A British Museum record describes an iron knife with a bone handle and wooden sheath. Another describes a circular wooden bowl on a four-legged round base with an incised pattern on top, while a further record describes a circular wooden food bowl with a round foot. A smoking pipe is made of black pottery with a stopper and wooden stem. The weapons include shields made of rhinoceros hide with hide handles, plaited grass, and rag, as well as spears made of iron and wood.

## Music & performance

The museum catalogue includes a wind musical instrument described as a shawm or algaita. It has an incised pattern and a leather band, with a copper and wood mouthpiece. This is the only musical instrument identified in the sources.

## Foodways

The museum catalogue records a circular wooden food bowl with a round foot and a circular wooden bowl on a four-legged round base. These records establish the presence of named food vessels.

## Language & religion

Sambaa, Shambaa, or Shambala is a Bantu language of Tanzania. It is spoken by the Shambaa in the Usambara mountains, in Lushoto District and Muheza District, Tanga Region, of northern Tanzania. The language is also called Kisambaa, (ki)Shambaa, and (ki)Shambala. The sources note dialectal variation between speech around Lushoto and speech around Mlalo and Mtae, and possibly between the Shambaa of the Western Usambara Mountains and the Eastern Usambara Mountains. Five vowels are noted: [i, ɛ, a, ɔ, u].
