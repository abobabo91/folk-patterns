## Overview

Sambaa, also called Shambaa or Shambala, is a Bantu language of Tanzania. The Shambaa speak Sambaa, Kisambaa, (ki)Shambaa, or (ki)Shambala in the Usambara mountains, in Lushoto District and Muheza District, Tanga Region, of northern Tanzania. The available sources describe the group primarily through its language and associated museum objects. These objects include weapons, household vessels, a hat, a smoking pipe, and a wind musical instrument. The sources used do not state the population, a river valley, a city, an oasis, or the broader folk-culture significance of the Shambala.

## Language & religion

Sambaa, Shambaa, or Shambala is a Bantu language of Tanzania. It is spoken by the Shambaa in the Usambara mountains, in Lushoto District and Muheza District, Tanga Region, of northern Tanzania. The language is also called Kisambaa, (ki)Shambaa, and (ki)Shambala. The sources note dialectal variation between speech around Lushoto and speech around Mlalo and Mtae, and possibly between the Shambaa of the Western Usambara Mountains and the Eastern Usambara Mountains. Five vowels are noted: [i, ɛ, a, ɔ, u]. The sources used do not describe writing systems, religion, spiritual practices, or religious institutions.
