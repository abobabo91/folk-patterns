## Overview

The Kanembu are an ethnic group of Chad, generally considered the modern descendants of the Kanem–Bornu Empire. They number an estimated 1,815,270 people and live primarily in Chad’s Lac Prefecture, with additional populations in Chari-Baguirmi and Kanem prefectures. Their language is Kanembu, a Nilo-Saharan language closely related to Kanuri; Arabic and sometimes French are also spoken as second languages. Historically, the Kanembu were associated with the Kanem–Bornu Empire, whose sphere of influence included Eastern Nigeria, Niger, the northern half of Chad, Cameroon, and Libya. Their present-day culture is described in the sources mainly through settlement, clothing, language, commerce, agriculture, and historical continuity.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources state that Kanembu housing and clothing have not changed much since the imperial period.

### Architecture

The sources state that the Kanembu gradually became sedentary and established a capital at Njimi.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe Mai Dunama Dabbalemi as ruler of the Sayfawa dynasty from 1221 to 1259. He was the first Kanembu to convert to Islam, declared jihad against surrounding tribes, and initiated an extended period of conquest.

## Foodways

The Kanembu raise wheat, millet, and corn near Lake Chad and also engage in agriculture and livestock raising.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that the Kanembu language has been written in Latin and Ajami alphabets.

## Language & religion

Kanembu is a Nilo-Saharan language spoken in Chad by the Kanembu people and by a smaller number of people in Niger. It is closely related to Kanuri. Kanembu is written with the Latin script and Ajami alphabets. In 2009, the Chadian government standardized both scripts for the country’s indigenous languages, including Kanembu, in the Chadian National Alphabet. Ajami has been used for Kanembu since the time of Dunama Dabbalemi and remains in use in the Tarjumo language and religious works. The sources also state that Dunama Dabbalemi was the first Kanembu to convert to Islam.
