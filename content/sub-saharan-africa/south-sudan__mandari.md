## Overview

The Mandari, also called Mundari, are a small ethnic group of South Sudan and one of the Karo ethnic groups. They live in traditional lands centered on Terekeka in Central Equatoria, roughly 75 kilometers north of Juba, between the White Nile and Laka Ma’di in Western Equatoria. Their territory is predominantly flat, with rivers, lakes, isolated hills, clay-based soil, and seasonal flooding. The Mandari include cattle-herders and agriculturalists. Cattle function as currency and markers of status, while sorghum cultivation and fishing are also important. Their language, Mandari or Mundari, is an Eastern Nilotic language and is also known as Kutuk na Mundari.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They do record a hair practice among Mandari men: some bathe their hair in cow urine, whose uric acid gives it a red, yellow, or orange color regarded as beautiful.

### Architecture

They name the main settlements in Mundariland as Terekeka, Gemeiza, Mangalla, Muni, Tombek, Tindilo, Tali, Rego, Rijong, Koweri, and Nyori.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They state that Mandari people catch fish using nets and spears.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources describe ritual scarification rather than jewelry. In common with other Nilotic tribes in Sudan, young Mandari men undergo scarification as a rite of passage into adulthood. The typical pattern consists of two sets of three parallel lines, one set on either side of the forehead, sloping downward and unconnected in the middle. Mandari men also sometimes bathe their hair in cow urine, producing red, yellow, or orange coloring regarded as beautiful.

## Festivals & rituals

They do document several social and ritual practices. Cattle serve as a form of currency and a mark of status. Marriages are arranged through the prospective groom’s offering of cattle to the bride’s family, and husbands may take as many wives as they can support. Young men undergo ritual scarification as a rite of passage into adulthood. The sources also state that the Mundari engage in perennial cattle-raiding wars with the Bor Dinka during the dry season.

## Foodways

The Mandari cultivate sorghum and catch fish using nets and spears.

## Language & religion

Mandari, also written Mundari, is an Eastern Nilotic language spoken by the Mundari people of South Sudan. It is also called Kutuk na Mundari and is described as similar to Kutuk na Kuku, Kutuk na Kakwa, Kutuk na Pojulu, Kutuk na Bari, and Kutuk na Nyangwara. The language source records tones represented by á, à, a, and â.
