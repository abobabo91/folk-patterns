## Overview

The Hutu are a Bantu people living mainly in Rwanda and Burundi. They share their language with Tutsi and Twa speakers. Their material culture centers on plaited plant fiber, worked iron, and carved ivory used in household objects and tools.

## Material culture

Hutu material culture combines plaited plant fiber, worked iron, and carved ivory into everyday household items.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Hutu tradition centers on plaiting and twining grass, straw, and fiber into mats, screens, and small objects.

- **Twined grass mats** — Sleeping mats and bed covers made of plaited and twined grass or straw with braided borders, sometimes alternating natural and black colors.
- **Patterned mat work** — Mats decorated with geometric designs like large diamonds on checkered black-and-white grounds or black squares on natural color.
- **Twined hut screens** — Curved fiber screens for dwelling walls, worked in fine black-dyed and natural-colored twine over a coarser woven base.
- **Plaited vessels** — Small oval dishes and containers made from plaited grass in black and brown natural colors.
- **Plaited anklet** — Body wear made from dull light brown plaited grass.

### Clothing & dress

Recorded dress consists of skin skirts with animal limbs and hair left attached.

- **Skin skirts** — Made from animal hides with hair left on and the animal's limbs attached, creating a fringed garment that keeps the hide's original outline.
- **Baby's neckband** — A composite piece of plaited skin with tassels of brown, black and white hair, hung with a string of wooden sticks.

### Architecture

The only documented evidence shows rounded huts made from plant fiber.

- **Hut screen** — A curved fiber section with fine twined black and natural-colored work over a coarser woven base, indicating rounded interior spaces.
- **Rain-guard** — A portable shelter made from an inner layer of grass stems and outer layer of leaves, knotted together with twisted leaf rope.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Iron tools and weapons dominate the collection, along with plain unglazed pottery.

- **Iron blades and swords** — Composite weapons with wooden hilts and iron blades, blackened except where filed, including spears with spiked butts and filed edges.
- **Iron arrows** — Notched and feathered arrows with iron heads bound to shafts with sinew, some with barbs.
- **Iron knives** — Blades ranging from single-piece iron strips where blade and handle are one, to blades set in wooden hafts.
- **Iron tools** — Includes composite adzes with tanged blades shaped like spear heads and tanged bill hooks with wooden handles.
- **Plain pottery vessels** — Unglazed clay pots with cord decoration, including small vessels tied together as loads and cotton-reel-shaped containers with shallow cavities.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment uses tusk ivory, brass, and plaited grass in simple forms, plus carved figurative ivory.

- **Ivory bangle** — A plain section of elephant tusk, dirty white in color.
- **Brass bracelet** — A coiled brass ornament.
- **Plaited grass anklet** — Body wear of dull light brown plaited grass.
- **Carved ivory figures** — Figurative works including human heads, a woman with a bag, a bird, and a hippopotamus tusk carved as a crocodile.

## Music & performance

Three instrument types — lamellophones, zithers, and rattles — survive with decorative pokerwork.

- **Sansa** — A lamellophone of wood with pokerwork decoration, ten keys, and small iron rattles attached to each key.
- **Trough zither** — A wooden string instrument with pokerwork decoration and seven strings, with one split repaired using wire.
- **Seed rattle** — A seed mounted on a stick handle.
- **Gourd rattle** — A gourd filled with gravel and stopped with a stick that serves as the handle, with additional seeds in an attached envelope.

## Dance & theatre

No sources document Hutu dance or dramatic traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

One divining board survives; no festival calendar or life-cycle rites are documented.

- **Divining board** — A semicircular wooden board, slightly hollow and painted white, accompanied by six wedge-shaped white counters cast or read against it for divination.
- **Baby's neckband** — A composite piece of plaited skin with tassels and wooden sticks, the only object tied to a life-cycle stage.

## Foodways

Food culture appears only through carrying vessels, serving dishes, and drinking equipment.

- **Corded pottery vessels** — Three small pottery pots with cord decoration bound together with twisted fiber rope as a single carrying load.
- **Cotton-reel container** — A solid pottery vessel shaped like a cotton reel with a shallow cavity in its top.
- **Plaited grass dish** — A small oval dish for serving, plaited from grass in black and brown natural colors.
- **Bamboo drinking straws** — A set of four straws held together in a bamboo holder, for drinking from a shared vessel.

## Oral tradition & literature

Etymology of the Hutu name was debated: whether it meant slave or lords in relation to neighboring peoples.

- **Hutu etymology** (*Hutu*) — Ernest Viaene (1910) argued the name derived from a word meaning slave, but René Bourgeois countered it originally meant lords, comparing to the Mongo term Bahoto or Bawoto for Bantu rulers of local Twa.

## Language & religion

Hutu speak Rwanda-Rundi, a Bantu language shared with Tutsi and Twa speakers.

- **Rwanda-Rundi** — A Bantu language of the Niger-Congo family with two dialects: Kinyarwanda (official in Rwanda) and Kirundi (official in Burundi).
- **Kinyarwanda** — The Hutu dialect of Rwanda-Rundi, standardized as Rwanda's official language.
- **Kirundi** — The Hutu dialect of Rwanda-Rundi, standardized as Burundi's official language.

## Glossary

- *Abahutu* — Hutu people (plural form)
- *Rwanda-Rundi* — Bantu language shared by Hutu, Tutsi, and Twa speakers
- *Kinyarwanda* — Rwanda dialect of Rwanda-Rundi
- *Kirundi* — Burundi dialect of Rwanda-Rundi
- *Sansa* — Lamellophone with keys and attached rattles
- *Trough zither* — String instrument made from hollowed wood
- *Pokerwork* — Decorative technique of burning patterns into wood
- *Plaiting* — Weaving technique interlacing plant fibers
- *Twining* — Fiber technique twisting lengths together
- *Niger-Congo* — Large language family of Africa
- *Bantu* — Language subfamily and people of sub-Saharan Africa
- *Composite* — Made from more than one material joined together
- *Tanged* — Having a projecting end fitted into a handle
- *Divining board* — Tool used for divination or fortune-telling
