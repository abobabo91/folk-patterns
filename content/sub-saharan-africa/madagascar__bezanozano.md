## Overview

The Bezanozano are an early Malagasy group living in the central inland forests of Madagascar. They were historically middlemen between highland kingdoms and coastal traders, and later became known as conscripted porters for the Merina crown. Today they maintain strong ancestral traditions and are known especially for honey gathering and forest preservation tied to their spiritual beliefs.

## Material culture

Bezanozano material culture emphasizes plaited and woven natural fibre work, unornamented pottery, and wooden objects shaped to household uses.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Women make plaited and woven items from natural fibres, creating patterns through colour contrast rather than design.

- **Plaited basketry** — Baskets built from natural fibre with some strands dyed, creating patterns in the plaiting itself; the same technique makes sleeping mats and head rings for carrying pots.
- **Fibre containers** — Soft plaited containers made for specific household uses, such as keeping spoons.

### Clothing & dress

Few garment traditions are recorded; the defining appearance feature is the many small plaits from which the people take their name.

- **Many small plaits** — The hairstyle distinctive enough that neighbours named the people Bezanozano ('those of many small plaits') for this trait.

### Architecture

Bezanozano settlement shifted from fortified villages under local chiefs to stone tombs and Merina-style houses following nineteenth-century highland conquest.

- **Stone tombs** — Now the ordinary burial architecture of the group, adopted from Merina culture.
- **Trano gasy houses** (*trano gasy*) — Merina-style houses built in Ankay alongside earlier dwelling forms after highland assimilation.
- **Vazimba tombs and mounds** (*vazimba*) — Ancient tombs and earthen mounds ascribed to Madagascar's first inhabitants, maintained as pilgrimage and sacrifice sites.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pottery is unornamented and functional; woodwork dominates the crafted objects, with iron tools arriving only with French colonisation.

- **Reaping knife** — A tool with an iron blade set in a wooden handle.
- **Honey pot** — A wooden pot bound with cord for storing honey, the region's signature product.
- **Blow-pipe** — A wooden instrument for hunting or rituals.
- **Drinking horn** — Made from cattle horn, serving as both drinking vessel and a symbol of wealth.
- **Seat and game-board** — A single wooden object serving both as a seat and as a surface for games.

### Jewelry & body adornment

No jewellery, tattooing or cosmetic practices are recorded; the sole documented body practice is the distinctive hairstyle.

## Music & performance

Three instruments — a drum and two wooden flutes — are preserved in collections, though song genres and professional musicians are not recorded.

- **Drum** — A drum with a wooden body and skin head used in ritual and ceremonial contexts.
- **Wooden flutes** — Two wooden flutes preserved in museum collections.
- **Famadihana ceremony** (*famadihana*) — The reburial ceremony shared with the Merina, the calendar's principal gathering where the dead are taken up and reburied.
- **Vazimba ritual** — Ritual at ancient tombs involving sacrifice and petition to spirits for favourable intervention.

## Dance & theatre

The Betatoato is the one named dance unique to this people; no theatrical or masked tradition is recorded.

- **Betatoato** — A dance unique to the Bezanozano; no details of steps, accompaniment or occasion are recorded.

## Festivals & rituals

The ritual year centres on the dead, with stone-tomb burial, reburial ceremonies, ancestor veneration, and propitiation of the vazimba as land owners.

- **Famadihana** — The reburial ceremony in which the dead are taken up again, shared with the Merina.
- **Razana veneration** (*razana*) — Most Bezanozano venerate ancestors, who in ritual are sometimes called zanahary — the most ancient and powerful ancestors invoked collectively.
- **Vazimba propitiation** (*vazimba*) — The masters of the land, understood as Madagascar's first inhabitants; every ancient tomb is treated as theirs and becomes a pilgrimage and sacrifice site.
- **Fady prohibitions** (*fady*) — Taboos structuring conduct: pork was historically forbidden at Ambatondrazaka, and the indri lemur could not be killed, eaten or trapped in some villages.

## Foodways

Rice in terraced, irrigated paddies is the staple; honey gathered from the forest is the region's signature product and trade good.

- **Vary verina** — A terracing technique with rice on the upper level and beans or corn on the terrace below.
- **Honey gathering and trade** — The Bezanozano were considered the best honey collectors; honey was sold at market and roadside stands and held importance in Merina royal ritual.
- **Indri taboo** (*indri*) — The indri lemur was probably taboo in many villages, where it could not be killed or eaten and had to be buried with human rites if trapped.

## Oral tradition & literature

Oral history preserves memory of small communities governed by village elders and justice settled by consensus, though also carries the wound of conscription into unpaid labour.

- **Vazimba descent narrative** (*vazimba*) — Belief holds that Bezanozano descend from Madagascar's first inhabitants, while simultaneously insisting that their living descendants cannot be identified.
- **Andevo association** (*andevo*) — The name Bezanozano became linked with the historic slave class because the group was heavily conscripted into unpaid labour for the Merina crown.
- **Ethnic identity movement** — After independence in 1960, a strong social movement worked to rebuild ethnic identity with class constraints discarded, seeking equal participation of nobles and descendants of slaves.

## Language & religion

Bezanozano speak a Malagasy dialect descended from Borneo languages; religion is ancestral, with little Christian conversion despite Protestant missionary presence since the nineteenth century.

- **Malagasy language** (*Malagasy*) — A Malayo-Polynesian language of the Barito group, with linguistic relatives in southern Borneo.
- **Zanahary** — Gods understood as the most ancient and most powerful ancestors, invoked anonymously and collectively so no ancestor is offended.
- **Tompontany** — The masters of the land, identified with the vazimba; sites of pilgrimage and ritual propitiation.

## Glossary

- *Ankay* — The forested inland region of Madagascar where the Bezanozano live
- *vazimba* — Madagascar's first inhabitants; spirits or ancestors associated with ancient tombs and land ownership
- *razana* — Ancestors venerated by the Bezanozano in ritual and daily life
- *zanahary* — The most ancient and powerful ancestors, understood as gods and invoked collectively
- *tompontany* — Masters of the land; the vazimba, whose tombs are sites of pilgrimage and sacrifice
- *fady* — Taboos or prohibitions that structure conduct and restrict certain foods or animals
- *trano gasy* — Merina-style houses built in Bezanozano territory following highland conquest
- *vary verina* — A terracing technique with rice on the upper level and beans or corn below
- *andevo* — The historic slave class; the Bezanozano name became associated with this class due to conscription into unpaid labour
- *Betatoato* — A dance unique to the Bezanozano people
- *indri* — A lemur species that was taboo in many Bezanozano villages and could not be killed or eaten
