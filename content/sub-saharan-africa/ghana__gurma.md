## Overview

The Gurma, also called Gourma or Gourmantché, are an ethnic group living mainly in northeastern Ghana, Burkina Faso around Fada N’Gourma, and northern areas of Togo and Benin, as well as southwestern Niger. Gurma is also the name of their language, while Gourmanché is a related name for the language, which belongs to the Gur family within the Oti–Volta languages. The language is spoken around the traditional Gurma capital Fada N’Gourma and in neighbouring parts of Burkina Faso, Togo, Benin, Niger, Ghana, and Nigeria. Ethnographic research cited in the sources discusses Gourmantché ideas of human being, religion, nature, lions, and biodiversity.

## Foodways

The Gurma language source mentions *ñima* “water,” *soama* “blood,” and *tibu* “tree” as linguistic examples, but these examples do not constitute a description of Gurma foodways.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources describe Richard Alan Swanson’s work on Gourmantché ethnoanthropology, which uses language texts to present Gourmantché perceptions of “human being” from the perspective of the people themselves. The work discusses concepts including God, destiny, the body, life, and death, as well as terms for human body parts.

## Language & religion

Gourmanché, also called Goulmacema, Gourma, Gourmantche, Gulimancema, Gulmancema, Gurma, or Gourmanchéma, is the language of the Gurma people. It is the largest language by number of speakers in the Gurma subgroup of the Oti–Volta languages, which includes the Moba language and the Konkomba language. Gourmanché is a tone language distinguishing high, mid, and low tones. Its standard orthography uses the symbols c and j for palatal stops, and the language has a noun-class grammatical system with eight classes. Verbs inflect for aspect rather than tense, and the language has subject–verb–object word order.

The sources discuss Gourmantché concepts of God, destiny, life, death, the body, and spiritual beings of nature. A complete translation of the Bible was dedicated in September 2005.
