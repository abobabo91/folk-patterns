## Overview

The Banyamulenge are a community living mainly in South Kivu, in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Their ancestral origins are described as lying in what is now Rwanda, Tanzania, Burundi and Uganda. They settled above the Ruzizi Plain, on the Itombwe Plateau, and are associated with the Mulenge Mountain, the high plateau of South Kivu, Mwenga, Fizi, Uvira, Bukavu, Minembwe and the Ruzizi Plain. Their main language is Kinyamulenge. Estimates cited for their population include around 50,000 to 70,000 and around 60,000–80,000. The group matters historically because its political status, territorial identity and relationship with neighbouring communities have been central to conflict in eastern Congo.

They do state that the Itombwe Plateau reached an altitude of 3000 meters and supported cattle grazing rather than large-scale agriculture.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do mention cattle, sheep, meat and milk in connection with pastoral and economic life.

## Music & performance

The material does state that the community has used peaceful strategies to address political and military problems, including a judicial inquiry or mission endorsed on 6 January to investigate crimes committed against Banyamulenge in Minembwe.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe several historical and political occasions affecting the community, including the Simba Rebellion of 1963–1965, the First Congo War of 1996–1997, the Second Congo War of 1998–2003 and the M23 War and present period identified as 2012–2025. These are conflicts and political periods rather than festivals or rituals.

The sources mention the overthrow of the Tutsi monarchy and colonial clashes between 1959 and 1973, the arrival of major groups of Tutsi refugees in 1959–1960 after the “Social Revolution,” and the arrival of about 50,000 Burundian refugees in late 1993 followed by almost one million mostly Hutu refugees the next year. They do not present these events as commemorative traditions.

## Foodways

The sources do state that pastoralists kept cattle and that urban Banyamulenge could earn a living by selling meat and milk from their herds to gold diggers. They also mention disputes involving cattle grazing: pastoralists were given the Mulenge Mountain to herd, and some Banyamulenge moved onto the Ruzizi Plain, where a few became chiefs among the Barundi through gifts of cattle.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do record competing historical narratives concerning the community’s origins. One Rwandan narrative attributes migration to attempts to avoid increasingly high taxes imposed by Mwami Rwabugiri of the Kingdom of Rwanda, while another account states that Rwandan Tutsis fled the violent war of succession that erupted in 1895 after Rwabugiri’s death. The source also reports that the RPF claims Banyamulenge were brought in the 18th century from Rwanda, Burundi and the Karagwa area of Tanzania, while records and historical data cited in the article place their arrival in the area in the 20th century.

## Language & religion

The main language of the Banyamulenge is Kinyamulenge.

The sources do describe several identity terms and historical classifications. *Mulenge* referred historically to mountains concentrated on the High Plateau of South Kivu and translates literally as “people of Mulenge”; the name was chosen in the early 1950s to avoid being called “Banyarwanda” and being seen as foreigners in what was then Zaire. In 1976, the word “Banyamulenge” came into wide usage after Gisaro Muhazo began an initiative to reclassify Banyamulenge of Mwenga, Fizi and Uvira into a single administrative entity. The attempt failed, but the term remained and later became a catchall label that included Rwandans living in the eastern parts.
