## Overview

The Songhai people, who collectively identify themselves as *Ayneha*, are an ethnolinguistic group in West Africa. They are primarily located in Niger and Mali, especially around the middle stretches and Niger Bend area of the Niger River. Songhai languages are spoken in Mali, Niger, Benin, Burkina Faso and Nigeria, including in Timbuktu, Niamey, Gao and Tillaberi. The Songhai languages are commonly classified as Nilo-Saharan, although this remains controversial and some linguists consider them an independent language family. Songhai history is closely linked to the Songhai Empire, which dominated the western Sahel in the 15th and 16th centuries. The group includes Zarma, Songhaiborai, Dendi and other sub-groups.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Arkilla Munga* is a tapestry-type marriage bed-screen used by the Songhay. One British Museum example is made from ten hand-woven narrow strips of locally produced sheep wool and locally spun cotton threads, hand-sewn selvedge to selvedge. Its decoration consists of coloured bands, including natural-coloured wool and alternating black, red, orange and blue narrow bands.

**Marriage wall-hanging** is a rectangular hand-woven tapestry-type piece composed of eleven narrow strips sewn together. Its warp is made of white cotton threads, while the weft includes industrial cotton threads and synthetic wool threads in black, yellow, red and green. Motifs are produced through supplementary weft work.

**Marriage bed-screen** is also a rectangular tapestry-type piece. One catalogue record describes seven narrow strips sewn together, with industrial white and coloured cotton threads, supplementary-weft motifs, and twisted and knotted threads forming tassels.

**Blanket** is made of cotton and woven in nine wide strips. Its decoration includes weft-faced bands and supplementary weft-float bands in several colours, with short tufts and two-tone tassels.

**Straw tray** is recorded among Songhai objects from Timbuktu at the Niger. Several examples were also used as lids for earthenware vessels.

**Straw bowl** is described as made of plant parts. A catalogue record gives its origin as Timbuktu, Mali, and identifies its ethnic group as Songhai.

**Motif vocabulary.** Coloured bands, transverse weft-faced arrangement, lozenge-shaped motifs, supplementary weft work, tassels.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum records identify several Songhai basketry objects from Timbuktu at the Niger, including *Korgbricka* basket trays and a *Korgskål* basket bowl. The trays were used as lids for earthenware vessels. The bowl was made of plant parts and measured 24 cm in diameter. One tray record gives a diameter of 17,5 cm and describes it as a straw tray used as a lid for cooking vessels. The catalogue also records two photographs of Songhai women in Bamako and the French Sudan, dated 1953 and 1958-01.

## Music & performance

The Wikipedia material states that the servile strata of Songhai-Zarma society included artists, musicians and griots.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that the Songhai are predominantly adherents of Islam and describe the conversion of Za Kusoy, the 15th ruler of the Zuwa dynasty, to Islam in 1009–1010 A.D. The social-stratification account describes Islamic clerics, guardian spirits associated with castes, polygynous marriages and preferred cross-cousin partners.

## Foodways

Songhai populations cultivate cereals, raise small herds of cattle and fish in the Niger Bend area. Agriculture is adapted to arid and semi-arid conditions, with a three-month rainy season and a dry period of eight to nine months. Historical descriptions of Gao mention rice, milk, chickens, fish and cucumber. The sources also state that Songhai people were fishermen and traders and were associated with caravan trade. Historical accounts describe buying and selling with cowries. The Songhai are predominantly Muslim.

## Oral tradition & literature

The early history of the Songhai is partly preserved through oral tradition. Al-Sadi’s *Tarikh al-Sudan* reports traditions concerning the Za dynasty and its legendary founder, Za Alayaman, also called Dialliaman. The *Tarikh al-Sudan* and the *Tarikh al-fattash* provide information on Gao and the Songhai Empire, although their earlier accounts are described as less reliable and sometimes conflicting. A few precolonial poems and letters composed in Songhay and written in the Arabic script exist in Timbuktu.

## Language & religion

Songhay, Songhai or Ayneha languages form a group of closely related languages and dialects centred on the middle stretches of the Niger River. They are spoken in Mali, Niger, Benin, Burkina Faso and Nigeria. Zarma is described as the most widely spoken Songhay language, while Koyraboro Senni is spoken in Gao and Koyra Chiini in the area west of Gao. Some varieties have little or no mutual intelligibility. Songhay has been written in the Arabic script in precolonial poems and letters from Timbuktu and is currently written in the Latin script. Its classification as Nilo-Saharan remains controversial; Dimmendaal considers it an independent language family. The Songhai are predominantly Muslim. The sources also describe Zima as priests and Islamic clerics and mention guardian spirits associated with social strata.
