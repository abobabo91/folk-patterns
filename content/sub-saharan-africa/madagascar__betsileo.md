## Overview

The Betsileo live in Madagascar’s southern highlands. Their culture is closely tied to rice farming, terraced fields, cattle raising and woven goods. They combine Christian practices with older beliefs about ancestors and spirits. Their kingdoms also had oral traditions dating back to the 17th century.

## Material culture

Betsileo material culture includes woven cotton, basketry, vegetable-fibre hats, wooden tools, pottery and carved objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Betsileo textiles use cotton, stripes, geometric designs, fringes and embroidery.

- **Woven cotton textiles** — Cotton cloths include striped and geometric patterns, fringes and embroidery.
- **Burial cloths** — Burial cloths are cotton textiles with wide stripes, thin stripes and geometric patterns.
- **Striped Betsileo tissue** — This tissue is sewn at the middle, striped in different colours, embroidered with silk and finished with fringed warp threads.
- **Brown embroidered cloth** — This rectangular brown cotton cloth has fringe on every side and a silver geometric design with the initials “EB”.
- **Basketry** — Basketry is used for hats, covers, mats and baskets.

### Clothing & dress

Recorded Betsileo dress includes woven waist-bands, fibre hats, grass caps and decorated cloths.

- **Cotton waist-band** — A waist-band is woven from cotton and finished with braided bands.
- **Basketry hats** — Several hats are made from basketry.
- **Vegetable-fibre hat** — One recorded hat is made from vegetable fibre.
- **Plaited-grass cap** — A recorded cap is made from plaited grass.
- **Cotton burial cloths** — Cotton burial cloths use stripes, fringes, geometric patterns and embroidery.

### Architecture

Traditional Betsileo homes included vegetable-fibre huts and wooden huts for nobles, while mud and brick houses are more common today.

- **Vegetable-fibre huts** — Common traditional huts were made of vegetable fibre.
- **Nobles’ wooden huts** — Wooden huts were reserved for nobles.
- **Zebu-horn decoration** — Huts could be decorated with motifs or zebu horns.
- **Rice terraces** — Rice paddies cover the sides of the hills and valleys around Fianarantsoa in elaborate terraces.
- **Mud and brick houses** — Mud and brick houses are more common today.

## Music & performance

Recorded Betsileo instruments include the valiha and several reed, bark and bamboo instruments.

- **Bamboo tube zither** (*Valiha*) — The valiha is a bamboo tube zither or guitar with strings made from bamboo bark or peel.
- **Reed flute** — Reed flutes are among the recorded Betsileo instruments.
- **Bark flute** — Bark flutes are also recorded.
- **Bamboo flute** — Bamboo flutes are also recorded.
- **Snuff-box instrument** — An unfinished reed or bamboo instrument is described as a snuff-box.

## Festivals & rituals

Betsileo ritual life combines Christian practice with ancestral traditions, including the famadihana ceremony.

- **Turning of the bones** (*famadihana*) — During famadihana, ancestors’ remains are taken from tombs, wrapped in new linens and celebrated.
- **Creator deity** (*Zanahary*) — Zanahary is a creator deity who is still invoked for ceremonies.
- **Divination** — Diviners and astrologists are consulted to set ceremony dates and read people’s futures.
- **Witch-doctor practice** — Witch doctors are believed to manipulate magic and communicate with ancestors.
- **Ancestral and Christian practice** — Christian practices continue alongside ancestral traditions and religious beliefs.

## Foodways

Rice is the Betsileo staple and economic mainstay, usually eaten with livestock and farm crops.

- **Rice** — Rice is cultivated widely and is the main staple food.
- **Livestock with rice** — Rice is commonly eaten with beef, chicken or duck.
- **Cassava and beans** — Cassava and beans are secondary crops eaten with rice.
- **Farm vegetables** — Other farm vegetables also accompany rice.
- **Rice terraces** — Rice fields are built into terraces in the Betsileo region.

## Language & religion

The Betsileo speak a Malagasy dialect and combine Christian practice with beliefs about ancestors and spirits.

- **Malagasy language** (*Malagasy*) — Malagasy is a Malayo-Polynesian language derived from the Barito languages of southern Borneo.
- **Ancestral spirits** — Earlier Betsileo beliefs recognized ancestral spirits.
- **Ghosts** — Earlier beliefs also recognized ghosts.
- **Nature spirits** — Nature spirits were part of earlier Betsileo beliefs.
- **Christianity** — About 94% of Betsileo are Christians, most of them Protestant or Catholic.
