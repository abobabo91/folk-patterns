## Overview

The Nsenga are a Bantu ethnic tribe of Zambia and Mozambique. In Zambia, they are found in Nyimba and Petauke districts of Eastern province, with Nsenga Luzi dialects in the Luangwa valley around Chief Nyalugwe, Mboloma and Lwembe, and the Chikunda of Luangwa Boma (Feira). Their language occupies an area on the plateau forming the watershed between the Zambezi and Luangwa river systems and Western Malawi, overshadowing Kachebere mountain near Mchinji. Nsenga is part of the Sabi family of languages. The sources describe Nsenga culture through bead work, basketry, agriculture, clan organization, migration traditions and relations with neighboring peoples. No population number is given in the sources.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Bead work** — The Nsenga are described as being known for bead work.

**Basketry** — Basketry is identified as part of Nsenga artwork.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention bead work as part of Nsenga artwork.

## Music & performance

They mention *Malombelo a Kamo Kamo Occasional (Pastoral) Offices in Nsenga*, described as Anglican liturgical material digitized by Richard Mammana and Charles Wohlers.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe historical organization under a clan system of governance. The clans named are *enetembo*, *enemwanza*, *eneng'oma*, *enelungu*, *enemumba* and *enemvula*, headed by *Tumvi*.

## Foodways

The Nsenga grow groundnuts, maize, millet and sorghum for consumption, and cotton as a cash crop.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources describe a migration tradition in which the Nsenga are believed to have moved into Zambia around the 14th century from the Luba–Lunda kingdom of Congo, identified in the source with the current Democratic Republic of Congo. The name Nsenga is explained as meaning “journey,” referring to the tribe’s long trek from Luba Kingdom. The account also describes encounters with the Bemba during this migration: under the instruction of their leader Chitimukulu, Bemba fighters captured a Nsenga woman named Lumbwe, and Nsenga hunters were then ordered by clan heads to hunt Bemba women. The sources connect these events with similarities between Nsenga and Bemba names and greetings, including Nkonde, Mwape, Ngosa, Ngoza, Lumbwe, “mulishani” and “mulityani.” No separate epic, folktale collection, proverb tradition, riddle tradition or contemporary literary revival is described.

## Language & religion

Nsenga is a Bantu language of Zambia and Mozambique and belongs to the Sabi family of languages. It is spoken on the plateau forming the watershed between the Zambezi and Luangwa river systems and in Western Malawi around Kachebere mountain near Mchinji. The sources distinguish Nsenga Luzi dialects in the Luangwa valley and mention the Chikunda of Luangwa Boma (Feira). They mention Anglican liturgical material in Nsenga, titled *Malombelo a Kamo Kamo Occasional (Pastoral) Offices in Nsenga*.
