## Overview

The Alur live around the northwestern Great Lakes region, mainly north of Lake Albert. They farm, herd animals, fish, and hunt. Their society has kings, chiefs, clans, and separate domestic spaces for men and women. Their material culture includes iron tools, weapons, bows, quivers, fish-hooks, and trumpets.

## Material culture

Alur material culture is marked by grass-thatched huts and practical iron tools, weapons, hunting gear, and sound-producing objects.

### Architecture

Alur homesteads traditionally contain grass-thatched huts built by men.

- **Grass-thatched hut** — Alur traditionally live in grass-thatched huts, and men build them.
- **Clan homestead** — Alur clan homesteads are placed in the central part of the territory to help keep it under clan control.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources record many iron tools and weapons but no Alur ceramics or named household vessels.

- **Iron-bladed knife** — The knife has an iron blade, a wooden handle, and a ferrule wrapped with iron or copper wire.
- **Reaping knife** — The reaping knife has a grip decorated with strips of copper and brass.
- **Chopper** — The chopper has a wide iron blade, a side projection, and a wooden handle bound with iron wire bands.
- **Iron adze or axe blade** — The adze or axe blade has a split hollow socket.
- **Iron fish-hook** — The fish-hook is made of iron and attached with string made from vegetable fibre.

## Music & performance

The sources record a decorated wooden trumpet but give no musical name, genre, or repertoire.

- **Elephant trumpet** — The long wooden trumpet is bound with monitor-lizard or python skin and decorated with cowrie shells, leather strips, brass rings, and bells.
- **Hide quiver** — The quiver is made of hide covered with long black hair and has hide carrying straps.
- **Wrapped bow** — Recorded bows are made from wood, hide, and reptile skin, or from cane, hide, and fibre cord.

## Dance & theatre

Agwara is a royal dance performed by men and women only in the king’s presence.

- **Royal Agwara dance** (*Agwara*) — Agwara is performed during community rituals or festivals in the presence of the king.

## Festivals & rituals

Alur public ritual life centers on royal authority, community events, and chiefly reconciliation.

- **Royal dance festival** (*Agwara*) — The Agwara dance is performed during community rituals or festivals before the king.
- **Royal title** (*Rwoth*) — The current Alur king is referred to as the Rwoth.
- **King’s distinguishing title** (*Ubimu*) — Ubimu is the title that distinguishes the king from other chiefs.
- **Royal spear-head tradition** — Alur kings are crowned according to the royal spear-head bearing tradition.
- **Clan reconciliation ritual** — In October 2012, Atyak-Winam and PaNduru chiefs held a ritual to lay to rest a long conflict between their clans.

## Foodways

Alur foodways combine farming, herding, fishing, hunting, and a strong social ranking of food.

- **Farmer-herder diet** — The Alur grew millet, cassava, maize, sweet potatoes, spinach, and pumpkins while keeping cattle, goats, and chickens.
- **Goat and chicken meat** — Goat and chicken were important sources of meat.
- **Seasonal fishing** — Fishing became important during the drought season.
- **Prestige food** — High-ranking men had first choice of food, especially meat and beer.
- **Cattle bride price** — Cattle were important because bride price was paid in cattle.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources preserve clan and chiefly histories.

- **Atyak Kingdom history** (*Atyak*) — Clan and chiefly accounts preserve traditions about the Atyak Kingdom.
- **Ukuru Chiefdom history** (*Ukuru*) — Traditions describe the foundation of the Ukuru Chiefdom.
- **Paidha and PaNduru history** (*PaNduru*) — Historical accounts describe the formation of Paidha and PaNduru.
- **Patrilineal family trees** — Alur patrilineages maintain family trees as important social realities.

## Language & religion

Alur is a Western Nilotic language with several dialects and no officially accepted writing system.

- **Alur language** (*Alur*) — Alur is spoken in southern West Nile and northeastern Ituri.
- **Highland Alur dialect** (*Okoro*) — Highland Alur, also called Okoro, speak a dialect that differs from lowland Alur.
- **Lowland Alur dialect** (*Jonam*) — Lowland Alur, also called Jonam or Lo-Naam, may have difficulty understanding Highland Alur.
- **Alur subdialects** (*Jokot*) — Jokot is one of the named Alur subdialects.
- **Alur subdialect** (*Mambisa*) — Mambisa is one of the named Alur subdialects.
