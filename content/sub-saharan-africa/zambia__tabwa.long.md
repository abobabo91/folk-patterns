## Overview

The Tabwa are associated with Zambia and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Their language, Taabwa, is a Bantu language spoken by half a million or so people. The language is also called Ichitaabwa, Kitabwa, and Rungu, with Malungu given as another name. The available sources provide limited ethnographic information beyond language and museum catalogue descriptions. The objects represented include carved wooden figures, staffs, a seat, a baton, and a comb with a standing female figure carved in ivory.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum records document several carved wooden objects. These include a wooden seat with a round seat, open-work carving around its base, a tall back topped by a human head with a ponytail draped down the back, and geometric designs carved into the back. The records also include a wooden baton or staff surmounted by a human head, several wooden staffs, and a wooden carving of a seated female figure. One comb is carved in ivory and has a standing female figure on top.

## Language & religion

Taabwa, also called Ichitaabwa, Kitabwa, and Tabwa, or Rungu, also called Malungu, is a Bantu language of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Zambia. The language is spoken by half a million or so people. The sources used do not cover Tabwa religious beliefs, religious institutions, scripts, dialect geography, or spiritual practices.
