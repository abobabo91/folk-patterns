## Overview

The Swazi are a Bantu people whose culture centers on language, homesteads, royal life and family rituals. Their main homeland is Eswatini, with large communities in South Africa. Important cultural events include Incwala, Umhlanga and the marriage ceremony called umtsimba. Beadwork, pottery, carving, grasswork, singing and dancing are also important.

## Material culture

Swazi material culture includes beadwork, fibre clothing, grass mats and baskets, pottery, carved wood and horn ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Swazi textile traditions include beadwork, grass mats and basketry.

- **Beadwork** — Beads are used to make jewellery and clothing items.
- **Grass mats** (*emacansi*) — These mats are made from special grasses.
- **Grass products** (*tihlantsi*) — Tihlantsi are named among Swazi grass products.
- **Basketry** — Special grasses are used to make baskets and other objects, including beer-pot covers.

### Clothing & dress

Swazi dress combines beadwork, fibre garments and ceremonial clothing.

- **Colourful necklace** (*ligcebesha*) — Ligcebesha is a colourful necklace.
- **Girls’ colourful skirt** (*indlamu*) — Indlamu is a colourful skirt worn by girls.
- **Fibre dress** — A museum record describes a girl’s fibre dress with a band of red cotton fabric.
- **Beaded fibre belt** — A belt made of beads and fibre is recorded in a museum catalogue.
- **Love-letter cloth** — One recorded love-letter is made of cotton cloth, glass beads and mother-of-pearl buttons.

### Architecture

The homestead is the main Swazi social unit, centered on a cattle byre and surrounded by huts and yards.

- **Beehive hut** — The traditional homestead hut is thatched with dry grass.
- **Wife’s hut and yard** — In a polygamous homestead, each wife has her own hut and yard enclosed by reed fences.
- **Cattle byre** (*kraal*) — The circular kraal is made from large logs and branches and stores wealth, grain and prestige.
- **Chiefdom** (*umphakatsi*) — Homes and land together form a chiefdom called umphakatsi.
- **Administrative group** (*inkhundla*) — Several chiefdoms form an inkhundla.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Swazi everyday objects include clay pots, carved wood, head-rests and a stone tobacco pipe.

- **Clay pots** — These pots are used for carrying water, cooking beer and decoration.
- **Meat-serving utensil** (*umcwembe*) — The umcwembe is a wooden utensil used for serving meat.
- **Carved head-rest stool** — A recorded wooden head-rest cum stool has four legs and alternating light and dark wood.
- **Wooden head-rest** — Another recorded head-rest has four legs with dark and light bands.
- **Stone tobacco pipe** — A recorded tobacco pipe is carved from steatite.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Swazi adornment uses beads, seeds, porcupine quills, wood, horn and red ochre.

- **Colourful necklace** (*ligcebesha*) — Ligcebesha is a colourful necklace.
- **Seed and quill necklace** — A recorded necklace is made from seeds and porcupine quills.
- **Horn-pendant necklet** — A recorded necklet has wooden beads and two horn pendants.
- **Horn hair-pin** — A recorded horn hair-pin ends in a snuff container.
- **Bride’s red ochre** — During umtsimba, the bride is smeared with red ochre after the cattle-kraal ritual.

## Music & performance

Singing and dancing are prominent in Swazi culture, including praise-singing and traditional instruments.

- **Musical bow** (*makhweyane*) — The makhweyane is a single-stringed musical bow with a gourd resonator.
- **Sansa** — A recorded sansa has nine metal lamellae, an iron bridge and pyro-decoration, and its sound comes from the wood of its body.
- **Journey rattles** — Two iron rings attached to a hoop served as rattles, and the instrument was used on journeys rather than for dancing.
- **Praise-singing** — Praise-singing is a prominent part of Swazi culture.

## Dance & theatre

Swazi dancing appears in marriage ceremonies, royal events, the Reed Dance and community gatherings.

- **Reed Dance** (*Umhlanga*) — Umhlanga is an eight-day ceremony in which unmarried, childless girls cut reeds, present them to the Queen Mother and dance.
- **Girls’ captain** (*induna*) — The induna is a commoner maiden appointed to lead the girls and announce the ceremony dates.
- **Bridal dancing** (*umtsimba*) — During umtsimba, the bridal party eats meat and dances at the groom’s homestead.

## Festivals & rituals

Swazi public and family rituals center on kingship, marriage, age groups and major life events.

- **First Fruits ceremony** (*Incwala*) — Incwala is a major Kingship Ceremony in which the King tastes the new harvest, and it cannot occur without a king.
- **Reed Dance** (*Umhlanga*) — Umhlanga honours the Queen Mother and aims to preserve girls’ chastity, provide tribute labour and encourage solidarity.
- **Age-regiment custom** (*umchwasho*) — Umchwasho was an older age-regiment system for young girls, and its chastity rite continued until 19 August 2005.
- **Traditional marriage** (*umtsimba*) — Umtsimba is usually held from June to August and includes dancing, meat, a cattle-kraal ceremony, red ochre and gifts.
- **Water people** (*bemanti*) — The bemanti, or water people, take part in the climax of the Big Incwala.

## Foodways

Swazi food traditions in the profile include meat at marriage, beer made in clay pots and the new harvest at Incwala.

- **Marriage meat** (*umtsimba*) — Goat or cow meat is served during umtsimba, including at a river gathering on Saturday morning.
- **Beer cooking** — Beer is cooked in clay pots called tინდziwo.
- **New harvest** (*Incwala*) — The King’s tasting of the new harvest is part of Incwala.
- **Sealed grain pits** — Traditional homesteads contain sealed pits for storing grain.

## Oral tradition & literature

Praise-singing is prominent, but the profile gives no named poems, tales or other literary forms.

- **Praise-singing** — Praise-singing is identified as an important part of Swazi culture.

## Language & religion

SiSwati unifies Swazi identity, while traditional beliefs recognize a creator, ancestors, diviners and healers alongside Christianity.

- **Swazi language** (*siSwati*) — SiSwati is a Bantu language of the Nguni group and an official language in Eswatini and South Africa.
- **Supreme creator** (*Mvelincanti*) — Mvelincanti means “he who was there from the beginning” and is the traditional supreme creator.
- **Traditional diviner** (*Sangoma*) — A Sangoma is a traditional diviner chosen by a family’s ancestors.
- **Diviner training** (*kwetfwasa*) — Kwetfwasa is the training of a Sangoma.
- **Trance divination** (*kubhula*) — Kubula involves communication through trance with natural super-powers.
