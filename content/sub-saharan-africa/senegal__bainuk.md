## Overview

The Bainuk, also called Banyuk, Banun, Banyun, Bainouk, Bainunk, Banyum, Bagnoun, Banhum, Banyung, Ñuñ, Elomay, or Elunay, are an ethnic group living primarily in Senegal, with communities in parts of Gambia and Guinea-Bissau. They are associated historically with the lower Casamance River and with the region between the Cacheu River and the Gambia. The Bainuk are described as skilled dyers and weavers. Their history includes several Bainuk states, participation in the population of the Kasa kingdom, and control of riverine and coastal trade. Their language is described as a Senegambian group of around 8 closely related languages spoken in Senegal and Guinea-Bissau.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Dyeing and weaving** — The Bainuk are known as skilled dyers and weavers.

## Music & performance

The Bainuk article mentions the *Kumpo* as a tradition that was originally Bainuk, mixed with Mande concepts, and later adopted by the Jola.

## Dance & theatre

They mention the *Kumpo* tradition, but provide no further description of its dance or theatrical characteristics.

## Festivals & rituals

Many Bainuk are adherents of Islam, a process that began around the 17th century through the influence of Muslim Mande scholars and merchants who settled in the region. Some Bainuk also practice their traditional animistic religion.

## Oral tradition & literature

Mandinka oral history from the Pakao area of the middle Casamance records that *Bainouk* was a pejorative term first used after the Mandinka defeated the Bainuk in battle in the late 16th century. The term was explained through the Mandinka word *bai*, meaning “chase away.” Other oral histories state that the Bainuk arrived in a land that was empty before their arrival and date their arrival to the 11th century CE, although archaeological finds indicate that human occupation of the region is much older.

The sources also describe a curse supposedly laid on the Bainuk by a tyrannical king at his execution by rebellious subjects. Some people are said to downplay their origins and language, while others are reclaiming Bainuk heritage and preserving their history. No Bainuk epic, folktale collection, proverb tradition, riddle tradition, or literary work is identified.

## Language & religion

Banyun, Banyum, Nyun, or Bainouk is a Senegambian language group of around 8 closely related languages in Senegal and Guinea-Bissau. The sources list the varieties Baïnouk-gunyaamolo, Baïnouk Samik, and Baïnouk gunyuño. Bainouk-Gunyaamolo was spoken by 30,000 people in 2013 in the northern Casamance River area, within a triangle formed by the towns of Bignona, Tobor, and Niamone, or north of Ziguinchor, and was also spoken in Gambia. Baïnouk-Samik was spoken by 1,850 people in 2006 around Samik and surrounding villages, approximately 20 km east of Ziguinchor. Baïnouk-Gunyuño, also called Bainounk-Gujaher, was spoken by 8,860 people in 2006 in the region of Cacheu and near São Domingos in Guinea-Bissau. Other names include Elomay and Elunay; the Gunyaamolo variety is also called Ñuñ or Nyamone, and Gunyuño is also called Guñuun or Samik. Speakers refer to the language as *gu-jaaxər*. The sources describe Islam and traditional animistic religion.
