## Overview

The Marka, also called Marka Dafing, Meka, or Maraka, are a Mande people of northwest Mali. Some Maraka people are found in Ghana. They speak Marka, a Manding language; Marka is also called Dafing and is spoken in northwest Burkina Faso. The Marka originated from Soninke people from the Wagadu Empire who migrated to the middle Niger between the 11th and 13th centuries. Today there are only around 25,000 Marka speakers, who are largely integrated among their Soninke and Bambara neighbors. Among the peoples of the Inner Niger Delta, the Marka are rice-growing specialists, cultivating rice despite dramatic and unpredictable variations in seasonal flooding.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources state that the Marka founded Nyamina and Sansanding during the early period of their history, and Barouéli and Banamba in the 19th century. These settlements were at various times prominent trading and religious centers.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that the Marka are adherents of Islam. They also describe Nyamina, Sansanding, Barouéli, and Banamba as trading and religious centers at various times.

## Foodways

The Marka are described as “rice-growing specialists” among the peoples of the Inner Niger Delta. They successfully cultivate rice despite dramatic, unpredictable variations in seasonal flooding, and many of their methods for dealing with these conditions are kept secret from neighboring tribes. Their economy was also based on slave plantation agriculture growing food and cotton to be traded.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources explain that the term “Maraka” means “men who rule” in Bambara.

## Language & religion

Marka is a Manding language, and is also called Dafing. It is spoken in northwest Burkina Faso, while the Marka people of northwest Mali speak Marka. The Marka are adherents of Islam. The sources also describe the Bambara Empire as practicing African spirituality.
