## Overview

The Gisu live mainly around Mount Elgon in eastern Uganda. They are agriculturalists who grow crops including coffee, cotton, maize, beans, millet, sorghum, yams, and cassava. Their cultural life includes Imbalu initiation ceremonies, banana foods, bamboo shoots, pottery, tools, shields, ornaments, and rattles. Their language is closely related to Bukusu in Kenya.

## Material culture

Recorded Gisu material culture includes pottery vessels, wooden and iron tools, shields, whistles, ornaments, and rattles.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not document Gisu textiles, patterns, or named motifs.

### Clothing & dress

The sources do not describe everyday or ceremonial clothing, but they record several forms of warrior ornament.

- **Seed girdle** — A girdle is made from strings of seed.
- **Tusk head ornament** — Warrior head ornaments are made from pierced tusks and attached with leather thongs.
- **Iron necklet** — An iron necklet is one of the recorded forms of body adornment.
- **Rattling leglet** — A leather leglet is bound with cotton cloth and fitted with an iron rattle.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Gisu houses, settlements, roofs, or building styles.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The catalogue records many pottery vessels along with a shield, knife, and whistle.

- **Handled pottery vase** — A reddish pottery vase has one handle.
- **Five-mouthed beer pot** — A spheroidal pottery beer pot has five mouths.
- **Strainer vase** — A pottery vase has a spout and a strainer.
- **Perforated bowl** — Some plain or reddish pottery bowls have holes in the bottom, including bowls with nine or ten holes.
- **Wood and wicker shield** — A small wooden and wicker shield has a central boss.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Gisu ornaments include iron, seed, leather, cotton, tusk, and a small rattle.

- **Ring-ended necklet** — An iron necklet has ring ends.
- **Seed girdle** — A girdle is made from strings of seed.
- **Rattling leglet** — A leather leglet is bound with cotton cloth and has an iron rattle containing two metal balls.
- **Pierced tusk ornament** — Warrior head ornaments are pierced and attached with leather thongs.

## Festivals & rituals

Imbalu is a circumcision initiation ceremony held among the Bamasaaba every two years in August.

- **Imbalu initiation** (*Imbalu*) — Imbalu is a circumcision initiation ceremony for men among the Bagisu and is held every two years during August.
- **Masaba's circumcision rite** — The story says Masaba underwent a Maasai circumcision rite before marrying Nabarwa.

## Foodways

Banana, called Matoke, is the Gisu staple, while bamboo shoots called Malewa are linked to their ancestors' food.

- **Cooked banana staple** (*Matoke*) — Banana is the staple food of the Gisu and is commonly called Matoke.
- **Bamboo shoots** (*Malewa*) — Bamboo shoots called Malewa are collected from bamboo trees on Mount Elgon.
- **Arabica coffee farming** — Gisu agricultural communities grow Arabica coffee, much of which is sold to Bugisu Co-operative Union.

## Oral tradition & literature

Gisu origin traditions connect the people to Mundu, Sera, and Masaba, and explain the name Bagisu.

- **Mundu and Sera ancestors** — The Masaba, Bukusu, and Luhya people believed Mundu and Sera were their ancestors.
- **Masaba migration story** — A tradition says Masaba migrated from the Ethiopian Mountains through Lake Turkana to Sironko and settled around Bududa.
- **Origin of the name Bagisu** — The story says Masaba's son Mwambu was nicknamed Nkisu, which Masaba pronounced as Mugisu, giving rise to the name Bagisu.

## Language & religion

Masaba is a Bantu language with several dialects, and most Bagisu are Christian.

- **Masaba language** (*Masaba*) — Masaba, also called Lumasaaba, is spoken by more than two million people in East Africa.
- **Gisu dialect** (*Gisu*) — Gisu is a Masaba dialect spoken in eastern Uganda and is mutually intelligible with Bukusu.
- **Lumasaba** (*Lumasaaba*) — Bamasaba speak a Lumasaba dialect that is understood by speakers of other dialects and by the Bukusu.
- **Church of Uganda** — Anglican Christianity through the Church of Uganda is the largest reported religious affiliation among the Bagisu.
