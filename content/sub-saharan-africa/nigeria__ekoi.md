## Overview

The Ekoi live in southeastern Nigeria and nearby Cameroon. They speak Ejagham, also called Ekoi. Their material culture includes Nsibidi designs, masks, sculpture, weaving, pottery and body-painting. Ceremonial societies and spoken stories are important in Ekoi life.

## Material culture

Ekoi material culture combines Nsibidi designs with woven fibre, calabash, pottery, wood, skin, feathers and metal.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Ekoi textile and pattern traditions use Nsibidi signs, body-painting, woven fibre, palm leaves and decorated calabashes.

- **Nsibidi designs** (*Nsibidi*) — Nsibidi is an ideographic writing tradition seen on vessels, bowls, dance-staffs, stands and masquerade headdresses.
- **Cursive body-painting** (*Nnimm*) — Nnimm initiates wore cursive body-painting with dresses made from calabash and shells.
- **Geometric fibre mats** — Mats made from vegetable fibre carry geometric patterns.
- **Palm-leaf bags** — Bags were made from palm leaf or vegetable fibre, sometimes with checks, fringes or other patterns.
- **Decorated calabashes** — Calabash vessels and bowls carry geometric decoration and burnt Nsibidi signs.

### Clothing & dress

Ceremonial clothing included painted bodies, calabash and shell dresses, feathers, leather and cowries.

- **Nnimm dress** (*Nnimm*) — Unmarried Nnimm girls wore body-painting, calabash and shell dresses, leather necklaces, feather headdresses and cowrie-fringed wrappers.
- **Nnimm feather** (*Nnimm*) — A single feather at the back of the head was called the Nnimm feather.
- **Jujudräkt** — A museum record describes a Jujudräkt as probably Ekoi and links it with Nyang in the Calabar area.
- **Cloth mask dress** — A wooden mask was attached to a coarse cloth dress.
- **Ceremonial headdresses** — Dance headdresses and head-dresses were made from wood, skin, metal and basketry.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Ekoi houses or settlement architecture.

- **Creation-story hut** — A creation story says that the first man and woman lived in a hut.
- **Sanctuaries** — The sources mention sanctuaries in connection with mural paintings but do not describe their construction.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ekoi everyday objects include pottery, calabashes, lamps, knives, shields, seats and game boards.

- **Pottery vessels** — Museum records include pottery vessels, bowls, a long-necked vessel with loop handles and a pedestal bowl.
- **Calabash bottle** — A calabash bottle has geometric decoration.
- **Calabash ladle** — A calabash bowl or ladle is decorated with burnt Nsibidi signs.
- **Composite knife** — A composite knife has an iron blade and a wooden handle wrapped in copper.
- **Crocodile mancala board** — A crocodile-shaped mancala board has two rows of six holes.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ekoi adornment uses leather, cowries, beads, feathers, monkey bones and animal teeth.

- **Leather necklaces** — Nnimm initiates wore leather necklaces.
- **Cowrie-fringed wrapper** — Nnimm initiates wore wrappers fringed with cowries.
- **Bead strings** — A standing female figure has strings of small black, white and pink beads around the neck.
- **Leopard-teeth ornaments** (*ntui*) — Leopard teeth were used in skull-caps and necklaces associated with ntuis.
- **Leopard-skin staff** — A staff could be bound with leopard skin.

## Music & performance

Ekoi music includes drums, a xylophone and rattles used in social and ritual life.

- **Female-figure drum** (*Tanze*) — The story of Tanze says that a dead king became the body of a female drum after becoming a fish.
- **Four-footed drum** — A large drum has a cylindrical wooden body standing on four mammalian feet.
- **Double drum** — A double drum has two membranes, one of snake skin and one of antelope skin.
- **Trough xylophone** — An incomplete xylophone has a trough crossed by slats, a human figure at one end and a beater.
- **Wicker rattles** — A pair of wicker rattles has gourd bases containing seeds.

## Dance & theatre

Ekoi masquerade uses skin-covered masks and carved headdresses, including masks with male and female sides.

- **Skin-covered masks** — The Ekoi are described as the first in the account to make skin-covered masks and dance in them.
- **Nkwa-mbuk masquerade** (*Nkwa-mbuk*) — The Nkwa-mbuk was a mask-wearing society that performed rituals including human sacrifice and head hunting.
- **Two-sided masks** — Two-sided masks used a darker side for male force and a whiter side for female force.
- **Bird-headed headdress** — Museum records include bird-headed masquerade headdresses.
- **Janus-faced mask** — Museum records include Janus-faced masks.

## Festivals & rituals

Ekoi ritual life centers on initiation, secret societies, ancestors, chiefs, warfare and funerary customs.

- **Mgbe society** (*Mgbe*) — The Mgbe society was for males.
- **Nnimm society** (*Nnimm*) — The Nnimm society was for females, including unmarried young initiates.
- **Victory mask** — After battle, enemy skin was made into a mask to proclaim victory.
- **Ancestor offerings** — When a village received a blessing, people presented offerings to the ancestors.
- **Ngbe return rite** (*ngbe*) — When a ntui died, people entered the jungle to bring back the ngbe as the ntui’s spirit returned to God.

## Foodways

Women traditionally farmed while men hunted, and coastal people also fished.

- **Yam farming** — Women traditionally raised yams.
- **Plantain farming** — Women traditionally raised plantains.
- **Corn farming** — Women traditionally raised corn.
- **Hunting** — Men hunted, and Obassi Nsi was associated with teaching humans to hunt.
- **Fishing** — People in coastal areas practiced fishing.

## Oral tradition & literature

Ekoi oral tradition includes creation stories, animal tales, script legends and the story of Tanze.

- **Creation story** — A creation tale says God made the first man and woman, who lived in a hut and became the ancestors of all people.
- **Eagle and Ox tale** — A story says Eagle hid on Ox’s horns, Fowl revealed him and Eagle then swore to take Fowl’s children.
- **Mermaid script story** — Ekoi folklore says mermaids taught people the Nsibidi script.
- **Tanze story** (*Tanze*) — Tanze tells of an old king who became a fish and later became the body of a female drum.
- **Leopard Society story** — The Tanze story says a man killed the woman who caught the fish and created the Leopard Society.

## Language & religion

Ejagham is an Ekoid language, and Ekoi religion centers on creator gods, ancestors, nature and secret societies.

- **Ejagham language** (*Ejagham*) — Ejagham, also called Ekoi or Jagham, is an Ekoid language of Nigeria and Cameroon.
- **Western dialects** — Western varieties include Bendeghe, Northern and Southern Etung, Ekwe and Akamkpa-Ejagham.
- **Eastern dialects** — Eastern varieties include Keaka and Obang, and Ekin is also listed as an Ejagham dialect.
- **Obassi Osaw** — Obassi Osaw is associated with the sky, moisture, light, droughts and storms.
- **Obassi Nsi** — Obassi Nsi is associated with the earth, nurture, hunting and food-growing.
