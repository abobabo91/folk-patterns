## Overview

The Bayanzi live in the southwest of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Their history is closely tied to river travel, canoe trading, ironworking and colonial encounters at Bolobo. Their largest political unit is the chieftainship, with about 120 chieftainships under three traditional kings. They speak Kiyansi, a Bantu language.

## Material culture

The recorded material culture centers on iron tools and weapons, raffia and palm cloth, body ornaments, musical horns, containers and wooden objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources record raffia and palm-cloth objects but name no textile patterns.

- **Currency cloths** — Six raffia cloths with fringes on all sides were recorded as currency cloths.
- **Embroidered palm-cloth girdle** — A man's girdle was embroidered and made of palm cloth.
- **Girdle ornaments** — Women's girdle ornaments used gourd, wood, animal tooth, vegetable-fibre string and cloth string.

### Clothing & dress

The sources give only a few examples of dress and mourning adornment.

- **Widower's cap** — A widower's cap was made of fibre and feathers.
- **Male mourner's head ornament** — A male mourner wore a fibre circlet on his head.
- **Embroidered girdle** — A man's embroidered girdle was made of palm cloth.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Yanzi buildings or house forms.

- **Elders' upstream district** — Village organization included an upstream district for elders.
- **Young families' downstream district** — Village organization included a downstream district for young families.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded everyday objects include many iron weapons and tools, containers, a fishing net and a head-rest.

- **Machete** — One machete had an iron blade, a brass-covered handle, a wire-bound hilt and a pommel with punched plates.
- **Sword** — A sword had an iron blade and a wooden handle.
- **Knife** — One knife had a broad forged iron blade and a carved wooden hilt.
- **Fishing net** — A fishing net was made of vegetable-fibre cord.
- **Wooden head-rest** — A wooden head-rest had brass edging and nails.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Body adornment included girdles, wooden and fibre ornaments, and a brass neck ring.

- **Women's girdle ornaments** — These ornaments used gourd, wood, animal tooth, vegetable-fibre string and cloth string.
- **Looped wooden girdle ornament** — One woman's girdle ornament was made of wood and had a loop.
- **Brass neck ring** (*halsring*) — A brass halsring from Öfre Kongo had a diameter of 23 centimeters.
- **Widower's cap** — A widower's cap was made of fibre and feathers.

## Music & performance

Music and signalling used horns, pan-pipes, whistles and rowing songs.

- **Antelope-horn trumpet** — A side-blown trumpet was made of antelope horn.
- **Pan-pipes** — Pan-pipes were made of cane, hide and fibre.
- **Wooden side-blown horn** — A wooden side-blown horn was used for war, hunting and funerals.
- **Hunting whistle** — A hunting whistle was made of wood, snake skin and vegetable-fibre string.
- **Canoe rowing song** — Rowers paddled to a monotonous song led by a team leader standing at the back of the canoe.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not document named Yanzi dances or theatre traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not describe a festival calendar or detailed rites, but they connect a horn and mourning ornament with ritual occasions.

- **War, hunting and funeral horn** — A wooden side-blown horn was associated with war, hunting and funerals.
- **Mourner's fibre circlet** — A male mourner wore a fibre circlet.
- **Dormitories** — Adolescents of each sex lived in dormitories, but no connected rites are described.
- **Reincarnated grandparents** — People believed grandparents could be reincarnated in their grandchildren, whether dead or still living.

## Foodways

The sources do not document Yanzi foods, cooking methods or named dishes.

- **Palm-oil plantations** — Historical material mentions palm-oil plantations and forced labour, but does not describe foodways.
- **Gourd purse** — A gourd purse was recorded, but its culinary use was not identified.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources record traditions about origins, migration, rivers, Kong Mukoko and reincarnation but give no story texts.

- **Kimput tradition** (*Kimput*) — Local traditions recorded by Jesuits mention a region called Kimput.
- **Great river tradition** — Local traditions mention a great river.
- **Kong Mukoko tradition** (*Kong Mukoko*) — Local traditions mention the land of Kong Mukoko.
- **Ancestor reincarnation tradition** — A tradition says Bayanzi ancestors or grandparents were reincarnated in their grandchildren.

## Language & religion

Yanzi is a Bantu language with several recorded varieties, while the sources give only limited information about religion.

- **Kiyansi** — Kiyansi, also called Eyans, is the Bantu language spoken by the Bayanzi.
- **West Yansi** (*Mbiem*) — Mbiem is listed as B.85A West Yansi.
- **East Yans** — East Yans is listed as B.85B.
- **Tsong** — Tsong, also called Itsong, Nsong, Ntsuo or Songo, is listed as B.85D.
- **Mpur** — Mpur, also called Mput, is listed as B.85E.
