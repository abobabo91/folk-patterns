## Overview

The Nyakyusa, also called the Sokile, are a Bantu ethnolinguistic group living in the mountains of southern Mbeya Region of Tanzania and the Northern Region of Malawi. They speak Nyakyusa, a member of the Bantu language family. Nyakyusa is spoken around the northern end of Lake Malawi, including northern Karonga District in Malawi and areas near the border with Tanzania. The available sources provide information about the group’s regional distribution, language, traditional god, and several museum objects.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Riabi or lyabi** — A woman’s loin-cloth made of barkcloth from Riabi tree bark. The catalogue identifies the garment by these vernacular names and materials.

**Lufwalilo** — A woman’s waist-band made of barkcloth from Riabi tree bark. It has a geometric pattern in black; the available catalogue record does not provide further information about its use or regional comparisons.

### Clothing & dress

The museum catalogue records a woman’s loin-cloth called *riabi* or *lyabi*, made of barkcloth from Riabi tree bark, and a woman’s *lufwalilo* waist-band made of the same material. The waist-band has a geometric pattern in black.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum catalogue records several objects associated with the displayed material culture. These include spears with iron heads and wooden shafts ornamented with an animal tail or hide, a wooden shield covered in cow hide, a composite bark-cloth-beater, and a wooden staff carved and decorated with metal studs. The bark-cloth-beater includes a section of cow’s horn cross-hatched on one end and a short wooden handle inserted into a hole at the centre.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state only that the traditional god of the Nyakyusa is called *Kyala*, the Nyakyusa-language name for God.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Nyakyusa language source states that the Language Mapping Survey for Northern Malawi contains a folktale, “the Tortoise and the Hare,” in Chinkhonde and other languages of Northern Malawi, together with comparative vocabulary. The source reproduces a sample text from the Bible in Nyakyusa and gives its English translation.

## Language & religion

Nyakyusa, also called Nyakyusa-Ngonde and, in Swahili, Kinyakyusa, is a Bantu language of Tanzania and Malawi. The source states that there is no single name for the language as a whole; its dialects are Nyakyusa, Ngonde or Konde, Kukwe, Mwamba or Lungulu, and Selya, also called Salya or Seria, of Tanzania. In Malawi, Nyakusa and Kyangonde are spoken in the northern part of Karonga District, while Nkhonde is spoken in the centre of the district, including Karonga. Other names include Konde, Nkhonde, Mombe, Nyekyosa, Nyikyusa, Sochile, and Sokili. The language is closely related to Kyangonde and Swahili and is related to Yao, Bemba, Mambwe-lungu, and Chichewa. The traditional god is called *Kyala*.
