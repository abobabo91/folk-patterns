## Overview

The Nyakyusa, also called the Sokile, are a Bantu ethnolinguistic group living in the mountains of southern Mbeya Region of Tanzania and the Northern Region of Malawi. They speak Nyakyusa, a member of the Bantu language family. Nyakyusa is spoken around the northern end of Lake Malawi, including northern Karonga District in Malawi and areas near the border with Tanzania. The available sources provide information about the group’s regional distribution, language, traditional god, and several museum objects, but do not give a population figure, a more specific river or mountain name, or a broader account of Nyakyusa folk-culture significance.

## Language & religion

Nyakyusa, also called Nyakyusa-Ngonde and, in Swahili, Kinyakyusa, is a Bantu language of Tanzania and Malawi. The source states that there is no single name for the language as a whole; its dialects are Nyakyusa, Ngonde or Konde, Kukwe, Mwamba or Lungulu, and Selya, also called Salya or Seria, of Tanzania. In Malawi, Nyakusa and Kyangonde are spoken in the northern part of Karonga District, while Nkhonde is spoken in the centre of the district, including Karonga. Other names include Konde, Nkhonde, Mombe, Nyekyosa, Nyikyusa, Sochile, and Sokili. The language is closely related to Kyangonde and Swahili and is related to Yao, Bemba, Mambwe-lungu, and Chichewa. The traditional god is called *Kyala*. The sources do not describe current religious affiliation, historical scripts, or other spiritual practices.
