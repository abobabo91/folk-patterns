## Overview

The Arma are an ethnic group of the middle Niger River valley, descended from Moroccan invaders of the 16th century. The name was applied by other groups and derives from *ar-rumah*, Arabic for “fusiliers.” The Arma are distinct from, although sometimes confused with, the 3.6 million Zarma people of western Niger. As of 1986, approximately 20,000 people self-identified as Arma in Mali, mostly around Timbuktu, the middle Niger bend and the Inner Niger Delta. The sources describe the Arma’s historical formation through the Moroccan expedition against the Songhai Empire.

## Material culture

### Architecture

After the destruction of the Songhai Empire in 1591, Moroccans settled into Djenné, Gao, Timbuktu and larger towns of the Niger River bend. The sources mention large fortifications.

## Music & performance

The sources identify the Arma as an ethnic group of the middle Niger River valley. They state that the Arma descended from the Moroccan expedition sent in 1590 to conquer the Songhai Empire trade routes, whose members were armed with European-style arquebuses; this is historical information rather than a documented Arma music tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

The historical account states that the expedition destroyed the Songhai Empire in 1591, that its leaders were abandoned by Morocco within a decade, and that some descendants later became identified with minor dynasties.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention contemporary Arabic manuscript finds from Timbuktu and works concerning Timbuktu and the Songhay Empire.

## Language & religion

The sources state that the Arma ethnicity is distinct from the Zarma people, who speak the Zarma language, a member of the Songhay languages. The name Arma derives from the Arabic word *ar-rumah*, meaning “fusiliers.”
