## Overview

The Ngbaka are associated with the Ngbaka language, also called Ngbàkà. The language is spoken by just over a million people in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and is used by several other peoples. Museum records document weapons and two harps linked with Ngbaka. The sources give little information about clothing, buildings, food, rituals, dance, oral tradition, or religion.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Museum records document iron and copper weapons with carved, engraved, wrapped, and bound details.

- **Throwing-knife with wrapped handle** — One iron throwing-knife has a handle wrapped with fibre and cloth.
- **Throwing-knife with engraved blade** — Another throwing-knife has a hilt covered with knotted string and an iron blade with a one-sided engraved pattern.
- **Copper-bound throwing-knife** — Another throwing-knife has an engraved blade and a handle bound in copper.
- **Sickle-pointed sword** — Some swords have double-headed iron blades with sickle-shaped points, geometric decoration, wooden handles, and bindings of iron, brass, or other material.
- **Copper-bladed sword** — One sword has a double-headed copper blade with slanting cuts and circular or half-circular motifs.

## Music & performance

Museum records include two harps, but the sources do not describe their music or performance settings.

- **Rectangular-faced harp** — One wooden harp has a rectangular-faced sound box, a skin membrane, two circular sound holes, a carved head, a curved neck, brass-tack decoration, five peg holes, four remaining pegs, and cane strings.
- **Bow-shaped harp** — Another harp is bow-shaped, with a resonator topped by a carved human head, stylised legs, a skin-covered top, and three broken twisted cotton strings.

## Language & religion

Ngbaka is a Gbaya language with several names and a writing system that marks nasalisation and tones.

- **Ngbaka language** (*Ngbaka*) — Ngbaka is a Gbaya language spoken by just over a million people.
- **Ngbàkà** — Ngbàkà is another name for the Ngbaka language.
- **Ngbaka Gbaya** — Ngbaka Gbaya is one name used to distinguish this language from other languages called Ngbaka.
- **Ngbaka Minagende** — Ngbaka Minagende is another name used to distinguish this language from other languages called Ngbaka.
- **Ngbaka writing system** — The writing system uses a tilde for nasalisation and several diacritics for tones.
