## Overview

The Jiji, also known as *Bajiji* and *Wajiji* in Swahili, are a Bantu ethnic and linguistic group based in Kigoma Region, Tanzania. Traditionally, they were organized into the separate kingdom of *Bujiji*, called *Ujiji* in Swahili. Bujiji formed part of *Buha*, also called *Uha* or Ha territory, together with the kingdoms of Heru, Bushingo, Ruguru, Muhambwe, and Buyungu. Most Jiji people are Muslim, while a minority are Christian.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Cotton cloth** — A British Museum catalogue record describes a cloth made of cotton, oblong in form, with broad borders of narrow dark brown lines at the two sides and narrower stripes in the middle. The warp ends are left loose.

**Barkcloth** — A square of barkcloth is described as coloured iron red.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The British Museum records two spears. One has a well-made socketed iron head, an iron-wire binding, a shaft of yellow wood, and a neatly made socketed iron point at the butt. The other has a well-made leaf-shaped iron head with a long tang, is bound with stout grass, and has a shaped butt.

Another catalogue record describes a small bowl with a neck, carved from solid wood and coloured red outside. The same record groups it with a tobacco-box and box.

### Jewelry & body adornment

A British Museum record describes a neck ornament made from a polished boar’s tusk. It is stained yellow and has four carved holes: two at the centre and two at the thicker end.

## Language & religion

The Jiji are described as a Bantu ethnic and linguistic group. The Jiji article states that, when groups are classified by language rather than race, the Jiji are part of the Ha people because their language is the same. The language article, however, places *Jiji* among language entries removed from Ethnologue in its 15th edition in 2005, with the code [JIJ], within an article about spurious and uncertain languages. Most Jiji people are Muslim, while a minority are Christian.
