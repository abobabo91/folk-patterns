## Overview

The Tongwe are a Bantu ethnic and linguistic group based in Kigoma District and Uvinza District of Kigoma Region, on the eastern shore of Lake Tanganyika in western Tanzania. In 2000, their population was estimated at 31,551. Tongwe belongs to a clade of Bantu languages that includes Bende; the two languages are coded Zone F.10 in Guthrie’s classification. The sources used here provide information about the Tongwe’s location, population, and linguistic classification, together with catalogue descriptions of wooden ladles and an iron adze.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Museum catalogue records document wooden ladles with incised carving on the handle. The records also describe a black stain in parts of the ladles, produced by burning, and an iron adze with a wooden handle.

## Festivals & rituals

No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription in the supplied material concerns the Tongwe.

## Language & religion

Tongwe, also called *Sitongwe*, and Bende, also called *Sibende*, constitute a clade of Bantu languages coded Zone F.10 in Guthrie’s classification. According to Nurse and Philippson (2003), they form a valid node; at 90% lexical similarity, they may be dialects of a single language. The supplied linguistic source states that Bende has four tones: high, low, rising, and falling.
