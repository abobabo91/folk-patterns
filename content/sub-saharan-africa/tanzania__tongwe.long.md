## Overview

The Tongwe are a Bantu ethnic and linguistic group based in Kigoma District and Uvinza District of Kigoma Region, on the eastern shore of Lake Tanganyika in western Tanzania. In 2000, their population was estimated at 31,551. Tongwe belongs to a clade of Bantu languages that includes Bende; the two languages are coded Zone F.10 in Guthrie’s classification. The sources used here provide information about the Tongwe’s location, population, and linguistic classification, together with catalogue descriptions of wooden ladles and an iron adze, but they do not document broader folk-culture practices.

## Language & religion

Tongwe, also called *Sitongwe*, and Bende, also called *Sibende*, constitute a clade of Bantu languages coded Zone F.10 in Guthrie’s classification. According to Nurse and Philippson (2003), they form a valid node; at 90% lexical similarity, they may be dialects of a single language. The supplied linguistic source states that Bende has four tones: high, low, rising, and falling. The sources used do not document Tongwe writing systems, religious traditions, or spiritual practices.
