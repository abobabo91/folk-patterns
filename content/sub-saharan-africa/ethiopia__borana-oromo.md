## Overview

The Boorana, also known as Borana, are one of the two major subgroups of the Oromo people, a Cushitic ethnic group. They primarily inhabit the Borena Zone of the Oromia Region of Ethiopia and the former Eastern Province in northern Kenya, specifically Marsabit County. They speak Boorana, a distinct dialect of the Oromo language, which belongs to the Cushitic branch of the Afro-Asiatic language family. One estimate places about 1,094,000 Boranas in Ethiopia, Kenya, and Somalia; another estimate from 2019 suggests 874,000 in Ethiopia, 210,000 in Kenya, and 10,000 in Somalia. Their folk-cultural importance is especially associated with the Gadaa system, practiced without interruption.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do describe salt extraction from the crater Chew Bet, an extinct volcano in southern Ethiopia. Borana men have extracted salt there for generations using traditional methods, and the salt is sold in the surrounding region.

## Music & performance

They do describe the Gadaa system as covering political, social, economic, and spiritual ways of life. The Borana parliament, called Gumi Gayo, is part of this system, and gatherings are opened with prayer by Fite Qalu or Qalu.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe institutions and ceremonies within the Gadaa system. The Abba Gadaa is anointed at an early age of 1–8 years, and the Abba Gadaa and others of his age pass through an education system lasting forty years before he assumes leadership.

The Borana Defense Force consists of special warriors between 32–40 years of age. They belong to the Dori stage of the Borana system, serve their community for eight years, and meet three times during that period. The first meeting takes place before they leave for different regions of Borana territory; the second takes place after four years; and the final meeting occurs at the end of the eight years, when protective roles and authority are transferred to upcoming members who were previously in Raba.

## Foodways

They state that the Borana are mixed farmers who rear cattle, camels, and goats and plant food crops. Their economy is also connected to grazing land, farming land, and water sources.

## Oral tradition & literature

They mention the phrase “Waaqaa Beekaa,” which is described as referring to unknown things and as a sentence that can be equated to “a Supreme Being knows.”

## Language & religion

The Borana speak Boorana, a distinct dialect of the Oromo language. Oromo belongs to the Cushitic branch of the large Afro-Asiatic language family.

Boranas believe in a supreme Being in charge of worldly affairs and upper dominion and believe in a creator called Waaq. Their supreme spiritual leader is Fite Qalu, who is responsible for prayer at Borana gatherings, including the Borana parliament, and for administration. Fite Qalu appoints Qalu, who administer smaller units; Qalu appoints Jalab, described as a governor; and Jalab appoints Qae, who is in charge of villages. Fite Qalu and Qalu alone have authority to pray at all gatherings.

Many Borana people prefer Islam to Christianity because Islam is described as corresponding in many ways with their tradition. The sources also state that some Boranas are Muslims and others are Christians, while all value the Gadaa system as an asset.
