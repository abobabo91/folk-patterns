## Overview

The Kami are a Bantu, matrilineal ethnic group from northern Morogoro Region of Tanzania, specifically indigenous to Morogoro District. Most Kami people live in the locality of Mikese, east of Morogoro City, while Mkunga Mhola, Dete, and (Lukonde) Koo are also locations where many Kami reside. In 2000, the Kami population was estimated to be 16,411. Most Kami are Muslim. Their language is a Bantu language with substantial lexical similarities to Kutu, Kwere, Zaramo, Doe, and Luguru.

## Language & religion

Kami is a Bantu language with substantial lexical similarities to Kutu, Kwere, Zaramo, Doe, and Luguru. It is endangered in Tanzania. A reported number of 5,518 Kami speakers does not indicate how proficient speakers are, and field studies conducted during trips in 2008, 2009, 2014, and 2016 found far fewer fully competent speakers in the area. No children or teenagers were found to be skilled in Kami. The youngest person interviewed, who was in his forties at the time, could understand Kami but could not speak it. Swahili is increasingly common as the only language allowed alongside English in government, media, educational institutions, and places of worship, while Luguru is described as the main danger to Kami’s survival. Most Kami are Muslim.

The sources also describe another, unrelated Kami language: a Nupoid language spoken in Niger State, Nigeria, in Ebo town, south of Lapai. That language has at most 500 speakers and is closely related to Gupa and Dibo. A fishing group known as Kede, Kyadya, Kiadia, or Kyedye speaks the same language.
