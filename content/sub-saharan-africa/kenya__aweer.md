## Overview

The Aweer, also known as the Boni, Waboni, Wa-Boni, and Sanye, are a Cushitic ethnic group inhabiting Kenya’s Coast Province in southeastern Kenya, with some members in southern Somalia. They have traditionally been indigenous foragers, subsisting on hunting, gathering, and collecting honey. In Kenya they have been concentrated particularly in the Lamu and Tana River districts and are also found near the Boni National Reserve and Dodori National Reserve. The 2019 Kenyan population census recorded around 20,103 Aweer in Kenya. Their language belongs to the Cushitic branch of the Afro-Asiatic family. Their importance for folk culture lies in their continuing relationship with forest-based foraging, wild honey, plants, building materials, and bush meat, despite major changes to their way of life.

## Material culture

### Architecture

 They state that the Aweer traditionally lived in dwellings along the Kenyan coast and that many were later settled in villages along the Hindi-Kiunga Road between the Boni National Reserve and the Dodori National Reserve.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They mention building materials gathered from nearby forests.

## Festivals & rituals

 The sources state that the Aweer historically practised traditional faiths such as Waaqism, although most have today adopted Islam.

## Foodways

Aweer traditional subsistence is described as hunting, gathering, and collecting honey. In Kenya, Aweer communities have also continued to collect wild honey, plants for traditional medicine and building materials, and bush meat to supplement their diets. They have been encouraged to adopt farming as their main livelihood.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They do report oral traditions that Aweer communities once had cattle and became foragers after losing them, possibly together with their former social status. The linguistic literature presents competing accounts of Aweer origins and language history, including proposals connecting them with earlier hunter-gatherer populations, Eastern Sam pastoralists, Garre-speakers, and Dahaloan communities.

## Language & religion

Aweer, also known as Boni, Bon, Bonta, or Aweera, is a Cushitic language of Eastern Kenya and belongs to the Afro-Asiatic family. The Aweer language has similarities with Garre, although Aweer and Garre speakers are described as distinct in culture and appearance. According to Ethnologue, there are around 8,000 speakers of Aweer, and most speakers are bilingual in the languages of immediate neighbours; about 20% speak only Aweer. The language is believed to be threatened by extinction. The sources state that Aweer historically practised Waaqism and that most have today adopted Islam.
