## Overview

The Aro people, or Aros, are an Igbo group originating from the Arochukwu kingdom in present-day Abia state, Nigeria. They are also found in about 250 other settlements, mostly in Southeastern Nigeria and adjacent areas. Because of their location, mixed origins, culture, and dialect, they are classified as Eastern or Cross River Igbos. The Aro Confederacy became a regional power in the Niger Delta and Southeastern Nigeria during the 18th and 19th centuries. Its expansion was supported by trade, migration, military alliances, and the influence of Chukwu Abiama through Aro priests. Aro society is associated with the Ekpe society, Nsibidi, Uri, masquerade activity, textile use, and warrior dance.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**George cloth** — The Aros were known for wearing the popular “George” cloth.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no textile motifs.

### Clothing & dress

The sources mention the popular “George” cloth as clothing worn by the Aros.

### Architecture

They mention the Chukwu Abiama Temple, which was mediated by the Aro priesthood and was destroyed during the British invasion, but provide no architectural description.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The writing system Uri occurred mostly in the form of body art.

## Music & performance

The sources identify the **Ikperikpe warrior dance**, which was very famous among warriors in the old days and continues to be used. They also describe the **Ekeleke masquerade activity** as important in Aro settlements; it was brought from the Aros in the western Niger Delta and eventually spread to the Oguta area. The Ekpe society was a sacred, highly religious, and judicial society that took a major part in Aro society.

## Dance & theatre

The **Ekeleke masquerade activity** was important in Aro settlements. According to the source, it originated among Aros in the western Niger Delta and later spread to the Oguta area. The **Ikperikpe warrior dance** was famous among warriors in the old days and continues to be used.

## Festivals & rituals

They describe the Chukwu Abiama Temple and the Aro priesthood as important religious institutions that influenced neighbors and allies before the British invasion and destruction of the temple and priesthood. The Ekpe society was sacred, highly religious, and judicial.

## Foodways

The sources state that palm oil and the slave trade were popular in the hinterland during the period preceding the expansion of Aro economic influence.

## Oral tradition & literature

They identify Nsibidi as a writing system based on secret societies such as Ekpe, and Uri as another writing system occurring mostly in the form of body art.

## Language & religion

The Aro people are described as an Igbo group and, because of their location, mixed origins, culture, and dialect, as Eastern or Cross River Igbos. Their god, Chukwu Abiama, was a key factor in establishing the Aro Confederacy as a regional power in the Niger Delta and Southeastern Nigeria during the 18th and 19th centuries. The Chukwu Abiama Temple was mediated by the Aro priesthood. The Ekpe society was a sacred, highly religious, and judicial institution. The sources also mention Nsibidi and Uri as writing systems.
