## Overview

The Nzema are an ethnic group numbering about 328,700 people. About 262,000 live in southwestern Ghana and 66,700 in the southeast of Côte d’Ivoire. In Ghana, their area includes Nzema East Municipal, also known as Evalue Gwira; Ellembele; and Nzema West, also known as Jomoro. Their language, Nzema, is also known as Nzima or Appolo. It is an Akan language, one of several Bia languages, spoken in southwestern Ghana and southeastern Ivory Coast. The Nzema are mostly farmers and maintain a matrilineal kinship system in which descent and property pass through maternal lines. Their documented folk-culture includes a traditional calendar, the annual Kundum Festival, ritual drumming, singing, dancing, and satirical *avudewene* songs.

## Music & performance

Music and performance are documented particularly in connection with the Kundum Festival. Ritual drumming and singing take place during the festival, alongside dancing. The festival is held annually throughout the Ahanta and Nzema areas, and its beginning is coordinated with the harvest period. Local communities determine when it starts. The festival begins in the easternmost part of Ahanta and advances southwestward together with the harvest period.

The Kundum Festival lasts four weeks in the description provided by the source. Its ritual drumming, singing, and dancing are understood as means by which the community expels devils and protects its good fortune. The festival is also the principal occasion for the performance of satirical *avudewene* songs by young men.

## Festivals & rituals

The principal documented Nzema festival is the annual Kundum Festival, held throughout the Ahanta and Nzema areas. Its beginning is timed to coincide with the harvest period, so local communities determine the starting point. The festival begins in the easternmost part of Ahanta and proceeds southwestward with the harvest period.

Ritual drumming, singing, and dancing continue for four weeks. These activities are considered a way for the community to expel devils and protect its good fortune. The festival is also the main occasion for young men to perform satirical *avudewene* songs. The sources describe the festival’s relationship to the harvest and its ritual and musical activities.

The Nzema traditional calendar orders days in cycles of seven, which follow one another in a three-week cycle.

## Language & religion

Nzema, also known as Nzima or Appolo, is an Akan language spoken by the Nzema people of southwestern Ghana and southeastern Ivory Coast. It is one of several Bia languages, has had considerable influence from Twi-Fante, is partially intelligible with Jwira-Pepesa, and is closely related to Baoulé. The source names notable towns in Nzemaland as Bonyere, Nkroful, Half Assini, Axim, Eikwe, Baku, Atuabo, Beyin, Essiama, and Anokyi.

The sources provide phonological information, including ten vowels, eight of which may be nasalized. Religious information is limited to the Kundum Festival, which is described as religious and associated with expelling devils and protecting good fortune.
