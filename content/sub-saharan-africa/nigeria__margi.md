## Overview

The Margi speak Margi, a Chadic language known for having one of the largest consonant inventories of any language. They live across Nigeria, Cameroon, and Chad. The surviving record of their material culture focuses on ironwork—weapons, edged tools, and ornaments—and beadwork worn by women. Named regional varieties include Madube, Izge, Lassa, Gulak, Damboa, Mulgwai, Uba, and Sukur.

## Material culture

Iron is the main material, worked with bamboo, wood, hide, and leather into weapons, tools, and ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

No weaving or dyeing tradition is documented; ornament instead uses prepared skin and string.

- **Beaded aprons** — Women's aprons made of beads on a leather or string foundation, some with iron rattles attached.
- **Plaited leather binding** — Throwing-knife handles and dagger hilts are covered in plaited leather, serving as both grip and decoration.
- **Incised sheath panels** — Daggers and swords have sheaths decorated with central grooves, patterns, or twisted string bindings.

### Clothing & dress

Only women's garments are documented in the sources.

- **Beaded aprons** — Two types recorded: one on a leather foundation with iron rattles, one on a string foundation.

### Architecture

No information is available on Margi house form, materials, or settlement layout.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Iron weapons and tools dominate the material record, made in combination with bamboo, wood, hide, and leather.

- **Arrows** — Narrow iron heads with barbs or foliate blade shapes, mounted on bamboo shafts and fletched with feathers, carried in sets of four, eight, thirteen, or eighteen.
- **Quivers** — Containers for arrows made of skin alone or wood with skin covering at the foot.
- **Bow** — Wooden bow strung with twisted leather.
- **Throwing-knives** — Composite weapons with iron blades and plaited leather grips, sometimes in red leather with a loop at the end.
- **Arm-dagger and sword** — Iron bladed weapons; the arm-dagger has an iron hilt and comes in a skin sheath, the sword in a composite sheath.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Iron and beads are the main adornment materials, often combined with leather.

- **Iron rattles** — Iron pieces strung on a leather thong or sewn onto beaded aprons; they sound when the wearer moves.
- **Beaded ornaments** — Beads mounted on leather or string as adornment for women.

## Music & performance

One instrument is documented; women's sound-making ornaments also serve as percussion.

- **Double-headed drum** — Circular wooden body with skin membranes at both ends.
- **Iron rattles** — Women's dance-rattles strung on leather or sewn to aprons; they function as body-worn percussion.

## Dance & theatre

No dance or dramatic tradition is documented; only material evidence exists in women's dance-rattles.

- **Iron dance-rattles** — Sound-making ornaments worn by women during movement.

## Festivals & rituals

No information is available on the Margi festival calendar or life-cycle rites.

## Foodways

No information is documented on Margi food, cooking, or dietary practices.

## Oral tradition & literature

No folktales, epics, or proverbs are recorded; the main documentation is linguistic.

- **Grammar of the Margi Language** — Published in 1963 by C. Hoffmann, the reference grammar that makes Margi the best described language in its branch.
- **Vowel system study** — 1987 linguistic study of Margi's distinctive two-vowel system.

## Language & religion

Margi is a Chadic language with an unusually large consonant inventory and a simplified vowel system; religion is not documented.

- **Margi language** (*Marghi, Margi Central*) — A Chadic language of the Afroasiatic family spoken in Nigeria, Cameroon, and Chad.
- **Named varieties** — Madube, Izge, Lassa, Gulak, Damboa, Mulgwai, Uba, and Sukur are recognized kinds of Margi; Marghi South and Putai are closely related.
- **Consonant inventory** — The 1963 grammar lists 84 consonantal phonemes (now analyzed as clusters or labialised consonants, reducing the count to 54–66).
- **Vowel system** — Only two phonemic vowels in native vocabulary: /ɨ/ and /a/, with /ɛ/ and /o/ appearing only in loan words.
- **Glottalised consonants** — Described as creaky-voiced or implosive sounds, similar to those in Hausa.

## Glossary

- *Margi (Marghi)* — A Chadic language spoken in Nigeria, Cameroon, and Chad
- *Chadic* — A branch of the Afroasiatic language family
- *Afroasiatic* — A large language family spanning Africa and the Middle East
- *Marghi South* — A closely related language sometimes treated as a Margi dialect
- *Putai* — A closely related language sometimes treated as a Margi dialect
- *Phonemic vowels* — Vowel sounds that distinguish word meanings in a language
- *Consonant inventory* — The set of consonant sounds in a language
- *Glottalised consonants* — Consonants produced with a constricted throat or creaky voice
- *Implosive* — A consonant produced by drawing air inward rather than outward
- *Beaded apron* — Women's garment made of beads on a leather or string foundation
- *Iron rattle* — Ornamental percussion made of iron pieces strung on leather, worn by women
