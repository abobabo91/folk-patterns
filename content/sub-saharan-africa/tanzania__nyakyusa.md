## Overview

The Nyakyusa live in the mountains of southern Tanzania and northern Malawi. They speak Nyakyusa and related dialects. Museum records show barkcloth garments and several weapons and tools. The sources give little information about architecture, food, music, dance, rituals, or wider folk-culture traditions.

## Material culture

The documented material culture centers on barkcloth clothing, weapons, shields, and a bark-cloth-beater.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The documented textile tradition uses barkcloth for women’s garments, including one piece with a black geometric pattern.

- **Woman’s loin-cloth** (*Riabi or lyabi*) — This woman’s loin-cloth is made from barkcloth taken from Riabi tree bark.
- **Woman’s waist-band** (*Lufwalilo*) — This barkcloth waist-band has a geometric pattern in black.

### Clothing & dress

- **The Tortoise and the Hare** — The Language Mapping Survey for Northern Malawi contains this folktale in Chinkhonde and other northern Malawi languages, with comparative vocabulary.

## Language & religion

Nyakyusa is a Bantu language with several dialect names in Tanzania and Malawi, and its traditional god is Kyala.

- **Nyakyusa language** (*Nyakyusa, Nyakyusa-Ngonde, or Kinyakyusa*) — Nyakyusa is spoken in Tanzania and Malawi and has no single name for the language as a whole.
- **Tanzanian dialects** (*Nyakyusa, Ngonde, Kukwe, Mwamba, and Selya*) — The source lists these dialects, with Ngonde also called Konde, Mwamba also called Lungulu, and Selya also called Salya or Seria.
- **Malawian varieties** (*Nyakusa, Kyangonde, and Nkhonde*) — Nyakusa and Kyangonde are spoken in northern Karonga District, while Nkhonde is spoken in the centre, including Karonga.
- **Related languages** (*Kyangonde and Swahili*) — Nyakyusa is closely related to Kyangonde and Swahili and is also related to Yao, Bemba, Mambwe-lungu, and Chichewa.
- **Traditional god** (*Kyala*) — Kyala is the traditional god named in the sources.
