## Overview

The Boki people, also known as Bokyi and Nki, are a tribe found in Cross River State, Nigeria. They are predominantly farmers and are also forest-dependent. Their language, Bokyi, is a regionally important Bendi language spoken by the Bokyi people of northern Cross River State. In 1979, the Bokyi population exceeded 190,000.

## Language & religion

Bokyi, also called Boki, Nfua, Nki, Okii, Osikom, Osukam, Uki, and Vaaneroki, is a regionally important Bendi language spoken by the Bokyi people of northern Cross River State, Nigeria. It is ranked amongst the first fifteen languages of the about 520 living languages in Nigeria, and it has a few thousand speakers in Cameroon. Major dialects include Abu, also called Abo and Baswo; Irruan; Osokom, also called Okundi; and Wula. In 2026, Roger Blench determined Abu to be a separate Bendi language that is not particularly closely related to Bokyi.
