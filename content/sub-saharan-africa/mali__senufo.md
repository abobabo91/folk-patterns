## Overview

The Senufo live across parts of Ivory Coast, Mali, Burkina Faso, and Ghana. They are mainly farmers who grow corn, millet, yams, peanuts, cotton, and cash crops. Their culture is known for music, wood carving, masks, figurines, textiles, and other crafts. Poro and Sandogo are important spiritual and social institutions.

## Material culture

Senufo material culture centers on painted and woven textiles, carved wood, brass jewelry, masks, musical instruments, and useful household objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Senufo textiles include painted cloth from Korhogo and woven cloth made from narrow cotton strips with dyed bands and added patterns.

- **Painted Korhogo cloth** (*Fila; filafani wii*) — This cotton cloth has painted motifs that were linked with hunters, ritual dancers, and initiated Senufo people.
- **Korhogo cloth** — This painted textile came from the village of Korhogo, and its motifs were connected with supernatural powers and people.
- **Indigo-dyed wrapper** — This wrapper has eight sewn cotton strips, indigo bands, a chequerboard layout, added weft patterns, and short fringes.
- **Figurative cloth** — This cloth has ten sewn strips painted with figures in mud and natural dye.
- **Patterned cotton cloth** — This textile combines white cotton, indigo stripes, orange and grey inserts, and pattern blocks.

### Clothing & dress

The profile gives little detail about everyday dress, but painted Korhogo textiles once marked hunters, ritual dancers, and initiated Senufo people.

- **Reserved painted textiles** — Korhogo painted textile motifs were formerly reserved for hunters, ritual dancers, and initiated Senufo people.
- **Wildlife-like brass jewelry** — Senufo people wear specially made brass jewelry that can imitate wildlife.

### Architecture

Senufo villages use small mud-brick homes with roof styles suited to wet or dry regions.

- **Mud-brick home** — Senufo villages consist of small homes made from mud brick.
- **Thatched roof** — Thatched roofs are common in rainy southern communities.
- **Flat roof** — Flat roofs are common in the dry, desert-like north.
- **Carved Senufo door** — A carved door from Ivory Coast has low relief, pivot projections, a wooden lock, and figures of a turtle, two toucan-like birds, and a snake.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Senufo artisans make carved wooden household, workshop, and ritual objects, alongside work in metal, pottery, and leather.

- **Three-legged wooden pot** — This wooden pot has three legs, carved lines, diamond shapes, and a lid topped by a bird.
- **Wooden door lock** — This lock has movable catches and a metal key.
- **Animal-head loom pulley** — Wooden loom pulleys are carved with animal heads.
- **Horse-shaped seat** — One carved wooden seat is shaped like a horse.
- **Dance staff** — A wooden dance staff has an animal head.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Senufo adornment includes wildlife-like brass jewelry, scarification, and hairstyles with carved animal forms.

- **Wildlife-like brass jewelry** — Senufo brass jewelry includes forms that imitate wildlife.
- **Scarification** — Museum figures show scarification on the face, cheeks, breasts, abdomen, back, and around the navel.
- **Bird-head hairstyle** — One seated female figure has a hairstyle with a stylized bird head.

## Music & performance

Senufo musicians are well known, and their instruments include wind, stringed, and percussive types.

- **Standing wooden drum** — This drum has a hide head, four feet, six wedges for tightening the skin, and reliefs of a woman, snakes, a turtle, a lizard, and slave bracelets.
- **Carved relief drum** — Another drum has a carved wooden body, hide top, wooden pegs, hide thongs, and reliefs of masks, animals, a flute player, and manacles.
- **Composite horn** — This wind instrument is made from antelope horn with a wooden end and animal fur and skin.
- **Musicians** — Musicians form a recognized occupational group within Senufo society.

## Dance & theatre

Senufo dance uses carved masks, staffs, and figures, especially in ritual and funeral settings.

- **Hyaena-head dance mask** — This mask has antelope horns and a bird on the forehead.
- **Spotted quadruped dance mask** — This dance mask represents a spotted four-legged animal.
- **Baboon-head dance mask** — This mask has a baboon head and is colored white and red.
- **Animal-head masks** — Senufo artists carve masks with animal heads and human faces.
- **Funeral dance sculptures** (*tyekpa*) — Carved figures are carried on dancers' heads during a tyekpa funeral ceremony.

## Festivals & rituals

Senufo ritual life centers on secret societies, initiation, divination, sorcery, and relationships with ancestors.

- **Poro society** (*Poro*) — This male secret society holds elaborate initiation rituals in a sacred forest and helps maintain a good relationship between living people and ancestors.
- **Sandogo divination society** (*Sandogo*) — Sandogo governs divination and is generally associated with women, though some men inherit the profession through the mother's line.
- **Wambele sorcery society** (*Wambele*) — Wambele performs sorcery and rituals.
- **Tyekpa rituals** (*Tyekpa*) — Tyekpa performs sorcery and rituals, including a funeral ceremony with carved dance figures.
- **Age-grade advancement figures** — Caryatid figures are used in ceremonies connected with advancement through the age-grade cycle.

## Foodways

The Senufo are mainly farmers who grow staple crops, cotton, and cash crops.

- **Corn farming** — Corn is one of the crops cultivated by the Senufo.
- **Millet farming** — Millet is one of the crops cultivated by the Senufo.
- **Yam farming** — Yams are one of the crops cultivated by the Senufo.
- **Peanut farming** — Peanuts are one of the crops cultivated by the Senufo.
- **Cotton and cash crops** — The Senufo grow cotton and other cash crops for the international market.

## Oral tradition & literature

## Language & religion

Senufo languages form their own branch of Niger–Congo, while Senufo religion centers on spirits, ancestors, and a Supreme Being.

- **Senufic languages** (*Senufic*) — About fifteen Senufo languages are spoken across northern Ivory Coast, southern Mali, and southwestern Burkina Faso.
- **Nafaanra** — Nafaanra is spoken in western Ghana.
- **Atlantic–Congo branch** (*Atlantic–Congo*) — Senufo languages form a branch of the Atlantic–Congo sub-family of Niger–Congo languages.
- **Ancient Mother** (*Maleeo; Katieleo*) — The Supreme Being is understood through a female Ancient Mother called Maleeo or Katieleo.
- **Creator God** (*Kolotyolo; Koulotiolo*) — The male Creator God is called Kolotyolo or Koulotiolo.
