## Overview

The Zafimaniry live in cold, wet highlands of Madagascar where they have built a distinctive culture centred on wood. Their houses are built entirely of carved wood without nails, and this craft—once common across Madagascar—now survives mainly here. They grow maize and beans using slash-and-burn cultivation, and their culture emphasizes monogamous marriage, family stability, and respect for ancestors. Their woodworking knowledge is inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage.

## Material culture

Nearly all Zafimaniry material culture is made of wood, with some objects combining wood with fibre, horn, or iron.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Zafimaniry textiles are chiefly burial cloths woven from raffia and vegetable fibre, along with traditional beaten bark cloth.

- **Burial cloth** (*lambamena*) — A rectangle of brown raffia with dark brown and beige stripes running down the centre and a fringe at each short side.
- **Burial shawls** — Woven vegetable-fibre cloths striped along the length in various colour combinations, fringed at both ends.
- **Bark cloth** — Traditional clothing was made of beaten bark cloth.
- **Geometric patterns** — Houses and wooden household items are decorated with symbolic geometric patterns blending Arab and Austronesian influences.

### Clothing & dress

Little is recorded about daily Zafimaniry dress; traditional clothing was made of beaten bark cloth.

- **Bark cloth garments** — Traditional daily clothing was made of beaten bark cloth.
- **Vegetable-fibre hat** — A hat made of plaited vegetable fibre.
- **Burial cloths** — Raffia and vegetable-fibre cloths serve as wrappings for the dead.

### Architecture

Zafimaniry houses are built entirely of wood without nails, with carved decoration expressing the value placed on monogamous marriage and family stability.

- **Wooden house** — Structures built entirely of carved wood without nails, with structural members serving as the decorated surface.
- **Carved wooden window** (*varankoaka*) — A window carved with geometric and symbolic designs.
- **Central house post** — A wooden post carved with designs on its front face, often in two sections.
- **Bird figures** — Wooden bird carvings used as fittings for house poles.
- **Over 20 tree species** — Woodworkers draw on many tree species, each chosen for its properties and uses.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Zafimaniry domestic life is met almost entirely in carved wood; no ceramics are recorded.

- **Honey pots** — Carved wooden vessels, some with lids, made to hold wild honey gathered from the surrounding forest.
- **Wooden bowls** — Carved wooden bowls for domestic use.
- **Mortar and stool** — Carved wooden objects for food preparation and seating.
- **Folding chair** — A wooden seat and back made as separate pieces that fold together.
- **Weapons** — A wooden and iron sword, wooden sheath, and wood-and-fibre dart; blowpipes and arrows are hardened by fire.

### Jewelry & body adornment

- **Wooden comb** — The only personal adornment object recorded.

## Music & performance

The Zafimaniry are documented to play one named instrument, the lokanga, whose shape is symbolic of a coffin.

- **String instrument** (*lokanga*) — Much favoured among the Zafimaniry, its coffin-like shape links it to the group's association of everyday objects with death and ancestry.

## Dance & theatre

Ritualized wrestling matches at funerals are the only performance tradition recorded; they often resemble a form of dance.

- **Funeral wrestling** — Ritualized wrestling matches that take place at Zafimaniry funeral ceremonies and often resemble dance.

## Festivals & rituals

Zafimaniry ritual life centres on life-cycle events and the veneration of ancestors rather than a fixed calendar.

- **Circumcision** — A boy to be circumcised is ritually measured for his coffin before the operation, linking the rite to death.
- **Funeral ceremonies** — The dead are entombed in wooden tombs placed in the forest, and ceremonies include ritualized wrestling matches.
- **Standing stones** — Tall standing stones are erected to commemorate an ancestor or an event in that ancestor's life.
- **Monogamous marriage** — Central to social stability; the house is built and decorated to express this value.
- **Birth of children** — This event fixes a relationship; parents are judged more successful the more children they raise.

## Foodways

Maize, adopted around 200 years ago, is the staple, grown with beans using slash-and-burn cultivation; wild forest products, especially honey, supplement cultivated crops.

- **Slash-and-burn cultivation** (*tavy*) — Fields are burned at the start of the planting season and cultivated four to ten years until fertility fails, then left fallow about five years.
- **Maize and beans** — The primary staple crops, grown together in tavy fields.
- **Potatoes and sweet potatoes** — Grown as fields weaken after several years of maize and beans.
- **Wild honey** — Gathered from the surrounding forest and the most important wild food supplement.
- **Rice terraces** — Recently adopted due to land exhaustion and climate warming; built by hired Betsileo specialists.

## Oral tradition & literature

Oral tradition appears not as named repertoires but as the manner of elders who speak with reference to proverbs and ancestral respect.

- **Elders** (*raiamandreny*) — Older family members respected as leaders who model harmonious communal living through calm, polite speech with reference to proverbs and ancestors.
- **Proverbs** — Referenced by elders in their speech as a guide to harmonious living.

## Language & religion

The Zafimaniry speak a Malagasy dialect nearly identical to Betsileo; their original religion centred on ancestor veneration and an indifferent cosmic force called Zanahary.

- **Malagasy language** — A Malayo-Polynesian language within the Barito group of southern Borneo.
- **Ancestor veneration** — The centre of Zafimaniry belief and practice.
- **Cosmic force** (*Zanahary*) — An indifferent, ever-present force closer to fate, manifested through the forces of nature, rendered 'God' by European missionaries.
- **Protestantism** — Conversion by the London Missionary Society from the late 1800s among free Zafimaniry villages.
- **Catholicism** — Jesuit missionaries converted especially among the recently freed slave class from the mid-1930s onward.

## Glossary

- *lambamena* — Brown raffia burial cloth with striped centre and fringed ends
- *tavy* — Slash-and-burn cultivation; fields burned at planting season
- *varankoaka* — Carved wooden window with geometric and symbolic designs
- *raiamandreny* — Elders; respected family leaders who speak calmly with reference to proverbs and ancestors
- *lokanga* — String instrument shaped like a coffin, favoured by the Zafimaniry
- *Zanahary* — Indifferent, ever-present cosmic force closer to fate than a personal god
- *Betsileo* — Ethnic group of which the Zafimaniry are a sub-group; neighbour to the west
- *Malagasy* — Malayo-Polynesian language descended from Barito languages of southern Borneo
- *London Missionary Society* — Christian missionaries who converted free Zafimaniry villages to Protestantism from the late 1800s
- *Ambositra* — Town to the northwest of the Zafimaniry highlands
- *Wooden tomb* — Forest structure in which the Zafimaniry place their dead
