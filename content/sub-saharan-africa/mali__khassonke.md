## Overview

The Khassonké are an ethnic group of Mali’s Kayes Region. They are descendants of the Fula and Malinké Khasso kingdoms. Their language, Khassonke/Xaasongaxango, belongs to the Manding language group and is similar to Bambara. The sources identify the Khassonké through their historical relationship to the Khasso kingdoms, their language, and their musical instruments.

## Music & performance

The Khassonké’s traditional musical instruments include the *dundunba*, a large cylindrical drum with two skins; the *jingò*, a small cylindrical drum with two skins; the *tantanwò*, a small drum; and the *tamandinwo*, an aisselle drum. The sources also mention lutes, harps, and hunters’ whistles.

## Language & religion

Khassonké is associated with the Khassonke/Xaasongaxango language, also called Xasonga or Western Maninka (Malinke). It is a Manding language spoken by the Khassonké and Malinke of western Mali and by the Malinke of eastern Senegal. Kassonke is an official language in Mali. Western and Eastern Maninka are described as 90% mutually intelligible, while remaining distinct from the Mandinka (Malinke) of southern Senegal, which is a national language there.
