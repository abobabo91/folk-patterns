## Overview

The Kaguru live in Ukagura, a homeland of mountains, plateau and lowlands. Most work in small-scale agriculture, with livestock especially important on the plateau. Their society has about one hundred matrilineal clans linked to land. Beadwork, initiation rites and ancestral religion are important parts of the profile.

## Material culture

Kaguru material culture includes colourful glass beadwork, basketry, carved wood, iron smelting and fortified settlements.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Glass beads were used on fibre aprons, waist bands, headwear and arm bands in bright patterns.

- **Glass bead apron** — Fibre aprons used black, white, red and other coloured beads in triangular patterns.
- **Beadwork waist band** — A waist band used white, pink, red, black and green glass beads with vegetable fibre.
- **Glass bead head dress** — Head dresses used fibre-stringed beads in bands, rectangles and streamers ending in cowrie shells or large red beads.
- **Glass bead head band** — A head band used blue, white and pink beads in a patterned design.
- **Glass bead arm band** — Arm bands used fibre-stringed beads in black, red, white, green and pink bands.

### Clothing & dress

Women wore skirts and men wore toga-like garments, while beadwork added colour and adornment.

- **Women’s skirt** — Women wore skirts all the time.
- **Men’s toga-like garment** — Men wore a toga-like garment even when showering so they would not appear naked.
- **Beaded apron** — Beaded aprons were among the recorded forms of Kaguru dress.
- **Beaded head dress** — Beaded head dresses included streamers ending in cowrie shells or large red beads.

### Architecture

Highland settlements were large and fortified, and houses included huts with mud-plastered roofs.

- **Fortified highland settlement** (*Ukagura*) — Ukagura had large fortified settlements in steep highland terrain with valleys useful for escaping raids.
- **Mud-plastered hut** — A museum model shows a wooden hut with mud plaster on the roof.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Kaguru material objects included iron-smelting work, fibre trays and carved wooden tools.

- **Iron smelting** (*Ukagura*) — Iron smelting took place in Ukagura and required much charcoal.
- **Winnowing tray** — Winnowing trays were made from basketry or plaited vegetable fibre with cane or wooden rims.
- **Ornamented wooden spoon** — A wooden spoon had burnt and incised decoration.
- **Carved antelope stick** — A wooden stick had a head carved to represent an antelope.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Glass beads, cowrie shells, red ocher and body practices formed the main recorded adornments.

- **Cowrie-shell bead streamer** — Some head-dress streamers ended in cowrie shells.
- **Red-bead streamer** — Some head-dress streamers ended in large red beads.
- **Red ocher beauty product** — Red ocher was used for beauty products.
- **Ear piercing** — Ear piercing was among the practices condemned by Christian missionaries.

## Music & performance

Songs, music, singing and dancing were part of female initiation and were also opposed by missionaries.

- **Female initiation songs** — Women sang songs inside the initiation house during the seclusion of a girl.
- **Obscene songs** — Women sang obscene songs during female initiation.
- **Kaguru musical instrument** — A recorded instrument had a flat wood board, a bowed wood stick and a split cane striker or beater.

## Dance & theatre

Women danced during female initiation as part of a girl’s seclusion and preparation for marriage.

- **Initiation dancing** — Women danced in the initiation house after singing and joking.

## Festivals & rituals

Kaguru rituals focused on clans, land, initiation, marriage, death and relations with ancestral ghosts.

- **Land-renewal clan ritual** — Each matrilineal clan held yearly rituals to revitalize its land.
- **Male initiation** — At puberty, a boy stayed in a bush camp until he recovered, symbolizing the death of childhood and rebirth as an adult.
- **Female initiation** — Around age 14, a girl was secluded in a house for weeks or months and expected to become beautiful, fat and pale.
- **Ancestral grave offering** (*ghostland*) — Wheat flour and the blood of sacrificial animals were poured on the graves of prominent ancestral ghosts.
- **Ghost placation rite** — Rites were held to placate, cool or quiet angry, disturbed or confused ghosts.

## Foodways

Kaguru foodways combine farming, livestock keeping and mutual help during difficult times.

- **Highland farming** — People in the cool, rainy highlands produced vegetables, some rice, millet and plantains, but no maize.
- **Plateau livestock keeping** — The plateau had the largest livestock holdings.
- **Shared harvests** — During lean times, people could share harvests with one another.
- **Borrowed livestock** — People could borrow livestock through social relationships.
- **Brewing** — Brewing could provide income for an older childless woman.

## Oral tradition & literature

The profile records songs and ancestral remembrance but no named folktale or literary tradition.

- **Kaguru songs** (*Kaguru*) — Kaguru songs and music were among the customs condemned by Christian missionaries.
- **Ancestral name-calling** — Ancestral ghosts were believed to want people to remember them and call out their names.

## Language & religion

Kaguru is a Bantu language, and Kaguru religious life connected the living, the dead and future generations.

- **Kaguru language** (*Kaguru*) — Kaguru is a Bantu language closely related to Gogo and Zaramo but not intelligible with other languages.
- **Kagulu name** (*Kagulu*) — Kagulu is another name for the Kaguru language and people.
- **Matrilineal descent** — Kaguru descent and inheritance passed through the female line.
- **Rainstones** — Earlier Kaguru religious practice included rainstones.
- **Magical medicines** — Earlier Kaguru religious practice included magical medicines.

## Glossary

- *Kaguru* — The ethnic group and its language
- *Kagulu* — Another name for Kaguru
- *Ukagura* — The Kaguru homeland
- *Gogo* — A closely related people and language
- *Zaramo* — A closely related language
- *ghostland* — The dimension where ancestral ghosts lived
- *Morogoro* — The Tanzanian region where the Kaguru live
- *Gairo* — A district where most Kaguru live
