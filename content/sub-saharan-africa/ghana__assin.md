## Overview

The Assin, also known as Asin and Asen, are a subgroup of the Akan people who live mainly in Ghana’s Central Region. They occupy territory around Assin Foso, the capital of the modern Assin area, within a forest–savanna transition zone. Their history is connected to early Akan migrations across the Pra River and to the wider Akan cultural and political world. By the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, they participated in Gold Coast trade as middlemen between inland gold-producing regions and coastal forts. Their political organization developed into the Kingdom of Assin, which was involved in regional wars and commerce before coming under Denkyira and later Asante influence.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources identify common Akan institutions among the Assin, including chieftaincy, matrilineal inheritance, and stool-based governance.

## Language & religion

The Assin are described as a subgroup of the Akan people and as historically linked to the wider Akan cultural and political world.

The Assin are divided into two main groups. The Assin Apemanim, also called Apimenem, are based east of the Cape Coast–Kumasi Highway, with their historic center at Assin Manso. The Assin Attendansu, also called Atandanso, live west of the highway, with their capital at Nyankumasi. Both divisions share Akan institutions such as chieftaincy, matrilineal inheritance, and stool-based governance, while maintaining distinct local leadership.
