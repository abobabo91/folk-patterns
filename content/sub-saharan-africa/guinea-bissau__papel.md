## Overview

The Papel live mainly around Bissau and in the Biombo Region of Guinea-Bissau. They have traditionally farmed and hunted. Their culture includes colorful strip-woven cotton cloth, matrilineal succession, and an oral story about the founding of the Bissau kingdom. The sources also record war dances, Catholic conversion, and local religious images called Xina.

## Material culture

The recorded material culture centers on patterned cotton cloth and iron tools, while many other everyday objects are not described.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Papel textiles include narrow cotton strips sewn together and decorated with checks, grids, diamonds, stripes, and colored bands.

- **Six-strip cotton cloth** — Six narrow strip-woven cotton lengths are sewn together and may use plain weave, dyed yarns, supplementary weft, silver tinsel, or plastic.
- **Black-and-white checked textile** — A white textile has black stripes forming a regular grid, with oblique groups in the middle and diamond patterns at the ends.
- **Blue textile with colored checks** — A dark blue textile has white bands and white, yellow, red, and green checks at each end.
- **Diamond-patterned textile** — A black-and-white square grid contains five diamonds in each segment.
- **Black and green textile with silver lurex** — A narrow cotton strip has black and green squares with geometric silver lurex patterns.

### Clothing & dress

One recorded cotton garment for men and women is made from six loosely sewn strips with three decorative bands.

- **Six-strip cotton garment** — The garment uses white and black yarns, a black-and-white grid with diamond decoration in the center, and extra patterns at both ends.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The recorded tools are an iron hoe edge-blade and an iron adze-blade.

- **Hoe edge-blade** — The British Museum records an iron edge-blade for a hoe.
- **Adze-blade** — The British Museum records an iron adze-blade.

## Dance & theatre

Historical sources mention war dances connected with warfare in 1891.

- **War dances** — After a killing at Intim, Papel forces celebrated with war dances and prepared for war.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe oral origins, matrilineal kingship, Catholic conversion, warfare, and burial, but no annual festival calendar.

- **Founding of the Bissau kingdom** (*Mecau*) — According to oral history, Mecau came from Quinara, settled near Bissau because of its fertile soil, and established a kingdom.
- **Seven clan origins** (*Punguenhum*) — Punguenhum and six wives of Mecau became the ancestral lines of seven main Papel clans.
- **Matrilineal succession** — Kingship passed through the sovereign’s maternal sister’s son or another close male relative in the maternal line.
- **Catholic conversion of King Bacompol Co** (*Bacompol Co*) — King Bacompol Co converted to Catholicism in April 1694 and became the first Christian king of Bissau.
- **Burial alive of King N’Kanande Ka** (*N’Kanande Ka*) — After his capture in 1696, King N’Kanande Ka was sentenced to death, tortured, and buried alive.

## Foodways

The sources identify farming and hunting as traditional activities.

- **Kola trade** (*Kola*) — Kola was traded with Europeans, and Maria Soares brought a cargo of it to Bissau from Sierra Leone in 1699.
- **Traditional hunting** — Hunting was one of the Papel’s traditional activities.
- **Traditional agriculture** — The Papel are traditionally farmers.

## Oral tradition & literature

The main recorded oral tradition tells how Mecau founded the Bissau kingdom and how seven clans began.

- **Kingdom origin story** (*Mecau*) — The oral history says that Mecau came from Quinara during a hunting trip and founded a kingdom at Bissau.
- **Clan animal symbols** — The seven main clans are associated with the Leopard, Frog, Hyena, hare, Goat, Aardvark, and Monkey.
- **N’nssassun clan** (*N’nssassun*) — The N’nssassun clan is represented by the Leopard.
- **N’nsso clan** (*N’nsso*) — The N’nsso clan is represented by the Frog.
- **N’ndjukomo clan** (*N’ndjukomo*) — The N’ndjukomo clan is represented by the Hyena.

## Language & religion

Papel is a Bak language of coastal Guinea-Bissau, with several dialects and historical Catholic and local religious traditions.

- **Papel language** (*Pepel*) — Papel, also called Pepel, Papei, Oium, or Moium, is spoken mainly in the central coastal regions and uses a Latin-based script.
- **Bissau Island** (*(b)uhlawʔ*) — Bissau Island is called (b)uhlawʔ or (b)usawʔ in Papel.
- **Biombo dialect** (*uyomʔ*) — The Biombo dialect is called uyomʔ and is spoken in the southwest.
- **Safim dialect** (*safli*) — The Safim dialect is called safli and is spoken in the northeast.
- **Animist images** (*Xina*) — Xina were described as Animist images, and disputes arose over attempts to remove them from Christian settlements.
