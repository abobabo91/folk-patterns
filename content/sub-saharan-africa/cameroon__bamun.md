## Overview

The Bamun live in Cameroon’s highlands. Their language is Bamum, an Eastern Grassfields language with about 600,000–700,000 speakers in 2025. King Njoya and his palace circle developed an original Bamum script around 1895. Museum records document brass figures, smoking-pipes, masks, a staff finial, a palm-wine bottle, a woven bag, and an arm-dagger with sheath.

## Music & performance

Bamum is used in song, and museum records show a xylophone figure and decorated smoking-pipes.

- **Bamum song** (*Bamum*) — Cameroonian musician Claude Ndam was a native Bamum speaker and sang in the language.
- **Xylophone** — A brass figure shows a man with a xylophone.
- **Elephant-head pipe** — One smoking-pipe has a bowl shaped like an elephant head with its trunk raised.
- **Two-heads pipe** — Another pipe-bowl represents two human heads flanked by two crocodiles.
