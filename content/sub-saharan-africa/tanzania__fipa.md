## Overview

The Fipa live on and around the Ufipa Plateau in southwestern Tanzania. Their culture includes organized chiefdoms, farming, cotton cloth, round-hut settlements, and hereditary ironworking. Millet was the main crop, and people also fished around nearby lakes and rivers. Religious life included spirit shrines, ancestral spirits, sacred pythons, and ritual specialists.

## Material culture

Fipa material culture centers on strong cotton cloth, round huts, ironworking, pottery, reed mats, and simple farming and hunting tools.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Men spun and wove strong cotton cloth, while women made reed mats.

- **Cotton cloth** — Men spun and wove locally grown cotton into heavy, strong, open cloth with a white ground and black-striped border.
- **Black-and-white striped cloth** — People wore durable cotton cloth with black and white stripes, usually measuring about six by five feet.
- **Loom weaving** — Wooden shed sticks were used for loom weaving, although the local weaving industry later declined.
- **Reed mats** — Women made reed mats for sleeping and for filtering beer.

### Clothing & dress

Men and women wore a large, durable cotton cloth as toga-like dress.

- **Toga-like cotton dress** — Men and women wore strong, heavy cotton cloth measuring about five by six feet or six by five feet.

### Architecture

Fipa settlements had groups of round huts with rooms and animal corridors.

- **Round-hut settlement** — Settlements had twenty to thirty round huts, usually spaced a few yards apart.
- **Livestock corridors** — Two corridor walls around each hut made space for small livestock.
- **Cow-hide bed** — Men made beds from cow hide or cow-hide strips stretched over wooden frames, with a reed mat on top.
- **Harvest hut** — People working far from home built round huts for sleeping and shelter during harvest.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Hereditary ironworkers made tools and weapons, while women made coil-built pottery.

- **Hereditary ironworking** — Iron smelting and forging passed through families and involved technical knowledge, magic, and special ingredients.
- **Coil-built pottery** — Women made pottery using the coil method.
- **Iron arrow-heads** — The museum catalogue records iron arrow-heads among Fipa objects.
- **Bow and arrows** — The catalogue includes a wooden bow with a hide string and cane-and-metal arrows.
- **Whistle** — A whistle was made from reed, wax, leaf, and rattan.

### Jewelry & body adornment

- **Labial extension** — Unmarried girls traditionally extended their labia minora by constant manipulation, which was thought to increase sexual attractiveness and help with childbirth.

## Music & performance

Fipa vocal and ritual performance included chanting, while the sources do not name formal music genres.

- **Women’s death chant** — When a woman died in childbirth, women wept and chanted inside the hut while men sat outside.
- **Spoken art** — A 1978 work titled There was a certain man: Spoken art of the Fipa records a spoken-art tradition.
- **Reed whistle** — The sources list a reed, wax, leaf, and rattan whistle.

## Dance & theatre

- **Epidemic dance ban** — Dances were forbidden during an epidemic, and children stopped playing noisy games.

## Festivals & rituals

Fipa ritual life focused on birth, death, twins, illness, and sacred powers.

- **Tooth extraction** — The Fipa had no initiation ceremony for either sex apart from removing two or four lower incisors before or after puberty.
- **Birth signs** — At a girl’s birth her father carried firewood on his head, while at a boy’s birth he carried a bow and arrow in his right hand.
- **Childbirth ritual** — If childbirth was difficult, midwives asked the unborn child’s father’s name because hidden adultery was thought able to cause death.
- **Twin acclamation** — Twins were treated as divinities, carried around the village on a tray, and honored with communal sacrifices at the new moon.
- **Funeral destruction** — After a woman died in childbirth, the unborn child was placed on her back in the grave and the hut was destroyed after burial.

## Foodways

Millet porridge and beans formed the main food, supported by farming, fishing, and communal threshing.

- **Millet porridge** — Millet was made into dry porridge and usually eaten with the fingers.
- **Beans** — Beans were commonly eaten with millet porridge.
- **Compost-pile farming** — Main crops were planted on earth-covered piles of vegetation, often far from the settlement.
- **Communal threshing** — Groups of relatives and neighbors often threshed millet together.
- **Lake and river fishing** — Fishing was important around Lake Rukwa, Lake Tanganyika, and nearby rivers.

## Oral tradition & literature

Fipa oral tradition includes spoken art, histories of chiefs, and prophetic accounts.

- **Spoken art of the Fipa** — Roy G. Willis recorded the work There was a certain man: Spoken art of the Fipa in 1978.
- **Prophecy of Kaswa** — Prophet Kaswa was said to have warned that terrible strangers would bring war and take the people’s country.
- **Chief and dynasty histories** — Oral historical material preserved accounts of chiefs, dynasties, and prophets.

## Language & religion

Fipa religious life joined a supreme creator with local spirits, ancestors, sacred pythons, and hereditary priests.

- **Fipa language** (*Fipa*) — Fipa is a Bantu language spoken on the Ufipa Plateau in Rukwa Region.
- **Mambwe-Lungu speech** (*Mambwe-Lungu*) — Some people who speak Mambwe-Lungu identify as Fipa and consider it a dialect of Fipa.
- **Supreme creator** (*Umweele*) — Umweele was recognized as the supreme god and creator, but had no cult.
- **Local spirits** (*Katai*) — Katai were localized spirits linked with hills, lakes, trees, rocks, and groves.
- **Sacred pythons** — Sacred pythons represented place spirits and received offerings of millet porridge and meat.

## Glossary

- *Fipa* — The Fipa language and people
- *Wafipa* — Another name for the Fipa
- *Mambwe* — A language spoken by some Fipa
- *Mambwe-Lungu* — Speech some Fipa consider a Fipa dialect
- *Kwa* — A Fipa dialect
- *Umweele* — Supreme god and creator
- *Katai* — Localized spirits of places
- *Ufipa Plateau* — The Fipa homeland plateau
