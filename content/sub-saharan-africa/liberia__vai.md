## Overview

The Vai are Mandé peoples living mostly in Liberia, with a small minority in south-eastern Sierra Leone. Sierra Leonean Vai are concentrated in Pujehun District around the Liberian border, where many villages are populated by Vai; about 1200 Vai live in Sierra Leone. The Vai speak Vai, also called Liberian, Vy or Gallinas, a Mande language spoken by roughly 104,000 people in Liberia and smaller populations, some 15,500, in Sierra Leone. The Vai are particularly notable for the Vai syllabary, an indigenous writing system developed in the 1820s by Momolu Duwalu Bukele and other Vai elders. Literacy became widespread during the 19th century, declined during the 20th century, and has experienced a revival through modern computer technology.

## Music & performance

The Vai are described as musical. They play many instruments and perform dances on special occasions. No specific wedding, funeral, court, festival, or other musical tradition is identified.

## Dance & theatre

The sources state that the Vai perform dances on special occasions. The sources also state that ceremonies are performed for the dead.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that the Vai are predominantly Muslim and have practiced traditions rooted in studying the Quran for centuries. Islam was adopted through the influence of Mandinka merchants from the 16th century onwards.

Monotheistic religious practice coexists with traditional beliefs in the supernatural. Shamanistic practices are common, and people consider themselves surrounded by spirits that can change into living creatures or objects. These spirits are believed to have the power to do evil to individuals or to the whole tribe. The Vai perform ceremonies for the dead, leaving articles of clothing and food near the graves of deceased people.

## Foodways

Rice is the staple crop of the Vai and is cultivated with other vegetables on upland plots of cleared land. Other crops include cotton, corn, pumpkins, bananas, ginger, coffee, and cocoa. The Vai also gather various nuts and berries from forests.

The palm tree is an important commodity. Its derivatives include nuts, butter, wine, fuel, soap, and baskets. They do state that food is left near the graves of deceased people during ceremonies for the dead.

## Oral tradition & literature

Oral histories transmitted by Momolu Massaquoi describe a Mandinka king sending expeditions south from the Manding region to discover the sea and stimulate a trade in salt. Members of these expeditions are said to have intermarried with local Gola people, creating the Vai while retaining strong trade links with the Mandinka of the interior.

The earliest written documentation of the Vai is attributed to Dutch merchants sometime in the first half of the 17th century. The sources state that the Vai were likely the people called Gallinas by the Portuguese, who had settled in the area by the mid 15th century. Led by the Camara clan, they had come southwest to raid and trade in salt and Kola nuts.

## Language & religion

Vai is a Mande language. It is also called Liberian, Vy, or Gallinas and is spoken by roughly 104,000 people in Liberia and smaller populations, some 15,500, in Sierra Leone. Vai is tonal and has 11 vowels and 31 consonants. The language is notable for having a writing system not based on the Latin or Arabic scripts.

The Vai script is a syllabary invented by Momolu Duwalu Bukele around 1833, although dates as early as 1815 have been alleged. Its existence was reported in 1834 by American missionaries in the Missionary Herald of the ABCFM and independently by Rev. Sigismund Wilhelm Koelle. The script was used to print the New Testament in Vai, dedicated in 2003.

The Vai are predominantly Muslim and have practiced traditions rooted in studying the Quran for centuries. Islam was adopted through Mandinka influence from the 16th century onwards. Traditional supernatural beliefs and shamanistic practices coexist with these monotheistic religions.
