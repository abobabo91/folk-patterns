## Overview

The Kposo, or Akposso, are an ethnic group living in the Plateau Region of southern Togo, west of Atakpamé, and across the border in Ghana. Their ethnic language is Kposo or Ikposo. Kposo is also known as *Akpɔsɔ*, while Ikposo is written *Ikpɔsɔ*. The Kposo language is mainly spoken in the Plateau Region of Togo and also in eastern Ghana. It is considered one of the Ghana–Togo Mountain languages, although it does not have the system of noun classes characteristic of other languages in that group. The available sources document Kposo agriculture, the cultural importance of fonio, the annual *Ɔvazu* festival, and a traditional calendar with five days in each week.

## Festivals & rituals

The Kposo traditional calendar has five days in each week: *Imle*, *Ekpe*, *Ewle*, *Eyla*, and *Eva*. An annual festival called *Ɔvazu* is held around harvest time. In Togo, *Ɔvazu* is held together with the Akebus.

## Foodways

Kposo farmers grow cocoa and coffee as cash crops. Traditional crops include yams, maize, and fonio. Maize is called *ɖzukklɔ* in Kposo, while fonio is called *ɔva*. Fonio is culturally important, and the annual *Ɔvazu* festival is held around harvest time.

## Language & religion

Kposo, or Ikposo, is the language of the Akposso people. It is spoken mainly in the Plateau Region of Togo, west of Atakpamé, and also in eastern Ghana. Kposo is considered one of the Ghana–Togo Mountain languages, but it does not have the system of noun classes characteristic of other languages in that group. The sources describe a phonological feature in which /ɰ/ may also be heard as a fricative [ɣ] sound in free variation.
