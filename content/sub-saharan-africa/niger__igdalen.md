## Overview

The Igdalen, also known as Igdalan or Agdal, are a Berber people inhabiting northwestern Niger and parts of Mali and Algeria. They are centered on Tanout and Tchin Tabaraden in northern Niger, while seasonal transhumance with livestock takes them well north and south. Their estimated population is 27,000. They speak Tagdal, a mixed Tuareg–Songhay language, and are closely related to the Idaksahak people of eastern Mali and the Sawaq of Ingal in Niger, with whom they share a very similar language. Culturally, the Igdalen are often considered a Tuareg faction. They have previously been a Marabout and herding caste within Tuareg society and have formed a sub-confederation within the Kel Ayr Tuareg confederation, also called the Drum Group.

## Music & performance

The sources identify the Kel Ayr Tuareg confederation as the Drum Group.

## Festivals & rituals

The Igdalen are Muslim, and they have previously been a Marabout, or religious, caste within Tuareg society.

## Language & religion

The Igdalen speak Tagdal, a mixed Tuareg–Songhay language. Their language is described as very similar to that of the Idaksahak people of eastern Mali and the Sawaq of Ingal in Niger. The Igdalen are Muslim. The sources also describe them as having previously been a Marabout, or religious, caste within Tuareg society.
