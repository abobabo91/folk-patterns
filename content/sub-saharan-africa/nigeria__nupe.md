## Overview

The Nupe are a people of North Central Nigeria, centered in Niger State with a population of about 4.5 million. Their kingdom emerged in the 14th–15th century under the Igala leader Tsoede. Nupe art is known for its abstract character, especially in carved wooden forms and woven textiles. They adopted Islam around 1770 and maintain strong oral traditions alongside their material culture.

## Material culture

Nupe material culture combines hand-woven textiles, carved wood, and cast brass with distinctive abstract patterns and geometric motifs.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Nupe weavers create narrow-strip cloths and wrapped garments in indigo, white, and dyed cotton, often assembled from dozens of individual strips.

- **Guinea-fowl check cloth** (*saki / etu*) — Indigo-dyed cotton with white lines woven through both warp and weft, creating a blue-grey check pattern used for men's large gowns.
- **Indigo strip cloth** — Very narrow strips of indigo alternating with white lines, sewn together so seams read as broader indigo bands.
- **Women's warp-striped wrapper** — Single-panel cotton cloth woven with red-dyed warp stripes broken by black and white stripes, with twisted warp-thread fringes.
- **Alaari-striped cloth** (*alaari*) — Six-strip hand-sewn cloth combining white and indigo cotton with magenta silk warp stripes.
- **Appliquéd leather patterns** — Circular cushion covers and horse trappings with alternating leather patches or patterns cut through dyed leather.

### Clothing & dress

Men wear wide-shouldered strip-woven gowns with embroidery; women wear wrapped cloths and woven hair-bands; facial marks signal prestige and family.

- **Men's gown** (*boubou / tobe / riga*) — Wide-shouldered, unshaped gown made from saki check cloth with sleeves that fan out, a front pocket, and heavy embroidery.
- **Hooded cloak** (*burnous*) — Green cloth cut in one piece, lined with machine-woven cotton, edged with silk tassels, and embroidered with a central star.
- **Women's hair-band** — Woven in white, red, brown and indigo cotton with coloured bands set against white, with twisted warp-thread fringe.
- **Riding boots** — Thigh-length boots of black Morocco leather ornamented with sewn green leather and iron spurs.
- **Facial marks** — Worn to signal prestige, family identity, protection, and adornment, though the practice is declining in some areas.

### Architecture

Nupe architecture is known through its carved wooden doors, which are the primary documented architectural element.

- **Carved door panels** — Assembled from two to four wood panels fixed by battens and pegs, reaching about 99 cm wide, each carved in relief with abstract and figurative motifs.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Nupe brass from Bida is the signature metalwork medium, alongside pottery and carved wooden vessels and tools.

- **Bida brass vessels** — Globular lidded bowls tinned inside, hinged-lid teapots, beaten ewers, hammered dishes, and copper-and-brass coffee pots, often decorated with punched designs.
- **Cast brass figures** — Small cast pieces including birds, dogs, oxen, crocodiles, cobras, balancing toy figures, and a man at a camera.
- **Repoussé spoons** — Brass spoons made by the repoussé technique (hammering from behind to create raised design).
- **Painted pottery lamp** — Triple lamp with glazy red painted surface and black, yellow and white striping.
- **Carved wooden stools** — Sub-rectangular stools with incised seats, used by wives at market.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Glass and brass ornaments dominate, along with facial marking as a key body practice.

- **Glass bangles** — Pale blue glass bangles with bosses around the outer circumference, and imported opaque blue bracelets of triangular section.
- **Glass beads** — Cylindrical beads in dark brown with white spirals and yellow-brown with yellow spirals.
- **Brass ornaments** — Finger-rings, pins with spatulate ends, hairpins used as curlers, and heavy cast manillas with incised patterns.
- **Facial marks** — Worn to identify prestige and family, for protection, and as adornment like jewellery.

## Music & performance

Nupe musicians, called Ningba, perform on stringed instruments, horns, and drums, with a rich tradition of remembered singers.

- **Calabash stringed instrument** — Half calabash covered with skin as resonator, with straight stick neck, three sinew strings, and a richly carved back with stylised animals and birds.
- **Ivory horn** — Horn cut from a tusk and slung on giraffe hair.
- **Side-blown trumpet** — Roughly trimmed from an antelope horn.
- **Emir's brass war trumpet** — Made in two parts with relief-decorated bell-and-stem section and separate mouthpiece stem.
- **Composite brass bell** — Brass bell with an iron clapper.

## Dance & theatre

Nupe performance includes wooden masks, hunter headdresses, and a modern film industry founded in the late 1990s.

- **Wooden face mask** — Bearded human face with facial marks, surrounded by oval horns flanking two human figures.
- **Hunter's headdress** — Built from a ground hornbill head and beak, covered with goat skin and bright red seeds, with shell or metal inlay for eyes.
- **Nupewood film industry** — Modern screen drama industry founded in the late 1990s and early 2000s, producing over a thousand films for Nupe audiences.

## Festivals & rituals

Nupe oral tradition preserves a multifaceted cultural practice of gift-giving and blessings in transitions and relational expressions.

- **Dzana** — A cultural gesture involving the giving of gifts, blessings or symbolic items, primarily in travel, transitions, and expressions of care.

## Foodways

Sources provide no information on Nupe staples or dishes, only the vessels used at table.

- **Brass serving vessels** — Lidded bowls tinned on the inside, hinged-lid teapots, beaten ewers, hammered dishes and repoussé spoons used for serving and consumption.

## Oral tradition & literature

Nupe cultural heritage is rooted in oral transmission through named forms of speech: parables, warnings, idioms, riddles and proverbs.

- **Eganmaganzhi** — Proverbs that educate, entertain and teach morality, carrying norms and ethics through songs, stories and fables across generations.
- **Parable** (*egankogi*) — A named form of oral narrative in Nupe speech.
- **Warning** (*gangba*) — A named form of oral communication in Nupe speech.
- **Idiom** (*egancin*) — A named form of figurative speech in Nupe language.
- **Riddle or tale** (*ecingi*) — A named form of narrative and puzzle in Nupe speech.

## Language & religion

Nupe is a Volta-Niger language with five dialects, and the people are primarily Muslim with Christian and Traditional Religion minorities.

- **Nupe language** (*Nupenci / Tapa*) — A Volta-Niger language of the Nupoid branch of Benue-Congo, closest to Kakanda, with five dialects including Central Nupe and Tako.
- **Central Nupe dialect** — One of the two most markedly distinct dialects of the Nupe language.
- **Tako dialect** (*Nupe Tako / Bassa-Nge*) — One of the two most markedly distinct dialects, also called Bassa-Nge.
- **Islam adoption** — Etsu Jibiri adopted Islam around 1770 under pressure from Fulani advance, with the 19th century Sokoto jihad changing many practices.
- **Koran board motif** — A motif carved on Nupe doors reflecting the integration of Islam into Nupe culture.

## Glossary

- *saki* — Hausa term for indigo-and-white check cloth
- *etu* — Yoruba term for indigo-and-white check cloth
- *boubou* — Large wide-shouldered gown (also tobe, riga)
- *burnous* — Hooded cloak
- *alaari* — Magenta silk
- *Ningba* — Nupe musician or singer
- *egankogi* — Parable in Nupe oral tradition
- *gangba* — Warning in Nupe oral tradition
- *egancin* — Idiom in Nupe speech
- *ecingi* — Riddle or tale in Nupe oral tradition
- *eganmaganzhi* — Proverbs in Nupe oral tradition
- *Dzana* — Cultural practice of gift-giving, blessings in transitions
- *Etsu* — Nupe king or ruler
- *Nupenci* — Hausa term for the Nupe language
- *Tapa* — Yoruba term for Nupe people and language
