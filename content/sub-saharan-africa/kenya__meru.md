## Overview

The Meru live in fertile areas around Mount Kenya and the Nyambene ranges. Their traditions include stories of migration, conflict and movement toward Mount Kenya. The sources describe farming, herding, fishing, hunting, age sets, clans and ritual authority. They speak Meru, a Bantu language with several mutually intelligible dialects.

## Material culture

The material record includes weapons, fishing tools, spoons, jewelry and limited clothing details, but not a complete account of Meru crafts.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not document a detailed Meru textile tradition.

- **Eight-sided cloth** — Koome Njoe supplied a cloth shaped like a maize cob with an eight-sided peeled husk.

### Clothing & dress

The sources provide only a few clothing descriptions and no named everyday Meru garments.

- **Shoulder-tied cloth** — A British Museum print shows a man wearing cloth tied at the right shoulder.
- **Red waist cloth** (*Nguo Ntuni*) — The remembered Nguo Ntuni wore a single red cloth tied around the waist and one shoulder.
- **Head cloth** (*Nguo Ntuni*) — The remembered Nguo Ntuni also wore another cloth bound around the head.
- **Plaited hair** — A pictured adult man had long plaited hair tied at the back.

### Architecture

The historical account mentions small moving huts and stockaded mainland settlements.

- **Small moving huts** — The migrating Ngaa probably lived in simple, small huts while constantly moving.
- **Log stockade** — The Nguo Ntuni normally lived on the mainland behind a log stockade.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources describe weapons, fishing tools and a wooden spoon but no Meru pottery.

- **Ribbed iron spear** — A British Museum spear has a ribbed, leaf-shaped iron head.
- **Composite arrow** — One arrow has a fibre body, iron blade, cane shaft, leather feathering and a decorated end.
- **Wooden spoon** — A British Museum record lists a wooden spoon.
- **Goat-hair fishing lasso** — Woven goat-hair lassos were used to catch fish by snagging their gills.
- **Forge** — The historical account mentions a forge along with iron knives, arrows, bows, hooks and spears.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Museum records show white-metal, copper-chain and bead ornaments associated with Meru.

- **White-metal ear ornament** — A thin rounded white-metal plate has three engraved fields on each side and part of a brass chain.
- **Chain-and-bead earring** — Another earring has copper chains, two rows of glass beads and a brown substance probably made from red earth and fat.
- **Shield-shaped finger ring** — A narrow oval white-metal ring has edge bands and a central ornament.
- **Tied plaits** — A pictured adult man wore long straight plaited hair tied at the back.

## Music & performance

The sources do not describe traditional Meru music, but they mention ritual public action and one Kimeru cover song.

- **High Pitch Band Afrika cover** — The Kenyan group High Pitch Band Afrika made a Kimeru cover of “Despacito” in 2017.
- **Kiama council** (*Kiama*) — The Kiama was a council of elders that interpreted difficult tasks and directed collective action.
- **Curse remover** (*Muga*) — Koome Njoe is remembered as a Muga who advised the Kiama.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not cover Meru dance, theatre or other named dramatic traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe migration rites, sacrifice, age sets and clan relationships rather than an annual festival calendar.

- **Migration carrying groups** — During the escape from Mbwaa, Abwekana carried yams, Kiniamburi carried the goat and Omo carried religious objects.
- **Three-man sacrifice** — Muthetu was to serve as an altar, Gaita was to have his stomach opened for omens and Kiuna was to replace him if needed.
- **Nkuthuku age set** (*Nkuthuku*) — The Nkuthuku age set was established at Kirorero, probably around the 1720s.
- **Mukuruma age set** (*Mukuruma*) — The Mukuruma age set was established probably in the 1730s.
- **Final elders’ feast** — Each family was to present a goat, whose blood was allowed to flow toward the Nguo Ntuni camp as a deception.

## Foodways

Migration foodways centered on goats, sheep, crops, fish, water seeking and hunting.

- **Goat and sheep diet** — The Ngaa mainly lived on goats and sheep brought with them.
- **Fishing with wooden hooks** — They supplemented their food by fishing with wooden hooks.
- **Grévy's zebra hunting** — They hunted Grévy's zebra when food and water became scarce.
- **Reticulated giraffe hunting** — They also hunted the Reticulated giraffe when supplies became scarce.
- **Milk-and-lime calf** — A calf was starved for a week and then fed milk and lime for four days to produce white dung.

## Oral tradition & literature

Meru oral history tells of the Ngaa, their defeat by the Nguo Ntuni, escape from Mbwaa and movement toward Mount Kenya.

- **Ngaa migration story** (*Ngaa*) — The story begins with settlement on the coast, defeat by the Nguo Ntuni, escape from Mbwaa and later division into Meru subgroups.
- **Horned dog task** — The elders were ordered to produce a dog with horns.
- **Cloud-reaching spear task** — The elders were also ordered to forge a spear long enough to reach the clouds.
- **One people saying** — The groups remembered that “Mwimbi, Tigania and Murutu were once one.”
- **Kiringo M'Munyari prophecy** — A prophecy attributed to Kiringo M'Munyari spoke of leaving the desert, finding better land and defeating its inhabitants.

## Language & religion

Meru is a Bantu language with seven mutually intelligible dialects, and traditions refer to Ngai, the Mugwe and ancestral blessings.

- **Meru language** (*KiMîîrú*) — Meru, also called KiMîîrú or Kimeru, is spoken around Mount Kenya and the Nyambene ranges.
- **Imenti lingua franca** (*Kiimenti*) — Imenti is commonly used in Meru and serves as a lingua franca among the nine Meru subtribes.
- **Seven dialects** — The named dialects are Kiimenti, Tiania or gitiania, kiigembe, Kimwimbi, Kïmüthambï, Gicuka and Kitharaka.
- **Meru God** (*Ngai*) — The Omo clan is associated with the Meru God Ngai.
- **Ritual authority** (*Mugwe*) — The Mugwe appears in traditions connected with religious objects and ritual authority.
