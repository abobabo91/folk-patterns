## Overview

The Banna, also called Banya, are an Omotic ethnic group in Ethiopia who inhabit the Lower Omo Valley, primarily between the Weyto and Omo rivers. Their area lies between the towns of Gazer and Dimeka and is divided into two ritual regions: Ailama, around Gazer, and Anno, extending from Benata to Dimeka. According to the 2007 census, the Banna number around 47,000 individuals. They engage primarily in agriculture, supplemented by pastoralism, hunting, and gathering. Most are traditionalists, while a significant share are Christians, and the Banna have their own king. Their language belongs to the South Omotic branch of the Afroasiatic language family and is closely related to the Hamar and Bashada varieties.

## Language & religion

Banna is a variety of the Hamar-Banna language, which is within the South Omotic branch of the Afroasiatic language family. It is spoken primarily in southern Ethiopia by the Hamar people, Banna people, and speakers of Karo. Some Banna also speak the related Aari language in and around Mokocha and Chali. Some Banna report only slight difficulty communicating with speakers of the Hamar and Bashada varieties, although a clear virtual border exists between the Banna and neighboring Hamar in specific contexts. Hamar has no official writing system, although several romanization schemes and a Gə'əz orthography have been proposed. The Banna are mainly traditionalists, while a significant share are Christians, and they have their own king.
