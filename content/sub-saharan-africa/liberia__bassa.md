## Overview

The Bassa are mainly based in Liberia and are one of its largest ethnic groups. Their culture includes farming, lagoon fishing, craft work, initiation societies, masks and oral history. They speak Bassa, which has several regional varieties and indigenous writing systems. Most Bassa people are Christian, while retaining parts of older religious traditions.

## Material culture

Bassa material culture includes masks, brasswork, woven objects, pottery, carved objects, quivers and fish traps.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Named textile traditions are not well documented, but masks use woven baskets and braided bonnets.

- **Geometric quiver ornament** — A skin quiver from Grand Bassa has geometric patterns on its leather covering.
- **Woven mask basket** — Some masks are fastened to the forehead area of a woven basket worn by the performer.
- **Braided bonnet** — Mask performers may wear a braided bonnet fastened at an angle to the forehead.

### Clothing & dress

The sources describe special mask clothing but give little information about everyday dress.

- **Mask performer’s bonnet** — A performer may wear a braided bonnet fastened at an angle to the forehead.
- **Mask basket headwear** — A performer may wear a woven basket with a mask attached to it.
- **Wooden helmet mask costume** — A wooden helmet mask has pierced edges for attachment to a costume.

### Architecture

Inland Forest Bassa villages use confusing hut clusters shaped by water, farmland and roofing materials.

- **Confusing hut clusters** — Huts are arranged in clusters without a main road so outsiders have difficulty finding their way.
- **Reed-roof village site** — Village locations depend partly on access to reeds used for roofing.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Bassa crafts include brass casting, blacksmithing, carving, weaving, pottery, quivers and fish traps.

- **Small head brass masks** (*ma go*) — These miniature brass-cast masks have open lips, flared nostrils, slit eyes and a tattoo pattern from forehead to chin.
- **Brass Finial** — This object is an iron rod covered with brass and topped by a woman’s head with patterned hair.
- **Arrow quiver** — A skin quiver from Grand Bassa holds five arrows with iron barbed points inside a wooden quiver.
- **Woven fish trap** — A woven fish trap is recorded from Grand Bassa.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mainly discuss Dan brass jewelry and do not document named Bassa jewelry types.

- **Miniature brass masks** (*ma go*) — Bassa production of miniature masks was linked to surplus brass from Dan jewelry.
- **Brass Finial woman’s head** — The Brass Finial represents a woman’s head with five rows of hair and a herringbone pattern.

## Music & performance

The sources do not name Bassa music traditions, but they describe several mask performances.

- **Reverence and fear masks** — Some masks use monstrous faces with protruding eyes, swollen lips and twisted human or animal features.
- **Ancestor masks** — Ancestor masks express beauty and are meant to bring peace and leniency to heirs.
- **Devil masks** — Devil masks are worn by soothsayers, acrobats and jugglers described as agents of social control.
- **Disfigured-person mask** — A performer used this mask while dancing and joking to give a disfigured person’s features a positive meaning.

## Dance & theatre

Mask performances combine dancing, crowd interaction and jokes.

- **Disfigured-person mask performance** — A performer danced with crowds and told jokes while wearing a mask based on a disfigured person.
- **Devil-mask performance** — Soothsayers, acrobats and jugglers wore devil masks during performances.

## Festivals & rituals

The Bassa practice Sande initiation, also called Bondo, with special helmet masks and secret rites.

- **Women’s initiation society** (*Sande; Bondo*) — Sande, also called Bondo, is a women’s initiation society that uses special helmet masks.
- **Female ancestor helmet mask** (*Sowei; sowo*) — A society official wears the mask, which represents a beautiful female ancestor spirit believed to live in water.
- **Men’s and women’s initiation rites** — The sources describe secret rites of passage for men and women connected with Sande and other initiation traditions.

## Foodways

Bassa farming traditionally centers on several root crops and plantain, alongside lagoon fishing.

- **Yam farming** — Yam is one of the crops traditionally grown by Bassa farmers.
- **Cassava farming** — Cassava is one of the crops traditionally grown by Bassa farmers.
- **Eddoe farming** — Eddoes are among the crops traditionally grown by Bassa farmers.
- **Plantain farming** — Plantain is one of the crops traditionally grown by Bassa farmers.
- **Lagoon fishing** — Lagoon fishing was one of the traditional Bassa occupations.

## Oral tradition & literature

Bassa oral history tells of migrations, leaders, kingdoms, conflicts and a mythical ancestor.

- **People of the East** (*Gbooh; Gbɔ̌ɔ̀*) — The name Gbooh or Gbɔ̌ɔ̀ is linked to the meaning “People of the East.”
- **Father Stone’s people** (*Bassa Sooh Nyombe*) — The name Bassa is connected with Bassa Sooh Nyombe, translated as “Father Stone’s people.”
- **Migration leaders** (*Ɓǎ Gìà-xwí Sàwá; Ɓǎ vɛ̀nɛ̀; Ɓǎ Sɔ́ɔ́*) — These leaders are associated with the journey and with relations between Bassa traders and Europeans.
- **Sma Vlen’s walking stick** (*Sma Vlen*) — A legend says the Brass Finial was the walking stick of the mythical ancestor Sma Vlen.
- **Nyanya kingdom** (*Nyanya*) — Oral history includes accounts of the Nyanya kingdom or state, the Dii artifact and conflicts among Kru groups.

## Language & religion

Bassa is a regional Kru language with indigenous writing, and most Bassa people combine Christianity with older beliefs.

- **Ehni Ka Se Fa writing system** (*Ehni Ka Se Fa*) — Ehni Ka Se Fa is an indigenous Bassa writing system.
- **Vah alphabet** (*Vah*) — Vah is an alphabet with 30 consonants, seven vowels and five tones marked by dots and lines inside vowels.
- **Bassa Bible** — The first Bible translation into Bassa appeared in 1922, and the entire Bible was published in 2005.
- **Traditional religion** — Traditional Bassa religion reveres ancestors and supernatural spirits and has a moral and ethical foundation.
- **Christianity and older beliefs** — Christian ideas were joined with beliefs in a Supreme Being and a powerful first ancestor.
