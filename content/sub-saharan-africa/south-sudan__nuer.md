## Overview

The Nuer are pastoralists of the Upper Nile whose entire culture revolves around cattle herds—from economy and kinship to ritual and personal names. Cattle provide raw materials for most objects, and the herd determines seasonal movement to protect animals from disease and find pasture. Life milestones like initiation, marriage and death are marked by rituals centered on cattle sacrifice and exchange. The Nuer diet relies on millet, fish and milk, with meat eaten sparingly and only under ritual conditions.

## Material culture

Nuer material culture is made overwhelmingly from cattle by-products: hide, horn, bone and dung, along with wood, iron and brass.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Nuer have a named body-marking tradition but no documented loom-woven textiles.

- **Facial scarification marks** (*gaar*) — Six parallel horizontal lines cut across the forehead at initiation, with patterns that vary by Nuer subgroup and identify both adult status and clan membership.
- **Worked hide and leather** — Cut and fitted skin used for women's aprons, dancing girdles, shields and other goods rather than woven cloth.

### Clothing & dress

Ornament rather than garment carries most visible dress, with beads and metal pieces at neck, arm, wrist and ankle.

- **Woman's apron** — Made of kid leather with the lower edge cut into two V-shapes and long leather fringes attached.
- **Dancing girl's girdle** — Made of hippopotamus leather hung with ten iron cones and an iron ring, designed as a sound-making piece for movement.
- **Waist-beads** — Worn by young men as documented in British Museum photographs.

### Architecture

The Nuer are seasonal pastoralists whose buildings are adapted to movement with the herds.

- **Thatched shelters** — Temporary structures shown in archival photographs, sometimes arranged behind compounds.
- **Fenced compounds** — Enclosures that protect space and animals, sometimes containing clay-built structures.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Nuer objects are domestic, pastoral and martial, made from pottery, wood, brass, iron and hide.

- **Smoking pipes** — Paired pottery bowls with long wooden stems and gourd or skin mouthpieces, sometimes decorated with cowrie shells.
- **Neck-rests** — Carved wooden headrests with three legs for resting while seated.
- **Shield** — Made of hippopotamus hide with a central wooden staff for protection in combat.
- **Triple bell** — Cast brass alloy bell with two smaller bells suspended inside and a clapper, decorated with grooves and relief rectangles.
- **Cow-bell** — Iron bell mounted on a ring with a plaited hide collar, worn by herding animals to make them audible.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Nuer ornament is simple and strong, made of metal and hide shaped into armlets, ear pendants and body marks.

- **Armlets** — Made of iron, brass wire or hide, some nicked along the edge to form rows of points.
- **Ear pendants** — Coiled brass wire or iron wire worn in pairs or singly.
- **Facial scarification** (*gaar*) — Marks cut at initiation that identify adult status and subgroup membership.

## Music & performance

Sound is central to Nuer ritual, with drums, bells and whistles used in ceremonies and dances.

- **Triple bell** — Cast brass bell with inner clapper, rung in ritual contexts.
- **Cow-bell** — Iron bell that makes the herds themselves audible in the landscape.
- **Boys' whistles** — Carved from animal hoof, used by young herders.
- **Dancing girl's girdle** — Hippopotamus leather hung with iron cones and ring that makes sound with the dancer's movement.

## Dance & theatre

The Nuer perform dances and songs collectively around cattle slaughter but have no documented dramatic or masking traditions.

- **Slaughter dances** — Collective dances and songs performed before and after an ox is killed in ritual.

## Festivals & rituals

The Nuer ritual year is organized by the herd and rains, with major rites marking initiation, marriage and death.

- **Initiation scarification** (*gaar*) — Forehead marks cut with a razor to mark entry into adulthood and identify subgroup membership.
- **Bridewealth exchange** — Cattle passed from husband's to wife's lineage at marriage, defining paternal descent and legitimizing children.
- **Ghost marriage** — A practice in which a dead man can father children through cattle exchange, or an infertile woman can take a wife whose children belong to her patrilineage.
- **Cattle sacrifice** — Ritual killing of animals where the ox's spirit could curse the killer if not properly performed; meat eaten only under ritual control.

## Foodways

The Nuer diet centers on millet and fish, with milk and meat consumed under ritual constraints.

- **Millet porridge** (*kop and walwal*) — Ground millet prepared as finely balled and boiled porridge, the staple grain especially in rainy season.
- **Yeast flatbread** (*injera or yɔtyɔt*) — A large pancake-like yeast-risen flatbread made from grain.
- **Fish** — A dietary mainstay eaten alongside millet and milk.
- **Milk and cheese** — Central to the diet when surplus exists; surplus milk is made into cheese.
- **Dung preparations** — Cattle dung chopped and hardened for containers and toothpaste; burned for smoke to keep insects off cattle.

## Oral tradition & literature

Genealogy is the documented durable oral form, carried through paternal naming across many generations.

- **Genealogical names** — A person's second name is their given name, then father's, then grandfather's, with many able to recite ten generations of paternal lineage.
- **Event-marking names** (*Dɔmaac, Mac*) — Children born in wartime may be named Dɔmaac (bullet) or Mac (fire or gun) to record the event.
- **Cattle names** — Boys take names of favourite oxen chosen for form and colour; girls take names of cows they milk; names can pass down through generations.
- **Women's name prefix** (*Nya*) — Standard prefix on all women's names.
- **Gender prefixes** (*Gat*) — Prefix meaning baby of, used for both boys and girls as in gatnyal (baby girl).

## Language & religion

Thok Naath is a Western Nilotic language with 39 letters and complex nominal inflection; religion centers on Kwoth, the sky god.

- **Thok Naath language** (*Thok Naath*) — Means people's language; a Western Nilotic language close to Dinka and Atuot, written in Latin alphabet since 1928.
- **Breathy vowels** — Vowels may be long or short, modal or breathy; breathy voice written with underscore as in kop.
- **Sky god** (*Kwoth*) — Creator spirit who comes through rain, lightning and thunder; the rainbow is his necklace and sun and moon are his signs.
- **Air spirits** — Most powerful lesser spirits below Kwoth, sometimes attached to clan-spear names.
- **Clan-spear spirit** (*WiW*) — A spirit of war associated with thunder, attached to certain clan identities.

## Glossary

- *Naath* — What the Nuer call themselves
- *Thok Naath* — People's language; the Nuer language
- *gaar* — Facial scarification marks cut at initiation into adulthood
- *Kwoth* — The sky god and creator in Nuer religion
- *WiW* — A spirit of war associated with thunder
- *kop* — Millet finely ground, handled until balled and boiled
- *walwal* — Millet ground, lightly balled and boiled to a solid porridge
- *injera* — A large pancake-like yeast-risen flatbread
- *yɔtyɔt* — A large pancake-like yeast-risen flatbread
- *Nya* — Standard prefix on women's names
- *Gat* — Prefix meaning baby of, used for both boys and girls
- *Dɔmaac* — Bullet; a name given to children born in wartime
- *Mac* — Fire or gun; a name given to children born in wartime
- *gatnyal* — Baby girl
- *ghost marriage* — A practice where a dead man or infertile woman can father or mother children through cattle exchange
- *bridewealth* — Cattle exchanged from husband's to wife's lineage at marriage, defining paternal descent
