## Overview

The Acholi are a Nilotic people living in northern Uganda and South Sudan near the Uganda–South Sudan border. They were organized historically into chiefdoms led by anointed rulers called rwodi. The Acholi are known for their hunting and farming traditions, skilled metalwork and ornament-making, and especially for their elaborate reconciliation rituals that restore peace after conflict.

## Material culture

Acholi material culture emphasizes personal ornament, iron metalwork, hide and fibre garments, and painted house walls as the principal decorated surface.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Acholi documented tradition is primarily in house-wall painting rather than woven textiles.

- **String trains** (*trains*) — Women's garments made of twisted vegetable-fibre string, sometimes combined with cane, gum and glass beads.
- **Painted house walls** — Women decorated mud hut walls with geometrical or conventional designs in red, white or grey.

### Clothing & dress

Acholi dress recorded in collections is chiefly women's garments of hide, fibre and beads, with display headwear marking status.

- **Hide aprons and belts** — Aprons worked with twine and brass wire; leather belts finished with iron-wire tags, leather strings and iron rings.
- **Human-hair head-dresses** — Display pieces built over felt or basketry foundations, decorated with beads, brass plates, feather tufts and sewn rings.
- **Wrist-knife** — A strip of iron worn on the arm with narrow hide edging or kept in a leather sheath.
- **Rain-maker's belt** — Ritual regalia of leather and iron worn by religious specialists.

### Architecture

Acholi settlements were organized hamlets of circular huts with high peaked roofs and mud interiors.

- **Circular huts** — Dwellings with peaked roofs, mud sleeping-platforms, jars of grain stored inside, and sunken fireplaces.
- **Painted mud walls** — Women daubed walls with mud and decorated them with geometrical or conventional designs in red, white or grey.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Iron dominates Acholi metalwork; the sources record no pottery.

- **Axes** — Iron-headed axes mounted on wooden handles.
- **Iron-bladed sword** — Blade mounted with wood and snake skin.
- **Shields** — Hide shields used in war, made from giraffe or ox hide.
- **Winnowing fan** — Double-thickness fan of twigs set at right angles and joined with bast strips in wrapped twine weave for grain processing.
- **Wooden head-rests** — Carved wooden pillows, some fitted with leather thongs.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Acholi ornament combines diverse materials—beads, shells, hair, bone, horn, iron and ivory—in necklaces, bracelets and head ornaments.

- **Giraffe-hair necklace** — Giraffe hair strung with glass beads.
- **Composite ornament kit** — A single piece combining hide string, seeds, cowrie shells, glass beads, wood, iron wire, bone and horn with two bracelets, tweezers and a bell.
- **Ostrich-eggshell necklace** — Beads of ostrich eggshell on hide and fibre string with iron wire and cowrie shells.
- **Ivory armlet** — Circular armlet cut from a section of ivory tusk.
- **Cowrie-set forehead ornament** — Leather forehead ornament set with cowrie shells.

## Music & performance

Acholi instruments span strings, percussion and wind, centered on the bow harp.

- **Bow harp** — Arched wooden neck with five strings (one dyed red), triangular sound-box of leather over wood with two circular sound-holes.
- **Trough zither** — String instrument of wood and cane with hide strings or cane alone.
- **Drum-rattle** — Percussion of wood with hippopotamus or cow hide, stones and sometimes cotton string.
- **Body rattle** — Four sticks each threaded with five seed pods, bound and secured with bark fibre and twisted fibre cords knotted at corners for attachment to the wearer.
- **War horn** — Wind instrument of wood and hide used in warfare.

## Dance & theatre

The sources document no Acholi dance or dramatic tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

Reconciliation ritual and purification ceremonies form the best-documented Acholi intangible domain.

- **Mato oput** — The indigenous accountability and justice process performed after killings, combining retributive and restorative aspects and valuing peace over justice.
- **Nyono tong gweno** — A reconciliation ceremony meaning 'stepping on the egg', performed for returnees to their home villages, including some 12,000 former Lord's Resistance Army returnees.
- **Blood money negotiation** — The victim's family led negotiations for compensation after a killing, followed by reconciliation ceremony.

## Foodways

Acholi foodways center on millet farming by women and herding and hunting by men.

- **Millet** (*kál*) — The primary grain crop cultivated by women.
- **Simsim** — A crop grown and processed by women.
- **Groundnuts** — Legume crop cultivated by women.

## Oral tradition & literature

Acholi verbal art is rich in proverbs and has produced major published literature.

- **Acholi Proverbs** — Collection compiled by Okot p'Bitek including 'Ange tyene lit, koko ange' (Regret has sore legs, that's why it arrives late).
- **Song of Lawino** — Epic poem by Okot p'Bitek written in Acholi in 1966 and later translated to English, one of the most successful works of African literature.
- **Song of Ocol** — Sequel to Song of Lawino, written in English by Okot p'Bitek.
- **Rupiny** — A Luo-language newspaper serving the Acholi region.

## Language & religion

Acholi is a Western Nilotic language with complex tonal and vowel-harmony systems; the people are predominantly Catholic and Anglican.

- **Acholi language** (*Leb Acoli or Leb Lwo*) — Western Nilotic language with vowel harmony in two five-vowel sets and a tonal system featuring high, low, downstep high and double downstep high tones.
- **Dhopaluo sub-dialect** (*Chope*) — Variant of Acholi spoken in Kiryandongo District in the kingdom of Bunyoro.
- **YA Latwer** — The one superior being believed in by all Acholi before colonialism.
- **Rwot** — A ruler; plural rwodi, the anointed chiefs who led Acholi chiefdoms.

## Glossary

- *Acholi* — A Nilotic people of the Luo group; the name derives from 'Shooli', applied by Arab traders
- *Leb Acoli* — The Acholi language; also called Leb Lwo
- *Lwo* — The Acholi call themselves this; people of the home
- *rwot* — Ruler; anointed chief with supernatural powers
- *rwodi* — Plural of rwot; the chiefs who led Acholi chiefdoms
- *mato oput* — Indigenous accountability and justice process for restoring peace after killing
- *nyono tong gweno* — Reconciliation ceremony meaning 'stepping on the egg', for returnees to home villages
- *YA Latwer* — The one superior being believed in by all Acholi before colonialism
- *trains* — Women's garments made of twisted vegetable-fibre string
- *kál* — Millet, the primary grain crop
- *Chope* — Dhopaluo sub-dialect of Acholi spoken in Kiryandongo District
- *simsim* — A crop cultivated and processed by Acholi women
