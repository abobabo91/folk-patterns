## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| Who | The Lunda, also called Balunda, Luunda and Ruund, are a Bantu ethnic group. |
| Where | Their historic homeland was along the Kalanyi River in the Democratic Republic of the Congo; communities also live in Angola, Zambia, Gabon and the Republic of the Congo. |
| How many | About 1.1 million in the Congo, 800,000 in Angola and 600,000 in Zambia. |
| Language | Most speak Lunda, also called Chilunda; Kazembe-Lunda speak Bemba. |
| Religion | Nzambi or Nzamb Katang is the Supreme Creator, approached through ancestral spirits. |
| Known for | The Kingdom of Lunda · Ceremonial medicine-man costumes and masks · Farming and fishing · Sansa musical instruments · Glass-bead head-dresses |

## Overview

The Lunda formed the Kingdom of Lunda in the 17th century under Mwata Yamvo, also called Mwaant Yav. Their capital was Musumba. Lunda communities spread across Central Africa through migration, political expansion, farming, fishing and trade. Their material culture includes ceremonial dress, masks, musical instruments, tools, vessels and personal ornaments.

## Material culture

Lunda material culture includes ceramic vessels, farming and hunting tools, musical instruments, ceremonial costumes, masks, bead ornaments and prestige necklaces.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The profile names bast-cord dance elements and a prestige necklace but gives no Lunda pattern vocabulary.

- **Crocheted bast cord** (*Bastsnöre*) — A rectangular piece of crocheted bast cord formed part of a Lunda dance costume and may have protected the hands.
- **Dance costume** (*Dansdräkt*) — A Lunda dance costume from Kassai included a mask, shirt, leg clothing, cords and crocheted bast elements.
- **Prestige necklace** (*Mitunda*) — Mitunda was a necklace type valued so highly that local people were reluctant to sell it.

### Clothing & dress

Ceremonial dress centered on a medicine-man costume, while men also wore body cloths, ornaments and head-gear.

- **Medicine-man costume** — At circumcision festivals, a medicine man wore a mask, shirt, leg clothing, cords, bast cord and ten fingers threaded onto two strings while leading the dance.
- **Men’s body cloths and head-gear** — A British Museum record shows Lunda men wearing cloths around their lower bodies, personal ornaments, head-gear and vests or shirts.
- **Beaded head-dress** — A Lunda head-dress had eight glass-bead parts, with some worn only on special occasions and others worn continually.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Lunda household and working objects included vessels, tools, weapons and containers.

- **Ceramic vessels** (*Mulondo*) — Three vessels called mulondo were Lunda or Lwena pottery with rounded or oval bodies, varied feet, cylindrical necks and geometric decoration or clay lids.
- **Wood-cutting axe** — A straight-edged, socketed axe was used for cutting wood.
- **Soil-working hoe** — A hoe was used for working the soil.
- **Flour-stirring spade** — A wooden spade was used for stirring flour.
- **Hunting and war bow** — A tough-wood bow with a twisted leather string, probably buffalo hide, was used in warfare and hunting.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Beaded head ornaments and prestige necklaces are the main named forms of Lunda adornment.

- **Glass-bead head-dress** — The head-dress had a forehead ornament, ear ornaments and arched ornaments across the head.
- **Prestige necklace** (*Mitunda*) — Mitunda necklaces carried substantial prestige value and were difficult to obtain.
- **Personal ornaments** — Lunda men in a British Museum photograph wore personal ornaments with their clothing and head-gear.

## Music & performance

Lunda musical life in the profile is represented by sansas, rattles, drums and a smoking pipe.

- **Lamellophone** (*Sansa*) — The sansa was a Lunda instrument played by men and tuned with small balls of wax.
- **Rattle sansa** — A British Museum record describes a wooden and iron sansa with a rattle.
- **Foot rattles** — Foot rattles were rows of calabashes threaded onto sticks and tied around the legs or ankles.
- **Large wooden drum** — A drum from Angola had a sound opening, wooden handles and straps.
- **Carved smoking pipe** — The catalogue records included a carved wooden smoking pipe among Lunda musical objects.

## Dance & theatre

The profile documents a medicine man leading a dance at circumcision festivals while wearing a mask and costume.

- **Medicine-man dance** — A medicine man led the dance at a circumcision festival in a costume with a mask, shirt, leg clothing, cords and bast elements.
- **Medicine-man mask** — Medicine-man masks were used at circumcision festivals and were made by particular people.

## Festivals & rituals

The main named ritual setting is the circumcision festival, alongside ancestor-based religious practice.

- **Circumcision festival** — Circumcision festivals featured medicine men, masks and a ceremonial dance.
- **Ancestor mediation** — Lunda religious practice approached Nzambi through the spirits of ancestors.
- **Royal polygyny** — The sources state that Lunda kings had twenty to thirty wives.

## Foodways

Lunda foodways combined farming, fishing, palm wine and foods taken from Ndongo.

- **Farming** — Lunda farmers grew maize, millet, yams, sorghum, squash, beans, sweet potatoes, oil palms and tobacco.
- **Fishing** — The Lunda are described as fishermen in a region of rivers, lakes, forests and savannah.
- **Palm wine** — Lunda people drank palm wine.
- **Cattle and pork** — After defeating Ndongo, the Lunda based part of their diet on cows and pigs taken from that kingdom.

## Language & religion

Lunda is a Bantu language, and Lunda religion centers on Nzambi and ancestral spirits.

- **Lunda language** (*Lunda*) — Lunda is a Bantu language spoken in Zambia, Angola and, to a lesser extent, the Democratic Republic of the Congo.
- **Chilunda** — Chilunda is another name for the Lunda language.
- **Bemba adoption** (*Bemba*) — The Kazembe-Lunda adopted Bemba from neighboring communities.
- **Supreme Creator** (*Nzambi*) — Nzambi is identified as the Supreme Creator in Lunda religious tradition.
- **Supreme Creator** (*Nzamb Katang*) — Nzamb Katang is another name for the Supreme Creator.
