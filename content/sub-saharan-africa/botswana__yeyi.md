## Overview

The Yeyi, also known as the Mayeyi or Bayeyi, are a Bantu-speaking ethnic group native to Southern Africa, with significant populations in Botswana and Namibia. They live along the Okavango River, and the Yeyi language is spoken in both Botswana and Namibia. The population is given as 68,400, while approximately 50,000 Yeyi people are described as speakers of Yeyi. According to oral tradition, the baYei emigrated from the kingdom of the Lozi people in the 18th century and were led into Ngamiland by Hankuzi, a skilled fisherman and hunter. Their folk-cultural significance in the sources is connected especially with fishing, canoe-building, agriculture, language, and relations with neighboring peoples.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They identify Sangwali as the settlement where the Mayeyi khuta, or royal homestead, is located.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources describe iron as important in the baYei economy because it was used to produce spearheads and tools. They also state that the baYei are credited with bringing canoe-building technology to Ngamiland.

## Dance & theatre

Batsara Batsapi is identified as an annual cultural festival of the Mayeyi people.

## Festivals & rituals

Batsara Batsapi is described as the annual cultural festival of the Mayeyi people and is conducted at Sangwali, the settlement that contains the seat of their khuta, or royal homestead.

The baYei traditionally had matrilineal succession: the inheritor of a kingdom was the son of the king’s sister.

## Foodways

The sources identify sorghum and tobacco as important crops in baYei culture. Maize and sweet potatoes are also widely grown, especially among people living in the Okavango Delta. The sources further describe fishing as important to Yeyi history and state that the baYei learned new fishing techniques from the baKhakwe people.

## Oral tradition & literature

According to oral tradition, the baYei emigrated from the kingdom of the Lozi people in the 18th century and were led into Ngamiland by Hankuzi, a skilled fisherman and hunter. When the baYei met the baKhakwe people, Hankuzi married one of their women, possibly as a guarantee of peace. The account also describes later immigration waves and the transmission of survival skills, including fishing techniques.

The Kamanakao Association was formed in 1995 as a pressure group for the linguistic and cultural rights of the Wayeyi tribe, promoting the Seyeyi language and preserving cultural heritage.

## Language & religion

Yeyi, also called Shiyɛyi, is a Bantu language spoken along the Okavango River in Namibia and Botswana. Its main dialect is called Shirwanga. The language has been influenced phonetically by the Ju languages and is characterized by click sounds; it is described as having dental, alveolar, palatal, and lateral clicks, although the number and classification of click series are debated. In Botswana, Yeyi is gradually being phased out in favor of Tswana, while speakers in the Caprivi Strip of north-eastern Namibia retain Yeyi in villages including Linyanti and may also speak Lozi. Silozi is used as a formal language in official, educational, and media contexts.

The baYei believed in a creator god who lived among humans but later went to heaven because of human wickedness. They also venerate ancestor spirits.
