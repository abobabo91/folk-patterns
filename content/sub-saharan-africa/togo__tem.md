## Overview

The Tem, also known as the Temba or Kotokoliare, are an ethnic group of West Africa living mainly in Togo, Benin, Burkina Faso, and Ghana. There are reported to be about 417,000 Tem: 339,000 in Togo, 60,000 in Ghana, and 18,000 in Benin. They speak Tem, a Gur language belonging to the Niger-Congo language family. The Tem originated as a coalition of Gurma chiefdoms that settled around Sokodé during the 17th or 18th century and founded the Tchaoudjo Kingdom. Their documented folk-culture includes oral tales, festive storytelling, and the fire dance festival.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources used mention that Tem houses are often made of flammable materials.

## Music & performance

The Tem are known for the fire dance festival, during which performers dance with fire and bring it into contact with their skin without appearing to be hurt. Some performers also appear to consume flames. The festival begins with the preparation of a bonfire to the sound of drums. Performers begin as children and practice during festivals held several times per year. At the end of the festival, the fire is extinguished with bare feet.

## Dance & theatre

The fire dance festival is a documented Tem performance tradition. Its performers dance with fire, bring fire into contact with their skin, and sometimes appear to consume flames. The practice begins in childhood, with performers learning and practicing during festivals held several times per year.

## Festivals & rituals

The Tem are known for the famous fire dance festival. The festival begins with the preparation of a bonfire accompanied by drums. Performers dance with the fire, bring it into contact with their skin, and sometimes appear to consume the flames. The festival is held several times per year. At its conclusion, the fire is put out with bare feet.

The sources explain that fires are common in the dry climate, water is not plentiful, and houses are often made of flammable materials.

## Oral tradition & literature

Tem literature predominantly consists of tales passed down orally. Several compilations have been made; in the 2000s, Silvano Galli compiled stories by approximately 100 authors and translated them into French and Italian. Tales are told at festive assemblies attended by renowned storytellers such as Seybou Sebabe Giafago and Aboulaye Djibirila.

After a story ends, the audience traditionally thanks the storyteller by saying in unison, “You and the Sky,” to which the storyteller responds, “You and the Earth.” Many tales feature domestic or savage animals undertaking adventures that reflect human society. Tem literature is known across Togo, Benin, and throughout West Africa.

## Language & religion

The Tem language is a Gur language within the Niger-Congo language family and is currently spoken by over 300,000 people. French is also sometimes used.

The Tem converted to Islam during the 19th century through the influence of Chakosi merchants. The majority of Tem profess Islam today, and the Tem have the highest percentage of Muslims in Togo.
