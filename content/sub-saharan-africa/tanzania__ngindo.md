## Overview

The Ngindo are an ethnic and linguistic group based in southern Pwani Region, eastern Ruvuma Region, southern Morogoro Region and northern Lindi Region. The area includes Liwale and Kilwa districts in present-day Tanzania, formerly Tanganyika. In 1987, the Ngindo population was estimated at 220,000. They speak the Ngindo and Ndendeule languages. The supplied sources identify the group’s location, population and languages.

## Oral tradition & literature

The supplied sources mention the poet Amir Abdallah Sudi, whose nickname is “Andanenga.” The nickname derives from two Ngindo words: *anda*, meaning “like,” and *nenga*, meaning “right me” or “me”; it is therefore better translated as “like me and some others.” No other Ngindo folktales, epics, proverbs, riddles, storytelling contexts or preservation efforts are described.

## Language & religion

Ngindo is a Bantu language of Tanzania. The Ngindo people also speak the Ndendeule language.
