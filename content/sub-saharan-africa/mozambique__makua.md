## Overview

The Makua are the largest ethnic group in Mozambique. Most live north of the Zambezi River, with communities in Tanzania. Their documented culture includes farming, hunting, ironwork, trade, oral tradition, and traditional religion. Makua objects include tools, pottery, pipes, rattles, flutes, boxes, ornaments, and carved figures.

## Material culture

Makua material culture uses iron, wood, bamboo, pottery, fibre, gourds, and seed pods.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Makua metalworkers made tools and many everyday objects from iron, wood, bamboo, pottery, fibre, gourds, and seed pods.

- **Iron axe** — Makua people extracted iron from ore in a wood-burning hearth and made axes with wooden handles and iron blades.
- **Hourglass pottery bowl** — Museum records include a black pottery bowl shaped like an hourglass.
- **Bamboo day reckoner** — A day reckoner used short bamboo lengths threaded on fibre.
- **Double-gourd smoking pipe** — Museum records include a smoking pipe made from two gourds.
- **Seed-pod rattle** — A rattle used thirteen seed pods filled with dried seeds and attached to a wooden stick.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Makua adornment records include wooden ear ornaments, tooth charms, and metal rings.

- **Wooden ear ornaments** — Seven small circular wooden ear ornaments have incised linear patterns.
- **Tooth charms** (*ngiri*) — Some charms use ngiri teeth suspended on cords or cotton cloth.
- **Metal rings** — Makua metalworkers made rings.

## Music & performance

Museum records include a bamboo flute and a seed-pod rattle, but give no performance details.

- **Bamboo flute** — The flute has one open end, one closed end, three side holes, and simple incised decoration.
- **Seed-pod rattle** — The rattle has thirteen seed pods threaded onto fibre string and fixed to a wooden stick.

## Festivals & rituals

- **Creation legend** (*Muluku*) — Muluku created the first man and woman from two holes in the ground and gave them tools.
- **Evil spirit** (*Minepa*) — Minepa is an evil spirit in traditional Makua religion.

## Foodways

Makua people have traditionally practiced agriculture and hunting.

- **Corn grinding** — A carved ebony figure shows a kneeling woman grinding corn in front of a basin.
- **Food trade** — Before the 18th century, Makua people exchanged food, ivory tusks, and metal products for textiles, salt, and other goods.

## Oral tradition & literature

Makua oral tradition includes origin stories about Namuli and a creation story about Muluku.

- **Namuli origin story** (*Namuli*) — A legend says the Makua ancestor was the first man and woman born from Namuli, their original home.
- **Human and monkey story** (*Muluku*) — A religious legend says Muluku changed the relationship between humans and monkeys after humans disobeyed.
- **1847 ethnography** — Eugene de Froberville published an ethnography in 1847 after learning Makua traditions from more than three hundred people in Mauritius plantations.

## Language & religion

Makhuwa is the most widely spoken indigenous language of Mozambique, while Makua religion includes ancestor and nature-spirit worship and coastal Islam.

- **Makhuwa language** (*Makhuwa*) — Makhuwa is the primary Bantu language of northern Mozambique and is spoken by roughly 5.8 million Makua people.
- **Central Makhuwa** (*Makhuwa-Makhuwana*) — Central Makhuwa, also called Makhuwa-Makhuwana, is the basis of the standard language.
- **Makhuwa varieties** (*Meetto*) — Named varieties include Meetto, Chirima, Marrevone, eNahara, eSaka, and Ruvuma Makhuwa.
- **Coastal Muslim Makua** (*Maka*) — Coastal Muslim Makua people are called Maka in Makua.
