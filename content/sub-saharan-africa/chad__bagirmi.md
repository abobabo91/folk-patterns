## Overview

The Bagirmi are one of Chad’s major ethnic groups. They live mainly in the Chari-Baguirmi region and speak Bagirmi. Their king is called mbang, a title linked to the former Sultanate of Bagirmi. Sources describe farming, livestock, Islam, hunting objects, musical instruments, dance skirts, and efforts to develop written Bagirmi.

## Material culture

The recorded material culture includes hunting weapons, pottery, musical instruments, dancing skirts, and small brass figures.

### Clothing & dress

The catalogue records two dancing skirts, but gives no details about everyday dress.

- **Hide dancing skirt** — This skirt is made of hide and decorated with cowrie shells.
- **Reed-string dancing skirt** — This skirt is made from strings of reed.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Bagirmi objects include pottery, hunting weapons, ceremonial weapons, leather sheaths, and small brass figures.

- **Pottery jug** — The catalogue records a jug made of pottery.
- **Kastkniv hunting knife** (*Kastkniv*) — This curved knife was thrown at running animals and aimed at their bones.
- **Ceremonial axe** — This wooden axe has a two-pointed, lozenge-shaped head.
- **Throwing weapons** — The catalogue records throwing-knives, throwing-clubs, a ceremonial throwing-knife, a leather and leopard-skin sheath, and a curved wooden throwing-stick.
- **Brass animal figures** — Small brass figures show a horseman, a giraffe, and an ostrich.

## Music & performance

The catalogue records a drum, a flute or clarinet, and a harp, but gives no performance details.

- **Wooden drum** — This drum is carved from wood and has a hide membrane tied on with leather thongs.
- **Tin and brass flute or clarinet** — This instrument is made of tin, brass, cowrie shells, leather, and bamboo.
- **Horse-hair harp** — This harp is made of wood and has horse-hair strings.

## Dance & theatre

The catalogue records two dancing skirts.

- **Hide dancing skirt** — This dance garment is made of hide and decorated with cowrie shells.
- **Reed-string dancing skirt** — This dance garment is made from strings of reed.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not document named festivals or rites, but they describe the spread of Islam and Sufi orders among the Bagirmi.

- **Islam** — Islam was introduced by the start of the 17th century but did not fully replace traditional beliefs.
- **Tijaniyyah** — The Tijaniyyah helped bring further conversions and strengthen Islam among the Bagirmi.
- **Sanusiyya** — The Sanusiyya also contributed to conversions and the consolidation of Islam, though to a lesser extent.

## Foodways

The Bagirmi mainly farm sorghum and millet and raise several kinds of livestock.

- **Sorghum farming** — Sorghum is one of the Bagirmi’s main crops.
- **Millet farming** — Millet is one of the Bagirmi’s main crops.
- **Livestock raising** — The Bagirmi raise cattle, chickens, goats, and sheep.
- **Eat** (*sa*) — The Bagirmi language source gives sa as the word for “eat.”

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not document traditional oral genres, but they describe the growth of written Bagirmi literature.

- **Bagirmi literacy texts** — Don and Orpha Raun helped compose basic literacy texts in Bagirmi during the 1990s.
- **Bagirmi alphabet font** — In 2003, Anthony Kimball developed a font for the Bagirmi alphabet.
- **Keyman input method** — Anthony Kimball also developed a Keyman input method for Latin keyboards.
- **Published Baguirmi literature** — David Raun distributed expanding Baguirmi literature in Chad.

## Language & religion

Bagirmi is a Central Sudanic language with the autonym tàrà ɓármà, and most Bagirmi people are Muslim.

- **Bagirmi language** (*tàrà ɓármà*) — The Bagirmi autonym is tàrà ɓármà, and the language was used by the Sultanate of Bagirmi and later the Wadai Empire.
- **Bagirmi royal title** (*mbang*) — The king of Bagirmi is known as mbang.
- **Fula** — Some Bagirmi people speak Fula in addition to Bagirmi.
- **Chadian Arabic** — Some Bagirmi people speak Chadian Arabic in addition to Bagirmi.
- **Central Sudanic** — Bagirmi belongs to the Central Sudanic language family.
