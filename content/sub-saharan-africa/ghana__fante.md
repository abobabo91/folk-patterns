## Overview

The Fante live along Ghana's coast and in neighboring inland areas. Their *asafo* companies have civic, political, and ceremonial roles. Each company can have its own insignia, including appliqu?d flags whose figures and colors make claims about identity and power. The flag motifs are not a single shared pattern: their meanings depend on the company and its history.

## Material culture

The museum selection is strongest in *asafo* flags. Several show a Union Jack in the corner, placing them in the era before Ghanaian independence; others carry animals, people, or proverbs in sewn cloth. The British Museum identifies one elephant-and-palm flag as Fante work and explains its image as an assertion of strength. Two Brooklyn Museum flags add different company images.

Carved *akua'ba* figures form a second group. The British Museum attributes the selected rectangular-headed figures to Fante makers and notes that older labels sometimes confused them with Asante examples. Terracotta heads and vessels from the Agona Swedru area show another material tradition. Their excavated funerary context is more specific than a claim that all Fante pottery served the same purpose.

The collection also has carved drums, a staff, a ceremonial paddle, and two historical portraits explicitly captioned Fante or Fanti. These older museum holdings document particular objects and sitters; they do not represent all present-day Fante life.

## Glossary

- *Asafo* ? A company with civic, political, and ceremonial roles.
- *Frankaa* ? An *asafo* company flag.
- *Akua'ba* ? A carved figure; the selected Fante examples have tall, rectangular heads.
