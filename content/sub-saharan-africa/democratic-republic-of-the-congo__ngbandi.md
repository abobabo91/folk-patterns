## Overview

The Ngbandi are an ethnic group from the region of the upper Ubangi River, in the northern Democratic Republic of the Congo and southern Central African Republic. They traditionally speak Ngbandi, a language of the Ubangian family. The Ngbandi population is described as around 300,000 people, while the Ngbandi language is described as being spoken by a half-million or so people in the Democratic Republic of Congo and the Central African Republic. The population is divided mostly between speakers of Northern Ngbandi and Southern Ngbandi. Their folk-culture significance in the supplied sources is associated with subsistence farming, hunting and gathering, warfare, metalworking, knives and lances, a distinctive harp-like instrument, and beliefs concerning twins.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ngbandi craftsmen were known for making some of the most prized African knives and lances.

## Music & performance

The Ngbandi share the use of a musical instrument described as a kind of harp. Its form is distinctive to the area, and the sources connect this musical culture with wider cultural connections to Sudan.

## Festivals & rituals

They do record a traditional belief concerning twins: twins are considered *ayafo te angbo*, translated as “children of snakes.” No further ritual practice, date, season, or ceremonial context is supplied.

## Foodways

Historically, the Ngbandi were subsistence farmers, and many still grow maize, manioc, and other food crops. Until recently, some subsistence also depended on traditional hunting and gathering.

## Oral tradition & literature

The supplied material records the expression *ayafo te angbo* in connection with the traditional treatment of twins.

## Language & religion

Ngbandi is a dialect continuum of the Ubangian family. It is spoken in the upper region of the Ubangi River along the border between the Democratic Republic of the Congo and the Central African Republic. The sources distinguish Northern Ngbandi and Southern Ngbandi, and state that Northern Ngbandi is the lexical source of the trade language Sango. Sango has as many native speakers as Ngbandi and is used as a second language by millions more in the Central African Republic.

Yakoma occupies a central position on the Ubangi River, which divides the Central African Republic from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and has a high degree of intelligibility with the other varieties of Ngbandi. Gbayi or Kpatiri is described as a divergent Ngbandic language. Nzakara, a Zande language, is spoken near Gbayi. The sources also state that a variety of Ngbandi may have been spoken in the Democratic Republic of the Congo villages of Kazibati and Mongoba near Uganda until the late 20th century, though this is uncertain. The phonology includes an alternation between the sounds /l/ and /r/.
