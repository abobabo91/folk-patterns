## Overview

The Okiek, also known as Ogiek or Akiek, are an autochthonous Southern Nilotic tribe within the Kalenjin-speaking peoples. They inhabit the highland forests of Kenya, especially the Mau Forest and Mount Elgon, as well as parts of northern Tanzania. Their presence in these highlands is described as extending from time immemorial, with archaeological and historical estimates dating their lineage in the area as far back as 4000 BCE. The 2019 Kenya Census recorded their population as 52,596. Historically characterized as hunter-gatherers and honey-harvesters, the Okiek have maintained a way of life centered on forest conservation and beekeeping. Their language and cultural practices have also figured in legal struggles concerning recognition, indigeneity, and ancestral land.

## Oral tradition & literature

Ogiek oral traditions are described as maintaining that the people did not migrate from elsewhere but were already present in the highland forests from time immemorial. They do identify studies of conversations, lives, oral history, political meetings, marriage arrangement, and initiation, as well as documentation of naturally occurring conversation among Kipchorng’wonek and Kaplelach Okiek communities.

## Language & religion

Ogiek, also called Okiek or Akiek, is a Southern Nilotic language of the Kalenjin family. The language consists of several varieties, including Kinare, Sogoo or Sokóò, and Akie or Akiek. Kinare, associated with the Kenyan place Kinare on the eastern slope of the Rift Valley, is described as extinct. Sogoo was spoken in the southern Mau Forest between the Amala and Ewas Ng’iro rivers, while Akie was spoken in the southern part of the Arusha region in Tanzania. Many speakers have shifted to Kipsigis, Maasai, Swahili, or Gikuyu; the Akie in northern Tanzania now speak Maasai, and the Ogiek of Kinare now speak Gikuyu. The Okiek language is classified as critically endangered, with fluent speakers possibly numbering as few as 500.
