## Overview

The sources give little information about the Ga-Adangbe people. The catalogue records a cap, several wooden coffins, model coffins, and a fertility figurine. These objects are not clearly identified as specifically Ga-Adangbe forms.

## Material culture

The recorded material culture consists mainly of one embroidered cap and several carved or painted wooden objects.

### Clothing & dress

The catalogue records one folded, embroidered cotton cap.

- **Four-panel embroidered cap** — This cotton cap folds flat in four panels and has yellow and pink embroidery on purple cloth, a white lower border, and a red and white button at the top.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum records several carved wooden coffins and model coffins in unusual forms.

- **Eagle coffin** — The eagle coffin has painted feather markings, hinged wings, carrying handles, and a textile-lined cavity.
- **Elephant coffin** — The elephant coffin has a cavity lined with green silk and red textile.
- **Catfish coffin** — The painted catfish coffin has a hinged wooden lid over its cavity.
- **Cocoa-pod coffin** — The painted cocoa-pod coffin has a hinged wooden lid over its cavity.
- **Lager-bottle coffin** — The painted lager-bottle coffin is marked “Premium Club” and has a hinged wooden lid over its cavity.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources do not document Ga-Adangbe jewelry or body-adornment traditions, but one catalogue record describes a decorated wooden figurine.

- **Wooden fertility figurine** — This standing male figurine has a blue bead girdle and necklace, another necklace, straw around the neck, and partial white paint.
