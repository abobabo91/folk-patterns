## Overview

The Ngoni are a hybrid people created when about 1,000 Nguni migrants from South Africa crossed the Zambezi in the 1830s and settled among Tumbuka and Chewa populations. They established a state in the Viphya Plateau region and grew to encompass hundreds of thousands by the early 20th century. Today they live primarily in Malawi and Zambia, with a distinct Ngoni language also spoken in Tanzania. Their ancestral Zulu dialect survives mainly in ritual contexts as local languages became dominant.

## Material culture

Ngoni visual identity comes from worked skin, hide, and feathers rather than woven textiles, seen in warrior regalia, shields, and ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Ngoni have no documented weaving or dyeing tradition; instead, worked skin, hide, and feather create visual patterns and identity.

- **Feather belts and bands** — Belts of skin with clipped feather tufts set along the length, and head-bands combining feathers with cotton cloth and string.

### Clothing & dress

Ngoni dress is documented mainly in its warrior and ceremonial form, with elaborate ornamentation distributed across the entire body.

- **Warrior head-dress** — Brown hide sewn thickly with cock's feathers and a tuft of cow's hair hanging at the front.
- **Feather and wire plume** — A separate plume made of iron wire, feather, wood, glass beads, and thread worn with the head-band.
- **Knee-band** — Cotton cloth, iron, and haired hide hung with bells worn around the knee.
- **Waist girdle** — A girdle made of glass beads, haired skin, hide, and fiber; a variant wound with copper wire is also recorded.
- **Penis-sheath** — Made from a gourd with burnt ornament.

### Architecture

No details of Ngoni house form, building materials, or architectural decoration are documented.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ngoni household objects are made from wood, gourd, basketry, bone, iron, and earthenware, with carved and functional designs.

- **Wooden bowls** — Including a bowl standing on four legs and one blackened on the exterior and hung with a bead-covered cord.
- **Spirally woven basket** — A basket with a broad rim and a circular bowl woven from stiff cane.
- **Ornamented gourd vessel** — A container with incised and burnt triangular decoration, its crack repaired by sewing.
- **Wooden snuff-box** — A bottle-shaped box with gourd stoppers at both ends, carved ornament, and binding of tin and copper wire.
- **Iron sweat-scraper** — An iron tool with a cloth loop for attachment.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Glass beads are the dominant ornamental material, combined with copper and iron wire, bone, and stone in personal adornment.

- **Glass bead bracelet** — White glass beads worked on four-ply thread loosely twined around a string core.
- **Beaded quill pendant** — A quill encased in beadwork spiraling in white and green.
- **Beadwork apron** — Glass bead aprons worn by men and shown on carved figures in the collection.
- **Lip ornament** — A stone spar ornament indicating lip piercing.
- **Copper-wire girdle** — Copper wire wound around a fibre girdle.

## Music & performance

Ngoni musical instruments include a musical bow with gourd resonator, drums, rattles, and whistles, with no documented song genres or performance occasions.

- **Musical bow** — A bow of wood and fiber with a separate gourd resonator.
- **Gourd rattle** — A rattle with a stick handle, catalogued as part of a diviner's bag and tied to divination as much as to music.
- **Wooden whistles** — Horn-like lengths of dark brown hard wood perforated through and bound at intervals with rattan, with fiber suspension cords.

## Dance & theatre

No Ngoni dance or dramatic tradition is documented; warrior regalia suggests possible ceremonial use but no named dances are recorded.

## Festivals & rituals

The Nc'wala first-fruits festival is the only documented Ngoni celebration; ritual practice is otherwise visible through divination kits and carved figures.

- **Nc'wala** — A celebration of first fruits held in late February at Mutenguleni, about 25 km from Chipata, by the Ngoni of Zambia.
- **Carved wooden figures** — A standing woman carrying a pot on her head and a standing man wearing a headring, both in glass beadwork aprons, with no recorded ritual role.

## Foodways

Almost no details of Ngoni food are documented; they were primarily agriculturalists, but cattle were the focus of raiding expeditions rather than herding trade.

- **Nc'wala first-fruits festival** — A late-February celebration, the only documented Ngoni foodway practice.

## Oral tradition & literature

Fifteen Ngoni folk tales collected by Margaret Read from an elderly man in 1939 represent the main documented oral tradition; ritual praise poems are another surviving verbal form.

- **Fifteen Ngoni folk tales** — Collected by Margaret Read from an old Ngoni man who heard them from his grandmother, edited by Geraldine Elliot and published in London in 1939, with many reprints in Britain and the United States.
- **Ritual praise poems** — Now one of the last uses of the ancestral Ngoni Zulu dialect.

## Language & religion

Multiple Ngoni languages and dialects exist across the region; the ancestral Zulu dialect survives mainly in ritual use, while no Ngoni religious system or cosmology is documented.

- **Ngoni language of Tanzania** (*Chingoni*) — A distinct Ngoni language spoken in Tanzania.
- **Ngoni dialect of Zulu** — Spoken in Zambia and Malawi, now used only in a few ritual praise poems and songs.
- **Ngoni dialect of Nyanja** — Spoken in Malawi.
- **Ngoni dialect of Tumbuka** — Spoken in the region, reflecting integration with local populations.

## Glossary

- *Nc'wala* — First-fruits festival celebrated in late February
- *Viphya Plateau* — Region where Zwangendaba established the Ngoni state
- *Zwangendaba* — Leader who led Ngoni followers north and established their state
- *Feather belts* — Belts of skin with clipped feather tufts set along the length
- *Beadwork apron* — Glass bead apron worn by men as shown on carved figures
- *Musical bow* — Bow of wood and fiber with separate gourd resonator
- *Bush buck horn* — Horn holding powdered red wood with wooden spindle, divination tool
- *Gourd rattle* — Rattle with stick handle, used in both music and divination
- *Ritual praise poem* — Verbal form preserving the ancestral Zulu dialect in ceremony
- *Mutenguleni* — Location about 25 km from Chipata where Nc'wala is celebrated
- *Ngoni dialect of Zulu* — Ancestral language now reserved mainly for ritual praise poems and songs
- *Zambezi* — River crossed by Ngoni migrants in the 1830s during northward movement
- *Nyasaland* — Historical region (now Malawi) where 245,833 people claimed Ngoni membership by 1921
- *Tumbuka* — Local population that mixed with Nguni migrants to form the Ngoni
- *Chewa* — Local population that mixed with Nguni migrants to form the Ngoni
