## Overview

The Kiga are native to southwestern Uganda and northern Rwanda. In Uganda, they are linked with the ancient Kigezi region. Their culture includes strong clan identity, oral accounts of migration and resistance, naming practices and traditional spiritual beliefs. Their recorded material culture includes iron tools, carved wood, cattle-hide shields and goats’ hair bound with copper wire.

## Material culture

Recorded Kiga material culture includes iron weapons and tools, carved wooden objects, cattle-hide shields and a bracelet made from goats’ hair and copper wire.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional weddings may feature Kikiga-Kinyakore attire, while older generations often used Western formal clothes.

- **Kikiga-Kinyakore wedding attire** (*Kikiga-Kinyakore*) — Traditional wedding ceremonies may feature Kikiga-Kinyakore attire and customary practices.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Kiga objects include iron weapons and tools, carved wood, and cattle-hide shields.

- **Iron weapons and tools** — Recorded objects include daggers, spears, staffs, axe heads, hammers, hoe blades, a woodworking tool, a gouge, a hook, bellows and a shovel.
- **Carved wooden bowls** — The museum catalogue records carved wooden bowls.
- **Decorated mug** — The catalogue records a mug with pokerwork decoration.
- **Pedestal stool** — The catalogue records a four-legged stool on a pedestal base.
- **Cattle-hide shield** — The Kiga are described as crafting shields from cattle hide.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources record one bracelet made from goats’ hair and copper wire.

- **Goats’ hair and copper-wire bracelet** — The bracelet has a core of twisted goats’ hair bound with copper wire.

## Music & performance

A traditional folk song preserves an account of Kiga migration from Rwanda.

- **Migration folk song** (*Abakiga twena tukaruga Rwanda, omu Byumba na Ruhenjere*) — The song says that all Bakiga came from Rwanda, from Byumba and Ruhenjere.

## Dance & theatre

The Kiga are described as enjoying dancing, but no named dance or theatre form is recorded.

## Festivals & rituals

Recorded ritual life includes circumcision in one clan, customary weddings and clan totem rules.

- **Mungura circumcision rite** (*Mungura*) — In the Mungura clan, male children were traditionally circumcised in their eleventh year.
- **Traditional wedding** — Many young people have begun returning to customary Bakiga wedding practices.
- **Clan totem prohibition** — Each clan had a totem animal that members were forbidden to hunt or eat.
- **Ba-Mungwe bushbuck prohibition** (*Ba-Mungwe*) — Members of the Ba-Mungwe clan could not hunt cape bushbuck.

## Foodways

The sources do not name Kiga dishes or cooking traditions, but they record clan rules against eating certain totem animals.

- **Totemic food prohibition** — Clan members were forbidden to hunt or eat their assigned totem animal.
- **Cape bushbuck prohibition** (*Ba-Mungwe*) — The Ba-Mungwe clan could not hunt cape bushbuck.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kiga oral tradition tells of migration, royal succession, clan formation and resistance.

- **Migration song** (*Abakiga twena tukaruga Rwanda, omu Byumba na Ruhenjere*) — The folk song preserves the story that the Bakiga came from Rwanda.
- **Kakiga’s migration** (*Kakiga*) — Oral tradition says that Kakiga, son of Mbogo, migrated from Rwanda and established the Bakiga community in Kigezi.
- **Royal drum story** (*Kamuhagama*) — Traditional accounts say Kakiga fled with the royal drum Kamuhagama.
- **Rwandeme legend** (*Rwandeme*) — The loss of the royal drum by Rwandeme is presented as a legend explaining the Abasigi and differences in Rukiga speech.

## Language & religion

Kiga is a Great Lakes Bantu language, and older Bakiga belief centered on the creator Ruhanga.

- **Kiga language** (*Rukiga*) — Kiga, also called Rukiga, Ruchiga or Chiga, is spoken mainly in the ancient Kigezi region.
- **Creator deity** (*Ruhanga*) — Before Islam and Christianity, the Bakiga followed a monotheistic faith centered on Ruhanga, a creator seen as neither male nor female.
- **Other names for Ruhanga** (*Mukama, Kazooba-Nyamuhanga and Biheeko*) — Ruhanga was also known by these names.
- **Nyabingi cult** (*Nyabingi*) — The anti-centralist Nyabingi cult influenced Bakiga identity.
- **Language prefixes** (*mu, ru, ba, ki*) — Kiga uses prefixes such as mu for a person, ru for a language, ba for people and ki for customs or traditions.
