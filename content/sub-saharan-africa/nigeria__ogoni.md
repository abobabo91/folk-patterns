## Overview

The Ogoni live in a 1,050-square-kilometre homeland in Rivers State, Nigeria. Their land includes the kingdoms of Babbe, Gokana, Ken-Khana, Nyo-Khana, Tai and Eleme. Ogoni culture is especially known for wooden sculpture, masks, masquerades, pottery, language diversity and oral tradition. The Ogoni also became internationally known through protests about oil production and environmental damage.

## Material culture

Ogoni material culture is especially represented by carved wooden masks and figures, pottery vessels, raffia performance clothing and an iron sword.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not document named Ogoni textile or pattern traditions.

- **Raffia clothing** — Raffia clothing is used in performances with Ogoni masks and headdresses.
- **Plaited fibre loops** — Some pottery vessels have fibre loops attached to handles or threaded through holes in their rims.
- **Raffia cloth hoods** — Several carved wooden masks have raffia cloth hoods, sometimes with long black hair or a raffia fringe.

### Clothing & dress

The sources describe performance clothing and head-worn forms but not everyday Ogoni dress.

- **Performance raffia clothing** — Masks and headdresses may be accompanied by raffia clothing during performances.
- **Braided head helmet** — A carved wooden figure from Ogoni has a braided lower part described as a helmet for the wearer’s head.
- **Head-mounted figures** — Some carved figures were mounted on bases and were probably intended to be worn on the head.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Ogoni houses, settlements or named architectural traditions.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ogoni museum records show a varied pottery tradition and an iron sword with a carved wooden hilt.

- **Three-handled crescent bowl** — Some wide-mouthed bowls have three handles and crescent-shaped decorations.
- **Pedestal dish** — Shallow red dishes are made with short pedestals.
- **Incised globular jar** — Some globular jars have short necks and sinuous diagonal decoration cut into their surfaces.
- **Ornamented pedestal bowl** — Some pedestal bowls have six ornamental projections around the rim.
- **Ogoni iron sword** — An iron sword has a broad flat blade, a slit opposite the handle and a carved wooden hilt with a knob.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources do not document Ogoni jewelry or named body-adornment traditions.

- **Mask grass fringe** — Some masks have grass fringes.
- **Mask raffia fringe** — Some masks have raffia fringes.
- **Attached black hair** — Some masks have long black hair attached to them.
- **Painted mask surface** — Some masks have painted surfaces, especially white or brown pigment around the eyes.

## Music & performance

Ogoni performance centers on masks, headdresses, dances, masquerades and puppets, but the sources do not name instruments or song types.

- **Amanikpo society performances** (*Amanikpo*) — The Amanikpo society sponsored dances and masquerades.
- **Karikpo society performances** (*Karikpo*) — The Karikpo society sponsored dances and masquerades.
- **Fertility puppets** — Full-figure puppets were used to promote fertility and dispel evil spirits.
- **Ancestral spirit masks** — Some masks depict ancestral spirits.
- **Regulatory masks** — Some masks had regulatory roles.

## Dance & theatre

Ogoni dances and masquerades use masks, headdresses, puppets, raffia clothing and movement shaped by the environment.

- **Human-face masks** — Some masks represent human faces and may have articulated lower jaws.
- **Animal masks** — Museum records include masks representing pigs and other animal forms.
- **Skull masks** — Some masks represent skulls.
- **Horned and tusked masks** — Some masks have horns, tusks or proboscises.
- **Articulated masquerade forms** — Articulated limbs and movement add complexity and emotion to carved forms.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not document an annual festival calendar but connect performance and worship with fertility, spirits and earthly concerns.

- **Fertility performances** — Performances with masks, headdresses and puppets were connected with fertility.
- **Spirit-dispelling performances** — Some performances were intended to dispel evil spirits.
- **Bari shrine consultation** (*Bari*) — Shrines were consulted concerning earthly issues through Bari, the supreme female god.
- **Death transition** — Death was understood as a transition to the spirit world but was also seen as unnatural.
- **Taa death spirits** (*Taa*) — Death was attributed to evil spirits called Taa, also known as Poro-Edon and Yo.

## Foodways

The sources describe agriculture and palm-oil production but do not name Ogoni dishes or recipes.

- **Palm oil** — Palm oil was the main production described in the source.
- **Yams** — The climate and agriculture support yams.
- **Palms** — The climate and agriculture support palms.
- **Fruits and vegetables** — The climate and agriculture support assorted fruits and vegetables.
- **Three-legged cooking pot** — A three-legged cooking pot is carved on top of one wooden mask.

## Oral tradition & literature

Ogoni oral tradition tells of migration, Bari’s creation of the world and major social changes in modern history.

- **Migration from ancient Ghana** — Oral tradition says the Ogoni migrated from ancient Ghana to the Atlantic coast before reaching the eastern Niger Delta.
- **Igoni** — The name Ogoni is said to come from the Ibani or Ijaw word Igoni, meaning strangers.
- **Bari creation account** (*Bari*) — Oral tradition says Bari created the world and was consulted through shrines.
- **Ken Saro-Wiwa** — Ken Saro-Wiwa was a writer, environmental activist and television producer who described rapid social changes.
- **Ogoni Bill of Rights** — The Movement for the Survival of the Ogoni People presented the Ogoni Bill of Rights in October 1990.

## Language & religion

Ogoni languages form East and West clusters, while traditional religion centers on Bari and spirits linked with death.

- **Kegboid languages** (*Kegboid*) — The Ogoni languages are also called Kegboid languages.
- **East language cluster** — The East cluster includes Khana, Tẹẹ and Gokana.
- **West language cluster** — The West cluster includes Eleme and Baan.
- **Bari** — Bari, also called Wa-Bari or Kara-Bari, is the supreme female god who lived in the skies.
- **Poro-Edon and Yo** (*Poro-Edon*) — Poro-Edon and Yo are alternate names for the evil spirits called Taa.
