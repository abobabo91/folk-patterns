## Overview

The Konso are a Lowland East Cushitic people living in south-western Ethiopia. They are known for their terraced agricultural landscape, large towns with elder councils, and carved memorial figures. Museum collections preserve their woven cotton cloth, wooden tools, metalwork, and the objects they wore to mark social standing.

## Material culture

Konso material culture emphasizes handmade everyday objects — woven cloth, carved wood, worked metal and animal materials — each made and decorated with care.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Konso weavers made narrow-strip cloth from cotton, patterned with stripes rather than figured design.

- **Narrow-strip loom weaving** — Wooden looms with beaters produced rectangular cotton webs that were cut and seamed into garments.
- **Striped cotton cloth** — Warp or weft stripes in black, red, yellow, orange, green and burgundy created the pattern.
- **Fibre string work** — Girls wore pubic aprons of cotton strings; women wore dance aprons of fibre string threaded with glass beads.
- **Ensete string ornaments** (*Ensete*) — Beetle wing cases were threaded on string made from Ensete plant fibre for chest ornaments.

### Clothing & dress

Konso made clothing from woven cotton and hide, with special dress for dancing and life events.

- **Woven cotton shorts and tunic** — Men wore shorts cut from two folded rectangles with a gusset, and tunics with a neck opening at the fold.
- **Fringed hide waist garment** — A man's garment decorated with antelope bone, beads and cowrie shells.
- **Twisted goat hide belt** — A belt with two pendants cut from aluminium bullet cases.
- **Post-partum waist band** — Worn by women after birth; made of cow hide with a single cowrie shell.
- **Skin head coverings** — A boy's goat-skin cap and a hat of two stitched pieces of soft white sheep leather.

### Architecture

The Konso live in large towns, each governed by a council of elders, with a landscape shaped by irrigation and terracing.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Konso made utilitarian earthenware and decorated wooden household tools, with iron for blades and points.

- **Ring-handled water pot** — An earthenware pot with two ring handles used for boiling water.
- **Water-pipe bowl** — A pottery bowl for smoking, still holding traces of tobacco.
- **Carved wooden stool** — A stool with geometric motifs incised into the top and a metal disc at the centre.
- **Iron-tipped digging stick** — A tool with an iron point for farming and earth-working.
- **Hide shield** — A shield with a wooden strut as a handle.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Konso wore metal, beads, and animal materials as adornment, with married women and men of standing wearing distinctive pieces.

- **Hallasha forehead ornament** (*hallasha*) — A cast-metal phallus-shaped ornament worn on the forehead, expressing a cult of sexual potency.
- **Brass bracelets** — Worn by married women as a mark of matrimony.
- **Glass-bead necklets** — Three-string necklaces of glass beads linked on cotton thread.
- **Aluminium wire armlet** — A woman's armlet made from worked aluminium wire.
- **Giraffe-tail-hair ornaments** — Ear-rings and necklaces finished with giraffe tail hair.

## Music & performance

Konso made wind, struck and rattling instruments, with performance objects worn on the body rather than played separately.

- **Pan-pipes** — Eight stalks of millet bound together with vegetable fibre.
- **Wooden flute** — A flute carved from wood with four fingering holes.
- **Wooden horn** — A trumpet sounded to call assemblies and gatherings.
- **Antelope-bone rattle** — A rattle strung with antelope leg bones and decorated with glass beads.
- **Iron bell** — A struck bell with an iron clapper hung from a leather loop.

## Dance & theatre

Konso dance dress consisted of worn ornaments that produced sound through the dancer's movement.

- **Men's seed corslet** — A corslet of seeds strung on fibre with a hide waist band, made for dancing.
- **Beetle-wing-case chestlet** — Beetle wing cases threaded on Ensete string, worn diagonally across the chest by men when dancing.
- **Women's beaded dance apron** — A fibre-string apron strung with glass beads, hung from a leather belt.

## Festivals & rituals

Konso ritual life centers on generation-grading, marriage, birth and the commemoration of the dead through carved memorial figures.

- **Gadaa generation system** (*gadaa*) — A generation-grading system of social organisation shared with Oromo culture, organizing male social and ceremonial roles.
- **Waga memorial figures** (*waga*) — Carved anthropomorphic posts erected to commemorate a dead man who killed an enemy or animal; figures are grouped showing the man, his wives and slain adversaries.
- **Phallicism cult** — A religious practice expressed through the hallasha ornament and representation of sexual organs on male waga figures.
- **Bride's stick ritual** — An ivory finger ring used together with a bride's stick to mark marriage.
- **Polygyny practice** — Multiple wives were an accepted social custom among Konso men.

## Foodways

Konso farming relies on irrigation and terraced slopes, with staple crops of sorghum and corn and livestock of cattle, sheep and goats.

- **Sorghum and corn** — The staple crops grown on terraced and irrigated slopes.
- **Cash crops** — Cotton and coffee grown for trade.
- **Livestock** — Cattle, sheep and goats raised for food and milk.
- **Tobacco** — Consumed smoked in a water-pipe or taken as snuff.

## Oral tradition & literature

Konso family traditions record their origins as a composite people drawn from all surrounding areas; written Konso is recent.

- **Family traditions** — Oral narratives describing the Konso as a composite people whose members came from many surrounding areas.
- **Konso alphabet** (*Afaa Xonso script*) — An alphabet developed by the SNNPR education bureau and SIL Ethiopia, used in a Konso dictionary and for general writing.
- **New Testament in Konso** — Translated into Konso and published in 2002.
- **Ethiopic script** — An older script now used alongside the Konso alphabet for writing the language.

## Language & religion

Konso speak Afaa Xonso, a Lowland East Cushitic language with unusual implosive consonants; about 60% are Christian, others worship Waaq.

- **Afaa Xonso language** (*Afaa Xonso*) — A Lowland East Cushitic language with about 200,000 native speakers and four main dialects: Kholme, Duuro, Fasha and Karatti.
- **Implosive consonants** — Konso has a rare series of implosive stops including the uvular implosive /ʛ/, rather than voiced or ejective consonants.
- **Vowel system** — Konso distinguishes five short and five long vowels.
- **Waaq worship** (*Waaq or Wakh*) — The traditional god of the early Cushitic faith, worshipped by Konso who do not follow Christianity.
- **Christianity** — About 60% of the Konso are Christians.

## Glossary

- *Afaa Xonso* — The Konso language, a Lowland East Cushitic tongue
- *waga* — Carved anthropomorphic memorial posts erected for dead men of standing
- *hallasha* — A cast-metal phallus-shaped forehead ornament
- *Ensete* — A plant whose fibre is used for making string ornaments
- *gadaa* — A generation-grading system of social organisation
- *Waaq* — The god of the traditional Cushitic faith
- *Xonsita* — An alternative name for the Konso people
- *Konso Zone* — The administrative region in South Ethiopia where most Konso live
- *Sagan River* — The river in whose bend the main Konso settlements lie
- *Lake Chamo* — A geographical marker; Konso live south of this lake
- *Lowland East Cushitic* — A language branch of Afro-Asiatic spoken in the Horn of Africa
- *implosive stops* — Consonants produced by drawing air inward; rare in world languages
- *phallicism* — A religious cult emphasizing sexual potency and fertility symbolism
- *narrow-strip loom* — A weaving tool producing long thin rectangles of cloth
- *polygyny* — The practice of a man having multiple wives
- *elder council* — The governing body of a Konso town
- *terracing* — The practice of cutting step-like levels into hillsides for farming
- *irrigation* — Controlled water flow to agricultural fields
- *post-partum* — Referring to the period after childbirth
