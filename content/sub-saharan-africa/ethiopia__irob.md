## Overview

The Irob are an ethnic group living in a predominantly highland, mountainous area by the same name in Eritrea and Ethiopia. They speak the Saho language, and most profess the Catholic religion; the Irob are mostly farmers. Their traditional center is Alitena. Irobland is bordered by Dabri-Mela, Hado, the Afar Region, Shoumezana, Gulomakeda, and Saesi Tsaedaemba. The Irob share language and certain social structures with Saho-speaking neighbors, while several cultural practices are more similar to those of Tigrigna-speaking neighbors, especially the peoples of Agame. Their distinctive poetry and dance traditions are among the practices documented in the source.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources state that dress is among the cultural practices that resemble those of Tigrigna-speaking neighbors, especially the peoples of Agame.

## Music & performance

They describe the Irob as having a dance and step routine called *Hora* and *Alkafo*. This routine was traditionally performed in preparation for battles and is still commonly displayed during weddings and other ceremonies. The source also states that the Irob share certain social structures with Saho-speaking neighbors and that many cultural practices resemble those of Tigrigna-speaking neighbors.

The Irob economy is primarily based on agriculture, including animal husbandry, and the region is renowned for its honey.

## Dance & theatre

*Hora* and *Alkafo* are an Irob men’s dance and step routine. Traditionally, the routine was performed in preparation for battles; it is still commonly displayed during weddings and other ceremonies.

## Festivals & rituals

They do document weddings and other ceremonies as contexts in which the men’s dance and step routine *Hora* and *Alkafo* is commonly displayed.

The Irob are divided into three major subgroups, or Houses: Adgadi-Are, Buknaiti-Are, and Hasaballa. Adgadi-Are and Hasaballa are predominantly Orthodox Christians, while Bouknaiti-Are consists of Catholics.

The source records that Aiga was the location of significant battles during the Ethio-Eritrean border war (1998–2000). It also states that in early January 2021, at least 52 civilians were extrajudicially killed in Irob by Eritrean soldiers, according to the Irob Advocacy Association. These events are historical records rather than descriptions of Irob festivals or rituals.

## Foodways

The Irob economy is primarily based on agriculture, including animal husbandry. The region is renowned for its excellent honey. *Miess* / *Tej*, described in the source as a local “Beer” or honey-wine made from honey, is praised throughout the region for its quality.

The source states that food is among the cultural practices more similar to those of Tigrigna-speaking neighbors, especially the peoples of Agame.

## Oral tradition & literature

Irobs trace their lineage to one man, Summe, son of Neguse Worede-Mehret. According to Irob oral history, Summe migrated to Irobland from Tsira'e in Kilite Awla'elo, a part of Tigray, about 700 years ago. The source presents this account as an element of Irob oral history.

The Irob have an elaborate poetry-telling tradition called *Adar*.

## Language & religion

The Irob speak the Saho language. Their name is written in Geʽez as ኢሮብ and is also spelled Erob. Irob’s elders say that the term comes from the Saho word “Oroba,” meaning “welcome to our home,” although the etymology of the name Irob is described as debatable.

Most Irobs profess the Catholic religion. The Adgadi-Are and Hasaballa are predominantly Orthodox Christians, while Bouknaiti-Are consists of Catholics. The source also describes Saho-speaking neighbors as predominantly Muslim, Afars as Muslim, and other neighboring populations as Tigrigna-speaking Christians.
