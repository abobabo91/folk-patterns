## Overview

The Jukun are descendants of the people of Kwararafa. Their main divisions are the Jukun Wanu and Jukun Wapa. The Wanu are fishermen along the Benue and Niger rivers. The Wukari Federation, led by the Aku Uka of Wukari, is now their main centre.

## Material culture

Jukun material culture in the profile is best known for patterned strip cloth, ceremonial metal axes, wooden masks and carved figures.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Jukun textiles use narrow strips, supplementary weft patterns and strong geometric designs.

- **Kyadze cloth** (*kyadze*) — This wrapper is made from narrow strips and may have indigo, red and yellow threads, reversible patterns, embroidery, border strips and short fringes.
- **Cotton and silk strip cloth** (*alaari*) — White cotton cloth is decorated with dark indigo cotton and magenta alaari silk in blocks, checks, lattice forms, waves and triangles.
- **Hand-spun cotton strip cloth** — One cloth uses black or purple supplementary weft threads to make twelve bands with checked squares, lines and triangles.
- **Indigo and magenta cloth** (*alaari*) — A strip combines white cotton, two shades of indigo and magenta alaari silk in repeating colour blocks.
- **Strip-weave cloth** — A planned larger cloth has five thick central lines and triangular shapes spreading toward the edges.

### Clothing & dress

The profile records patterned kyadze wrappers but gives little information about other clothing.

- **Kyadze wrapper** (*kyadze*) — Some wrappers are made from nine or ten narrow strips and have reversible patterns, multicoloured decoration, embroidery, border strips and short fringes.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Jukun architecture.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The profile records ceremonial metal axes, wooden masks and carved human figures.

- **Ceremonial axe or hoe** — One object has an iron handle, a brass butt, an openwork disk-base and ring, and a brass head ending in a crested human head.
- **Metal axe** — Another catalogue entry simply describes an axe made of metal.
- **Horned wooden mask** — A wooden mask has eye holes and two horns.
- **Helmeted wooden figure** — One standing figure has a helmet-like head-dress, a tapering body, joined arms, a scarified face and aluminium-looking eyes.
- **Conical-head wooden figure** — Another figure has a conical head-dress and one remaining eye apparently made of aluminium coated with resin.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources do not cover Jukun jewelry or other body adornment.

## Music & performance

The sources do not identify Jukun music, but they record an ankle rattle.

- **Ankle dance rattle** — This rattle is made of palm leaf and fibre, but the catalogue does not name its dance or explain its musical role.

## Dance & theatre

The profile gives no named Jukun dances or theatre traditions.

- **Ankle dance rattle** — A palm-leaf and fibre rattle was worn on the ankle.
- **Wooden mask** — A wooden mask has eye holes and two horns, but its performance context is unknown.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not describe a Jukun festival calendar or named ceremonies.

- **Ceremonial axe** — A catalogue calls one axe ceremonial but does not explain the ceremony where it was used.
- **Traditional religions** — Before Christianity and Islam, the Jukun followed their own traditional religions.

## Foodways

The sources do not describe Jukun foods or cooking, though the Jukun Wanu are river fishermen.

- **River fishing** (*Jukun Wanu*) — The Jukun Wanu live along the Benue and Niger rivers and are fishermen.

## Oral tradition & literature

Jukun traditions tell of eastern migration, the founding of Kwararafa and rule by the Aku.

- **Migration of Agadu** (*Agadu*) — One tradition says that Agadu led a migration from the east, possibly Yemen, through Kordofan, Fitri, Mandara and the Gongola area to the Benue region.
- **Migration with the Kanuri** — Another tradition places the Jukun migration alongside the Kanuri through Wadai to Ngazargamu and describes an earlier settlement near Lake Chad.
- **Kororofa state** (*Kororofa*) — The Jukun established a state centred on the Benue River with its capital at Kororofa.
- **Divine King** (*Aku*) — The ruler of the state was known as the Aku, or Divine King.

## Language & religion

Jukun names cover several dialect groups and related language names, while older Jukun religion was distinct from Christianity and Islam.

- **Wukari dialect** (*Wukari*) — Wukari is one of the named Jukun dialect groupings.
- **Jukun Wapa** (*Wapa*) — Jukun people in Wukari, Ibi, Dampar and Wase use Wapa for themselves.
- **Jukun Jiba** (*Jiba*) — The Jukun of Kona call themselves Jiba.
- **Hausa name for the Jukun** (*Kwararafa*) — The Hausa call the Jukun Kwararafa, Kororafa or Kororofa.
- **Named language varieties** (*Djugun*) — The sources also associate Jukun with names such as Donga, Kona, Gwana and Pindiga, Jibu, Wase Tofa, Wapan, Wurkum and Djugun.
