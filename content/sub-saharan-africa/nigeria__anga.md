## Overview

The Ngas are a farming people of Nigeria's Jos Plateau, living in settlements shaped by granite-strewn terrain. They are known for narrow-strip cotton weaving, distinctive musical instruments, and a major harvest festival that marks the agricultural year. Their material culture and oral traditions are documented in museum collections and ethnographic writing, though many details of their ceremonies and daily life remain unrecorded.

## Material culture

Ngas material culture shows mastery of natural materials—woven cotton strips, basketry, ironwork, and carved wood—often decorated with geometric patterns and burnt designs.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The mbat narrow-strip cloth is the signature Ngas textile, made with white and brown cotton yarns in a pattern band down the centre.

- **Mbat narrow-strip cloth** (*mbat*) — Cotton cloth with white and brown warp and white weft, featuring a single dark pattern band running down the centre, with loose fringe at both ends.
- **Strip-sewn man's bag** — Made from seven hand-woven strips sewn together with green stripes and bleached white sections, with the original cloth fringe preserved inside.
- **Coiled basketry** — Vegetable-fibre or leaf-strip baskets with patterns worked into the coiling in red, including broad-rimmed hats and circular food covers.
- **Netted fibre bag** — Closely worked net of white vegetable-fibre string with a carrying handle and pendent strings at sides and base.

### Clothing & dress

Dress varies by rank and gender, with men wearing loin-cloths and caps while women wear waist ornaments with hanging strings and elaborate body adornment.

- **Tanned skin dress** — Probably goat skin with the central area dyed black in a pattern following the animal's natural outline, worn as ceremonial dress.
- **Chief's formal dress** — A tunic, cap, ivory bracelet, spotted cat hide worn across one shoulder, with a whip and long pipe as insignia of rank.
- **Woman's waist ornament** — String or leaf bunches hanging at front and back, worn with arm and neck ornaments, ear-plugs, and a narrow head-band.
- **Reed rain cape** — A rolled reed cape balanced on the head while walking, documented in photographs of women.

### Architecture

Ngas houses are circular structures with thatched roofs, built from granite boulders as foundations and walling, arranged in close compounds.

- **Circular dwelling** — Round house with thatched roof, using local granite boulders as foundation and wall material.
- **Relief-patterned walls** — Geometric patterns worked into the mud of exterior walls on dwellings and granaries, creating multiple patterned surfaces on a compound.
- **Granary structure** — Distinctive building type within compounds, also decorated with relief patterns in mud.
- **Doorway sitting place** — Doorways serve as social gathering spaces for sitting and holding animals or infants.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ngas pottery includes plain vessels and decorated pieces, while ironwork focuses on weapons and tools engraved or decorated with pokerwork.

- **Ritual beer vessel** — Large two-chamber pottery with an open bowl above joined by four tubes to a spheroid chamber below, decorated with rows of punched bosses.
- **Incised water-pot** — Terracotta pot with pointed base and incised ornament, used for storing water.
- **Tanged axe blade** — Iron blade set into wooden handle, some engraved or decorated with pokerwork, some weighted with iron rings.
- **Forged spearhead** — Iron weapon with riveted or tanged attachment, used in hunting and warfare.
- **Wooden stool with burnt ornament** — Four-legged stool decorated with burnt geometric patterns.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Women wear elaborate bead and metal ornaments including necklaces, arm ornaments, head-bands, and ear and lip-plugs, with hair dressed into a bowl shape.

- **Bead necklace** — Beaded neck ornament worn by women as part of regular dress.
- **Cylindrical ear-plug** — Cylindrical ornament inserted into the ear.
- **Disc-shaped lip-plug** — Disc-shaped ornament inserted into the lip, worn by women.
- **Ivory bracelet** — Ivory ornament worn by high-ranking men as a mark of status.

## Music & performance

The Ngas have two distinctive instruments: a reed zither-rattle and a leg rattle for dancing, and horn ensembles play together at gatherings.

- **Zither-rattle** — Reed or grass stems bound together with five groups of cane strings across two bridges, with a woven leaf panel on the back that closes a seed-filled cavity to create a rattle effect.
- **Leg rattle** — Six iron pieces hung with rings and strung on hide thongs, worn on the body so the dancer's steps sound it.
- **Horn ensemble** — Two or more men playing horns together outdoors, often in groups of men and boys wearing loin-cloths and head-cloths.

## Dance & theatre

Masked performance and dance are attested through their equipment: leg rattles for dancing and a fibre-and-seed helmet mask for masquerade.

- **Masquerade helmet mask** — Fibre and abrus-seed mask worn in masked performance, though its specific occasion and actors are not recorded.

## Festivals & rituals

Tsafi Tar, the major harvest festival also called Mos Tar, includes the Shooting the Moon rite that marks the close of one season and opening of the next.

- **Tsafi Tar** (*Tsafi Tar (also Mos Tar)*) — Harvest festival held at harvest time, serving as the hinge in the agricultural year rather than a fixed calendar date.
- **Shooting the Moon** — Brief rite within Tsafi Tar that marks the close of one season and opening of the next.
- **Tuput belief** (*tuput*) — Belief that a person who observed certain ritual practices may, after death, have their soul return to the body within two days and resume living.

## Foodways

Ngas farming focuses on grains grown on terraced fields in granite-strewn terrain, with millet, guinea corn and maize as main crops.

- **Millet** — Grain crop grown on terraced fields in Amper district.
- **Guinea corn** — Grain crop grown alongside millet and maize on the plateau.
- **Maize** — Cereal crop grown on terraced fields in the granite-littered plains.
- **Beer** — Prepared drink made from grains, consumed at festivals and communal gatherings, served in special ritual vessels.

## Oral tradition & literature

Ngas origins are carried in local folklore telling of migration from Bornu through villages to Plateau State, with the story naming early leaders.

- **Migration narrative** — Oral tradition holding that the Ngas came from Bornu, moved through villages, and settled on Plateau State highlands, splitting into sub-groups.
- **Gwallam** — Leader who led settlers at Kabwir according to oral tradition.
- **Kendim** — Chief of the Amper people named in oral tradition.
- **Sons of the Moon** — Ethnographic work by Deirdre LaPin (1984) that documents Ngas oral material and moon imagery linked to the Shooting the Moon rite.

## Language & religion

Ngas is an Afro-Asiatic language with possible geographic dialect divisions, and most Ngas practise a syncretic Christianity in which pre-Christian beliefs survive.

- **Ngas language** (*Ngas*) — Afro-Asiatic language spoken by the people, with no recorded writing tradition but possible geographic dialect divisions.
- **Syncretic Christianity** — Religious practice combining Christian faith with absorbed pre-Christian beliefs such as the tuput concept.
- **Tuput** — Pre-Christian belief in the return of the soul to the body within two days, surviving within Christian practice.

## Glossary

- *mbat* — Narrow-strip cotton cloth, the signature Ngas textile with pattern band and loose fringe
- *Tsafi Tar* — Major harvest festival marking the agricultural year, also called Mos Tar
- *Mos Tar* — Alternative name for Tsafi Tar harvest festival
- *Shooting the Moon* — Rite within the harvest festival marking season change
- *tuput* — Belief that the properly observant dead may return to life within two days
- *Ngas* — Afro-Asiatic language spoken by the people, also spelled Anga or Agas
- *zither-rattle* — Reed or grass instrument with cane strings and seed-filled woven panel
- *leg rattle* — Iron-piece dance rattle worn on the body and sounded by the dancer's steps
- *Gwallam* — Early leader of Ngas settlers at Kabwir in oral tradition
- *Kendim* — Chief of the Amper people in Ngas oral tradition
- *Jos Plateau* — Highlands of Plateau State, Nigeria, where the Ngas settled
- *Amper* — District of Ngas settlement with granite-strewn plains and terraced fields
- *Kabwir* — District of Ngas settlement in the Jos highlands
- *Bornu* — Homeland from which the Ngas migrated according to oral tradition
- *syncretic Christianity* — Religious practice blending Christian faith with surviving pre-Christian beliefs
