## Overview

The Itsekiri live around rivers, creeks, mangrove swamps and the Atlantic coast in southern Nigeria. Warri City is the main urban centre, while Ode-Itsekiri, also called Ale Iwere, was the historic royal capital. Fishing, trade and river commerce have shaped their history. Their culture is also known for royal traditions, oral histories and carved objects.

## Material culture

The documented material culture includes carved wooden paddles, an earthenware pot and a photograph showing personal adornment, but many everyday traditions are not recorded.

### Clothing & dress

- **Head-gear, necklace, rings and bracelets** — A museum photograph shows a young girl wearing head-gear, a necklace, finger rings and bracelets, but the catalogue does not identify them as distinctively Itsekiri.

### Architecture

The sources describe river settlements and royal centres.

- **Historic royal capital** (*Ode-Itsekiri*) — Ode-Itsekiri, also called Ale Iwere, was the historical royal capital.
- **Main urban centre** (*Warri*) — Warri City is the principal urban centre within the Itsekiri homeland.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The clearest documented objects are carved wooden paddles and one earthenware pot.

- **Crocodile ceremonial paddle** — One ceremonial paddle is shaped like a crocodile.
- **Openwork crocodile paddle** — A carved paddle has an openwork blade with a crocodile in the centre, lizards at the sides and a chain-link handle.
- **Saurian ritual paddle** — One ritual paddle has an end shaped like a saurian.
- **Leaf-blade paddle** — One wooden paddle has a leaf-shaped blade.
- **Earthenware pot** — A British Museum record lists a roughly ovoid earthenware pot with a small circular rim, but its ethnic attribution and use are not established.

### Jewelry & body adornment

- **Scarification** — A museum photograph shows a young girl with scarification on her arms, but the catalogue gives no further cultural interpretation.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources mention ceremonies and royal burials.

- **Royal burials** (*Ijala*) — Royal burials are connected with Ijala, where Ginuwa was founded and later died and was buried.
- **Olu kingship** (*Olu*) — Before the interregnum, the monarch was known as the Olu, or king, of Warri.
- **Restored monarchy** (*Ginuwa II*) — The monarchy was restored under Ginuwa II in 1936, and the title Olu of Warri returned to official use in 1952.

## Foodways

Itsekiri trade linked fish and river products with crops and goods from hinterland communities.

- **River products** — Itsekiri communities traded fish, crayfish, mangrove salt and pottery for yams, plantains, pepper and other farm produce.
- **Palm oil export** — Palm oil became the principal export as overseas trade in enslaved people declined.

## Oral tradition & literature

Itsekiri history is preserved mainly through varied oral traditions about community origins and the royal dynasty.

- **Sea-deity origin** (*Umale Okun*) — Inorin claimed descent from a son of Umale Okun, the sea deity.
- **Royal founder** (*Ginuwa*) — The main dynastic tradition traces the monarchy to Ginuwa, a son of Oba Olua of Benin.
- **Royal successor** (*Ijijen*) — After Ginuwa died, his son Ijijen succeeded him and moved the group to Okotomu.
- **Bible translation** (*Itsekiri*) — A complete Itsekiri translation of the Bible was finished in 2002.
- **Book of Common Prayer translation** (*Itsekiri*) — A translation of parts of the Book of Common Prayer was published in London in 1909.

## Language & religion

Itsekiri is a Yoruboid language with its own writing system, and the sources record early Portuguese missionary activity.

- **Itsekiri language** (*Isekiri*) — Itsekiri, also written Isekiri, is spoken mainly around Warri and nearby river communities.
- **Language branch** (*Edekiri*) — Itsekiri belongs to the Edekiri branch of the Yoruboid language family.
- **Language code** (*its*) — The language has ISO 639-3 code its.
- **Glottocode** (*isek1239*) — The language has Glottocode isek1239.
- **Portuguese mission** (*São Tomé*) — An Augustinian mission from São Tomé established itself at Ode-Itsekiri.
