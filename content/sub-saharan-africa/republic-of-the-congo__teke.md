## Overview

The Teke live mainly in the Republic of the Congo, with communities in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Gabon. Their history includes a kingdom ruled by the Makoko. Their material culture includes masks, textiles, sculptures, weapons, metal ornaments, and ritual objects. Religious life centers on Nzambi, ancestors, and spirits.

## Material culture

Teke material culture combines raffia textiles, geometric masks, figurative sculptures, weapons, metal ornaments, and ritual objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Teke textiles use raffia, color, geometric designs, and ritual maps for the dead.

- **Funerary maps** (*raffia funerary textiles*) — Three rolled raffia textiles are placed with the dead as maps of the sky, earth, and water.
- **Mask dresses** (*woven raffia dress*) — Woven raffia, fabric, feathers, and fibers attach to masks and conceal the dancer.
- **Geometric mask patterns** (*geometric motifs*) — Masks use abstract shapes, a horizontal dividing line, narrow eye slits, and dark or clay-colored paint.
- **Catalogue cloth garments** — Museum records list a braided cloth coat, silk head-cloth, fringed boy’s bib, embroidered cap, and knitted socks.

### Clothing & dress

The profile gives limited information about daily Teke clothing but describes mask costumes and several catalogue garments.

- **Dancing costume** (*raffia, feathers, and fibers*) — A woven raffia, feather, and fiber costume hides the wearer during traditional dancing ceremonies.
- **Woman’s braided coat** — A museum catalogue records a woman’s coat made of braided cloth.
- **Boy’s decorated cap** — A small embroidered cap is red with black, white, and yellow designs and has a beaded metal chain on top.
- **Child’s socks** — A museum catalogue records knitted child’s socks with geometric decoration.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Teke buildings, but they mention huts and temples used for religious life.

- **Ancestor hut** — A special hut at the edge of each village holds ancestor statues called Butti.
- **Spirit huts** — Huts near Cult of Spirits temples contain spirit statues and are places for sacrifices.
- **Spirit temples** — The Cult of Spirits meets in temples.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Teke catalogues include weapons, metal fittings, headrests, figures, and sculptures, but not pottery.

- **Bateke sword** (*Bateke*) — One catalogue sword is identified as Bateke.
- **Brass-fitted swords** — Teke swords have brass fittings and ornamented handles.
- **Openworked dagger** — A dagger has an openworked blade and a brass-mounted handle.
- **Iron-pointed spear** — The catalogues record a spear with an iron point.
- **Medicinal figures** — Teke figurative sculptures have hollowed areas for medicines or magical substances.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Teke adornment includes childhood scarification, metal jewellery, and prestige ornaments on ancestor figures.

- **Facial scarification** (*scarification*) — Scars are mainly placed on the face from the temple down the cheek during childhood.
- **Brass arm and neck rings** — The catalogues record brass arm rings and neck rings.
- **Fetish ring** (*fetish*) — An iron ring was described by its donor as a fetish against diseases and as belonging to a muteke.
- **Ancestor necklaces** — Metal necklaces on ancestor figures mark the ancestor’s prestige.
- **Sculpture facial lines** — Teke sculptures can show facial striation and tattoo-like lines.

## Music & performance

The profile names no instruments or song styles, but it connects masked performance with ceremonies and religious leadership.

- **Masked ceremonies** — Masks are used in weddings, funerals, and initiation ceremonies for young men entering adulthood.
- **Mask as identifier** — A mask identifies social and political relationships within a tribe or family.
- **Chief’s spirit rites** (*potions and spiritual bones*) — A village chief uses potions and spiritual bones in ceremonies to speak to spirits and protect the people.

## Dance & theatre

Teke masked dancing uses geometric wooden masks and costumes that hide the performer.

- **Wedding mask dance** — Masks are used in traditional dancing ceremonies for weddings.
- **Funeral mask dance** — Masks are used in traditional dancing ceremonies for funerals.
- **Adulthood initiation dance** — Masks are used in initiation ceremonies for young men entering adulthood.
- **Bite-bar mask** (*bite bar*) — The round wooden mask has a bite bar held between the wearer’s teeth.

## Festivals & rituals

Teke ritual life includes masked ceremonies, ancestor worship, spirit worship, sacrifices, and funerary maps.

- **Ancestor Cult** — The Ancestor Cult maintains places where dead ancestors are believed to live and provides food or sacrifices.
- **Cult of Spirits** — The Cult of Spirits meets in temples and uses spirit statues in nearby huts where sacrifices take place.
- **Animal sacrifice** — Animal sacrifice replaced human sacrifice by the nineteenth century.
- **Funeral map rite** — Three rolled raffia textiles are placed beside the dead to guide them toward the village of the dead and the ancestors.

## Foodways

Teke foodways are based on farming, hunting, fishing, trade, and offerings to ancestors and spirits.

- **Maize farming** (*maize*) — The Teke farm maize.
- **Millet farming** (*millet*) — The Teke farm millet.
- **Banana farming** (*bananas*) — The Teke farm bananas.
- **Fishing** — The Teke are skilled fishermen.
- **Ritual food offerings** (*offerings*) — Food offerings and sacrifices are given to deceased ancestors and spirits.

## Oral tradition & literature

The profile gives no Teke literary tradition but identifies the Makoko as the ruler of the historic kingdom.

- **Makoko kingship** (*Makoko*) — The Makoko ruled the Teke kingdom and local chiefs.
- **Royal legitimacy** — Religion formed the basis of royal legitimacy, and the Makoko was regarded as both divine and human.

## Language & religion

Teke languages are Bantu languages, and Teke religion centers on Nzambi, ancestors, and nature spirits.

- **Teke languages** (*Teke*) — Teke languages are Bantu languages spoken in the western Congo and Gabon.
- **Supreme deity** (*Nzambi*) — Nzambi created the earth, water, and sky and controls life and death.
- **Waterfall spirit** (*Nkwimbali*) — Nkwimbali is associated with waterfalls and receives sacrifices.
- **Religious elder** (*Mpugu*) — The village religious elder is called the Mpugu.
- **Authority staff** (*Tara-Mantsie*) — The Mpugu’s authority is marked by the large wooden Tara-Mantsie.
