## Overview

The Buduma are an ethnic group of Chad, Cameroon, and Nigeria who inhabit many of the islands of Lake Chad. They are predominantly fishers and cattle-herders. Their neighbors call them Buduma, meaning “people of the grass (or reeds),” but they prefer to be called Yedina. Their language is known as Yedina; Yedina is a Chadic language of the Biu–Mandara branch spoken around Lake Chad in western Chad and neighbouring Cameroon and Nigeria. The Buduma traditionally claim descent from the peoples of the Sao civilization and Kanem-Bornu Empire. Their material culture is closely connected to water, reeds, fishing, cattle, and movement across the lake.

### Architecture

The Buduma extensively make use of papyrus reeds. The reeds are used for constructing lightweight huts that can be moved to higher ground if the lake rises. The sources do not provide a vernacular name for the huts, describe their roof types or decoration, or discuss settlement organization, urban traditions, workshops, or other architectural forms. A museum record describes a model passenger canoe made of reed, while another describes a model boat made of two bundles of rushes held together with twisted cotton [?] cord; the rushes were bound and bent up at the tapered end.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum catalogue records pottery water-pots, a pottery jug or mug with a handle at the side, and a pottery bowl with a flaring foot whose rim is damaged. It also records an iron spear with a bifid point resembling a pitch-fork and a wooden shaft, a socketed iron hoe with a wooden haft, and a fishing implement made of a sickel-shaped bone. Other listed household and working objects include a sieve or strainer made of reed and grass, a wicker basket with no handle and a pointed base, a coiled dish made in basketry, and an oval mat made from reeds. The sources do not describe local names, manufacturing centers, decoration, or exchange practices.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The museum records bracelets made of brass and iron. One iron bracelet is penannular, half-twisted, and has coiled terminals; another is twisted with three loops round the circumference. The catalogue also lists additional bracelets made of brass and iron. The sources do not identify their ritual functions or describe tattoos, henna, hair practices, or other forms of body adornment.

## Foodways

The Buduma are largely fishermen and livestock herders. Some are engaged in commercial fishing, while many fish for personal or familial subsistence. Staples foods include fish, cow milk, water lilly roots that they grind to flour, and other foods native to the region. Although they use or consume many products derived from their cattle, the Buduma do not commonly kill and eat them. The sources do not describe named dishes, cooking methods, ceremonial foods, tea or coffee traditions, or dietary rules beyond these statements.

## Language & religion

Yedina, also known as Buduma or Boudouma, is a Chadic language of the Biu–Mandara branch. It is spoken around Lake Chad in western Chad and neighbouring Cameroon and Nigeria. The source states that 200 speakers live in Cameroon and that the rest live in Chad. The Buduma prefer the name Yedina, while Buduma is the name used by their neighbors and means “people of the grass (or reeds).” The Buduma are Muslims. They were converted by Islamic missionaries during the era of French colonialism in Chad, and they still incorporate many traditional beliefs and practices into their Islamic practices. The sources do not discuss dialects, historical scripts, named religious sects, or specific spiritual practices.
