## Overview

The Mafa, natively called *Mofa*, are an ethnic group localized in northern Cameroon and Nigeria. They live in the central part of the Northern Mandaras, a region formed by the North area of the Mokolo Plateau and the mountains of northern Mokolo. Their society is divided into several cantons, including Moskota, Koza, Gaboua, and Mokolo arrondissement. Population estimates place the group at 365,200, while 136,000 Mafa speakers are recorded in Cameroon. The Mafa belong to the Chadic language group and speak Mafa, which has Central Mafa, West Mafa, and East Mafa dialects. Their documented folk-culture significance includes highly developed hillside terraces and other soil-management practices, as well as the internationally circulated *Vie de Jesus Mafa* artwork.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They do document constructed terraces on hillsides, which are part of the agricultural landscape and are described as having reached a state of exceptional perfection.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Mafa people used alluvial methods of mining to search for iron sand and utilize it for magnetite ore in Cameroon.

## Music & performance

The sources do document *Vie de Jesus Mafa*, also called *Life of Jesus Mafa* or *Jesus Mafa*, a series of artwork created in the 1970s through collaboration between the French Catholic priest François Vidil and the Mafa community. The works depict events in the life of Jesus through Black depictions rather than White ones and were based on real-world recreations of biblical scenes by Mafa people. They became popular worldwide, particularly among African Americans, as an inculturated form of Catholic iconography. The works were acquired by the Josephites, a religious society of priests serving African Americans; a collection remains at their seminary in Washington, D.C., where its pastoral center continues to sell prints. The collection has also been added to the United States Library of Congress.

## Festivals & rituals

They do document the creation of *Vie de Jesus Mafa*, whose subject is the life of Jesus and whose scenes were recreated by Mafa people in collaboration with François Vidil in the 1970s.

## Foodways

Mafa agricultural practice is based on a wide assortment of soil-management techniques. These include small-scale irrigation, canalization, drainage systems, crop rotation, mixed cropping, agro-forestry, biomass management, and nutrient management. Mafa agriculturists also use an intensive livestock-raising system in managing soil fertility. Livestock includes smallstock and a limited amount of cattle. During the dry season between December and May, livestock is allowed to roam freely so that it can consume crop residues and leaves of wild bushes. During the farming season, livestock is placed in a pen and fed. Manure accumulated in the stables is collected, preserved, and spread in the fields at the end of the dry season. Termites are used to digest harvest residues, which are then fed to chickens.

## Language & religion

Mafa is an Afro-Asiatic language spoken in northern Cameroon and Northern Nigeria by the Mafa people. It belongs to the Chadic language group. Mafa is widely spoken in the department of Mayo-Tsanaga from Mokolo to the north and includes Central Mafa in Koza commune and Mokolo town, West Mafa in the northwest of Mokolo Commune at Magoumaz, and East Mafa in the northeast in Soulede and Roua communes. The language has 136,000 speakers in Cameroon.
