## Overview

The Afizere, also known as Afizarek and by the exonym Jarawa, are an ethnic group in Nigeria. They occupy Jos East, Jos North, parts of Jos South and Mangu Local Government Areas of Plateau State, as well as parts of Toro and Tafawa Balewa Local Government Areas of Bauchi State. More than 500,000 Afizere people, distributed over 16 major traditional districts, inhabit these territories. They speak Izere, which is considered part of the Benue-Congo language group and is spoken in five dialects. Their cultural life includes a traditional chieftaincy system headed by an Agwom, named dances including Asharuwa and Amata, distinctive clothing, traditional beliefs, and a range of foods prepared from muster, maize, millet, guinea corn, vegetables, beans, meat, and other ingredients.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Kings wear *kukpra* and have their hair barbed in a row along the middle of the head, with step-by-step horizontal cuts throughout; this signifies the crown. Kings carry a ceremonial staff called an *akbong*, with one strand. Priests wear the same clothes as kings, but their hair and staffs distinguish them: the priest’s *akbong* has two strands.

Men wear *itak* around their waists. Women wear *akpi*, made from *atufa* or *agindo*, covering the chest and going around the waist. *Kurus* are used to keep babies tight on their mothers’ backs and are water resistant. Brides and grooms wear the clothing they would normally wear, but it is freshly made.

The sources also describe Afizere colors and their order. Green, *naya*, signifies agriculture; white, *cha’an*, signifies peace; yellow, *izizere*, signifies the language; black, *aga’ab*, signifies strength; and cowries, *icribi*, signify wealth.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources describe the barbed hairstyle associated with kingship. The hairstyle consists of hair like a row in the middle of the head and step-by-step horizontal cuts throughout, signifying the crown. The sources also mention cowries, *icribi*, as signifying wealth.

## Music & performance

They state that beating drums and singing on the farm is customary, with the *asum-mata* leading.

The Afizere maintain dance groups associated with **Asharuwa**, a traditional dance. Asharuwa dance groups have performed in the US, UK, Canada, Germany, South Africa, Ukraine, and Kenya.

## Dance & theatre

The sources name several Afizere dances and specify their performers or contexts. **Asharwa** is predominantly performed by young Afizere boys and men on all occasions. **Amata** is predominantly performed by Afizere women, both young and old, on all occasions. **Agba** is performed by men, both young and old, during royal outings, coronations, or royal ceremonies.

**Agafu** is performed by Afizere men on all occasions. **Asurbe** is performed by all categories of Afizere during the funeral rite of an elderly person only. **Natoo abarshi/ikap/isun** is performed mostly during farming and harvest, primarily by Afizere men. **Apanga** is performed only by royalty during ceremonies and coronations. **Natoo rekuron** is predominantly performed by young and elderly women only during farming activities.

## Festivals & rituals

They do, however, associate particular dances with royal ceremonies, coronations, farming, harvest, and funerals.

**Agba** is performed during royal outings, coronations, or royal ceremonies, while **Apanga** is performed only by royalty during ceremonies and coronations. **Natoo abarshi/ikap/isun** is connected with farming and harvest, and **Natoo rekuron** is performed only during farming activities. **Asurbe** belongs specifically to the funeral rite of an elderly person.

For marriage, the sources state that brides and grooms wear the clothing they would normally wear, but that it is freshly made.

## Foodways

The Afizere have different foods and modes of preparation that distinguish them from other ethnic groups. The sources name several dishes and ingredients.

*Ikam Itson*, described as hungry rice, is made from muster and water. *Akare*, also called *Gwate* and *Pate*, is made from ground maize, vegetables, garden eggs, sorel, *yakuwa*, and water. *Iririr* is made from ground *abusu*, meat, a little water, salt, pepper, and *nami rifar*, identified as olive oil.

*Nakan* is made from hungry rice, identified as muster seed, honey, and water. *Akpam* is made from hungry rice, identified as muster, red oil, and water. *Asirik itcha* is made from millet and tamber. *Asirik Itson* is made from ground muster, or hungry rice, and water. *Asirik Ariron* is made from *ariron* and guinea corn.

*Rining Kapkok*, identified as soup, is made from *Izōs*, *rituh*, *natok*, *kapkok*, salt, and water. *Ishutuk*, identified as round-about beans, is made from *Ishutuk*, *abusu*, salt, pepper, and *namai rifar*.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state only that Izere people, like many other tribes or ethnic groups, have unique names given to children at birth, and that a child could be named according to a situation or circumstance leading to the child’s birth.

## Language & religion

Izere is the language of the Afizere and is considered part of the Benue-Congo language group, described in the source as prominent in Central Nigeria. Izere is spoken in five dialects: *Ibor*, spoken largely in the Fobur district; *Isum*, spoken in Forsum villages; *Iganang*, spoken in Shere; *Ifudere*, spoken in Federe; and *Ikyo*.

Christianity and Islam are the two major religions among the Afizere, while some Afizere continue to follow traditional beliefs. Traditional Afizere religion recognizes a supreme deity, *Adakunom*, described as “father of the sun,” “father, the sun,” or “mighty sun,” and regarded as the creator and source of life and health. Minor gods act as mediators to Adakunom, while spirits or witches are described as sources of both good and evil.

Christianity came through Sudan Interior Mission preachers. Islam came to the region after the Fulani jihad, when part of Afizere territory came under the authority of the Emir of Bauchi.
