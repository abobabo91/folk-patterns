## Overview

The Egba are a subgroup of the Yoruba people, an ethnic group of western Nigeria. Most Egba are from the central part of Ogun State, corresponding to Ogun Central Senatorial District, while others are located in Lagos West, Lagos East, Oyo North, and Oyo South senatorial zones. Ogun Central comprises Abeokuta North, Abeokuta South, Ewekoro, Ifo, Obafemi Owode, and Odeda local governments. The people are directly connected to the Ogun River and historically occupied territory bordered by Ketu (Benin), the Lagos Colony, Ijebu, Oyo, Ibadan, and Isoya near Ile Ife. Their historical centre is Abeokuta, a city whose name means “under the rock,” founded after wars with the Dahomey in which Olumo Rock provided protection.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The source describes clothing for men and women. Men wear trousers called *Kembe* or *sokoto*, with a *Buba* and *Agbada* as tops. Their cap is a *Fila*, also identified as *abeti aja*. Women wear a wrapper called *Iro*, a blouse called *Buba*, and a headgear or head tie called *Gele*. An additional cloth, *Ipele*, is placed on the shoulder or wrapped around the waist.

### Architecture

 They identify Abeokuta as the principal historical settlement and record that the original town and settlement of the Egba nation was under and around Olumo Rock.

## Music & performance

The source records an Egba anthem. Its text refers repeatedly to Abeokuta, Egba identity, Olumo, freedom, and Lisabi. The anthem includes the refrain “Maa yo, maa yo, maa yo o; l’Ori Olumo.” It also names Abeokuta as “ilu Egba” and expresses attachment to the city and to Olumo.

The sources identify several Egba-associated individuals as musicians, including Ebenezer Olasupo Obey-Fabiyi, Olufela Olusegun Oludotun Ransome-Kuti, Femi Kuti, Seun Kuti, Made Kuti, Sir Shina Peters, Fela Sowande, and Waheed Ayinla Yusuf, also known as Omowura.

## Dance & theatre

 They identify several Egba-associated performers and musicians, including Tunde Kelani, a cinematographer and director, and Olu Jacobs, an actor, but provide no description of performance traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

 They record that the Jagunna of Itoko, an Oke Ona chief, is the high priest of Olumo and that Olumo Rock is under the control of the Itokos. The source also states that Adagba, a hunter, discovered Olumo Rock while searching for game animals from Obantoko township.

Historically, the Egba group was originally under the Oyo Empire and became independent after the collapse of Oyo in the first half of the 19th century. Wars with the Dahomey contributed to the founding of Abeokuta. The Egba nation is described as comprising Ake, Owu, Oke Ona, and Gbagura, each with its own king. During colonial rule, the British recognised the Alake as the paramount ruler of the whole clan and territory.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They record an Egba anthem and identify several literary and cultural figures associated with the group. J. F. Odunjo is described as a Yoruba literary icon and writer of the *Alawiye* series. Wole Soyinka is identified as an author, activist, and Nobel laureate. Tunde Kelani is identified as a cinematographer and director.

Egbaland was also the home of the first newspaper in Nigeria, *Iwe Iroyin*. The source states that its people became the first of the many Nigerian nations, until recently the only one of them, to have an anthem.

## Language & religion

The sources identify the Egba as a subgroup of the Yoruba people. They record the religious title of the Jagunna of Itoko, an Oke Ona chief, who is described as the high priest of Olumo.

The etymology of “Egba” is disputed in the source. One proposed derivation connects it with *Ẹ̀gbálugbó*, meaning wanderers towards the forest, while another connects it with *Ẹsẹ̀gbá*, the title of a chief who led several groups of the Egba to their present location. The source also links the Egba ancestors with the region of the Oyo Empire and the Egba Forest.
