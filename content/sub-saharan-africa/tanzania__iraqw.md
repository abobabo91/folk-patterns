## Overview

The Iraqw, also called Iraqu, are a Cushitic ethnic group living in northern Tanzania, especially in southwestern Arusha and Manyara regions near the Rift Valley. They are concentrated southeast of Ngorongoro Crater in northern Karatu District, Arusha Region, and in Manyara’s Mbulu, Babati, and Hanang districts. Their core area is Iraqw’ar Da/aw, also called Mama Issara, in the Mbulu Highlands. In 2001, their population was estimated at around 462,000, while current estimates in the source place it at about 1,000,000. The Iraqw speak Iraqw, a South Cushitic language of the Afro-Asiatic family, and also speak Swahili. Their folk-culture significance in the sources is connected chiefly with intensive agriculture, irrigation, oral traditions, and historical associations with the Engaruka complex.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The Iraqw are associated in historical accounts with the Engaruka complex in Monduli District, Arusha Region, Tanzania. The site contains stone-walled canals, dams, and furrows connected with irrigation practices. The sources state that the modern Iraqw practice an intensive form of self-contained agriculture resembling the agricultural system represented by these remains.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They describe irrigation structures at Engaruka, including stone-walled canals, dams, and furrows.

## Foodways

 They do describe agriculture and animal husbandry as practices associated with the Iraqw and with related historical populations, and they characterize Iraqw agriculture as intensive and self-contained. No particular crop, preparation, or food tradition is specified.

## Oral tradition & literature

Iraqw oral tradition refers to Lake Manyara as *Tlawta Moyar tsar*, meaning “Second Lake Moya.” The associated legend says that the first Lake Moya lies far north, probably at Lake Turkana near the Kenya–Ethiopia border. Historical traditions also relate that the Iraqw’s last significant migration to their present area occurred after conflicts with the Barbaig subgroup of the Datog Nilotes. The sources describe oral traditions concerning the Engaruka area and the arrival of Maasai ancestors during the 18th century.

## Language & religion

Iraqw is a Cushitic language spoken in Tanzania’s Arusha and Manyara regions. It belongs to the South Cushitic branch of the Afro-Asiatic family and has many Datooga loanwords, particularly in poetic language. Gorowa, spoken to the south, shares numerous similarities with Iraqw and is sometimes considered a dialect. Iraqw also has fourteen different plural suffixes according to the source, and nouns have masculine, feminine, and neuter genders. The language’s popular orthography largely follows Swahili orthography, with additions including x and q. The sources mention Iraqw Folk Religion and its prophet Saigilo.
