## Overview

The Budu live in an isolated tropical rainforest region in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. They live on both sides of the Nepoko River and consider themselves one people despite different dialects. Their economy is based mainly on subsistence agriculture, with palm oil as the main cash crop. Their material culture includes bark clothing, iron weapons, a war whistle, a necklace of human teeth, and an ivory armlet.

## Material culture

Budu material culture combines beaten bark, palm leaves, iron, wood, fibre, human teeth, ivory, copper, and hide.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Budu people wear beaten-bark loin cloths and also use second-hand western clothes.

- **Budu bark loin cloth** — This loin cloth is made from beaten bark.
- **Second-hand western clothes** — The sources report that Budu people use second-hand western clothes.

### Clothing & dress

The clothing described consists of beaten-bark loin cloths and second-hand western clothes.

- **Bark loin cloth** — People wear loin cloths made from beaten bark.
- **Second-hand western clothes** — People also use second-hand western clothes.

### Architecture

Budu houses are square huts with mud walls and palm-leaf roofs.

- **Square hut** — Budu people live in square huts with mud walls and roofs thatched with palm leaves.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The recorded Budu objects include iron weapons and knives with wooden, iron, copper, and hide parts.

- **Arm-dagger** — This weapon has an iron blade, wooden handle, and sheath.
- **Sword** — The sword has an iron blade and wooden handle.
- **Spears** — The spears have iron spear-heads and barbs.
- **Ceremonial knife** — Some knives have sickle-shaped iron blades, iron-wire-covered handles, or plain wooden handles.
- **Sickle-shaped knife** — One ceremonial knife has a copper blade and wooden handle.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Recorded adornments include a necklace of human teeth and an ivory armlet.

- **Human-tooth necklace** — The necklace is made of human teeth strung on twisted fibre cord.
- **Ivory armlet** — The circular armlet is made of ivory and is now brown with oil.

## Music & performance

The only recorded musical object is a war whistle made from hollow wood and metal strips.

- **War whistle** — The whistle is a long conical piece of hollow wood bound with iron and copper strips, with a plaited fibre band for suspension.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not describe Budu dances, theatre, masked performance, or dramatic traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe elder consensus and Christian activity but no festival calendar or life-cycle rites.

- **Elder consensus** — Important decisions are made by a consensus of elders.
- **Catholic church activity** (*Catholic church*) — The Catholic church has been active with the Budu.
- **WEC International activity** (*WEC International*) — WEC International, or Worldwide Evangelisation for Christ, has been active with the Budu.
- **Charles Studd’s burial** — Charles Studd, the founder of WEC, died in Budu territory and is buried in Ibambi.

## Foodways

The Budu mainly farm crops and trade with the Mbuti for meat and other forest resources.

- **Subsistence agriculture** — The main crops are cassava, yams, corn, rice, peanuts, plantains, and pulses.
- **Palm oil** — Palm oil is the main cash crop and gives an income per capita of about $120 a year.
- **Poultry and goats** — Animal husbandry is rare and is limited to poultry and some goats.
- **Forest exchange** (*Mbuti*) — The Budu obtain meat and other forest resources through trade with the Mbuti people.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not describe Budu stories or literature, but they mention the local Matta variety of the language.

- **Matta variety** (*Matta*) — A variety called Matta is spoken both north and south of Maboma.

## Language & religion

Ɨbʉdhʉ is the Budu language, with local dialect differences and active Catholic and WEC missions.

- **Budu language** (*Ɨbʉdhʉ*) — Ɨbʉdhʉ, also called Budu, is a Bantu language spoken in Wamba Territory.
- **Matta variety** (*Matta*) — Matta is spoken locally both north and south of Maboma.
- **Koya dialect** (*Koya*) — The sound [z] occurs only in the Koya dialect.
- **Budu dialects** (*Budu*) — People on the two sides of the Nepoko River speak different dialects but consider themselves one people.
- **WEC International** (*Worldwide Evangelisation for Christ*) — WEC International, also called Worldwide Evangelisation for Christ, has been active with the Budu.
