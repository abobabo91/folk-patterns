## Overview

The Nalu are a West African people living mainly along the coasts of Guinea and Guinea-Bissau. They settled in the region before the Mandé or Mandinka peoples and have been described as “pre-Mandingas.” Their language is endangered, with speakers shifting toward Susu in Guinea. Sources document a small group of everyday objects and a crocodile-shaped wooden mask, but give little information about clothing, buildings, music, rituals, or stories.

## Language & religion

Nalu is an endangered Atlantic language spoken mainly by adults, while Nalu religious beliefs are not documented.

- **Nalu language** (*Nalu*) — Nalu, also called Nalou, nalɛ, or nul, has six dialects and is spoken in Guinea and Guinea-Bissau.
- **Nalu language variants** (*Nalou*) — Nalou is another spelling of the Nalu language.
- **Nalu language variant** (*nalɛ*) — nalɛ is another name for the Nalu language.
- **Nalu language variant** (*nul*) — nul is another name for the Nalu language.
- **Shift toward Susu** (*Susu*) — Nalu speakers in Guinea are shifting toward Susu, although the language continues in a few remote villages around Katoufoura.
