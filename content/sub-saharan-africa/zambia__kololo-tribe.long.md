## Overview

The supplied sources identify the Kololo tribe in Zambia / Sub Saharan Africa. The general discussion of tribes describes the term as contested and as referring, in different contexts, to social groups organized through factors such as kinship, ethnicity, language, dwelling place, political affiliation, religious beliefs, oral tradition, or cultural practices. It also notes that tribal boundaries can be fluid, heterogeneous, and dynamic. No source supplied here describes the Kololo tribe’s distinctive contribution to folk culture.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

### Clothing & dress

The sources provide no garment names, head coverings, belts, footwear, hairstyles, body adornment, or distinctions between men’s and women’s dress. The museum catalogue does not describe clothing among the objects listed for the group. One Museum of Ethnography record contains catalogue terms including “män, stol, käpp, kung, fotografi, photograph,” together with the names or terms “marote,” “rotse,” “barotsi,” “malozi,” “kololo,” “roz,” “silozi,” and “lozi,” but the supplied record does not explain their relation to dress.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum catalogue records several objects with iron, wood, brass wire, and brass nails. These include a dagger with an iron blade and wooden sheath bound with brass wire; a knife with an iron blade in a black-wood sheath bound with brass wire; an axe with an iron blade and a knob-headed handle covered with brass binding; a sword or long knife with a two-edged iron blade and a handle covered with brass wire; and another axe with a flat iron blade and dark-wood handle bound with brass wire. The catalogue also lists a knife with an iron blade and wooden sheath, an axe with a wooden handle through which an iron blade passes, and a “Despatch box” with a hinged lid, brass nails, and a handle terminating in the shape of a gun stock.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Brass wire and brass binding occur on several museum-catalogued weapons and objects, but the records do not identify these materials as jewelry or body adornment.
