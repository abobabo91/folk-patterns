## Overview

The Yakoma are an ethnic group who primarily reside in the Central African Republic. As of June 2008, they made up 4% of the country's population, while 10,000 lived in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Their lands were used by the French for their post at les Abiras, the first capital of Ubangi-Shari, predecessors to the modern-day Central African Republic. The city of Yakoma takes its name from the people. The Yakoma speak a dialect known as Yakoma, similar to Sango, within the Ngbandi dialect continuum of the Ubangian family.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources identify Adrienne Yabouza as a writer and do not provide further information about Yakoma folktales, epics, proverbs, riddles, storytelling, or literary preservation.

## Language & religion

Yakoma is a dialect of the Ngbandi language continuum of the Ubangian family. Ngbandi varieties are spoken in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and the Central African Republic; Yakoma has a central position on the Ubangi River, which divides the Central African Republic from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and has a high degree of intelligibility with other varieties of Ngbandi. Northern Ngbandi is the lexical source of the trade language Sango, which is also used as a second language by millions in the Central African Republic.
