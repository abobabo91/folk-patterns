## Overview

The Kotoko, also called Mser, Moria, Bara and Makari, are a Chadic ethnic group located in northern Cameroon, Chad and Nigeria. Their population is approximately 90,000, with the majority living in Cameroon. The Kotoko form part of the Chadic people, and their mother tongue is Lagwan alongside other Mandage languages. They founded the Kotoko kingdom in c. 1500 CE and are considered descendants of the Sao civilization. Most Kotoko are Sunni Muslims. Their documented subsistence includes fishing with long canoes and agriculture; fish is smoked or dried and sold in local markets, while wealthier families raise cattle.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state that most Kotoko profess Islam and are Sunni Muslims. The Kotoko began adopting Islam around the 16th century, while other communities did not convert until the 18th century. Their conversion was associated with the growing presence of Muslim merchants and clerics from the Bornu Empire, and many traditional beliefs and practices are incorporated into Kotoko Islamic practices.

## Foodways

The sources document fishing and agriculture among the Kotoko. Fish caught with the aid of long canoes is subsequently smoked or dried and sold in local markets. Wealthier families also raise cattle.

## Language & religion

The Kotoko are associated with the Chadic branch of the Afro-Asiatic family. Their mother tongue is Lagwan, alongside other Mandage languages. The related Central Chadic or Biu–Mandara languages are spoken in Nigeria, Chad and Cameroon. A reconstruction of Proto-Central Chadic was proposed by Gravina in 2014. The Proto-Central Chadic phonology is described as consisting of 31 consonants, three vowels and a morpheme-level palatalization prosody; the three vowel phonemes are /a/, /i/ and /ɨ/. Most Kotoko are Sunni Muslims. Islam began spreading among the Kotoko around the 16th century, and traditional beliefs and practices are incorporated into Islamic practices.
