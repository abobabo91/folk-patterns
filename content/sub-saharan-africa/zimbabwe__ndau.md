## Overview

The Ndau are based mainly in south-eastern Zimbabwe and also live in Mozambique and Malawi. They have historical ties with Kalanga, Venda, and Shona peoples, with later Nguni influence. Ndau is a Shona language and became an official language in Zimbabwe in 2013. Recorded traditions include distinctive textiles, songs, proverbs, healing, divination, and spirit practices.

## Material culture

Ndau material culture is known mainly through traded textiles, herbs, skins, bones, beads, and objects linked with divination.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Ndau traded distinctive red, white, and blue cloths in Mapungubwe-centered networks.

- **Red cloth** (*Mpalu*) — Mpalu was a distinctive red textile traded by the Ndau.
- **White cloth** (*Njeti*) — Njeti was a white textile traded by the Ndau.
- **Blue cloth** (*Vukotlo*) — Vukotlo was a distinctive blue textile traded by the Ndau.

### Clothing & dress

The sources name traded cloths, animal skins, and bones but do not describe how Ndau people dressed.

- **Traded cloths** (*Mpalu, Njeti, Vukotlo*) — The Ndau traded red, white, and blue cloths, but the sources do not say how they were worn.
- **Animal skins and bones** — Animal skins and bones were among the goods traded by the Ndau.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Ndau houses, buildings, or architectural traditions.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded Ndau-associated goods include trade materials and objects linked with divination.

- **Gourd flasks and containers** — The museum catalogue records flasks and containers made of gourd as divination equipment.
- **Cane basket** — The catalogue records a basket made of cane among divination objects.
- **Mixed-material container** — The catalogue records a container made of gourd, skin with hair, cord, and metal.
- **Porcelain chief’s ornament** — The catalogue records a chief’s ornament made of porcelain.
- **Traditional herbs** (*mushonga*) — Traditional herbs were among Ndau trade goods, and mushonga is traditional medicine used by healers.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention golden beads and several bead, shell, leather, and cord ornaments.

- **Golden beads** — Golden beads were historical Ndau trade goods.
- **Cowrie-shell head-band** — The catalogue records a head-band made of cowrie shells, leather, and string.
- **Glass-bead belt** — The catalogue records a belt with thongs made of glass beads, leather, and cord.
- **Glass-bead necklet** — The catalogue records a necklet made of glass beads, cloth, and string.
- **Fly-whisk** — The catalogue records a fly-whisk with a ring made of wood, horse tail, cord, and glass beads.

- **Dance songs** — The sources refer generally to Ndau dance songs.

## Festivals & rituals

Ndau ritual life includes rain ceremonies, healing, spirit capture, weather work, and divination.

- **Spirit capture ceremony** (*ukufemba*) — In ukufemba, a spirit introduces itself and explains how it entered a family.
- **Traditional healers** (*n'anga*) — N'anga communicate with spirits and use mushonga to heal or harm people.
- **Weather appeals** (*nzuzu*) — N'anga appeal to the nzuzu to influence weather during droughts or floods.
- **Divination spirits** (*madzvoka*) — N'anga invite madzvoka to possess them so they can perceive distant places and events.
- **Rain ceremony** (*Mate'ka*) — Mate'ka is a song connected with the rain ceremony.

## Language & religion

Ndau is a mutually intelligible Shona language, and traditional identity centers on Musikavanthu and Mwari Musikavanthu.

- **Ndau language** (*chiNdau*) — Ndau, also called chiNdau, Chindau, Ndzawu, Njao, and Chidanda, is a Bantu and Shona language.
- **Common ancestor** (*Musikavanthu/Mlambo*) — Traditional Ndau identify as one people through Musikavanthu/Mlambo, regarded as a common ancestor and rainmaker.
- **Creator** (*Mwari Musikavanthu*) — Mwari Musikavanthu, also called Musikavanhu, is regarded as the earthly embodiment of the Creator of the Universe.
- **Christianity** — Many Ndau people embraced Christianity through missionary activity.
