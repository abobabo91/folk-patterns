## Overview

The Tabwa are associated with Zambia and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Their language is Taabwa, a Bantu language spoken by about half a million people. The profile has limited ethnographic information beyond language studies and museum records. Those records describe carved wooden objects, an ivory comb, and figures wearing glass beads.

## Material culture

The documented material culture centers on carved wood, ivory, human figures, geometric designs, and glass beads.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Museum records describe several carved wooden objects and an ivory comb.

- **Carved wooden seat** — It has a round seat, open-work carving around its base, a tall back topped by a human head with a ponytail, and geometric designs.
- **Wooden baton** — It is topped by a carved human head.
- **Wooden staffs** — The records include several carved wooden staffs.
- **Seated female figure** — It is a wooden carving of a seated woman.
- **Ivory comb** — It has a standing female figure carved on top.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention glass beads worn by carved human figures.

- **Glass-bead necklace or string** — One standing male figure wears a string of glass beads, and another female figure wears glass beads.

## Language & religion

Taabwa is a Bantu language spoken in Zambia and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, while religion is not covered.

- **Taabwa** — Taabwa is a Bantu language spoken by half a million or so people.
- **Ichitaabwa** — Ichitaabwa is another name for Taabwa.
- **Kitabwa** — Kitabwa is another name for Taabwa.
- **Rungu** — Rungu is another name for the language.
- **Malungu** — Malungu is given as another name for Rungu.

## Glossary

- *Taabwa* — Name of the Tabwa language
- *Ichitaabwa* — Another name for Taabwa
- *Kitabwa* — Another name for Taabwa
- *Tabwa* — Name used for the people and language
- *Rungu* — Another name for the language
- *Malungu* — Another name for Rungu
