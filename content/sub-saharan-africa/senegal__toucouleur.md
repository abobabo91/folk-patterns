## Overview

The Toucouleur live mainly along Senegal’s Senegal River valley, with smaller communities in nearby countries. Farming, fishing and cattle raising are central activities. They were among the first Muslims in the area that became Senegal. Their history includes the spread of Islam and the 19th-century Toucouleur Empire.

## Festivals & rituals

Islam shapes Toucouleur heritage, and the sources describe marriage and childbirth naming practices.

- **Marriage and wedding feast** — Marriage requires a bride price, is validated by a mosque, and begins with a feast for the couple and village members.
- **Bride’s preparation** — Before the marriage is consummated, the bride’s aunt traditionally checks her virginity, then bathes and massages her.
- **Childbirth naming** (*pémbougale*) — One week after childbirth, the baby is named through prayers, whispered words and a village announcement.
- **Father’s sister cuts the baby’s hair** (*gorgol*) — The father’s sister, called gorgol, cuts the child’s hair during the naming ceremony.
- **Griot announces the name** (*gawlo*) — The gawlo, or griot, announces the chosen name to the village after receiving it from the marabout.

## Oral tradition & literature

Toucouleur oral tradition traces their ancestry to Serer and Fula ancestors and includes joking relationships with Serers.

- **Toucouleur ancestry tradition** (*Toucouleur*) — Oral traditions say that the Toucouleur descend from Serer and Fula ancestors.
- **Joking relationship** — Serers and Toucouleurs have a mutually acceptable bantering-style interaction.
- **Village naming announcement** (*gawlo*) — The gawlo, or griot, announces an infant’s chosen name to the village.

## Language & religion

Pulaar is the Toucouleur language, and Islam has shaped their history and vocabulary.

- **Pulaar** — Pulaar is a dialect of Fula spoken mainly in Mauritania, Senegal and Gambia.
- **Haalpulaar’en** — The Toucouleur call themselves Haalpulaar’en, meaning “those who speak Pulaar.”
- **Ajami writing** (*Ajami*) — Pulaar once had a literary tradition using the Arabic script, now called Ajami.
- **Adlam script** (*Adlam*) — Adlam script has also been gaining ground among Pulaar speakers.
- **Islamic influence** (*marabout*) — The Toucouleur were Islamized in the 11th century, and Arabic words entered Pulaar through Islam.
