## Overview

The Oulad Delim, also written Oulad Dlim or Oulad Dalim, are a Bedouin Sahrawi tribe of Arab descent. They trace their origins to the Banu Hassan, part of the larger Maqil, and were formerly considered to have Hassane status, meaning that they belonged to the ruling warrior stratum. They traditionally lived in the southern regions of Western Sahara, especially around Dakhla. Communities are also found in Morocco, including Rabat, Marrakech, Sidi Kacem and El Jadida, and in Mauritania between Nouadhibou and Idjil. Their traditional lifestyle was nomadic and based on camel herding. They speak Hassaniya Arabic, a Bedouin dialect described as very close to pure classical Arabic. The sources describe extensive connections with northern Mauritanian tribes and identify the Oulad Delim as Muslims adhering to the Maliki school of Sunni Islam.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources used identify the Oulad Delim as Muslims adhering to the Maliki school of Sunni Islam.

The sources describe the traditional political and economic practice of enforcing the *horma* on tributaries in exchange for protection. In the second half of the 18th century, the Oulad Delim forced the zawiya tribe Oulad Tidrarin to submit to their authority and pay the horma. In 1888, conflict began when Oulad Tidrarin refused to pay it, and after later battles the Oulad Delim extracted less tribute than before. The sources present this as a historical institution and conflict practice rather than as a religious or life-cycle ritual.

## Foodways

A historical passage states that the Oulad Dalim travelled to the province of Draa to barter animals for dates.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Oulad Delim’s eponymous ancestor is identified as Delim. One tradition says that Delim was the son of Oudei bin Hassan. In this account, Oudei had a servant whose small size led her to be called Delima, meaning “little animal skin”; she had a son through him called Delim, who was also of small size. The story says that Delim was initially disowned by his father because of the jealousy of his Hilalian wife, but later proved himself when a group attacked his father’s wife while her own sons fled. The source states that this legend is taken from the Arabic novel *Antar*.

Another tradition states that Delim was the son of Hassan himself. Paul Marty divided the Oulad Delim into the Remeithia, descendants of Remaith, and the Oulad Chouikh, descendants of Chouikh.

## Language & religion

The Oulad Delim speak Hassaniya Arabic, described as a Bedouin dialect very close to pure classical Arabic.

The Oulad Delim are Muslims and adhere to the Maliki school of Sunni Islam.
