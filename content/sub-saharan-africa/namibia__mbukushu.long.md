## Overview

The Mbukushu, also known as the Hambukushu and Hakokuhu, are a Bantu-speaking ethnic group indigenous to Southern Africa. They are part of the larger Lozi ethnic group and have significant populations in Angola, Botswana, Namibia, and Zambia, numbering around 120,000. They primarily reside along the Kavango River and in Ngamiland, within the Okavango Delta. Their settlements reflect reliance on fishing, farming, and livestock keeping. In Namibia, they hold traditional authority and are one of the five Kavango Kingdoms. The Hambukushu are associated with rain-making abilities in the Okavango Delta and with basket weaving, while their women are known for using traps to catch fish.

## Language & religion

Mbukushu or Thimbukushu is a Bantu language. It is spoken by 45,000 people along the Kavango East Region in Namibia, where it is a national language, and in Botswana, Angola, and Zambia. The sources also describe the Mbukushu language as a Bantu language and state that oral traditions are important in the community. In 2022, Mbukushu was selected among a variety of Mother Tongue languages to be taught in Botswana Primary Schools in 2023.
