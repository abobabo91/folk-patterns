## Overview

The Mbukushu, also known as the Hambukushu and Hakokuhu, are a Bantu-speaking ethnic group indigenous to Southern Africa. They are part of the larger Lozi ethnic group and have significant populations in Angola, Botswana, Namibia, and Zambia, numbering around 120,000. They primarily reside along the Kavango River and in Ngamiland, within the Okavango Delta. Their settlements reflect reliance on fishing, farming, and livestock keeping. In Namibia, they hold traditional authority and are one of the five Kavango Kingdoms. The Hambukushu are associated with rain-making abilities in the Okavango Delta and with basket weaving, while their women are known for using traps to catch fish.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Basket weaving** — The sources identify basket weaving as an artistic practice associated with the Hambukushu.

**Fibre objects** — Museum records document fibre as a material in a head-ornament and in a leg-band or leg ornament. The records do not describe their patterns, manufacture, or social meaning.

**Hair ornaments** — Museum objects include a ring or hair ornament made of palm leaf, glass beads, and fibre thread, as well as a hair ornament made of glass beads, a bean, and fibre thread.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no motifs.

### Clothing & dress

Museum records document a leg-band or leg ornament made of giraffe hair, leather, fibre, and copper beads, and another leg-band or leg ornament made of giraffe hair and glass beads. They also record a necklace with a scraper, made of glass beads, iron, a metal button, and fibre thread. These catalogue descriptions identify objects and materials.

### Architecture

The sources state only that villages are strategically located near water sources along the Kavango River and surrounding lands.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum records include an arrow made of iron, wood, feather, and fibre, and a necklace with a scraper made of glass beads, iron, a metal button, and fibre thread. They also record a bracelet made of giraffe hair and a bundle of giraffe tail hair tied into a circle and decorated with twelve copper beads.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Museum catalogue records document a necklace with a scraper, a bracelet, a ring or hair ornament, hair ornaments, head-ornament, and leg-bands or leg ornaments. Materials include glass beads, iron, a metal button, fibre thread, giraffe hair, giraffe tail hair, copper beads, palm leaf, leather, fibre, wood, and a bean. The bracelet is specifically described as a bundle of giraffe tail hair tied into a circle and decorated with twelve copper beads.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources identify rain-making as an ability for which the Hambukushu are renowned in the Okavango Delta, and refer to royal rainmaking in the cited historical work, but give no account of the ritual sequence, participants, objects, or calendar.

## Foodways

The sources state that Hambukushu settlements reflect reliance on fishing, farming, and livestock keeping, and that their women are known for using traps to catch fish.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that oral traditions are paramount in Hambukushu society, with storytelling and oral history passing down from generation to generation. The cited reference is Maria Fisch’s *The Mbukushu in Angola (1730-2002): A History of Migration, Flight and Royal Rainmaking*, published by Koeppe Verlag in 2005; the supplied material does not describe contemporary literary revivals or preservation projects.

## Language & religion

Mbukushu or Thimbukushu is a Bantu language. It is spoken by 45,000 people along the Kavango East Region in Namibia, where it is a national language, and in Botswana, Angola, and Zambia. The sources also describe the Mbukushu language as a Bantu language and state that oral traditions are important in the community. In 2022, Mbukushu was selected among a variety of Mother Tongue languages to be taught in Botswana Primary Schools in 2023.
