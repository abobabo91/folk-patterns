## Overview

The Akie are a Tanzanian ethnic and linguistic people living in south western Simanjiro District of Manyara Region. In 2000, their population was counted at 5,268. They are one of the last actual hunter-gatherer groups left on the African savanna. The Akie hunt, collect honey, and make beer from honey. Competition for land with the Maasai has increased their reliance on maize, although maize rarely produces enough food to last year around. Agriculture, poaching, and other hunters have diminished natural resources and contributed to rivalry over land and water. The Akie language is a moribund endangered Kalenjin language, and no more than 200 people spoke it as of 2015.

## Music & performance

The Akie were featured by Bruce Parry in the BBC series *Tribe*, in its 10th episode in 2007.

## Foodways

The Akie hunt and collect honey. Honey collection involves “steaming” out the bees, making it possible to reach into the hive and take the honey. They make beer from honey. Because of competition for land with the Maasai, they have become more reliant on maize, although maize rarely produces enough food to last year around.

## Language & religion

Akie, also called Mosiro, Nandi, or “(N)dorobo,” is a Kalenjin language spoken in Tanzania. Its name is given as *kuuti táá akie*, meaning “mouth of the Akie people.” Akie is a moribund endangered language spoken by only a few elders. The Akie people have adopted Maasai and Swahili, and a 1981 report stated that younger generations were becoming less fluent in Akie. There is no dialectal variation in Akie. Vowel length is distinctive, and some consonant distinctions are not made by all speakers.
