## Overview

The Sebei are a Nilotic ethnic group inhabiting eastern Uganda, primarily on the slopes of Mount Elgon in the districts of Bukwo, Kapchorwa and Kween. They number about 300,000 people and occupy an area of 1,730.9 square km. The Sapiiny, also known as Sapiny, live in these districts and speak Kupsabiny, a Kalenjin language within the Southern Nilotic family. The Sebei territory borders the Republic of Kenya, where related Kalenjin groups live. Their documented way of life centers on cattle keeping, growing crops and making beer. They are also known for circumcising teenage boys and girls as a rite of passage to adulthood.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe circumcision of teenage boys and girls as a rite of passage to adulthood. The sources also state that the Sebei lead a fairly simple lifestyle centered on cattle keeping, growing crops and making beer. The majority of Sabiny are Christians. According to the 2002 Census of Uganda, 40.5% were Anglican, 23.4% Roman Catholic, 18.3% Pentecostal, 9.7% Muslim and 7% followed other religions.

## Foodways

The sources state that growing crops and making beer are central parts of Sebei life.

## Language & religion

Kupsabiny, also called Sabiny or Sebei, is a Kalenjin language within the Southern Nilotic family of the Nilotic family. It is spoken in eastern Uganda and is described as solely spoken in Uganda in the language source. Kupsabiny is highly tonal, with 9 vowels and 14 consonants; the language source also describes 6 vowels in its phonology section. Tone is marked as high, mid or low. The language is written with Latin script since 1975, revised in 2010, and is taught in primary schools with literacy campaigns. The closest relative of Kupsabiny is Sabaot, spoken across the border in Kenya. The majority of Sabiny are Christians, while the 2002 Census of Uganda recorded Anglican, Roman Catholic, Pentecostal, Muslim and other religious affiliations.
