## Overview

The Tsimihety are a Malagasy ethnic group living in the north-central and mountainous regions of Madagascar, especially in the Sofia Region and around Mandritsara. Their population estimates range between 700,000 and over 1.2 million, placing them as the fourth-largest ethnicity in Madagascar. Their name means “those who never cut their hair,” a designation associated with their historical independence from the Sakalava kingdom. They speak Tsimihety, a northern dialect of Malagasy belonging to the Austronesian language family and the Northern Malagasy subgroup. Tsimihety society has been described as strongly egalitarian, organized around biological family and kin, and historically resistant to centralized authority. Agriculture, rice cultivation, and cattle raising are central to their economy.

### Jewelry & body adornment

They identify the refusal to cut the hair as the practice underlying the group’s name.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that the Tsimihety did not create symbols or rituals or tribal rules in the usual sense attributed to tribes, and that their relationships were centered on biological family and kin. The sources also describe an anarchist social system before the nineteenth century. Informal consensus was the basis of local decisions, anyone behaving like a leader was considered suspicious, and giving orders was regarded as wrong.

## Foodways

Tsimihety society and economy are primarily focused on agriculture. Rice is the staple crop, and Tsimihety people raise cattle. Working on cropland on Tuesday is *fady*, meaning taboo, among the Tsimihety.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that Peter Wilson described Tsimihety people as lacking the symbols, rituals, and tribal rules normally associated with tribes, while their social relationships centered on family and kin.

## Language & religion

Tsimihety is a northern dialect of Malagasy spoken by Tsimihety people in the Sofia Region of Madagascar. It belongs to the Austronesian language family and the Northern Malagasy subgroup, together with Northern Sakalava, Bushi, Northern Betsimisaraka, and Antakarana. It is also used by Tsimihety diaspora communities in Mahajanga and Antananarivo and is spoken in the Sava Region, particularly in the Andapa District.
