## Overview

The Embu are a Bantu people living on Mount Kenya's fertile southern slopes in Kenya's Eastern Province, bordered by the Kikuyu, Meru and Kamba. They consist of two subgroups: the Embu proper and the semi-arid Mbeere. Most are now Christian, though they traditionally worshipped Ngai (God) and the spirits of ancestors. They built their livelihood on highland agriculture growing maize, beans, yams and bananas.

## Material culture

Embu material culture is dominated by wooden, hide, horn, gourd and bamboo objects, with weapons and ritual specialists' equipment comprising the main museum collections.

### Textile & pattern traditions

No weaving tradition is recorded; pattern is found mainly in beaded and shell-covered leather ornaments.

- **Cowrie shell and bead ornaments** — Leather headdresses and head-rings faced with cowrie shells, glass beads and shell beads.
- **Bead-covered pendants** — A head-band of leather hung with pendants covered in red and pink beads.
- **Reed and seed fringe** — A headdress fringe made of reeds and seeds strung on a plaited fiber cord.
- **Pigmented shield** — An oval shield of bark covered with red pigment.

### Clothing & dress

Embu dress is documented only through elaborate head ornaments combining marine shells, glass beads and leather.

- **Head-rings** — Leather head-rings faced with cowrie shells, glass beads and shell beads.
- **Beaded head-band** — A leather head-band hung with pendants covered in red and pink beads.
- **Head-dress fringe** — Reeds and seeds threaded onto a plaited vegetable-fiber cord.
- **Ear-plug** — A wooden ear-plug indicating pierced and distended earlobes.

### Architecture

Embu architecture is not described in available sources.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Embu objects are chiefly made from wood, hide, horn, gourd and bamboo, with weapons and ritual equipment dominating collections.

- **Sword** — A sword made of iron, wood and hide.
- **Bow** — A bow of wood strung with vegetable fiber, sometimes bound with haired skin.
- **Game arrows** — Arrows of wood, reed, feather and vegetable fiber.
- **Shield** — An oval shield of bark under red pigment and a wooden parrying shield.
- **Club or knobkerrie** — A striking weapon with a stone head sheathed in hide on a wooden handle.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Embu body adornment centers on beaded leather headgear and shell work, with no recorded metal jewelry, tattooing or scarification.

- **Beaded leather headgear** — Cowrie shells, glass beads and shell beads worked onto leather rings and bands.
- **Beaded pendants** — Pendants covered with red and pink beads.
- **Wooden ear-plug** — A wooden plug indicating pierced and distended earlobes.

## Music & performance

Embu musical instruments recorded are wooden war horns; the Embu are known to love music and dance, though specific genres and ensembles are not documented.

- **War horn** — A wooden side-blown horn used as a signaling instrument for territorial defense.
- **Carved war horn** — A second carved wooden side-blown war horn.

## Dance & theatre

Embu dance is known for agility, acrobatics and body movements resembling those of the Akamba, but no individual dances or dramatic traditions are named.

## Festivals & rituals

No annual festivals or life-cycle rituals are documented; the record instead details extensive equipment of ritual and healing specialists.

- **Wizard's bag** — A bag cut from a single piece of folded hide and stitched with a carrying strap, used by ritual specialists.
- **Medicinal powder tubes** — Bamboo tubes holding medicinal powder and chalk powder for ceremonies.
- **Medicine vessels** — Gourd vessels of medicine and medicinal powder, variously mounted with skin and decorated with hide bands.
- **Divination powder vessel** — A gourd and skin vessel containing divination powder.
- **Horn charms** — Charms made of horn, some from gerenuk antelope, others combining horn with hide, wood, skin, claws and conch shell.

## Foodways

Embu agriculture on Mount Kenya's windward slopes yields two growing seasons yearly, supporting diverse crops and livestock.

- **Nyeni** — Traditional vegetables.
- **Mukimo** — Mashed banana with traditional vegetables or potatoes.
- **Githere** (*githere or muthere*) — Unfettered corn seeds cooked with beans or peas and traditional vegetables.
- **Nyama cia gwakia** — Roasted meat.
- **Irio** — Mashed dry beans, corn and potatoes.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Embu preserve an origin myth centered at the Mwenendega grove, telling of a divine wife and the founding of their land.

- **Mwenendega origin myth** (*Mwenendega*) — A creation story set at the Mwenendega grove near Runyenjes, in which Ngai created Mwenendega and gave him a divine wife at the Gogo River Salt Lick.
- **Ciũrũnjĩ** (*Ciũrũnjĩ or Nthara*) — The divine wife sent by God, who made Mwenendega swear never to abuse her.
- **Mount Kenya naming** (*Mwenenyaga or Mwenenjerũ*) — Mount Kenya is regarded as the seat of the lord and owner of the snow, from words meaning 'white'.

## Language & religion

The Embu speak Kĩembu, a Bantu language with two dialects; traditionally they worshipped Ngai and ancestors, now mostly Christian.

- **Kĩembu language** (*Kĩembu*) — A Bantu language of the Niger-Congo family with two main dialects, Embu proper and Mbeere, and a Mount Kenya variety called Kirũgũrũ.
- **Ngai** (*Ngai or Murungu*) — A single invisible and transcendent God living in the sky, omnipotent creator of life, merciful but distant.
- **Ancestor veneration** — The spirits of the departed are regarded as intercessors between the living and Ngai, remembered in family offerings and libations at individual altars.

## Glossary

- *Aembu* — The Embu people (singular Muembu)
- *Kĩembu* — The Bantu language spoken by the Embu
- *Mwenendega* — A founder figure in Embu origin myth
- *Ciũrũnjĩ* — The divine wife in the origin myth, also called Nthara
- *Ngai* — God in Embu tradition, also called Murungu
- *Nyeni* — Traditional vegetables
- *Mukimo* — Mashed banana with vegetables or potatoes
- *Githere* — Corn seeds cooked with beans and vegetables, also muthere
- *Nyama cia gwakia* — Roasted meat
- *Irio* — Mashed dry beans, corn and potatoes
- *Kirũgũrũ* — A variety of Kĩembu spoken near Mount Kenya
- *Mwenenyaga* — The lord and owner of the snow, a name for Mount Kenya
- *Mwenenjerũ* — Another name for the owner of Mount Kenya's snow
- *Gogo River Salt Lick* — The location of Mukuuri, where the divine wife was found
- *Mbeere* — A subgroup of the Embu occupying semi-arid areas
