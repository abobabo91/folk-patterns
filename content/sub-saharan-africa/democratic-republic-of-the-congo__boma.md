## Overview

The Boma are a Bantu people of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. They live mainly along the lower Kasai and Lukenié rivers. Their kingdom began in the 17th century and lasted until Belgian colonial conquest. Museum records document several objects and photographs of Boma people.

## Material culture

Documented material culture includes cloths, personal ornaments, bows, baskets, swords, and bronze status rings.

### Textile & pattern traditions

- **Bronze status ring** (*Ngondo*) — Ngondo is a bronze ring described as a coin, a status symbol, and part of the bride-price.
- **Catalogue term** (*Bamboma*) — Bamboma appears in a Museum of World Culture record associated with the Bakongo tribe and the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

### Clothing & dress

Photographs show Boma adults and children mostly wearing cloths around the lower body.

### Architecture

Photographs show rectangular, single-storey, thatched-roof buildings and some European buildings.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Museum records document bows, baskets, swords, and bronze rings, but no Boma ceramic tradition.

- **Bow** — The documented bow is made of wood and string.
- **Cane basket** — A documented basket is made of cane.
- **Sword** — The documented sword has a skin-covered sheath and is also identified as a knife with a sheath.
- **Bronze ring** (*Ngondo*) — Ngondo is a bronze ring described as a coin and status symbol.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The best-documented adornment is the bronze ring Ngondo, while photographs also show unnamed personal ornaments.

- **Bronze ring** (*Ngondo*) — Ngondo is about 6 cm wide, formed part of the bride-price, and was collected on bast cords or similar materials.
- **Holding or fastening** (*Nsimbi*) — Nsimbi is explained through simba, meaning to hold, hold fast, or hold in.

## Festivals & rituals

- **Bride-price ring** (*Ngondo*) — The catalogue states that the bronze ring Ngondo formed part of the bride-price, but gives no further ritual details.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources record the Boma Kingdom.

- **Boma Kingdom** (*Boma*) — The Boma formed the Boma Kingdom in the 17th century, and it lasted until Belgian colonial conquest.

## Language & religion

Boma is a Bantu language, and the profile does not document Boma religion.

- **Boma language** (*Boma*) — Boma is a Bantu language of the Democratic Republic of the Congo.
- **Related language** (*Mpe*) — Mpe, also called B.821 Mpe or Kempee, is considered closely related to Boma.
- **Related language** (*Nunu*) — Nunu is considered closely related to Boma, and the source distinguishes it from the Nunu dialect of Ngiri.
- **Southern Boma community** (*Boma Nku*) — Boma Nku live mainly in the Kasai region and the middle stretch of the Kasai River.
- **Northern Boma community** (*Boma Ngeli*) — Boma Ngeli live in the north, in the region of Mushie.
