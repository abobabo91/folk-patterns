## Overview

The Anyuak, also known as Anyuaa and Anywaa, are a Luo Nilotic ethnic group living predominantly in western Ethiopia and South Sudan. They have lived around the Upper Nile for hundreds of years and many live along the Baro River and the Akobo River, including within the Gambela Region of Ethiopia. Their population is given as between 200,000 and 300,000 people worldwide. Their language, Dha-Anywaa, is a western Nilotic Luo language. Anuak communities are associated with agriculture, animal husbandry, hunting, fishing, pastoralism, gathering, riverine settlement, and tightly knit village life. Many Anuak people now follow Christianity, while traditional religion placed particular emphasis on trees and on the almighty spirit Jwøk.

### Architecture

They state that Anuak villages are tightly knit and largely self-contained, and that many Anuak people establish temporary villages in good fishing areas.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe religious practices associated with traditional Anuak religion: some villages had “holy” trees, and traditional belief placed emphasis on an almighty spirit known as Jwøk. Anuak philosophy stated that there were no “God-men,” and village Headmen could be removed if the people considered their conduct unsatisfactory or dictatorial.

## Foodways

Anuak livelihoods are based substantially on agriculture, hunting, fishing, pastoralism, gathering, and animal husbandry. Sorghum is a common crop, while cattle, sheep, and goats are common livestock. Crops are grown among riverbanks, which provide a stable and efficient supply of food. During the dry season, Anuak people hunt animals seeking waterways; many also fish, especially outside the dry season, and hunt wild birds as part of their diet. Fishing can lead to the establishment of temporary villages in suitable fishing areas.

## Language & religion

Anuak or Anywaa is a Luo language belonging to the western Nilotic branch of the Nilotic language family. It is spoken primarily in western Ethiopia and also in South Sudan. Other names given for the language include Anyuak, Anywa, Yambo, Jambo, Yembo, Bar, Burjin, Miroy, Moojanga, and Nuro. Anuak, Päri, and Jur-Luwo comprise a dialect cluster. The language is notable for lacking phonemic fricatives. Many Anuak people are now evangelical Christians. Traditional Anuak religion emphasized trees, with some villages having “holy” trees, and included belief in the almighty spirit Jwøk.
