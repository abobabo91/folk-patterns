## Overview

The Nyangatom, also known as Donyiro and pejoratively as Bumé, are Nilotic agro-pastoralists living along the border of southwestern Ethiopia, southeastern South Sudan, and the Ilemi Triangle. They speak Nyangatom, a Nilotic language also known as Inyangatom, Donyiro, Dongiro, and Idongiro. The Nyangatom number approximately 30,000, with populations in South Sudan and Ethiopia. Many are nomadic and live in mobile livestock villages that may migrate several times a year, while others live in semi-permanent villages. People commonly move between mobile cattle camps and semi-permanent villages. The Nyangatom belong to the Ateker or Karamojong cluster, alongside groups including the Turkana, Toposa, Karamojong, Iteso people, Ibo People, and Jie.

## Language & religion

Nyangatom is a Nilotic language spoken in Ethiopia by the Nyangatom people. It is an oral language only and has no working orthography at present. Related languages include Toposa and Turkana, both of which have a level of mutual intelligibility; Blench counts Nyangatom as a dialect of Turkana. The language has contrastive vowel length, as in *dʒík*, meaning “completely,” and *dʒíík*, meaning “always.”
