## Overview

The Kassena are an ethnic group living along the northern Ghana and Burkina Faso border. They speak Kasem, also called Kasena or Kassem, a Gur language spoken in the Upper East Region of northern Ghana and in Burkina Faso. Their king lives in Tiébélé, and the Kassena form an arc of towns extending from Ouagadougou to northeast Ghana. In Ghana, Kassena communities include the Kassena-Nankana administrative district, Navrongo, and Paga; in southern Burkina Faso they include the Naouhri province and the cities of Tiébélé and Pô. The Kassena are associated with wooden masks, clay sculptures, wooden jewelry, music, dance, chiefdoms, ancestral courtyards, and a traditional political system led by rulers called *Peh*.

## Language & religion

Kasena, also called Kassena, Kasem, or Kassem, is the language of the Kassena ethnic group. It is a Gur language spoken in the Upper East Region of northern Ghana and in Burkina Faso. The language has three tones: high, mid level, and low. Tonal changes can alter the lexical meaning or grammatical function of a word. Kasena has five noun classes for singular nouns and five for plural nouns. Its basic word order is subject–verb–object. Reciprocity is expressed by the pronoun *daanɪ*, while reflexivity is expressed by a personal pronoun with *tɪtɪ* or *katɪ*, meaning “-self.”

Historically, the Kassena are described as monotheistic animists. They worship the Creator God *We* and venerate the ancestors. The syllable *We* appears in Kassena names including *Webedouh*, *Wettelne*, and *Wepouli*.
