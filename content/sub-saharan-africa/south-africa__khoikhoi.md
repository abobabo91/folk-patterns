## Overview

The Khoikhoi are traditionally linked with nomadic pastoralism. Khoekhoe-speaking peoples include the Nama, Damara, Orana, Khoemana or Griqua, and Gqunukhwebe or Gona groups. Their culture is known for herding, click sounds in the language, clan structures, and surviving material objects. The profile gives limited information about buildings, food, music, dance, and rituals.

## Material culture

Recorded material culture includes leather, beadwork, cowrie shells, ostrich shell beads, tortoise shell, wood, stone, and glass beads.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The profile records leather items decorated with beads but names no textile pattern system.

- **Leather beadwork** — Leather back aprons were decorated or faced with beadwork.
- **Ostrich-shell bead ornament** — Ostrich shell beads decorated a quiver and a composite pouch.

### Clothing & dress

Recorded clothing includes leather aprons and a cowrie-covered head-band.

- **Leather back apron** — Leather back aprons were decorated with beadwork.
- **Cowrie-covered head-band** — A leather head-band was faced with cowrie shells.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded everyday objects include a tortoise-shell pouch, a quiver model, and smoking pipes.

- **Tortoise-shell pouch** — A composite pouch used a small tortoise carapace, a leather handle, ostrich shell beads, seeds, greenish powder, and soft fibre.
- **Quiver model** — A leather quiver model was decorated with ostrich shell beads and sinew strings.
- **Wooden smoking pipe** — Some smoking pipes were made from hard brown wood.
- **Serpentine smoking pipe** — Other pipes used dark green serpentine and serpentine pipe-bowls.
- **Steatite pipe-bowl** — One catalogue records a European-style steatite pipe-bowl.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Recorded adornment includes bead ornaments, cowrie shells, ostrich shell beads, and seeds.

- **Glass-bead wrist or leg ornament** — An ornament for the wrist or leg was made of glass beads attached to a backing.
- **Cowrie head-band** — A leather head-band was faced with cowrie shells.
- **Seed bead ornament** — Reddish brown seeds were attached to the ends of two pouch strings.

## Festivals & rituals

The profile names no festivals or life-cycle rites but describes Khoe-speaking religious beliefs.

- **Moon significance** — The Moon receives special significance and may have been viewed as the physical form of a supreme being linked with heaven.
- **Creator and guardian** (*Thiǁoab*) — Thiǁoab, called Tsui'goab in Nama and ǁGamab in Damara mythology, is described as the creator and guardian of health.
- **Sickness or death figure** (*ǁGaunab*) — ǁGaunab is associated with sickness or death.

## Foodways

Pastoral communities kept sheep, goats, and cattle, but the profile names no dishes or cooking methods.

- **Sheep herding** — Pastoral communities kept sheep that grazed in fertile valleys.
- **Goat herding** — Pastoral communities kept goats that grazed in fertile valleys.
- **Cattle herding** — Pastoral communities kept cattle, including large herds of Nguni cattle in the Cape region in the 17th century.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources name one published collection of Khoekhoe-related tales but no living oral tradition.

- **Reynard the Fox in South Africa** — Wilhelm Bleek published Reynard the Fox in South Africa; or Hottentot Fables and Tales in 1864.

## Language & religion

Khoekhoe is a Khoe-family language with click consonants, several dialects, and religious traditions centered partly on the Moon.

- **Khoekhoe language** (*Khoekhoegowab*) — Khoekhoe, also called Khoikhoi or Khoekhoegowab, is spoken mainly by Namakhoen, ǂNūkhoen, and Haiǁomkhoen.
- **Nama–Damara dialect** — Nama–Damara is one of the three modern Khoekhoe dialects and includes Sesfontein Damara.
- **Haiǁom dialect** (*Haiǁom*) — Haiǁom is one of the three modern Khoekhoe dialects.
- **ǂĀkhoe dialect** (*ǂĀkhoe*) — ǂĀkhoe is one of the three modern Khoekhoe dialects.
- **Click consonants** — Khoekhoe has 20 click consonants among its 31 consonants.
