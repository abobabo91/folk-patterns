## Overview

The Manyema, also called WaManyema, are a Bantu ethnic group in the African Great Lakes region of Eastern Africa and Central Africa. They are Muslim and religiously influential in the region. In the past, they were described as powerful and warlike. Many Manyema, like many Nyamwezi, are descendants of porters who emerged during the height of the Swahili-Arab trade in the Sultanate of Utetera. During the early to mid-1800s, Manyema traversed Lake Tanganyika towards the Swahili coast as caravan porters, merchants, mercenaries, war refugees, slaves, and, to some extent, slave traders. Their territory was extensively incorporated into Swahili economy and culture.

## Foodways

The name Manyema is explained as *Una-Ma-Nyema*, “eaters of flesh.”

## Oral tradition & literature

Manyema memories of war refugees are mentioned in connection with nineteenth-century movement across Lake Tanganyika.

## Language & religion

The Manyema are a Bantu ethnic group. They are Muslim and religiously influential in the region. Ethnicities including Manyema, Nyamwezi, Arab, and Swahili are described as mutable, although constraints existed on the shifts people could make. WaSwahili in Ujiji town, on the border between Tanzania and Democratic Republic of Congo, many Manyema by origin, identify themselves as Swahili.
