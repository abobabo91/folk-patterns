## Overview

The Bongo are a sparse population living in South Sudan's Bahr al Ghazal region who speak a tonal Central Sudanic language with a quinary-vigesimal counting system. Most knowledge of Bongo culture comes from nineteenth-century travelers' word lists and museum collections of objects rather than from recent ethnographic study. The surviving record focuses on material goods—carved wooden items, weapons, instruments, and ornaments—that reveal skilled work in wood, bone, and metal.

## Material culture

Bongo material culture centers on carved wood, worked ivory, and iron, particularly in stools, hunting gear, musical instruments, and personal ornament.

### Textile & pattern traditions

No textile traditions are documented in available sources.

### Clothing & dress

Garments are not described, but women wore distinctive wooden lip ornaments along with ivory armlets, necklaces, and a skin pad worn on the head for carrying loads.

- **Wooden lip ornaments** — Carved in wood and painted black, worn by women.
- **Ivory armlets** — Bracelets of worked ivory worn as personal adornment.
- **Head pad** — A circle of skin with sandy-colored hair around the edge, worn on the head to carry loads.

### Architecture

House structure is not described, but carved wooden ornaments decorated huts.

- **Bird hut ornament** — Solid wood carving of a bird with a collar around its neck, originally painted brown and yellow, with a red eye bead inlaid.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Iron blades and carved wooden pieces—especially stools—dominate household and working equipment.

- **Hoe currency** — An iron blade shaped like an ace of spades with a long handle, used as a form of money.
- **Decorated iron knife** — An oval blade with punched decoration on one side.
- **Smoking pipe** — Made from a clay bowl, bamboo stem, and gourd mouthpiece partly covered with skin.
- **Tusk seal-stamp** — Carved from an elephant tusk end with its face engraved in intersecting lines.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Women wore carved wooden lip ornaments and necklaces strung with ivory pieces and berries, with no records of tattooing or scarification.

- **Cowrie-shell ivory necklace** — Ten flat oval ivory pieces carved to resemble cowrie shells strung on fiber with a flat ivory disc.
- **Necklace of conjuring beads** — Five strings of twisted fiber strung with round brown berries, with two transverse pieces of hide, used for ritual purposes.

## Music & performance

Bongo musicians played drums, a harp-like lyre, and multiple kinds of horns carved from wood, bone, or ivory.

- **Tom-tom drum** — A hollowed wooden log open at both ends with skin heads held by skin thongs.
- **Harp** — A semi-circular sounding chamber with wood back covered in skin parchment, a neck of two sticks joined by a bar, and gut strings.
- **Ivory hunting horn** — Made from an elephant tusk end, carved with incised lines and pricked dots.
- **Wooden hunting horn** — A carved wood horn used for hunting.
- **Ivory trumpet** — An ivory instrument for performance or signaling.

## Dance & theatre

No information is available about Bongo dance or dramatic performance.

## Festivals & rituals

Funerary commemoration is the best-documented ritual, involving carved wooden grave figures and commemorative posts.

- **Ngya** — A commemorative post carved in mahogany and erected for the dead.
- **Grave figure** — Life-size standing wooden figures of men and women, carved in wood or red wood and placed at graves.

## Foodways

No Bongo staples, dishes, or cooking methods are documented.

## Oral tradition & literature

No folktales, epics, or proverbs are recorded, but the Bongo language itself has been documented through vocabularies and grammars since the nineteenth century.

- **Bongo Grammar Book** — Produced in 2018 by Bongo authors with SIL-South Sudan, including a companion vowel and consonant book.
- **Bongo language documentation** — Vocabularies gathered by Petherick (1861), von Heuglin (1869), Schweinfurth (1873), Evans-Pritchard (1937), and Kilpatrick (1985).

## Language & religion

Bongo is a tonal Central Sudanic language with ten vowel qualities and a quinary-vigesimal counting system; religious practice is largely undocumented except for Christian influence.

- **Tonal system** — The language uses high, mid, low, and falling tones to distinguish meaning.
- **Vowel harmony** — Five heavy vowels and five light vowels interact in words according to harmonic rules.
- **Jesus Film translation** — The Bible film has been translated into Bongo, indicating Christian missionary activity.

## Glossary

- *Bongo* — The language and people of Bahr al Ghazal, South Sudan
- *Bungu* — Alternative name for the Bongo language
- *Dor* — Another name for the Bongo language
- *Central Sudanic* — Language family to which Bongo belongs
- *Tonal language* — A language where pitch distinguishes meaning
- *Quinary-vigesimal* — Counting system based on five and twenty
- *Vowel harmony* — Vowel types in a word must match certain patterns
- *Ngya* — Commemorative post carved for the dead
- *Hoe currency* — Iron blade shaped like an ace of spades used as money
- *Lip ornament* — Wooden carved piece worn in the lip by women
- *Tom-tom* — Hollowed log drum with skin heads
- *Cowrie shell* — Small sea shell shape imitated in ivory carvings
- *Conjuring beads* — Ritual necklace of berries and hide with spiritual purpose
- *Bahr al Ghazal* — Region in South Sudan where the Bongo live
- *Mahogany* — Type of wood used for carving commemorative posts
- *Ivory trumpet* — Wind instrument carved from elephant tusk
- *Hide* — Animal skin used for shields, quivers, and other objects
- *Thongs* — Strips of leather or skin used to hold drum heads
