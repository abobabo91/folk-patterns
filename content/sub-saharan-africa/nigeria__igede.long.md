## Overview

The Igede are a Nigerian ethnic group native to the Oju and Obi local government areas of Benue State, Nigeria. The Oju Local Government Area shares boundaries with Obi, Ado, Konshisha and Gwer East in Benue State, with Ebonyi and Izzi in Ebonyi State, and with Yala in Cross River State. Obi is headquartered at Obarike-Ito and takes its name from the Obi stream. In 2006, the population was estimated at 267,198 people. Igede communities also exist in Osun State and Ogun State, and Igede is spoken in Cross River State. The language belongs to the Benue-Congo subgroup of the Niger-Congo language family. Igede culture includes farming, the Igede-Agba yam-harvest festival, striped traditional clothes, songs, drama, pottery, arrows, drums and wooden figures.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Museum records describe pottery vessels used as beer-pots or water-pots. One beer-pot is made of pottery with vegetable “varnish,” a medial ridge, and impressed and incised patterns. Another carinated vessel has a long slender neck, with hatched designs alternating with plain bands. A two-handled pottery water- or beer-pot has three spouts, grooved decoration, and vegetable “varnish.” The records also include poisoned arrows with iron heads and reed shafts, a pottery musical pot used as a drum, and a double figure made of wood.

## Music & performance

The sources mention a record and drama piece titled “Ego ny'Igede,” which recounts the migration of the Igede from the region of Sabon Gida Ora to present-day Benue State through Nsukka in Enugu State. This historical event is commonly recounted in song and drama. The museum catalogue also records a musical pot made of pottery, splashed with vegetable “varnish,” globular in form, and having a single hole at the side; it was used as a drum. The sources do not provide a name for this instrument, describe its playing technique, identify song genres, or specify performance contexts such as weddings, funerals, courts, or religious ceremonies.

## Dance & theatre

## Festivals & rituals

Igede is home to the Igede-Agba festival, described as a colourful annual celebration marking the yam harvest season in September. The sources do not cover other annual festivals, life-cycle ceremonies, initiation rites, weddings, funerals, religious rituals, or the detailed sequence of Igede-Agba.

## Foodways

The Igede are predominantly farmers who cultivate maize, cassava, groundnut and yams. The sources connect Igede-Agba with the yam harvest season, but do not describe dishes, cooking methods, preservation techniques, ceremonial foods, beverages, dietary rules, or eating customs.

## Oral tradition & literature

Igede oral tradition traces the group’s origin to Sabon Gida Ora in present-day Edo state, where they are said to descend from Agba, a high chief. A skirmish between the Igede and the natives of Ora is said to have led to migration through Nsukka in Enugu state to present-day Benue State. The story is commonly recounted in song and drama, including “Ego ny'Igede.” Archival records instead portray the Igede as migrants from Ogoja province who were later grouped under the name Idoma. The sources do not cover folktales, proverbs, riddles, epic poetry, contemporary literature, or preservation projects.

## Language & religion

Igede is spoken in Lower Benue State and Cross River State, Nigeria. It is described as a language of 461,000 people and as referring also to the people and the land occupied by them. Igede belongs to the Benue-Congo subgroup of the Niger-Congo language family. The sources do not cover dialects, scripts, religion, religious institutions, spiritual practices, or syncretism.
