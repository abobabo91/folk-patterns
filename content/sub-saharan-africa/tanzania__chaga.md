## Overview

The Chaga are the third-largest ethnic group in Tanzania, living on the slopes of Mount Kilimanjaro. They are known for their highly engineered agricultural landscape, using terracing and irrigation on fertile volcanic soil. The kihamba—a fenced homestead with banana groves and round beehive houses—organizes both daily life and farming. Their ritual life centers on ancestors and Ruwa, the sun god whose shrine sits on the mountain peak.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The kihamba—a fenced farmhouse enclosure—is the core of Chaga settlement and social life.

- **Kihamba** — A fenced farmhouse enclosure ringed by masale plants, holding a banana grove with a round beehive house in the center, plus structures for family members, livestock, and storage.
- **Masale plant** (*masale*) — Dracaena fragrans, a revered shrub that rings the kihamba and symbolizes peace and forgiveness.
- **Beehive house** — A round, earth-walled dwelling roofed with grass or banana leaves, with a central fire and sleeping area by the door.
- **Mtaa** — A chiefdom made up of several clans, whose homesteads are linked by interior paths and separated by hedges or earth banks.
- **Raised granary** — A small structure on poles used to store millet, bananas, beans, honey and banana beer, kept well apart from the house.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Blacksmiths work in many homesteads, while women make and fire earthen pots less commonly; water systems are the most elaborate everyday engineering.

- **Beehive** — A hollowed tree trunk fitted with stoppers and hung from trees to hold honey.
- **Water system** — Water gathered from distant sources is led along hillsides and cliffs, dropped into reservoirs, and distributed through channels giving all households equitable access.
- **Hoe** — The principal farming tool, kept in the sleeping quarters alongside the husband's other equipment.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Male circumcision is the main documented body practice, marking readiness for marriage in the life cycle.

- **Male circumcision** — A common rite that marks the point in the life cycle when a child is prepared for marriage.

## Festivals & rituals

Ritual life centers on proper life-cycle sequence, ancestor veneration, and communication with Ruwa through animal sacrifice.

- **Ruwa** — The supreme deity and sun god, also called Iruva in western states, who resides on Kilimanjaro's summit and grants sustenance and mercy.
- **Animal sacrifice** — The principal channel of communication with the dead, in which the stomach and intestines are offered as a link between living and ancestral worlds.
- **Circumcision initiation** — A rite in the central mountain zone that transmits tribal lore and secret symbols; female circumcision and age sets were later abandoned.
- **Chaga solar calendar** — Twelve thirty-day months plus a five-day intercalary period (thirteen units total), alongside a lunar reckoning giving each of 28 lunar days its own character.
- **Kisiye season** (*Kisiye*) — A season of heavy rains (March–May) followed by cold weather (June–September), divided into substages like Asuma and Kirangeta.

## Foodways

Bananas are the staple crop, grown in homestead groves; annual crops and coffee are secondary, and beer is currency and ritual item.

- **Banana grove** — The cultivated area within each kihamba, where bananas are interplanted with yams, beans and maize to shade smaller crops.
- **Mbege** — Eleusine (finger millet), an annual crop grown on shamba land held year by year.
- **Banana beer** — A beverage stored in the raised granary and used as masiro, the annual payment acknowledging a loan of kihamba land.
- **Lineage feast** — An occasion where male cattle, goats and sheep are slaughtered to provide meat, since households keep only two or three head of stall-fed animals.

## Oral tradition & literature

Clan histories are the main narrative form, tracing origins to the plains, the Usambara, or ancient mountain residence.

- **Clan history** — An origin narrative recording whether a clan arose locally or came from the plains, and describing ancestors as hunters, blacksmiths, livestock keepers or cultivators.
- **Wakoningo** — Pygmies who, according to legend, crossed Kilimanjaro from east to west toward the Congo Basin.
- **Usambara Kilindi dynasty** — An ancient dynasty of the Nguu Mountains to which some Chaga clans trace ancestry.

## Language & religion

Chaga is a Bantu dialect continuum with nine named dialects; Christianity and Islam took root in the nineteenth century over an earlier earth-based, ancestor-venerating faith.

- **Chaga language** (*Kichaga*) — A Bantu dialect continuum spoken south of Mount Kilimanjaro, with nine named dialects including Kivunjo, Kimarangu and Kimachame.
- **Mangi** — The word for 'king', common to all Chaga dialects.
- **Ancestor veneration** — A religious practice strongly maintained to this day, centered on the belief that ancestors influence the living and must be honored with offerings.

## Glossary

- *kihamba* — A fenced farmhouse enclosure that is the core household and farming unit
- *masale* — Dracaena fragrans, a plant symbolizing peace and forgiveness
- *mtaa* — A chiefdom made up of several clans
- *masiro* — Annual payment of banana beer acknowledging a loan of kihamba land
- *shamba* — Land held for one year at a time for annual crops
- *mbege* — Eleusine (finger millet), an annual crop
- *Ruwa* — The supreme sun deity and provider of sustenance in Chaga belief
- *Iruva* — Alternative name for Ruwa used in western mountain states
- *Kichaga* — The Chaga language, a Bantu dialect continuum
- *Mangi* — King or ruler
- *Wakoningo* — Pygmies in Chaga legend who crossed the mountain west toward the Congo
- *Kisiye* — A season of heavy rains and cold weather
- *Asuma* — The first rains in the Kisiye season
- *Kirangeta* — The deep cold period within Kisiye
