## Overview

The Vezo are semi-nomadic people who live by fishing along Madagascar's western coast. They define themselves not by ancestry but by the learned skills of fishing and swimming, which mark their bodies with calluses they see as proof of who they are. Their life centers on the ocean—from launching canoes to long offshore camps—and on keeping close contact with their dead through ritual and conversation.

## Material culture

Vezo material culture is shaped by sea fishing and survival: canoes, nets, paddles, and utilitarian fiber work mixed with new materials.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Textile work is strictly practical and tied to the sea.

- **Raffia hat** — A practical hat woven from raffia fiber.
- **Carrying basket** — A utilitarian basket made of basketry for transport.
- **Canoe sail** — Assembled from rope, cotton cloth, raffia and polythene to catch the wind.

### Clothing & dress

Little is recorded about everyday Vezo dress.

- **Raffia hat** — A woven hat of raffia fiber.
- **Sandal** — Made from zebu hide and goat skin fastened with nails.

### Architecture

Houses are built to shelter families and mark a place where the dead must be informed of events.

- **Ancestor's house** — Wooden model showing the house as a form worthy of miniature reproduction and as home to the presence of the dead.
- **Fishing camp** — Temporary camps set up on sandbars for six to nine months, housing entire families with all possessions.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Objects center on fishing equipment and household cooking.

- **Dugout canoe** (*lakana or pirogue*) — Made by hollowing a large log, driven by sail and paddle; the central artifact of Vezo life.
- **Paddle** — Carved wooden paddle for propelling the canoe.
- **Bailer** — Wooden tool for removing water from the canoe.
- **Fish spears** — Three spears of wood and iron used for fishing.
- **Water vessel and pot** — Clay household vessels for water storage and cooking.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ornament is mainly metal bracelets, but the calluses from fishing and swimming are treated as the deepest adornment.

- **Copper bracelets** — Three bracelets worn on the arm, identified as belonging to women and men.
- **Aluminium bracelets** — Two metal bracelets worn as arm ornament.
- **Seed necklace** — Strung on cotton thread from the seeds of the harea tree.

## Music & performance

The Vezo hold five official cultural ceremonies called Fomba, though little is recorded of their music.

- **Bilo** — An official Fomba ceremony also practiced by the neighbouring Masikoro.
- **Tromba** — An official Fomba ceremony also practiced by the neighbouring Masikoro.
- **Savatse** — An official Fomba ceremony also practiced by the neighbouring Masikoro; also the name given to a circumcised boy.
- **Takasy** — An official Fomba ceremony unique to the Vezo.
- **Soro** — An official Fomba ceremony also practiced by the neighbouring Masikoro.

## Festivals & rituals

Ritual life centers on five Fomba ceremonies and the constant obligation to keep the dead informed of family events.

- **Fomba ceremonies** (*Fomba*) — Five official cultural ceremonies: Bilo, Tromba, Savatse, Takasy, and Soro.
- **Circumcision ceremony** — Runs from 4 am to 9 am; an uncle holds the child while a nurse or doctor makes the cut, followed by drinking.
- **Funerary sculpture** — Wooden figures, often erotic and shown in copulation, placed in forest cemeteries hidden by vegetation.
- **Ancestor address** — The senior family head calls the dead aloud to inform them of moves, new canoes, births, exams, or conflicts; done so the dead are not surprised and do not cause trouble.

## Foodways

Rice is the staple, bought with money from selling the fish catch; the sea and trade provide the rest.

- **Line, net and spear fishing** — Men fish mostly 5 km or less from shore for everyday food.
- **Reef gleaning** — Women harvest invertebrates from reef flats including octopus and sea cucumbers.
- **Offshore seafood** — Sharks and sea cucumbers taken on long trips, both in high demand in Asian seafood markets.
- **Turtle hunting** — Turtles hunted for meat and carapaces and their nests raided, though protected by Decree 24 of 1923.

## Oral tradition & literature

The dead speak to the living through dreams, creating a standing channel of communication.

- **Ancestor speech** — The senior family head calls the dead aloud and talks to them; the dead answer through dreams.
- **Spirit forms** (*fanahy and angatse*) — At death the fanahy (spirit) departs; in disembodied form it becomes angatse, invisible like wind, and appears in dreams with its original uncorrupted body.

## Language & religion

The Vezo speak a Malagasy dialect and practice ancestral religion centered on obligation to the dead.

- **Malagasy dialect** (*Malagasy*) — A Malayo-Polynesian language derived from Barito languages of southern Borneo.
- **Taboos** (*fady*) — Rules governing daily conduct, such as restrictions on eating food from sexually active female kinswomen.
- **Male disease** (*hanimboky*) — A disease affecting only men caused by eating food bought with lover's gifts; the name means swollen with food and symptoms resemble pregnancy.
