## Overview

The Songhai are a West African people with a history linked to the Songhai Empire. They live mainly in Niger and Mali, especially along the Niger River. Their languages include several related varieties, though some are not mutually intelligible. The profile gives the most detail about textiles, basketry, history, trade and religion.

## Material culture

The profile highlights hand-woven textiles, straw objects and basketry, but gives little information about clothing, buildings, jewelry, metalwork or wooden tools.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Songhai textile traditions include hand-woven marriage screens, wall-hangings and blankets with coloured bands, supplementary-weft motifs and tassels.

- **Arkilla Munga marriage bed-screen** (*Arkilla Munga*) — This tapestry-type screen is made from narrow strips of sheep wool and cotton, with coloured bands in natural wool, black, red, orange and blue.
- **Marriage wall-hanging** — This rectangular tapestry is made from eleven narrow strips sewn together and decorated with supplementary-weft motifs.
- **Marriage bed-screen** — This rectangular piece is made from seven narrow strips with supplementary-weft motifs and twisted, knotted tassels.
- **Blanket** — This cotton blanket is woven in nine wide strips with coloured bands, short tufts and two-tone tassels.
- **Straw tray** — This straw tray was recorded among Songhai objects from Timbuktu and was also used as a lid for earthenware vessels.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe Islam, guardian spirits, polygynous marriages and preferred cross-cousin partners, but no annual festival calendar.

- **Conversion of Za Kusoy** (*Za Kusoy*) — Za Kusoy, the 15th ruler of the Zuwa dynasty, converted to Islam in 1009–1010 A.D.
- **Islamic clerics** — Islamic clerics are described in accounts of Songhai social life.
- **Guardian spirits** — Guardian spirits are associated with castes and social strata.
- **Polygynous marriages** — The social-stratification account describes polygynous marriages.
- **Cross-cousin partners** — The account describes preferred cross-cousin partners.

## Foodways

Songhai foodways combine cereal farming, cattle raising, fishing and trade along the Niger Bend.

- **Cereals** — Songhai populations cultivate cereals in arid and semi-arid conditions.
- **Rice** — Historical descriptions of Gao mention rice.
- **Milk** — Historical descriptions of Gao mention milk.
- **Fish** — Songhai people fish in the Niger Bend area, and historical descriptions of Gao mention fish.
- **Cowries** — Historical accounts describe buying and selling with cowries.

## Oral tradition & literature

Songhai history is partly preserved through oral tradition, Arabic-script histories and a few precolonial poems and letters.

- **History of the Sudan** (*Tarikh al-Sudan*) — Al-Sadi’s Tarikh al-Sudan records traditions about the Za dynasty and its legendary founder.
- **History of the Conqueror** (*Tarikh al-fattash*) — The Tarikh al-fattash provides information about Gao and the Songhai Empire.
- **Za Alayaman** — Za Alayaman is described as the legendary founder of the Za dynasty.
- **Dialliaman** — Dialliaman is another name for Za Alayaman.
- **Precolonial poems and letters** — A few precolonial poems and letters in Songhay and Arabic script exist in Timbuktu.

## Language & religion

Songhay is a group of related languages centered on the middle Niger River, and the Songhai are predominantly Muslim.

- **Ayneha languages** (*Ayneha*) — Ayneha is a name the Songhai use for themselves and their languages.
- **Zarma** — Zarma is described as the most widely spoken Songhay language.
- **Koyraboro Senni** — Koyraboro Senni is spoken in Gao.
- **Koyra Chiini** — Koyra Chiini is spoken west of Gao.
- **Zima** — Zima are described as priests.
