## Overview

The Sena are a Bantu ethnolinguistic group originating in northwestern Mozambique, especially Sofala Province and Zambezia Province. They are also found in Malawi and Zimbabwe near their respective borders with Mozambique. Their population is described as around 2 million, including about 1.4 million in Mozambique and about 0.5 million in Malawi. The Sena have lived mainly in the Zambezi River valley, between the Shona people of Zimbabwe and the Nyanja-Chewa people of Malawi and Mozambique. Their occupations have included farming, fishing and migrant labor. They speak Sena, also called Chisena or Cisena, a Bantu language with many dialects. Their folk-culture profile in the sources is shaped by river-valley settlement, Portuguese colonial history, Catholic conversion, traditional religious practices and community dances.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources name no Sena pattern-bearing textile tradition or documented motif vocabulary.

### Clothing & dress

In the hot, humid region where Sena people have been geographically distributed, traditional clothing was limited. Women were described as bare-breasted and wearing *nsalu* or *chitenje*, a wrap-around cloth. Showing bare legs was culturally considered immodest, and both men and women tried to keep their legs covered. During community dances, including the **Nyau dance**, people wore very little clothing.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum catalogue records supplied for this atlas contain no objects.

## Music & performance

The sources identify community dances, including the **Nyau dance**. The account of **Nyau** concerns clothing during community dances rather than instruments or repertoire. No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription is supplied for Mozambique, and no museum catalogue record describes a Sena musical object.

## Dance & theatre

The **Nyau dance** is the only named Sena dance in the supplied sources. It is presented as a community dance during which Sena people wear very little clothing. No other named dance, theatre form, puppet tradition or mask tradition is documented.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe several life-cycle and religious practices. Traditional death rituals have been burials. Weddings among Sena people in river-valley regions required a brideprice called *lobolo*, a payment to the bride’s family compensating them for the loss of her work output in her birth home. Some Sena people of Mozambique have retained traditional beliefs in polygamy, child marriage and tribal religious practices, although Sena people converted to Catholicism in bulk during the colonial era under Portuguese Christian missionaries.

## Foodways

They mention farming and fishing among Sena occupations but give no further account of the foods produced, prepared or consumed.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that Sena culture developed through interaction with Portuguese settlers from the 1500s through contemporary times and that this interaction led Sena people to absorb many Portuguese customs. The source material therefore does not support a fuller account of Sena oral literature.

## Language & religion

Sena is a Bantu language spoken in the four provinces of central Mozambique associated with the Zambezi valley: Tete, Sofala, Zambezia and Manica. In 1997, there were an estimated 900,000 native Sena speakers in Mozambique, with at least 1.5 million speakers when those who used it as a second language were included. Sena is one of the Nyasa languages. Its dialects include Rue, also called Barwe or Cibalke, and Podzo; the Sena of Malawi are described in the source as a distinct language, and Barwe, or Chibarwe, has official recognition in Zimbabwe.

Sena people converted to Catholicism in bulk during the colonial era under Portuguese Christian missionaries. Some Sena people in Mozambique retained traditional beliefs in polygamy, child marriage and tribal religious practices. Churches operating among the Sena use an Africanized version of the Bible and ignore other versions; the version identified in the source is *Buku Lopatulika*, an old Chichewa version of the Bible.
