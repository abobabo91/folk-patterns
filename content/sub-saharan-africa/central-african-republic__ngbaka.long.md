## Overview

The Ngbaka are associated with the Ngbaka language, also called *Ngbàkà*, a Gbaya language spoken by just over a million people in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The language is regionally important and is used by the Gilima, Ngbundu, Mbandja and Mono peoples. The sources used do not establish a specific settlement area, river valley, city, or connection with the Central African Republic. They describe Ngbaka as a common local ethnic name and distinguish the language as Ngbaka Gbaya or Ngbaka Minagende. Ngbaka is taught in primary schools, and 10% are literate in it.

One is made of wood with a rectangular-faced sound box, a skin membrane secured by cane pins, two circular sound holes, a carved wood head, a curved wood neck, brass-tack decoration, five peg holes, four remaining pegs, and cane strings. Another is a bow-shaped harp with a resonator topped by a carved human head and terminating in stylised legs. Its skin-covered top is secured to a wood frame with cane pins, and its string bar has three twisted cotton strings attached, though broken. ## Language & religion

Ngbaka is a Gbaya language. It is also known as Ngbàkà, and the language may be distinguished from other languages called “Ngbaka” as Ngbaka Gbaya or Ngbaka Minagende. There are no significant dialectal differences within Ngbaka, and it may be mutually intelligible with members of the Manza dialect cluster. The writing system marks nasalisation with a tilde and uses diacritics for tones, including acute, grave, vertical-line, circumflex, and caron signs. The sources used do not document Ngbaka religious beliefs or practices.
