## Overview

The Nanumbas are an ethnic group in the Northern Region of Ghana. Their traditional homeland, the Nanung Kingdom, extends through the Eastern corridor and includes Duoni, Bimbilla, Nakpayili, Wulensi, Lungni, Chichahi, Chamba, Chifili, Bunach and Puduya in the southeast of the Northern Region. In 2000, the population of the Nanumba in Ghana was estimated at 78,812. They speak Nanungli, also called Nanungsili, a Gur language. Bimbilla is the capital town of Nanumba, the capital of Nanumba North Municipal, the capital of the Nanumba State and the seat of the Overlord of Nanumba, the Bimbilla Naa. The Nanumba are closely related to the Dagomba and Mamprusi, and more remotely to the Mossi of Burkina Faso.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources identify the Gmantambo Palace as the seat of the Bimbilla Naa. The sources state that the area occupied by the Nanumba is known as Nanung and that the palace is associated with the traditional authority of the Bimbilla Naa.

## Festivals & rituals

The Nanumba celebrate the Islamic festivals Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha. They also celebrate Bugum Chugu, Damba and Naa Jigli festivals.

Islam is the most-practised and characteristic religion of the Nanumba and Dagomba, although Islam is described as less prevalent among the Nanumba than among the Dagomba. Many people also consult non-Muslim diviners and give offerings to ancestral and other shrines. There are a few Christians, mostly Roman Catholics.

## Oral tradition & literature

One passage records statements of Mampruli speakers concerning the shared origin of the Mamprusi, Dagomba and Nanumba at Chama, including the statement “We are all one.”

## Language & religion

Nanungli, also called Nanungsili, is identified as the language spoken by the Nanumba and is classified as a Gur language. The Nanumba are described as a homogeneous cultural and linguistic group closely related to the Dagomba and Mamprusi, and more remotely to the Mossi of Burkina Faso.

The separate language source discusses Dagbani or Dagbanli, a Gur language spoken in Ghana and northern Togo. It describes Dagbanli as the language, Dagbamba as the people and Dagbaŋ as the land, and identifies Eastern Dagbani and Western Dagbani as major dialects. It records that Dagbani was first written in Ajami script and is now mostly written in a Latin alphabet. For Nanumba religion, the sources describe Islam, consultation with non-Muslim diviners, offerings to ancestral and other shrines, and the presence of a few Roman Catholics.
