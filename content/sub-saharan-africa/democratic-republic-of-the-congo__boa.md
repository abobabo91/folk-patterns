## Overview

The Boa people, also called Baboa, Ababua, Ababwa, Babua, Babwa, or Bwa, are an ethnic group in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. They live in the savanna region in the north of the country and are in close contact with the Mangbetu and Zande peoples. Most inhabitants of Bas-Uele District, whose population was 900,000 in 2007, are Boa. The Boa speak Bwa, a Bantu language also known as Boa, Boua, Bua, Kibua, Kibwa, Libua, Libwali, or Bwa proper. Their documented livelihoods include subsistence farming, hunting, and some river commerce. Their masks, statues, and anthropomorphic harps are notable elements of the material and ceremonial culture described in the sources.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do describe carved statues, masks, and harps as Boa objects.

## Music & performance

The Boa make harps whose neck has a carved human head, or whose whole body represents a male or female figure.

## Dance & theatre

They state that Boa masks are thought to be used to enhance a warrior’s courage before battle and in ceremonies celebrating victories.

## Festivals & rituals

They do state that Boa masks are used in ceremonies to celebrate victories and are thought to enhance a warrior’s courage before battle. No dates, festival names, ritual sequences, or ceremonial foods are provided.

## Foodways

They state that the Boa live mainly through subsistence farming and hunting, with some river commerce.

## Language & religion

Bwa is a Bantu language spoken in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It is also identified by the names Boa, Boua, Bua, Kibua, Kibwa, Libua, and Libwali. The listed dialects are Leboa-Le, described as Bwa proper, Yewu, Kiba, Benge, also called Libenge, Bati, also called Baati, Boganga, also called Boyanga, Ligbe, and Pagibete, which is close and might be considered another dialect.
