## Overview

The Nara live mainly in southwestern Eritrea, near Sudan and north of Barentu. They are mostly subsistence farmers. Their society has four subtribes: Higir, Mogareb, Koyta and Santora. Their cultural record includes patterned bamboo, gourd, cane and fibre objects held in the British Museum.

## Material culture

Nara material culture in the supplied sources is marked by plant-based objects with detailed geometric decoration.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The supplied sources show geometric patterns on cane, fibre, gourds, bamboo and wood.

- **Cane belt** — This belt uses finely plaited dark brown cane cord, barkcloth and beaten bark ribbons.
- **Plant-fibre netbag** (*bilum*) — This rounded bag has red and black bands, diagonal lines, repeated rectangles and dark circular patches.
- **Pyroincised gourd decoration** — Gourd lime-containers carry spirals, circles, triangles, linear bands and geometric borders cut into the surface.
- **Pyroincised bamboo decoration** — Bamboo tobacco-pipes and cigar-holders carry serrated squares, diamonds, zigzags, chevrons, triangles and vertical bands.
- **Dwarf-coconut charm** — This charm has carved panels with zigzags, triangles, chevrons, diamonds and traces of red pigment.

### Clothing & dress

The sources describe one wearable belt.

- **Cane belt** — The belt is made from finely plaited cane cord, barkcloth and lightly beaten bark ribbons.

### Architecture

The Nara live in villages and hamlets, but the sources give no details about their buildings.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The documented everyday objects are mainly made from bamboo, gourds, plant fibre, cane, wood and coconut.

- **Bamboo tobacco-pipe** — The pipes have circular perforations, bamboo nodes, twisted fibre cords and incised geometric decoration.
- **Bamboo cigar-holder** — This pyroincised bamboo object has vertical chevron-filled bands and filled triangle motifs.
- **Gourd lime-container** — Some elongated or globular gourds held traces of lime powder and have carved geometric decoration.
- **Plant-fibre netbag** (*bilum*) — This rounded rectangular bag is made from unstained plant fibre and has a single handle.
- **Wooden model bullroarer** — This flat wooden model has diamond decoration and a hole threaded with twisted fibre cord.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources do not document jewelry or body practices, but they do record one carved charm.

- **Dwarf-coconut charm** — This charm is carved from a whole dwarf coconut, open at one end and hung by twisted plant-fibre cord.

## Music & performance

The sources do not document Nara music, but they record a model bullroarer.

- **Model bullroarer** — This flat wooden model has incised diamond decoration and a twisted fibre cord, but its sound and use are not described.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources give no festival or ritual descriptions, but they record a change from animist beliefs to Islam.

- **Animist beliefs** — The Nara traditionally followed animist beliefs.
- **Introduction of Islam** — The Nara were introduced to Islam by the 15th century, and most adopted it after the Egyptian occupation in the 19th century.

## Foodways

The Nara are mostly subsistence farmers.

- **Lime powder** — Some gourd containers and tobacco-pipes contain traces of lime powder.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Nara traditionally had no writing system, and the few recorded literary works use Tigre or Arabic writing.

- **Nara-Bana** — This name for the Nara language means “Nara-Talk.”
- **Literary transcription** — The few pieces of Nara literature were transcribed using either the Tigre or Arabic writing system.
- **Cultural preservation** — The Nara take part in cultural and linguistic preservation efforts through Eritrea’s Ministry of Information.

## Language & religion

Nara is spoken around Barentu and is classified either as Northern Eastern Sudanic or as a language isolate.

- **Nara language** (*Nara-Bana*) — Nara-Bana means “Nara-Talk,” and its endangerment status is unclear.
- **Higir dialect** (*Higir*) — Higir is the standard literary dialect spoken just north of Barentu.
- **Mogoreeb dialect** (*Mogoreeb*) — Mogoreeb is spoken from the outskirts of Haykota to Bisha village.
- **Saantoorta dialect** (*Saantoorta*) — Saantoorta is spoken west of Barentu.
- **Koyta dialect** (*Koyta*) — Koyta is spoken northeast of Barentu.
