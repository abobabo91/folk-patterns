## Overview

The Tiriki are associated with Kenya and the Kisumu area of East Africa. Museum records show wood carvers, adult men, adult women, and children. The sources describe clothing, tools, pottery, jewelry, musical instruments, and masks.

## Material culture

Tiriki material culture in the records includes pottery, wood, iron, leather, horn, cane, banana fibre, cow skin, animal skin, hide, glass beads, and reed.

### Clothing & dress

Recorded clothing includes blankets, shirts, shorts, cloths, loin-cloths, ornaments, scarification, and elaborate hairstyles.

- **Blanket** — One adult male wood carver wore a blanket with a beard, an American shirt, and shorts.
- **American waistcoat** — Another adult male wore an American waistcoat with shorts.
- **Cloths and loin-cloths** — Adults wore cloths, while children wore loin-cloths.
- **Women’s dress and adornment** — Two adult women wore loin-cloths, personal ornaments, torso scarification, and elaborate hairstyles.
- **Child’s patterned hairstyle** — A child was described as wearing personal ornaments and a patterned hairstyle.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Tiriki houses or settlement layouts, but one photograph shows a pot on a hearth inside a dwelling.

- **Tiriki dwelling interior** — A photograph shows a pot on a hearth, a partition on the left, and a basket on the right inside a Tiriki dwelling.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Tiriki objects in the records include pottery, wooden tools and vessels, iron tools, leather goods, horn containers, and cane baskets.

- **Salt-filtering double vessel** — A double pottery vessel was used for filtering salt.
- **Cooking bowls** — Pottery bowls were used for cooking vegetables and meat, including one meat-cooking bowl made by women.
- **Adze** — An adze with an iron blade and wooden handle was used by male woodworkers to carve bowls.
- **Hoe** — A hoe with an iron blade and wooden handle was used for digging but not cultivating.
- **Food basket** — A food basket was made of cane.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The records describe iron necklaces, rings, ear-ornaments, charms, armrings, scarification, hairstyles, and personal ornaments.

- **Iron necklace with rings** — One iron necklace had rings and a model knife.
- **Iron-wire necklace** — One iron-wire necklace had two small rings, one with a bell and the other with beads.
- **Iron necklace with pendants** — Another iron necklace had pendants.
- **Calf necklace** — A necklace hung from a calf’s neck was described as a charm against leanness.
- **Cow bell with charms** — A cow bell was hung with charms on a leather strap.

## Music & performance

The records include lyres, drums, horns, flutes, rattles, a musical bow, and a cow bell.

- **Lyre** — The collection includes lyres made from wood, hide, wire, and string.
- **Wooden drum** — A wooden drum had a cow-hide membrane fixed with hide thongs.
- **Horn trumpet** — A trumpet was made from antelope and cow horn joined with cow skin.
- **Reed flute** — The records include reed flutes with three or five holes.
- **Leg rattle** — Leg rattles were made from folded iron with iron balls or were strung on leather.

## Dance & theatre

The records mention wooden clubs or staffs reused as dancing staffs, but name no Tiriki dance or theatre form.

- **Dancing staff** — Wooden clubs or staffs with a round knob were re-used as dancing staffs.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not document a Tiriki festival calendar or named ceremonies, but they record circumcision masks and a calf charm.

- **Circumcision mask** — Two circumcision masks were woven from string, and one had two circular eyelet holes and animal fur between them.
- **Calf charm** — A calf necklace was described as a charm against leanness.

## Foodways

Food-related records include salt filters, cooking bowls, water and milk vessels, a milking jug, a beer-pot brush, and a fat container.

- **Gourd water or milk vessel** — Gourd vessels were used for carrying water or milk.
- **Beaded milk gourd** — A gourd with a cow-skin lid carried milk and was decorated with glass beads.
- **Wooden food bowl** — A wooden food bowl had a short handle.
- **Milking jug** — A wooden milking jug had an iron ring.
- **Beer-pot brush** — A brush made from a cow’s tail was used to clean a beer-pot or as a fly-whisk.
