## Overview

The Doma, or vaDoma, also known as Dema or Wadoma, are a band living in the Kanyemba region in the north of Zimbabwe, especially in the Hurungwe and Chipuriro districts around the basins of the Mwazamutanda River, a tributary of the Zambezi River Valley. They are described as the only traditional hunter-gatherers indigenous to Zimbabwe. Historically, they lived largely nomadic lives in the mountains through hunting, fishing, trapping, honey hunting, and gathering wild fruits and roots. Their cultural distinctiveness is also associated with the Dema language, their relations with neighboring Shona and Kunda people, and the inherited condition of ectrodactyly found among some vaDoma families.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They state that vaDoma are reluctant to wear textile fabrics, but provide no further account of everyday or ritual dress.

### Architecture

Many vaDoma families now live settled lives as semi-foragers. They build houses on wooden platforms to avoid predators, and during rainfall they cover the shelters with thatching. The mountain homeland of the vaDoma has become the Chewore Safari Area.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention hunting, fishing, trapping, honey hunting, and gathering.

### Jewelry & body adornment

They do describe ectrodactyly, in which the middle three toes are absent and the two outer ones are turned in; those with the condition are reported to be well integrated into the community.

## Music & performance

They mention vaDoma mythology and rumors among nearby peoples that the vaDoma can disappear in the forest and perform magic.

## Festivals & rituals

According to vaDoma mythology, their ancestors emerged from a baobab tree; after descending from it, they walked upright to hunt and gather the fruits of the land.

## Foodways

Historically, the vaDoma lived through hunting, fishing, trapping, honey hunting, and gathering wild fruits and roots. They state that the vaDoma’s continuing dispossession has made them Zimbabwe’s only non-agricultural society.

## Oral tradition & literature

VaDoma mythology says that the ancestors emerged from a baobab tree and then descended to hunt and gather. The name vaDoma is also used in the Zambezi region for a semi-mythical people characterized as magical, capricious, hard to find, and living among the trees. Rumors also persist among nearby peoples that the vaDoma can disappear in the forest and perform magic.

## Language & religion

The vaDoma speak the Dema language, which is closely related to the dominant Shona language of Zimbabwe and is largely comprehensible to speakers of the Korekore and Tande Shona dialects. Living alongside Shona and Kunda people in Kanyemba, they also speak Korekore Shona and Kunda. In 2014, the Seventh-day Adventist Church built Mariga Primary School to educate vaDoma children.
