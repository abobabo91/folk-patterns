## Overview

The Kabye, also known as Kabiye, Kabre and Cabrai, are a people living in the north central mountains and northern plains of Togo. They also live in northwestern Benin near the Togolese border. The Logba or Lugba people of Benin are closely related to them. The Kabye speak Kabiye, an Eastern Gurunsi Gur language. Broadly defined and including subgroups, they are the second largest ethnic group in Togo after the Ewe people. They are primarily known for farming and cultivation of harsh, dry, infertile lands, growing cotton, millet and yams. The Kabye are a patrilineal society primarily devoted to subsistence farming, while many are migrant laborers in the contemporary economy.

## Festivals & rituals

Evala is a form of traditional wrestling practised mainly by the Kabyé of northern Togo. Competitors meet yearly at a festival following a retreat that marks the initiation of young men into adulthood. Evala is the penultimate element of this initiation rite. During the preceding retreat, young men are separated from their families for one week and reside in special huts where they are fed and subjected to mental training.

Before wrestling, participants go on a pilgrimage involving the climbing of three mountains. Those who do not complete the pilgrimage are not initiated into adulthood. Wrestlers are initiated whether they win or lose, although losing is considered shameful to the family name. The final element of the initiation rites is circumcision.

## Foodways

The Kabye are primarily devoted to subsistence farming and cultivate harsh, dry, infertile lands of Togo. They grow cotton, millet and yams. The Evala initiation retreat includes feeding the young men who reside in special huts for one week.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kabiye publications include two books of proverbs, folktales, poetry, medical booklets, agricultural booklets, translations of the Bible, political tracts, religious tracts, a short novel, primers and other pedagogical materials. The earliest known publications in Kabiye appeared in the 1930s. Altogether, there have been about 200 publications in Kabiye, though not all remain in print or are easily available for purchase.

## Language & religion

Kabiye is an Eastern Gurunsi Gur language spoken primarily in northern Togo. Throughout the 20th century, there was extensive migration of Kabiye speakers to the centre and south of Togo and also to Ghana and Benin. Kabiye is one of two national languages of Togo, along with Ewe, and is promoted in national media and in the formal education sector as an optional examination subject in grades 9 and 10.

Kabiye was first written in the 1930s. In the early 1980s, the Comité de Langue Nationale Kabiyè, now the Académie Kabiyè, standardized its orthography. Kabiye is written in a modified Roman script based on the character inventory of the African reference alphabet. An alternative orthography promoted by R.P. Adjola Raphaël is widely used among Catholics.
