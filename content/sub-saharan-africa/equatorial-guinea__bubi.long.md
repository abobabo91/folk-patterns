## Overview

The Bubi, also known as Bobe, Voove, Ewota and Bantu Bubi, are a Bantu ethnic group indigenous to Bioko Island in Equatorial Guinea. Bubi communities also exist in the diaspora and among Bubi native to Gabon and Cameroon. The group once formed the majority population in the region but now numbers around 100,000 worldwide. The Bubi speak the Bube language, a Bantu language with North, South and Central-East variants. Their cultural history includes regional forms of self-designation, a distinctive language and belief system, Bubi royal dynasties, and material objects recorded from Fernando Po, Gabun or Ogove, and Equatorial Guinea.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources used do not provide an annual Bubi festival calendar or describe named seasonal celebrations. They also do not document Bubi birth, coming-of-age, wedding, or funeral rites. Traditional pre-Christian beliefs are described in the religion section of the Bubi article. The Bubi spiritual world was said to include Labako-ppua, heaven and the angels; Ommo ich'ori, hell and the bad angels; and Ommo boeboe, limbo. The head god was called Rupe, or Eri in the southern region of the island. Natural landmarks, including rivers, lakes and mountains, were associated with spirits, while sinister spirits were blamed for illness, accidents and misfortune.

## Language & religion

Bube, also called Bubi, Bohobé, Bube–Benga or Etyö, is a Bantu language spoken predominantly by the Bubi. It was brought to Bioko from continental Africa more than three thousand years ago. The language has around 50,000 speakers and three variants: North, South and Central-East. It is tonal and distinguishes words by gender. The Bube language is divided into six dialects, including Rebola and its variations—Basile, Banapa and Basupa—in the North, and Bakake in the North-East. Bube is also spoken in Gabon and Cameroon and in a small mainland area where speakers are shifting to Wumboko.

The Bubi are mainly Catholics, a religious orientation associated in the source with Spanish influence during the colonial age. Traditional pre-Christian beliefs remain prevalent in Bubi spirituality. Rupe, or Eri in the southern region, was the head god who created and cared for the world. The spiritual and material worlds were understood to overlap.
