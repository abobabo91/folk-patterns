## Overview

The Khoikhoi, also called Khoekhoe, are an indigenous population of Southern Africa traditionally associated with nomadic pastoralism. They are often discussed together with the hunter-gatherer San under the designation Khoisan, although the two categories do not fully describe the historical relationships among pastoralists, foragers, and agriculturalists. Khoekhoe-speaking peoples include the Nama of Namibia, Botswana, and South Africa, the Damara of Namibia, the Orana clans of South Africa, the Khoemana or Griqua nation, and the Gqunukhwebe or Gona clans associated with Xhosa-speaking polities. Their language belongs to the Khoe language family and is spoken primarily by Namakhoen, ǂNūkhoen, and Haiǁomkhoen. Khoekhoe culture is significant for its pastoral traditions, click consonants, regional clan structures, and surviving material culture.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The supplied sources do not document named Khoekhoe textile or pattern traditions beyond leather garments and beadwork recorded in museum catalogues.

**Leather beadwork** — British Museum records describe several back aprons made of leather and decorated or faced with beadwork. The catalogues do not identify a named pattern system, motif vocabulary, or distinction from neighboring cultures.

**Ostrich-shell bead ornament** — A quiver model is ornamented around the mouth with ostrich shell beads and eight small sinew strings carrying similar beads. A composite pouch is also ornamented with sixteen strings of ostrich shell beads.

### Clothing & dress

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum records describe a composite pouch made from a small tortoise carapace with a leather handle. It is ornamented with sixteen strings of ostrich shell beads, two of which have reddish brown seeds attached to their ends; the pouch contains greenish powder and a plug of soft fibre. Other catalogued objects include a leather quiver model, smoking pipes made from hard brown wood, dark green serpentine, and serpentine pipe-bowls, as well as a European-style steatite pipe-bowl. No ceramics, named metalworking tradition, or household technology is documented in the supplied sources.

They also record a leather head-band faced with cowrie shells, ostrich shell bead strings on a quiver and pouch, and reddish brown seeds attached to two pouch strings. ## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not provide an annual festival calendar, named festivals, seasonal celebrations, wedding rites, funerals, initiation ceremonies, or other life-cycle rituals. They do describe religious beliefs in Khoe-speaking cultures. The Moon receives special significance and may have been viewed as the physical manifestation of a supreme being associated with heaven. Thiǁoab, called Tsui'goab in Nama and ǁGamab in Damara mythology, is described as the creator and guardian of health, while ǁGaunab is associated with sickness or death. Many Khoe-speakers have converted to Christianity, and Nama Muslims make up a large percentage of Namibia’s Muslims.

## Foodways

The sources do not document named dishes, cooking methods, food restrictions, tea or coffee traditions, or ceremonial foods. They do state that pastoral communities kept sheep, goats, and cattle, and that these animals grazed in fertile valleys. Husbandry provided a stable and balanced diet and supported the expansion of pastoral lifestyles. In the Cape region, Khoekhoe maintained large herds of Nguni cattle in the 17th century. The sources do not identify particular preparations of meat or milk, nor do they describe a distinct food vocabulary.

## Language & religion

Khoekhoe, also called Khoikhoi or Khoekhoegowab, belongs to the Khoe language family. It is spoken in Namibia, Botswana, and South Africa, primarily by Namakhoen, ǂNūkhoen, and Haiǁomkhoen. Modern scholars generally recognize three dialects: Nama–Damara, including Sesfontein Damara; Haiǁom; and ǂĀkhoe. Eini is described as extinct. Khoekhoe is a national language in Namibia, and state-owned broadcasting corporations in Namibia and South Africa produce radio programmes in it. The language has 20 click consonants among its 31 consonants, and its grammar includes masculine, feminine, and neuter noun classes, together with singular, dual, and plural numbers. Khoe-speaking religious mythology gives special significance to the Moon. Thiǁoab, Tsui'goab, and ǁGamab are associated with creation and health, while ǁGaunab is associated with sickness or death. Christianity and Islam are also present among Khoe-speaking communities.
