## Overview

The Kakwa, also rendered Kakuwâ, are a Nilotic ethnic group primarily found in the border regions of northwestern Uganda, southwestern South Sudan, and northeastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, particularly west of the White Nile river. In South Sudan, they are present in Yei and Morobo County, while related communities live in Koboko District, Imgbokolo, and Aba. The Kakwa are part of the larger Karo people, an intermarried group that also includes the Bari, Pojulu, Mundari, Kuku, Ngepo, and Nyangwara. They are described as a small minority. Their language, Kutuk na Kakwa, is an Eastern Nilotic language, and Kakwa communities share linguistic and cultural traditions across political borders.

## Foodways

Kakwa livelihood has traditionally included cultivating corn, millet, and cassava, along with fishing and cattle. Foods traditionally consumed include maize, cassava, sorghum, millet, beans, cowpeas, sesame, groundnuts, and palm oil; the diet also features yams, sweet potatoes, and local fruits.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kakwa oral tradition holds that the people migrated from East Africa, described as the Nubian region, from the city of Kawa between the third and fourth cataracts of the Nile. According to this account, they first moved into South Sudan and later southwards into Uganda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

## Language & religion

Kakwa, also rendered Kakuwâ, is a Nilotic language spoken by the Kakwa people in Uganda, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and South Sudan. The Kakwa language is identified as an Eastern Nilotic language, while the people are classified within the larger Karo grouping. Some Kakwa who bordered Uganda converted to Islam and accepted the Maliki school of Sunni theology in the medieval era. Christian and Islamic traditions form part of Kakwa cultural value systems and living style.
