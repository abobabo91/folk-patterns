## Overview

The Biafada are an ethnic group of Guinea-Bissau, Senegal and Gambia, often considered a subgroup of the Tenda people. They are also known as Biafara, Beafada, Biafar, Bidyola, Dfola, Dyola, Fada and Yola. In Guinea-Bissau, they live in several areas: a small group occupies the north bank of the Geba River; two large groups live in Quinara Region in the southwestern part of the country; and another group lives in Tombali, on the border with Guinea Conakry. The Biafada language belongs to the Niger-Congo language family. Their documented folk-cultural life includes farming, livestock raising, puberty ceremonies, circumcision, Islam combined with animistic rites, and the historical kingdoms of Biguda, Guinala and Bissege.

## Festivals & rituals

A Biafada ceremony celebrates each child’s preparation to enter puberty. Circumcision is characteristic of these ceremonies and normally applies to males, although it sometimes also applies to females.

In more conservative families, pregnancy outside marriage is forbidden for a woman. When this occurs, the woman and the man are subject to a punishment known as *di minjer justisa*, literally “justice for women.” Guinea-Bissau banned this punishment in the 1970s. Polygamy is common.

## Foodways

The Biafada are farmers, and their staple crops are cassava and rice. They also raise maize, squash, melons, potatoes, peppers and tomatoes, crops described as having originated in other parts of the world. Biafada livestock raising includes sheep and goats for meat; they do not drink milk from those animals.

## Language & religion

Biafada, also called *ga-njoola*, is a Senegambian language of Guinea-Bissau and belongs to the Niger-Congo language family. It is heavily influenced by Mandinka. Recorded variants of the name include Beafada, Bedfola, Biafar, Bidyola, Dfola and Fada. In Guinea-Bissau, the Biafada speak the dialects Gool, Bubwas, Guinala and Bagandada in different areas. Historically, they were grouped into the kingdoms of Biguda, Guinala and Bissege.

The majority of Biafadas are Sunni Muslims. Some are Catholics or animists who believe that objects have spirits, and Biafadas mix Islam with animistic rites. About a dozen Biafada are Protestants, most resident in Biafada, while other Biafadas are Catholics in the capital.
