## Overview

The Banda are an ethnic group of the Central African Republic, also found in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Cameroon, and South Sudan. Traditionally found in the northeastern part of the Central African Republic, they numbered around 1.3 million people at the turn of the 21st century. They speak languages belonging to the Niger-Congo family, known as Banda or Ubangian languages, with nine distinct geographically distributed vernaculars. Their cultural record includes dispersed home groups guided by a headman, hunting, fishing, gathering wild foods, crop cultivation, carved wooden objects, animal-shaped slit drums, wooden pipes, rites of passage, and religious practices that combine Christianity with retained elements of traditional religious systems and values.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Banda are locally famous for craftsmanship, especially carved wooden objects used for rituals and general utility. The sources also describe large animal-shaped slit drums, which were used for musical celebrations and as tools for transmitting messages.

Museum catalogue records held by the British Museum include throwing knives made of iron or steel, in some cases with handles bound with fibre, vegetable fibre, plaited cane, or cane strips. One steel throwing knife has three blades. The catalogue also records iron circumcision knives, including one with a wooden handle bound with vegetable fibre, and wooden staffs, including one terminating in a carved human head with a diamond-shaped mid-section marked by lined and diamond-shaped incisions.

## Music & performance

The sources identify large animal-shaped slit drums as important Banda instruments. These drums, attributed names such as *Banda-Yangere*, served both musical and communicative functions: they were used in musical celebrations and for transmitting messages. The Banda-Linda group is known for music using wooden pipes, also called Banda-Linda Horns.

## Festivals & rituals

The Banda have rites of passage, including *Semali*, which recognizes the crossing into adulthood.

Banda religious practice includes making sacrificial offerings to ancestral spirits for seasonal success of crops. Under French colonial rule, most Banda converted to Christianity while retaining elements of traditional religious systems and values. In contemporary times, most Banda people are described as Protestants or Catholics: 52% are Protestant and 38% Catholic.

The sources state that dowries in the form of bridewealth traditionally included iron implements for the family. Polygyny was practiced historically but has declined in modern times.

## Foodways

The sources state that the Banda sustain themselves through hunting, fishing, gathering wild foods, and growing crops. In contemporary times they are settled farmers in the savannas. Cotton and cassava farming was promoted among them by French colonial officials.

## Language & religion

The Banda speak languages belonging to the Niger-Congo family, known as Banda or Ubangian languages. The languages have variations, and nine distinct geographically distributed vernaculars are known.

Under French colonial rule, most Banda converted to Christianity but retained elements of traditional religious systems and values. In contemporary times, most Banda people are Protestants or Catholics, with 52% identified as Protestants and 38% as Catholics. Traditional beliefs continue alongside Christianity, including sacrificial offerings to ancestral spirits for seasonal success of crops.

The Banda are patrilineal. Traditionally, they lived in dispersed home groups guided by a headman. During crises, they selected war chiefs to resist slave raids and respond to wars; after the crisis, the warriors were relieved of their powers.
