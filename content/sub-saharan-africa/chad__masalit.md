## Overview

The Masalit are a farming people of the dry savanna at the Chad–Sudan border. They speak Masalit, a Nilo-Saharan language, and practice Islam mixed with older customs. Most live in West Darfur around Geneina, with communities in eastern Sudan and Chad. They are known for subsistence agriculture, distinctive conical dwellings, and a sultanate history.

## Material culture

The Masalit material culture is documented mainly through metalwork, with little recorded information about textiles, ceramics, or household objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

No textile or weaving traditions are documented for the Masalit in available sources.

### Clothing & dress

Little is known about everyday or ceremonial dress, but a distinctive weapon is documented.

- **Arm-dagger** — A blade of iron and leather worn on the arm, paired with a sheath of wood and leather, held by the British Museum.

### Architecture

The typical Masalit dwelling is a cone-shaped structure built from local materials.

- **Conical house** — A dwelling built of wood frame and grass thatch, designed to shed seasonal rain in the dry savanna.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Metalwork is the only craft documented, with iron smiths and hide workers collaborating on weapons.

- **Arm-dagger with sheath** — Iron and leather blade paired with wood and leather sheath, made by specialized smiths and leather workers.

### Jewelry & body adornment

No jewellery, beadwork, henna, scarification, tattooing or hairdressing practices are documented.

## Music & performance

No musical instruments or song genres are documented, but the language itself has two registers for performance.

- **Heavy Masalit** (*heavy variety*) — The complex form of the language used by higher-ranking people and in rural areas.
- **Light Masalit** (*light variety*) — The simplified form of the language used at home and in markets, with many Arabic loanwords.

## Dance & theatre

No Masalit dance or dramatic performance is documented.

## Festivals & rituals

No festival calendar or life-cycle ceremonies are documented, but Islam blended with older beliefs shapes religious life.

- **Islam** — Muslim practice arrived around the seventeenth century through itinerant holy men and incorporates older local customs.

## Foodways

The Masalit are subsistence farmers whose diet centres on two staple crops.

- **Peanuts** — A principal crop grown throughout Masalit territory.
- **Millet** — A principal grain crop grown throughout Masalit territory.
- **Sorghum** — A grain crop grown in the southern, wetter part of Masalit territory.

## Oral tradition & literature

Masalit oral traditions have been collected by scholars, and origin narratives trace the people's migration from Tunisia.

- **Masalit stories** — A collection published by John Edgar in African Languages and Cultures in 1990.
- **Origin narrative** — The Masalit trace their beginnings to Tunisia, migrated through Chad, and settled in Sudan.

## Language & religion

Masalit belongs to the Nilo-Saharan family and exists in several dialects and social registers, with most speakers also fluent in Arabic.

- **Masalit language** (*Masalit*) — A Nilo-Saharan language written in Latin script, related to Marfa, Maba and Karanga.
- **West Darfur dialect** — The variety of Masalit spoken in West Darfur around Geneina.
- **South Darfur dialect** — A distinct variety of Masalit spoken in South Darfur.
- **Northern dialect** — A dialect spoken east and north of Geneina.
- **Sudanese Arabic** — The region's lingua franca, spoken by most Masalit alongside their native language.

- *Geneina* — capital of West Darfur and Masalit centre
- *Ouaddaï* — region in Chad where Masalit communities live

- *sultanate* — historical political system of the Masalit
