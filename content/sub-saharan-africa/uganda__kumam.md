## Overview

The Kumam are an ethnic group of about 720,000 people according to the 2024 census. They live mainly in the Kumam Sub-Region of Kaberamaido, Soroti, Soroti City, Kalaki, parts of Serere, Dokolo and Amolatar, in the western areas of the Teso sub-region and the south-east of the Lango sub-region. They are also found around the Lake Kyoga Basin and at the shores of Lake Kyoga, including Namasale. The Kumam share Soroti district with the Iteso and parts of the former Lira district with the Langi. Kumam belongs to the Southern Lwoo group and has 82 percent lexical similarity with the Acholi dialect and 81 percent with the Lango dialect. Music, dance, storytelling, marriage customs and clan organization are documented aspects of Kumam cultural life.

## Music & performance

Music played an important part in Kumam life. It could be made at ceremonial, religious and political functions. The political organization included *wegi ikodeta Cel*, described as leaders of dancing groups, and *wegi Cel*, leaders of *Asonya* homes.

## Dance & theatre

Dance formed part of Kumam social life, and music and dance were used at ceremonial, religious and political functions.

## Festivals & rituals

They describe several social and life-cycle practices. In the past, parents arranged marriages and girls could be betrothed to boys at an early age. A betrothed girl might be taken to the boy’s home to grow up there, and when she came of age a ceremony was organized to formalize the relationship. In the described contemporary practice, a boy might take a girl to his home at night without the consent of the parents. After about a week, the girl’s relatives would look for her; once she was found, a fine was exacted from the boy, arrangements were made to settle the bride wealth, and the marriage was formalized.

The political structure traditionally included clan leaders known as *wegi Atekerin*. These leaders maintained law and order, administered affairs and arbitrated matters of politics and social life. The sources also describe a contemporary cultural chiefdom led by the supreme cultural leader, the Won Ateker.

## Oral tradition & literature

Storytelling was part of Kumam lifestyle and oral tradition. In the evening, older people narrated stories to the younger generation.

The sources describe the Kumam language as related to the language spoken by the Lango, with some Ateso vocabulary. They also state that Kumam has lexical similarities with Acholi and Lango. Kumam traditions say that the Kumam are part of the Iteso people. The sources further describe contact and intermarriage with neighboring peoples and the adoption of some words from the Western Nilotic Luo spoken by the Lango.

## Language & religion

Kumam is a language of the Southern Lwoo group spoken by the Kumam people of Uganda. It is estimated to have 82 percent lexical similarity with the Acholi dialect and 81 percent with the Lango dialect. The sources describe a Kumam dialect with ten vowels and a vowel-harmony system based on the presence or absence of advanced tongue root. They identify six tones: low, high, falling, rising, downstep high and double downstep high. The sources also provide examples of tone sandhi, consonant gemination and morphological processes affecting vowel length.

The sources mention religious functions and refer to a biblical translation using the terms *Rubanga*, *polo* and *piny*.
