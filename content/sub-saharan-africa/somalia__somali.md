## Overview

The Somali are the pre-eminent camel pastoralists of the eastern Horn of Africa, organized around patrilineal clan-families and a segmentary lineage system. Their economy historically balanced pastoral herding on interior plateaus against maritime trading cultures on the Benadir coast. Somali culture is exceptionally poetry-rich, with an oral literary canon recognised by UNESCO, and is known for distinctive material crafts including coiled basketry, coastal narrow-strip weaving, and fine silverwork. They are almost entirely Sunni Muslim with long-established Sufi traditions.

## Material culture

Somali material culture is centred on basketry, wood, leather and silver, with relatively little indigenous ceramics.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The coastal Benadiri weaving tradition produces indigo-and-red checked or striped cloths on pit treadle looms, while interior textiles rely on imported cotton shaped into traditional wraps.

- **Alindi** (*alandi*) — A red-and-indigo checked or striped women's wrap that serves as the classic Benadiri bridal cloth.
- **Shuqqad** (*shuqa*) — A plain white cotton body cloth worn as a shoulder wrap by men, sometimes finished with a coloured stripe.
- **Guntiino** — An untailored length of cloth wrapped over one shoulder and knotted to form the classic Somali woman's dress.
- **Dirac** — A long, sheer voile or polyester over-dress worn over a petticoat and shoulder shawl, the standard formal dress of Somali women.
- **Benadiri weaving** (*dhar Banaadiri*) — Coastal narrow-strip loom tradition of Mogadishu, Merca and Brava using handspun cotton dyed with indigo and local reds.

### Clothing & dress

Men wear checked sarongs, long shirts and embroidered caps, while women centre their dress around wrapped cloths and headscarves, with formal contexts requiring layered ensembles and heavy jewellery.

- **Macawiis** — A sarong-like waist wrap, most often in South-East Asian checked cotton, worn by men in the interior.
- **Khamiis** — A Western or long shirt worn above the macawiis by men, paired with a turban on formal occasions.
- **Koofiyad** — An embroidered cap worn by men as a marker of adult identity.
- **Shash** — A headscarf worn by married women to cover the hair in the pastoral interior.
- **Xirsi** — Amuletic leather cases containing Qur'anic verse, sewn to a cord or belt and worn by children and travellers.

### Architecture

Pastoral dwellings are portable dome-huts with hemispherical frames, while coastal towns feature coral-stone townhouses with carved plaster and wooden doors.

- **Aqal** (*aqal Soomaali, aqal geed*) — A portable dome-hut framed with bent acacia saplings and covered with woven fibre mats, assembled and packed by women.
- **Mundul** — A sturdier conical thatched round-house of sedentary agro-pastoral and riverine communities, built with a central pole and mud-and-wattle walls.
- **Benadiri townhouses** (*jiingad*) — Flat-roofed coral-limestone houses built around interior courtyards, with carved plaster and teak doors in the Swahili-Omani tradition.
- **Fakhr ad-Din mosque** — A 13th-century mosque in Mogadishu representing historic monumental architecture.
- **Zeila and Berbera** — Medieval trading towns on the Gulf of Aden coast.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Somali craftsmanship excels in coiled basketry, silver work, and carved wood, with little indigenous fired ceramics.

- **Xeedho** — A tightly coiled, lidded milk-and-food container with a narrow neck, decorated in bands of red, black and natural fibre; ceremonial versions are opened by the groom's kin on the seventh night of weddings.
- **Dhiil** — A taller coiled milk vessel used for camel milk and its fermented form caano geel.
- **Somali silver** — Silversmiths produce heavy anklets, spiral bracelets, chest amulets and filigree clasps using granulation, filigree and repoussé techniques.
- **Barkin** — A small wooden stool-like neck-rest used by pastoralists to preserve elaborate hairstyles during sleep.
- **Billaawe** — The classic Somali men's dagger with a leaf-shaped blade and leather-and-silver sheath, historically an item of male adult dress.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Somali jewellery emphasises silver over gold and amuletic over purely decorative forms, with prayer beads and henna as essential dress items.

- **Jimbaar** — Spiral cuff bracelets made of silver, worn by both men and women.
- **Tusbax** — Prayer beads, typically 99 or 33 beads of wood, amber, coral or plastic, carried by adult men as both dress and devotion.
- **Henna** (*xenna*) — Geometric patterns worked on hands and feet, central to weddings and Eids.
- **Koofiyad** — An embroidered cap serving as a primary marker of adult male identity.
- **Xirsi** — Triangular or cylindrical amuletic amulet cases worn on a cord across the chest.

## Music & performance

Somali music is predominantly vocal and poetry-driven, organised by work genre and ritual context, with sung poetry as the prestige form.

- **Gabay** — A long-lined alliterative poem of praise, invective or political argument, the prestige adult form requiring strict alliteration on a single consonant.
- **Geeraar** — A shorter martial or horseback poem, often sung while riding.
- **Heello** (*hees*) — A modern song form that grew out of the balwo love-lyric of Borama in the 1940s and became enormously popular in the twentieth century.
- **Shareero** — A lyre cognate with the Ethiopian krar, used to accompany vocal poetry.
- **Somali oral poetry** (*Hees Diiwaan*) — Inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2022.

## Dance & theatre

Social dancing is organised in facing lines or circles with rhythmic clapping and stamping, while formal theatre emerged in the mid-twentieth century.

- **Dhaanto** — A northern courting dance associated with the Ogaden, now emblematic of Somali dance, pairing sung couplets with stepping and hip-swinging line movements.
- **Saylici and jaandheer** — Related northern dance forms to the dhaanto.
- **Shirib and saar** — Southern dances of inter-riverine and coastal populations showing Bantu and Arab influences, linked to possession-cult drumming and saar healing traditions.
- **Wedding dances** (*aroos*) — The principal public dance context, with the bride's henna evening and seventh-day xeedho opening as choreographed set-pieces.
- **Riwaayad** — A sung-play emerging in the mid-twentieth century at the National Theatre in Mogadishu, dominating Somali stage culture from the 1950s until 1991.

## Festivals & rituals

The Somali ritual year is framed by Islamic festivals and is dominated by weddings as the great social spectacle, with life-cycle and pastoral rites marked by poetic genres.

- **Ciid al-Fitr and Ciid al-Adha** — Islamic festivals celebrated with new clothes, henna, communal prayer and animal slaughter.
- **Wedding ceremonies** (*nikaax*) — Structured over several days from contract through henna evening to the seventh-night xeedho, in which the groom's kin untie an elaborately bound ceremonial basket.
- **Henna evening** (*xafladda xenna*) — The night before or early in a wedding when henna is applied to the bride's hands and feet in dense geometric fields.
- **Dabshid** (*Neyruus*) — A pre-Islamic fire festival marking the solar new year, historically celebrated with bonfires and mock stick-fights, surviving in attenuated form in the south.
- **Naming ceremony** (*siyaaro, aqiiqa*) — Held on the seventh day after birth, marking entry into the community.

## Foodways

Somali cuisine blends pastoral, Arab, Persian, Indian and Italian influences, with camel and goat milk as the dietary base of the interior.

- **Bariis iskukaris** — A spiced pilau of basmati rice with cardamom, cumin and cloves cooked with lamb or goat.
- **Canjeero** (*laxoox*) — A sourdough pancake close to Ethiopian injera but smaller and finer, eaten at breakfast with ghee and sugar or with stew.
- **Sabaayad** — A layered flatbread of clear Indian derivation.
- **Muqmad** (*oodkac*) — Dried and clarified-butter-preserved meat carried by travellers.
- **Qaad** (*jaad, khat*) — Chewed leaves consumed in afternoon male sociability sessions called mafrish, a defining Somali social institution.

## Oral tradition & literature

Somali culture is one of the most poetry-saturated societies on earth, with a hierarchy of verse genres composed and memorised without writing until the 1972 script reform.

- **Gabay** — A long-lined alliterative poem of praise, invective or political argument, the prestige adult form.
- **Buraanbur** — The women's alliterative form, performed at weddings and social gatherings.
- **Abtirsiinyo** — Clan genealogies and historical narratives forming part of the prose tradition.
- **Maahmaah** — Somali proverbs, a large body of traditional wisdom sayings.
- **Somali kitab** — Arabic-script religious books, Qur'ans, and Sufi devotional works often with Somali marginalia in wadaad's writing, carried by brotherhoods and preserved in coastal towns and interior xer settlements.

## Language & religion

Somali is a Lowland East Cushitic language written in Arabic script, Osmanya alphabet, and Latin orthography since 1972, and Somalis are almost entirely Sunni Muslim with strong Sufi traditions.

- **Af Soomaali** — The Somali language, a Lowland East Cushitic tongue with three main dialect clusters: Northern, Benadiri, and Maay.
- **Wadaad's writing** — Arabic-script adaptation used for religious purposes from at least the 13th century.
- **Osmanya** — An indigenous alphabet invented by Cismaan Yuusuf Keenadiid around 1920.
- **Qadiriyya** — A long-established Sufi brotherhood dominant in Somali religious life.
- **Qubbo** — Shrine-tombs of local saints, venerated widely in the countryside despite reformist pressure.
