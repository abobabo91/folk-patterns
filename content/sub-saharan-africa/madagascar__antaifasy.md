## Overview

The Antaifasy are an ethnic group of Madagascar living in the southeastern coastal region around Farafangana. Their homeland is historically called Ifasy, between Imoro to the north and Isaka to the south. Their name means “people of the sand.” Approximately 150,000 Antaifasy inhabited Madagascar as of 2013. Historically, they were fishing and farming people, with rice cultivation and fishing in freshwater lakes and rivers among their traditional livelihoods. Antaifasy society was traditionally divided into three clans—the Randroy, Andrianseranana and Marofela—each governed by its own king and guided by strict moral codes. They speak a dialect of Malagasy, a branch of the Malayo-Polynesian language group derived from the Barito languages of southern Borneo.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Bark cloth** — Traditional Antaifasy clothing used bark cloth made from a mixture of fibers blended for sheen and softness. Bark cloth from the Antaifasy region became a specialty trade product of the area.

**Woven reed and sedge mats** — Antaifasy clothing was also made from woven mats of beaten reeds or sedges sewn together.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Antaifasy clothing consisted of bark cloth or woven mats of beaten reeds or sedges sewn together. Women wore the material as a tube belted at the waist or pulled up at the shoulder. Women and adolescent girls also often wore a *mahampy* reed band or short top, with or without sleeves, to cover the breasts. Men wore a bark loincloth and typically added a vest or tunic over it; sleeves were added for older men. Woven hats were commonly worn by Antaifasy men.

### Architecture

Antaifasy funeral practice includes the *kibory*, a funeral house located in a sacred and distant patch of forest.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe strict moral codes guiding Antaifasy social life. They do document funeral practice: like the Antaisaka, the Antaifasy do not bury their dead, but place them in a *kibory* funeral house located in a sacred and distant patch of forest.

## Foodways

The sources identify rice cultivation and fishing from freshwater lakes and rivers as traditional Antaifasy livelihoods.

## Language & religion

The Antaifasy speak a dialect of the Malagasy language. Malagasy is described as a branch of the Malayo-Polynesian language group derived from the Barito languages spoken in southern Borneo. They do refer to sacred and distant forest areas associated with the *kibory* funeral house, but provide no further account of Antaifasy spiritual practices.
