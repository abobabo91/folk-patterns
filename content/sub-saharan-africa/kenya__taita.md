## Overview

The Taita are a Bantu people who settled the Taita Hills between 1000 and 1300. They organized themselves into independent clan territories rather than a unified tribe. Their culture centers on honoring the dead and ancestors through ritual practices, and they are documented for their distinctive nineteenth-century ornament traditions of beadwork, ear-plugs and metal.

## Material culture

Taita material culture is known primarily through ornament and ritual objects — beadwork, carved ear-plugs, iron bells and metal armlets — rather than textiles or household goods.

### Textile & pattern traditions

No woven textile traditions are documented; the Taita expressed their ornamental vocabulary through beadwork and fiber worn on the body.

- **Beaded waist ornament** — A band of red beads hung with single-strand tassels of red, white or dark blue beads, representing the nineteenth-century female bead palette.
- **Sem-sem bead collars** (*sem-sem*) — Dark blue and red beads worn in large quantities around the neck, massed so thickly the wearer's chin tilted backward.

### Clothing & dress

Nineteenth-century Taita dress featured short cotton kilts with multiple strands of beads, shaved heads with a crown of beaded hair, and body decoration with oils and clay.

- **Cotton loincloth or kilt** — Women wore short cotton kilts with bead girdles and multiple strands of beads.
- **Beaded hair crown** — A circular crown of hair on an otherwise shaved head, dressed with strands of beads falling over ears and shoulders.
- **Nasal and lip studs** — Some women wore ivory or wooden studs set into nostrils and lips, following styles seen among Zanzibari enslaved women.
- **Body oils and clay** — Animal and coconut oils layered with clay were applied to skin against harsh conditions.

### Architecture

Taita built and natural landscape held ritual and practical significance, centered on sacred hills, caves and forests rather than domestic structures.

- **Magamba rocks** (*Magamba*) — Prominent rocks in the Taita Hills treated as places of worship.
- **Mbanga caves** (*Mbanga*) — Caves used as resting places for the dead, and also as shelter, security and isolation wards for people with contagious diseases.
- **Sacred forests** (*Fighi*) — Closed forests comparable to the Mijikenda's Kaya, reserved for ritual use and functioning as conservation reserves.
- **Wabasi sacred meeting places** (*Wabasi*) — The secret Wabasi cult maintained its own sacred forests and meeting places separate from general Taita worship.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Museum collections show ornamental and ritual metalwork and carving rather than domestic pottery or tools.

- **Crescent iron leg bells** — Iron bells with iron or lead clappers threaded on hide thongs, worn on the leg to produce sound with movement.
- **Knobkerrie** — A club carved from ebony with an ovoid head.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ear ornament is the best-documented Taita jewelry form, along with extensive armlets, leglets and body modification showing wealth and status.

- **Wooden ear-plugs** — Circular carved wood discs with central holes, concave on top and bottom with concave outer edges.
- **Tin-inlaid ear ornament** — Carved wood inlaid with circular tin decorations.
- **Wire ear hoops and glass rings** — Numerous wire hoops set with small beads worn in one ear and larger glass rings in the other, symbolizing wealth.
- **Pewter and bead armlets** — Large armlets worn by women, made of pewter and beads.
- **Tooth filing and discoloring** — Teeth were filed and discolored as a mark of identity and status.

## Music & performance

No song genres or instruments are documented, though crescent iron leg bells with clappers produced sound.

## Dance & theatre

No named Taita dance or dramatic tradition is documented in available sources.

## Festivals & rituals

Taita ritual centered on the dead and ancestors rather than annual festivals, with skull burial in caves and routine ancestor sacrifice.

- **Skull burial practice** — The Dawida kept skulls of elderly men over seventy; the Saghalla kept skulls of women, children, men and occasionally strangers who died in their territory.
- **Ancestor sacrifice** — Routine sacrifice was made to ancestors and household gods; the supreme god Mlungu was invoked only during calamity or misfortune.
- **Wabasi secret cult** (*Wabasi*) — A feared secret cult with membership rules about burial; only an Abasi member could bury another member.
- **Marriage negotiation** (*Wupe*) — Pre-arranged polygamous marriage was negotiated between the bride's father and maternal uncles over dowry paid in livestock installments.
- **Bride kidnapping** — When girls reached marriageable age, prospective in-laws took them in the evening as they went to fetch water or firewood.

## Foodways

No staples, dishes or cooking methods are documented; only the practice of provisioning isolated people with food during contagious illness.

## Oral tradition & literature

No folktales, epics, proverbs or riddles are documented; one remembered historical figure resisted British colonization.

- **Mwangeka (Joseph Mwambi)** — A legendary figure in Taita memory for resisting British colonial encroachment on Wataita land.

## Language & religion

Taita speaks Kidawida, a Bantu language with multiple dialects, influenced by now-extinct Cushitic languages; most are Christian but some practice ancestor religion.

- **Kidawida language** (*Kidawida*) — A Bantu language closely related to Chaga, with Saghalla variety distinct enough to count as a separate language.
- **Saghalla language** (*Kisaghala*) — The Saghalla dialect variety, closely related to Kigiriama or Mijikenda.
- **Taita Cushitic loanwords** — Dawida and Saghalla carry loanwords from two extinct South Cushitic languages, whose lateral obstruents were still pronounced within living memory.
- **Mlungu** — The supreme god in Taita ancestral religion, invoked during times of calamity for appeasement or thanksgiving.
- **Household gods** — Minor spirits to whom routine sacrifice was made for everyday blessing and protection.

## Glossary

- *Kidawida* — The Taita language, a Bantu language with multiple dialects
- *sem-sem* — Dark blue and red beads worn by Taita women in large quantities around the neck
- *Magamba* — Prominent rocks in the Taita Hills treated as places of worship
- *Mbanga* — Caves used as burial places for the dead and isolation wards for the sick
- *Fighi* — Sacred forests closed to other uses and serving as conservation reserves
- *Wabasi* — A feared secret cult with sacred forests and ritual membership requirements
- *Wupe* — Marriage negotiation conducted between the bride's family and groom's family
- *Mlungu* — The supreme god in Taita ancestral religion
- *Kisaghala* — The Saghalla dialect variety of Taita, closely related to Kigiriama
- *Kitaita* — Alternative name for the Kidawida language
- *Taita Cushitic* — Two now-extinct South Cushitic languages whose words were borrowed into Taita
- *Abasi* — A member of the Wabasi secret cult
- *Chaga* — A related Bantu language group of Kenya and Tanzania
