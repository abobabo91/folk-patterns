## Overview

The Bakota, also called Kota, are a Bantu ethnic group from northeastern Gabon and Congo. They speak iKota, also known as Bakota, ikuta, or Kota; among the Fang they are called Mekora. The language includes the dialects Ndambomo, Mahongwe, Ikota-la-hua, Sake, Menzambi, and Bougom, with regional variations. Estimates indicate at least 43,500 Kota speakers worldwide: 34,442 in the Ogouee-Ivindo province of northeastern Gabon and 9,055 in neighboring Congo-Brazzaville. Kota people form the majority of the population of Makokou. Their documented folk-culture significance includes patriarchal and, among some Mahongwe, matrilineal social organization; circumcision and widow-purification rituals; and reliquary guardian figures associated with Bwete.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

**Mbulu ngulu** are copper and brass reliquary guardian figures associated with the Kota. In the iKota language they are called *mbulu ngulu*. The sources state that it is unclear whether the figures were made by the Kota themselves or by other people living in the same area of Gabon. They formed part of Bwete, described as a powerful religious and mystical order. During the conversion of people in Gabon to Christianity in the 18th and 19th centuries, missionaries and colonial officials collected the figures; most are now held in museums in Europe and North America.

## Festivals & rituals

The Kota are described as having distinctive circumcision and widow-purification rituals, which are generally kept secret. The sources identify these practices as important features of Kota culture.

Kota society is traditionally patriarchal, although sub-groups such as the Mahongwe have adopted a matrilineal system of lineage. The sources explain that *Mahongwe* means “from your father.” Kota children are taught to value tradition, respect for the elderly, and *Ewele*, glossed as “pride.” No annual festivals, seasonal celebrations, wedding rites, birth rites, or funeral ceremonies are described.

## Language & religion

Kota is a Bantu language spoken by the Bakota of Gabon. It is also referred to as iKota, Bakota, ikuta, and Kota; among the Fang, the people are known as Mekora. The documented dialects are Ndambomo, Mahongwe, Ikota-la-hua, Sake, Menzambi, and Bougom, with regional variations.

Most Kota people are Roman Catholic. The area of Gabon where they live is home to the Apostolic Vicariate of Makokou. The Kota reliquary guardian figures called *mbulu ngulu* are associated with Bwete, described as a powerful religious and mystical order. The sources also connect circumcision and widow-purification rituals with Kota cultural life, while noting that these rituals are generally kept secret.
