## Overview

The sources describe the Lango people, an ethnic group in north-central Uganda, rather than a group specifically called Langi. The Lango region includes Amolatar, Alebtong, Apac, Dokolo, Kole, Lira, Oyam, Otuke, and Kwania. The Lango speak Lango, an Eastern Sudanic language classified as related to the Luo languages, although several authors have rejected ethnic ties between the Lango and Luo peoples. The sources also describe a separate Rangi or Langi language, whose native name is *Kɨlaangi*, spoken by the Rangi people of Kondoa District in the Dodoma Region of Central Tanzania. The available material is therefore insufficient to establish a distinct Ugandan ethnic group named Langi.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

An account cited in the Lango article states that Lango women wore little clothing or embellishment beyond west-bands, necklaces, armlets, and anklets. The same account describes elaborate and highly fantastic head-dress among the Lango, with a prevailing fashion resembling a kind of helmet.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention necklaces, armlets, anklets, and west-bands in an account of Lango women’s adornment. They also describe elaborate head-dress, characterized as a kind of helmet. No materials, manufacturing techniques, ritual functions, tattooing, henna, or hair practices are documented.

## Music & performance

They state that the Lango have traditional dances, but provide no corresponding account of instrumental music.

## Dance & theatre

The Lango people are described as having several traditional dances, including **Okeme** and **Abuda**. These dances are popular and are performed during cultural functions, weddings, and other ceremonies.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that Okeme and Abuda are performed during cultural functions, weddings, and other ceremonies. The sources also describe traditional religious ideas in which each individual has a guardian spirit and a metaphysical soul, while ancestral cults and belief in the supreme god Jok played a large role in Lango religion. On 27 to 29 November 2024, the Lango participated in a reunion with other Ateker peoples hosted by the government of Uganda.

## Foodways

They state only that hunting, farming, and raising livestock are primary occupations of the Lango people.

## Oral tradition & literature

Lango oral tradition states that the Lango were part of the “Lango race” during a migration period and that this group later split into several distinct groups before entering Uganda. The name “Lango” is reported in Teso, Kumam, Karamojong, Jie, and Labwor vocabularies. Lango oral literature recounts that soldiers retreating toward the Nile helped Kabalega and Mwanga, the deposed King of Bunyoro and Buganda respectively, cross the Nile River. They were moved along the northern corridor of Lake Kwania, and Obol Ario is believed to have helped smuggle them toward Dokolo, where they settled at Kangai.

## Language & religion

The Lango speak Lango, an Eastern Sudanic language. Although it is classified as related to the Luo languages, several authors have rejected ethnic ties between the Lango and Luo peoples. They distinguish this language from Rangi or Langi, a Bantu language spoken by the Rangi people of Kondoa District in the Dodoma Region of Central Tanzania; that language has the native name *Kɨlaangi*. Many Lango practice Islam or Christianity, while traditional beliefs continue to influence religious life. Traditional Lango myth includes a guardian spirit and metaphysical soul for each individual, and ancestral cults and belief in the supreme god Jok are also described.
