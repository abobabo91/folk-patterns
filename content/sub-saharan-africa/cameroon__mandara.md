## Overview

The Mandara people, also known as Wandala or Mandwara, are a traditionalist ethnic group living in north Cameroon, northeastern Nigeria, and southeastern Chad. Their historic region lies in the Mandara Mountains and the valleys north of the Benue River, between the Benue River and Mora, Cameroon. Mora has been the capital of their sultans. The Mandara speak Wandala, a language belonging to the Chadic branch of the Afro-Asiatic language family. Their origins are unclear, and accounts of their history differ between oral traditions and Islamic historians. The group has long been connected with the Mandara Sultanate. Their folk-culture profile includes dispersed villages with mosques, sorghum cultivation, ironworking, horse raising, Muslim dress, leather amulets, and a socially stratified system of occupational castes.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The Mandara typically wear Muslim dress associated with northern Africa. They do, however, record leather amulets worn around the neck. These amulets contain verses from the Quran and are part of the group’s documented bodily and religious adornment. No further distinction between men’s and women’s clothing is given.

### Architecture

Mandara villages are described as dispersed settlements, with each village containing a mosque. Mora is identified as the capital of the Mandara sultans.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ironworking is a documented Mandara skill. People produced iron tools that were sought by traders and other ethnic groups, and iron workers and smiths formed distinct occupational castes within Mandara society.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources document leather amulets worn around the neck. These contain verses from the Quran and are associated with the Mandara people’s Muslim dress and religious life. No other jewelry types, metals, tattoos, henna practices, hair practices, or named adornment forms are described.

## Festivals & rituals

The Mandara converted to Sunni Islam under Sultan Bukar Aaji in the 1720s according to one oral tradition recorded in the source. Another account states that an unnamed ruler was converted by two Moroccans from Fez who were returning from Mecca; this account associates the introduction of circumcision, prayer, zakat, and fasting among the Mandara with the early 18th century. Islamic historians also give an account in which the Mandara converted to Islam in the 16th century. The sources therefore preserve differing accounts of the timing and circumstances of Islamization.

## Foodways

The Mandara grow sorghum as their principal crop. Their historic lands are described as a densely populated river valley surrounded by volcanic mountains rich in iron ore.

## Oral tradition & literature

The origins of the Mandara people are described through differing oral traditions. One tradition traces their beginning to King Agamakiya in the 13th century, who led the people as invasions came from the Sahel. Another states that Wandala Mbra was one of the sons of Mbra of Turu and Katala, the daughter of Vaya, and that his lineage formed the patrilineal Muslim ethnic group. The source says that these oral traditions may have been reconstructed later, when Mandara came under the influence of and cooperated in Fulani jihads and slave raids.

## Language & religion

The Mandara speak Wandala, which belongs to the Chadic branch of the Afro-Asiatic language family. The group converted to Sunni Islam according to the historical and oral accounts summarized in the source. Islamic practices associated with the Mandara include circumcision, prayer, zakat, and fasting. They wear leather amulets containing verses from the Quran. The Mandara language source supplied with the materials concerns a different language also called Mandara, or Tabar: it is an Austronesian language spoken on the Tabar Group of islands in New Ireland Province, Papua New Guinea, with the dialects Simberi, Tatau, and Tabar.
