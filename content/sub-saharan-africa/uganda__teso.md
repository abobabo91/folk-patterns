## Overview

The Iteso are a Nilotic people who live mainly in eastern Uganda and western Kenya. They traditionally kept cattle but became prolific farmers within a century, adopting the ox plough in the early 1900s. Their material culture is dominated by iron, horn, hide and gourd. They maintain rich oral traditions centered on the hero Oduk and practice ceremonies tied to cattle, life stages and the dead.

## Material culture

Iron dominates Iteso material culture, along with horn, hide, gourd and ivory.

### Textile & pattern traditions

No weaving, dyeing or embroidery tradition is recorded.

- **Incised gourd vessel** — A subspherical gourd whose skin is carved with human and animal figures.

### Clothing & dress

Everyday and ceremonial clothing is not documented in sources.

### Architecture

House forms and building traditions are not covered in sources.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Iron work dominates, with domestic objects of wood and gourd.

- **Finger-knife** — A small iron blade on a loop handle that fits over the finger, carried as a personal tool.
- **Hide shield** — A rectangular shield decorated at the top edge with an applied feather pom-pom.
- **Wooden stool** — A three-legged stool with two legs joined to the third by a horizontal bar beneath a rectangular seat.
- **Incised gourd vessel** — A vessel whose surface is carved with human and animal figures.
- **Ox plough** — An imported tool rapidly adopted by the Iteso in the early 1900s that reshaped their economy.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Horn, ivory and iron ornaments for the head and arms are the main forms.

- **Head-dress** — An ornament built on an iron frame carrying two horns, tipped with leather and sometimes set with glass beads.
- **Frontlet** — An iron ornament worn across the forehead.
- **Arm ornament** — A boat-shaped ivory piece with a pronounced ridge running round its lower and side edges.
- **Armlet** — A plain ivory bracelet worn on the arm.

## Music & performance

Song is the main performance form, often educative in purpose.

- **Ataikatiaka Kitiso** — A traditional folksong that treats the unity of the Teso people.
- **Akidai Imojong** — A traditional folksong that enjoins respect and care for elders.
- **Iyalama Imojong** — A traditional folksong that offers appreciation to elders.
- **Horn whistle** — A wind instrument made from horn, sometimes hung with fur tassels or sheathed in snakeskin with brass mounts.

## Dance & theatre

Dancing accompanied educative folksongs performed on different occasions.

## Festivals & rituals

Cattle and drink mark social occasions; life stages and death involve elaborate ritual.

- **Kakapel mountain cultural event** — A cultural gathering held on 26 December each year at Kakapel mountain where cultural practices are performed.
- **Ajon** — A fermented millet brew placed in a newborn's mouth after the mother's three-day confinement, and drunk at ceremonies and social gatherings.
- **Body exhumation and cooling** — The practice of exhuming bodies from the bush years after death to cool them and make spirits amenable to the living, credited to the legendary Oduk.

## Foodways

Finger millet and sorghum are the staples, supplemented by grains, vegetables, meat and milk.

- **Akima** — Finger millet, one of the two main staple grains.
- **Imomwa** — Sorghum, one of the two main staple grains.
- **Ajon** — A fermented brew made from dried finger millet, usually of the emiroiti variety, drunk at ceremonies and social gatherings.
- **Cotton** — The main cash crop, grown by men and women in separate plots during the short rains.
- **Tobacco** — A secondary cash crop grown when cotton sales failed in the 1980s.

## Oral tradition & literature

Oral tradition centers on the hero Oduk and his wife Among'in, who led migration around 1500 AD.

- **Oduk** — The legendary hero credited with organizing the Iteso militarily and originating the practice of body exhumation.
- **Among'in** — The wife of Oduk, credited with leading the people in their migration to Busia County.
- **Ekitabo Loka Ailip Naka Ituŋa Kere** — The 1957 Ateso prayer book, one of the first published Ateso texts after the 1947 orthography agreement, now digitised.

## Language & religion

Ateso is Eastern Nilotic with 22 letters; traditional religion centered on Akuj and Edeke, now largely Christian.

- **Akuj** — An omnipotent god in traditional Iteso religion, the name carried into Christian usage in the Ateso Bible.
- **Edeke** — A god of calamity in traditional Iteso religion, the name also carried into Christian usage.
- **Ateso orthography** — A 22-letter alphabet agreed in 1947 that omits f, h, q, v, x and z except in loanwords and adds ŋ and the digraph ny.
- **Enoch Olinga** — An Iteso Knight of Baháʼu'lláh and the youngest person appointed a Hand of the Cause.
