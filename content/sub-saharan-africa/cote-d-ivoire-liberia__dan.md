## Overview

The Dan are a Mande people of northwestern Ivory Coast and Liberia, with their largest settlement in Man. They moved from the northern savanna into the high forest region. They are renowned for their wood sculpture, especially masks with distinct forms and purposes, as well as brass casting and elaborate masquerades performed by men's societies.

## Material culture

Dan material culture centers on carved wood and cast brass, with patterns and adornment rendered through carving, casting, and attached natural materials rather than woven cloth.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Dan pattern-bearing material is carved and cast rather than woven, using incised lines on wood and natural fibers as trim and attachments.

- **Spoon handle incisions** — Handles bear repeated incised lines and geometric patterns.
- **Stool top carving** — Four-legged stools have geometric patterns carved into the top surface.
- **Mask facial markings** — Scarification rendered as two horizontal lines under each eye on carved heads; crescent of triangular peaks forms the brow of some masks.

### Clothing & dress

The masked figure embodies a dressed persona controlled by men's societies to represent powerful spirit forces.

- **Spirit mask costume** (*ge or gle*) — Masks are perceived to embody the most powerful spirit forces and are completed with attachments like fringe, leather or cotton beards, and bast fringe.
- **Basketry head-dress** — A surviving example is decorated with black feathers and blue cotton fringe along the rim.

### Architecture

Traditional Dan dwellings were small single-room mud and thatch huts; today houses are larger and rectangular with multiple rooms.

- **Traditional hut** — Single-room structures of mud and thatch, with each wife having her own hut for her children.
- **Village quarter** — A Dan village is divided into quarters, each housing an extended family or lineage and headed by a quarter chief.
- **Clan settlement** — Towns grouped together under central government; communities are allied through the Leopard society for peacemaking.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Wood carving is the principal Dan medium, particularly masks and ceremonial ladles, often inlaid with cast metal.

- **Ceremonial ladle** (*wunkirmian*) — Carved to honor a hospitable woman, with handle typically carved as a human head with narrow eyes and oval face of the buangle mask.
- **Buangle mask** (*buangle*) — A mask form portraying a particular woman, whose features appear on ceremonial ladle handles.
- **Carved wooden spoon** — Spoon heads often have six metal teeth inserted in the mouth and incised patterns on handles.
- **Carved stool** — Four-legged household furniture with carved geometric tops.
- **Brass casting** (*cire-perdue*) — Lost-wax method used by blacksmiths working at night to keep the object from onlookers.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Brass casting was central to Dan adornment, producing bracelets, rings, and anklets, with figurative sculpture emerging after a 1930s metal availability shift.

- **Brass bracelets** — Worn as personal adornment, cast by the lost-wax method.
- **Brass anklet with crotals** — An anklet strung with nine metal rattles for sound during performance.
- **Cast brass figure** — Prestige items about eight inches tall, such as a boy holding a long-beaked bird with a ring and bundle on his head.
- **Scarification** — Lines recorded below the eyes on carved heads; three rings around the neck shown in portraiture.
- **Pierced ears** — Body adornment shown on carved heads.

## Music & performance

Women's performance is documented at the Feast of Merit, where honored women dance while brandishing rice-filled ladles and singing in strident voices.

- **Feast of Merit singing** — Highly respected women sing in strident voices while dancing with their ceremonial ladles during the feast.
- **Ankle bells** — A pair of metal ankle bells from the early 19th century suggests sound in performance.

## Dance & theatre

Dan performance centers on masquerade through which men's fraternal societies demonstrate power, settle disputes, and enforce rules.

- **Spirit masquerade** — Men's societies call upon tutelary spirits from the bush who appear in public as masked figures to settle disputes and correct behavior.
- **Janus-faced mask** — A pair of heads on a staff with faces looking in opposite directions to signify the spirit's ability to see in all directions.
- **Performance mask** — Masks built for performance, some with movable jaws and boss-like eyes, others with steel teeth and horns across eyebrows.
- **Women's feast dancing** — Honored women dance holding their ladles; a woman's standing may be shown by her being carried through the village in a hammock by quarter women.

## Festivals & rituals

The Feast of Merit honors hospitable women; male initiation in bush school and the Leopard society form the main ritual complexes.

- **Feast of Merit** — Women recognised for hospitality prepare food for many guests; the honoured woman marches at the head and distributes food or uses her ladle to direct distribution.
- **Peanut and candy competition** — At some feasts honoured women compete in generosity by giving out peanuts, candy, coins and other foods, with other quarter women contributing gifts in her name.
- **Bush school initiation** (*bon*) — All Dan males attend as adolescents to meet the mysteries of the spirit world and learn the rules of adult Dan men.
- **Women's initiation society** — A comparable initiation body for women parallel to the men's bush school.
- **Leopard society** (*Gor*) — A peacemaking body at community level whose spirit reconciles communities that have been in conflict; communities are allied through it.

## Foodways

The Dan are farmers of forest clearings, with staple crops of rice, cassava and maize; men hunt and fish while women tend household plots and prepare meals.

- **Rice and soup** — Staple foods carried in pots at feasts; rice fills the ceremonial ladles women brandish as they dance.
- **Cash and staple crops** — Rice, cassava, sweet potatoes and maize are staples; cocoa, coffee and rubber are grown today.
- **Women's vegetable plots** — Women are given small plots for household vegetables or market sale, and gather greens from garden and wild forest plants.
- **Palm oil** — Extracted from many wild oil palms and used for cooking and fuel.
- **Ritual livestock** — White animals are eaten only on special ritual occasions involving much feasting, or sacrificed for the forgiveness of sin.

## Oral tradition & literature

Dan history is preserved through remembered accounts of named warrior chiefs and their achievements.

- **Grougbay Zobaneeay** — A warrior chief who drove other groups from what is now Nimba County as far as Loguatuo in Ivory Coast.
- **Kipko Toh'ah-Gbeu** — A chief who pushed the Kru from Tapitah and whose last major engagement was at Sanniquellie; gave his daughter in marriage to Grougbay Zobaneeay.
- **Bho'Yaah** — Among the last chiefs to resist the Americo-Liberian advance into Nimba before making a truce.
- **Dream communication** — Dreaming is the means by which people communicate with Zu, the Holy Spirit.

## Language & religion

Dan is a tonal Southern Mande language with nine to eleven tones; religion centers on Zlan the Supreme God and Zu the Holy Spirit, with belief in reincarnation.

- **Dan language** — Spoken by around 800,000 in Ivory Coast, 150,000–200,000 in Liberia and about 800 in Guinea; tonal with four to five level tones.
- **Dan dialects** — Gio (Liberian Dan), Gweetaawu (Eastern Dan), Blowo (Western Dan), and Kla; alternative names include Yacouba and Yakubasa.
- **Zlan** — The Supreme God who created the universe; no one can reach or see Zlan.
- **Zu** — An independent power regarded as Zlan's Holy Spirit, present throughout the universe and appealed to for many kinds of help.
- **Reincarnation belief** — Most Dan believe that Zu can pass a person into another person or an animal after death.

## Glossary

- *ge or gle* — Powerful spirit forces embodied in masks
- *wunkirmian* — Ceremonial ladle carved to honor a hospitable woman
- *buangle* — A mask form with narrow eyes and oval face, portraying a particular woman
- *cire-perdue* — Lost-wax method of brass casting
- *bon* — Bush school where adolescent Dan males learn spirit mysteries and adult rules
- *Gor* — Leopard society; a peacemaking body for reconciling communities in conflict
- *Zlan* — The Supreme God who created the universe
- *Zu* — The Holy Spirit; an independent power present throughout the universe
- *Gio* — Name for Dan in Liberia; also a Bassa word for slave
- *Mano-Dan* — Alternative name for the Dan people
- *quarter chief* — Leader of a village quarter, chosen for age or forceful personality
- *clan* — Towns grouped together under central government
- *Feast of Merit* — Ritual feast honoring women recognized for generosity and hospitality
