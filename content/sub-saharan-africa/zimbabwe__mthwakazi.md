## Overview

Mthwakazi is the traditional name of the Northern Ndebele people and their kingdom in what is now Matabeleland, Zimbabwe. The kingdom was located between the Sanyati and Limpopo rivers, and Mthwakazi is also used for inhabitants of Matebeleland Province. The people are also known as the Ndebele or *amaNdebele*; “Matabele” is an anglicised term associated with British usage. The territory became a center of diverse cultures, with local groups retaining autonomy while intermarriage and fluid boundaries connected them. Later Nguni migration, including Mzilikazi’s arrival around the 1830s, contributed to the formation of a centralized Ndebele kingdom. The sources describe Mthwakazi as a multi-ethnic society shaped by conquest, incorporation, poetry, song, military organization, and political change.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention that Shona groups built stone structures such as *khami* and the great Zimbabwe ruins.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention arms, gold, diamonds, cattle, and properties in historical accounts of conquest.

## Music & performance

The sources describe poetry and song. *Imbongi*—poets—poetically described the social structure of Mthwakazi, including the phrase *uMbuthwa okazi*, glossed in the source as “the great collective union.” The source records a saying attributed to Mzilikazi: “Saze sabasihle isizwe sakoMthwakazi, uMbuthwa okazi!, undlela zimhlophe!, njenginsimu yamaluba,” loosely translated as an expression of admiration for a beautiful, great, and diverse union like a garden of flowers.

The sources also record a children’s song: “Kudala kwakunganje, umhlaba uyaphenduka, kwakubusa uMambo no Mzilikazi.” It is loosely translated as “A long time ago, it wasn’t like this, Mambo and Mzilikazi ruled together.” No instruments, musical scales, dances accompanying these songs, or specialized performance occasions are identified. No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription is supplied for Zimbabwe.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that Mthwakazi had a long history of diverse cultures and arts and that *imbongi* described its social structure.

The historical material does record political and military events associated with Mthwakazi. Mthwakazi was invaded on 3 November 1893, and the kingdom’s later history is described in relation to conquest, occupation, and colonial rule. These are historical events rather than festivals or ritual observances.

## Foodways

The historical account mentions cattle and provisions of forced labour and mineral wealth, but does not present these materials as a description of Mthwakazi foodways.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources describe an oral and poetic tradition through *imbongi*, identified as poets who described the social structure of Mthwakazi. The phrase *uMbuthwa okazi* is presented as a poetic reference to the “great collective union.” The source also preserves a saying attributed to Mzilikazi praising the diversity and beauty of the Mthwakazi union, together with a children’s song recalling a time when Mambo and Mzilikazi ruled together.

## Language & religion

The sources identify the Ndebele language and the names *Ndebele* and *amaNdebele*, but do not classify the language by family or describe dialects, historical scripts, or writing systems. The term “Matabele” is described as an anglicised form used by the British, while Sotho-Tswana communities used *ma* for certain unfamiliar people and the Ndebele used the Nguni prefix *ama*.

The sources describe the *abaThwa* as San people and mention the arrival of Bantu people and later Nguni peoples.
