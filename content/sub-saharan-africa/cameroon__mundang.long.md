## Overview

The Mundang are an ethnic group in West Africa living in parts of Cameroon, Chad, and Nigeria. They speak Mundang, an Mbum language. In Cameroon, Mundang is spoken mainly in Mayo-Kani department, Far North Region, including Mindif, Moulvouday, and Kaélé, and by 44,700 speakers according to SIL 1982. Mundang is also spoken in southern Mayo-Kebi in Chad, in the east of Bibemi commune in Bénoué department, and toward the Chadian border. Mundang communities are documented as agricultural people whose material and intangible culture includes farming, cotton production, cattle and goats, pottery, weaving equipment, vernacular architecture, and music accompanying dance.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources used do not document a named Mundang textile or pattern tradition.

**Weaving equipment** — Museum catalogue records describe wooden and cane parts of looms, including a heddle pulley made of wood and leather, a shuttle, a weaving reed, a reed beater, and a wooden weaving implement. One canoe-shaped shuttle contains a piece of cane and a wooden cylinder wrapped with white cotton spun thread.

**Cotton thread** — The catalogue records cotton thread in connection with loom equipment, including white cotton spun thread and cotton thread wrapped around a wooden stick.

**Indigo-dyed cotton cloth** — An arm-dagger sheath is recorded as made of split cane bound with indigo-dyed cotton cloth and carrying a strap tied at the lower end with cotton thread.

**Motif vocabulary.** No named textile motifs are given in the sources.

### Architecture

Mundang people in Léré built mud houses with straight roofs and polished interior walls. They also constructed circular corn silos or granaries that were accessed through the roof. The sources do not provide a vernacular name for these buildings, further architectural decoration, or information about courtyards, workshops, or urban building traditions.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The museum catalogue records pottery objects including a lamp with a round base tapering in, then out, then in, and flaring out for the rim; a round vase with a nearly vertical neck and a slightly flaring rim; a vase with a shoulder decorated with bands of alternating diagonal lines; and another vase made of pottery. Recorded iron objects include two knife blades, a sword, a comb cum razor, a razor blade, a double fish-hook, and decorative bands of iron wire on a club. Other objects include wooden throwing-clubs, ceremonial clubs, a fish trap made of fibre tied to a wooden frame, and a gourd held at the base of the net.

## Music & performance

The Mundang people of Chad use the *billim* drum, described as a double-headed cylindrical drum with laced membranes. The drum accompanies dance rhythmically alongside an end-blown trumpet and a gourd-vessel wrattle. The drummer places the instrument on the ground and strikes each side with a hand.

The sources also describe families of musicians called griots by western writers. Sona Jobarteh is identified as a modern performing act outside of Africa who acknowledges her Mundang heritage and sees its influence in the bluesy sound of her music. The sources do not name specific song genres, repertories, performance ceremonies, or musical forms beyond the accompaniment of dance.

## Dance & theatre

The sources state that the *billim* drum is used to accompany dance rhythmically. They do not name a Mundang dance, theatrical tradition, mask performance, dramatic form, or distinction between ceremonial and entertainment dance.

## Foodways

Historically, the Mundang were an agricultural people. At the beginning of the 20th century they grew and harvested peas, beans, potatoes, nuts, and durra. They also produced cotton and raised cattle and goats. Mundang communities brewed beer from millet. The sources do not provide named dishes, recipes, cooking methods, ceremonial foods, tea or coffee traditions, or dietary rules.

## Language & religion

Mundang is an Mbum language of southern Chad and northern Cameroon, spoken by the Mundang people. The Gelama dialect of Cameroon may be a separate language. In Cameroon, Mundang is mainly spoken in Mayo-Kani department, Far North Region, in Mindif, Moulvouday, and Kaélé, and is also spoken to a lesser extent in the south of Mayo-Kebi, the east of Bibemi commune in Bénoué department, and toward the Chadian border. Mundang of Lere in Chad and Mundang of Cameroon, centered in Lara and Kaélé, are highly similar. Nasalization is marked by a tilde in the writing system. The sources used do not document Mundang religious traditions or spiritual practices.
