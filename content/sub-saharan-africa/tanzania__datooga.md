## Overview

The Datooga, also known as Datoog, Datoga, Taturu, Mang'ati, Tatoga, or Tatog, are a Nilotic ethnic group from Tanzania. They are located in Karatu District of Arusha Region and historically in areas of southwest Manyara Region and northern Singida Region. In 2000, their population was estimated at 87,978. The Datooga are closely related to the Kalenjin people in Kenya and Uganda. Their language belongs to the Southern Nilotic group and is spoken in the Great Rift Valley of Tanzania. The name Wamang'ati comes from Maasai and means “enemy”; it was adopted into Swahili, where Mang'ati became the standard name for the group.

## Foodways

The historical discussion states that Southern Nilotic-speaking communities kept domestic stock and possibly cultivated sorghum and finger millet between 1000 and 700 BC. The linguistic source also states that domestic animals are counted in pairs, so that the word used for “five” when counting other objects means “ten” when referring to goats or cattle. These references do not constitute a description of Datooga foodways.

## Language & religion

Datooga is a Nilotic language or dialect cluster of the Southern Nilotic group. It is spoken by the Datooga people of the Great Rift Valley of Tanzania. The language has substantial dialect diversity, and mutual intelligibility can be difficult. Blench lists East Datooga and West Datooga as distinct languages. The sources name Bajuta, Gisamjanga, Barabayiiga, Asimjeeg, Rootigaanga, Buraadiiga, Bianjiida, and Gidang'odiga among the Datooga varieties and groups. Gisamjanga and Barabaig speech are very close and can be considered a single dialect. The sources state that Datooga has been strongly influenced by South Cushitic languages and has in turn influenced Iraqw.

The language has almost no literacy in some accounts, while literacy in Swahili has been reported to be very low in some communities. Barabaiga and Gisamjanga have been written, and work is being done on Asimjeeg. Some varieties have verb-initial word order; numbers follow nouns, question words come at the end of a clause, and the numeral system is vigesimal.
