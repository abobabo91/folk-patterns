## Overview

The Sherbro are an indigenous coastal people of Sierra Leone who have absorbed cultural influences from neighbouring Mende and Krio communities. Before the colonial era they were dominant in their territory, but today form a small minority. They work chiefly as fishermen and traders, and their culture shows strong Western influence from centuries of intermarriage with Portuguese, English, and Krio peoples.

## Material culture

Sherbro material culture is dominated by textiles, beadwork, and wood-carving, with little recorded documentation of ceramics or metalwork.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Hand-spun cotton cloths with supplementary weft floats in indigo and geometric cross patterns are the signature textile tradition.

- **Display cloth** (*Njaye kpoikpoi*) — Eleven strips of hand-spun cotton in white and indigo, hand-woven and hand-sewn together, each strip carrying a different design that resolves into a single composition with a large cross dominating the field.

### Clothing & dress

Women wore beaded rectangles as aprons and belts, patterned with coloured triangles and worked on braided fibre rope.

- **Beaded apron** — A rectangle of strung glass beads on braided fibre rope, patterned with yellow, red, white and blue triangles.
- **Beaded belt** — A rectangle of strung glass beads on braided fibre rope, patterned with black, yellow, red and blue triangles outlined in white.

### Architecture

No documented information on Sherbro house forms, building materials, roofing or architectural decoration.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Wood-carving is the primary documented craft, producing household implements and standing figures with carved details.

- **Snuff pestle with mortar** — A wooden implement with a rounded stick and flat spoon end, tied to the mortar with twined string so they stay together as a single object.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment was almost entirely bead-based, using imported glass beads and black shell beads worked into flat patterned rectangles.

- **Shell bead necklace** — Necklaces of black shell beads worn around the neck, shown on carved figures.
- **Glass bead necklace** — Necklaces of red glass beads worn around the neck, shown on carved figures.

## Music & performance

Almost no musical practice is documented, except that John Akar, an entertainer of Sherbro origin, composed the music of Sierra Leone's national anthem.

## Dance & theatre

No documented information on Sherbro dance or dramatic traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

No festival calendar or life-cycle rites are documented, but chieftaincy families governed the region and several prominent clans trace descent from Afro-European intermarriage.

- **Sherbro Caulkers** — A chieftaincy family descended from Thomas Corker (died 1700) and Seniora Doll, continuing as the Shenge and Bonthe Caulkers.
- **Kpana Lewis** — A Sherbro chief (1830–1912) who opposed colonial rule.

## Foodways

No staples, dishes or cooking practices are documented, except that snuff-taking is attested indirectly through carved wooden implements.

## Oral tradition & literature

The first recorded publication in Sherbro was a selection of seven parables from Matthew and Luke translated in 1839, marking the beginning of Sherbro as a written language.

- **Sherbro New Testament parables** — Seven parables from Matthew and Luke translated by James Schön of the Church Missionary Society and published in 1839, the first recorded publication in the language.
- **Barnabas Root** — A clergyman and missionary born Fahma Yahny (died 1877) who worked among his own people.

## Language & religion

Sherbro is an endangered Mel language declining in use among its speakers in favour of Krio and English, and many Sherbro assimilate as Krio with the Christian faith.

- **Sherbro language** — Also known as Southern Bullom, Shiba, Amampa, Mampa and Mampwa, it is classed as endangered and appears in the UNESCO Red Book of Endangered Languages.
- **Joseph Christian Humper** — A notable Sherbro who was a bishop in the United Methodist Church.

## Glossary

- *Njaye kpoikpoi* — A display cloth of hand-spun cotton strips with supplementary weft floats forming a cross pattern
- *Mel language* — A branch of the Niger–Congo language family
- *Niger–Congo* — A major African language family
- *Southern Bullom* — An alternative name for the Sherbro language
- *Supplementary weft floats* — Decorative threads woven across cloth to create patterns
- *Braided fibre rope* — Twisted plant material used to finish beaded panels
- *Krio* — A people and culture of Sierra Leone with whom Sherbro share Christian faith and Western names
- *Mende* — A neighbouring people with whom southeastern Sherbro are closely allied
- *Bonthe District* — The southern coastal homeland of the Sherbro where they make up 40% of the population
- *Moyamba District* — A coastal district where Sherbro people also live
- *Western Area* — A region including Freetown where Sherbro live and are allied with Krio
- *Afro-European* — Resulting from intermarriage between African and European peoples
- *Chieftaincy* — The system of governance by chiefs, foundational to Sherbro regional administration
