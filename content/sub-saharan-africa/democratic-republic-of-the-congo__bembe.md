## Overview

The Bembe live west of Lake Tanganyika in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo and western Tanzania. They speak Kibembe and are related to other Bantu-speaking peoples. Their documented culture includes mountain farming, hunting, fishing, drums, dance, cassava foods, and many named objects. Oral traditions trace the Babembe to a common ancestor named Mbondo.

## Material culture

Bembe material culture includes named wooden, metal, ceramic, textile, and woven objects, but the sources give little detail about buildings, clothing, and textile patterns.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources name textile reliquary figures.

- **Textile reliquary figure** (*muzidi*) — A muzidi is generally seated, with a long narrow body and extended limbs ending in clearly shaped fingers and toes.

### Clothing & dress

The sources mention a straw hat and rings.

- **Straw hat** (*mpu yangara*) — The mpu yangara is a brimmed, Europeanized straw hat associated with Babembe.
- **Neck ring** — A neck ring was worn by both sexes.
- **Iron head or neck ring** — An iron head or neck ring is associated with Babembe-bambamba.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Bembe objects include knives, axes, pots, bowls, pipes, bells, baskets, and other tools made from wood, metal, clay, hide, and bark.

- **Knife** (*kwiti*) — Kwiti is a named Bembe knife form.
- **Knife** (*mbesi*) — Mbesi is another named Bembe knife form.
- **Axe** (*pukweto*) — Pukweto is a named Bembe axe form.
- **Clay pot** (*Bisa*) — Bisa is a named Bembe clay pot.
- **Wooden bell** (*madibu ma banganga*) — Madibu ma banganga are wooden bells with three clappers.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Bembe adornment includes iron neck rings and yellow-metal rings for the arms or feet.

- **Looped iron neck ring** — Some iron neck rings have loops containing pieces of yellow metal.
- **Opening iron ring** — Some iron rings can be opened with a simple hand movement.
- **Arm or foot ring** — Yellow-metal arm or foot rings were recorded, including one associated with Bakuta smiths and export to Basundi-babembe.

## Music & performance

Bembe folk music uses drums and is played in celebration, with a six-beat style and links to newer genres.

- **Drum music** — Bembe folk music traditionally uses drums, and the way the drum is played can be called a six-beat tune.
- **Large wooden bells** (*madibu ma banganga*) — Madibu ma banganga are large wooden bells with three clappers.
- **Women’s pipe** (*Kipi*) — Kipi is a named women’s pipe.
- **Wooden powder horn** (*Mpipi*) — Mpipi is a named wooden powder horn.

## Dance & theatre

The Bembe folk dance Esuba uses shoulder and waist movements and goes with drum music.

- **Folk dance** (*Esuba*) — Esuba uses movements of the shoulder and waist and accompanies folk music played on drums.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not name festivals or life-cycle rites; they only say that music and dance are performed in celebration.

- **Celebratory music and dance** — Bembe music and its accompanying dance are performed in celebration of any sort.

## Foodways

Babembe cuisine centers on cassava foods, fish, and vegetable stews.

- **Cassava dish** (*Kyenge*) — Kyenge is a cassava food compared in the source to fufu, and the Wabembe are especially fond of it.
- **Cassava dish** (*eswaka*) — Eswaka is a cassava food compared in the source to fufu.
- **Cassava-leaf stew** (*sombe*) — Sombe is made from pounded cassava leaves, preferably with ground nuts and palm oil.
- **Small fish** (*Dagaa*) — Dagaa is described as a very small fish.
- **Fish** (*Mkeke*) — Mkeke, also written Mkebuka, is a named fish eaten by the Babembe.

## Oral tradition & literature

Traditional griots preserve genealogies and stories about peace and the origins of the Babembe.

- **Common ancestor** (*Mbondo*) — Genealogical accounts trace the Babembe to a common ancestor named Mbondo, also written M'mbondo.
- **Peace tradition** (*Kalembelembe*) — The name Kalembelembe is linked to the declaration “Tunapenda Kalembe,” translated as “We love peace,” and signified “abundant peace.”

## Language & religion

Bembe is a Bantu language called Kibembe, while the profile mentions Christian missions and Arab-Swahili Muslim customs.

- **Bembe language** (*Kibembe*) — Kibembe is the Bantu language spoken by the Bembe in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and western Tanzania.
- **Language prefix** (*Ki*) — Regional languages were classified by adding the noun-class marker Ki to place-name roots, giving Kibembe for the language of the Bembe.
- **Christian missions** — Christian missions, catechism, adult baptisms, and campaigns against slavery are described at Kibanga and Mpala.
- **Arab-Swahili Muslim customs** — The historical material mentions Arab-Swahili Muslim customs among Wanguana.
