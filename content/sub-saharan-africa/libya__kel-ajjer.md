## Overview

The Kel Ajjer, also called Kel Azjar or Kel Azjer, are a Tuareg confederation inhabiting southwestern Libya and southeastern Algeria. Their main stronghold was Ghat, followed by Ubari, both in the Fezzan region of southwestern Libya, and Djanet south of the Tassili n'Ajjer in Algeria. They speak Tamahaq, or Northern Tuareg. They describe a society historically organized through ethnic groups and leaders, with camel breeding, trade, raiding, seasonal movement, settlement, warfare, and resistance to French colonization. Their documented history includes relations with the Chaambas, the Kel Ahaggar, the Ottoman Empire, and the Senusiyya.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

A French report from 1880 describes the Tuareg encountered among the Kel Ajjer as “tall, strong and lithe” and compares their appearance to medieval knights.

### Architecture

They mention settlements and oases, including Ghat, Ubari, Djanet, Tarat, and Temassinine, as well as ksur, a Bordj, forts, a Zawiya of the Senusiyya, and gardens near Ubari.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Historical accounts mention rifles, ammunition, a cannon captured from retreating Italians in 1914, camels, weapons, wells, and gardens.

## Music & performance

They state that the period of resistance between 1913 and 1920 led to numerous Tuareg poems and songs, and that the heroes of this period also appeared in French novels and films.

## Festivals & rituals

They do document the religious and political importance of the Senusiyya in southern Libya during the second half of the nineteenth century and at the beginning of the twentieth century. Ghat was one of the Senusiyya’s religious centers, and inhabitants of Ghat and some Kel Ajjer sympathized with the movement. Sheikh Amoud Ag El Mokhtar, the last chief of the Imenan ethnic group, became the Senusiyya’s representative in Djanet and opposed French colonization plans.

## Foodways

They state that camel breeding remains connected to the sale of camels as a source of meat. In the Wilaya of Illizi, the sources give a total of 321 camel owners or breeders and around 23,000 camels; in the Bordj Omar Driss area, they mention 47 breeders and around 3,000 camels. Most surveyed breeders had additional income as municipal employees, traders, or keepers of sheep and goats.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that the “heroic” period of Kel Ajjer resistance produced numerous Tuareg poems and songs. They also mention French novels and films in which the period’s heroes entered history. They identify Sheikh Amoud and Brahim ag Abakada as important leaders in the history of Kel Ajjer resistance.

## Language & religion

The Kel Ajjer speak Tamahaq, or Northern Tuareg. Islam is documented through the Senusiyya, which developed into an important religious and political force in southern Libya during the second half of the nineteenth century. Ghat was one of its religious centers. The inhabitants of Ghat and some Kel Ajjer sympathized with the movement, while Sheikh Amoud Ag El Mokhtar represented it in Djanet and opposed French colonization.
