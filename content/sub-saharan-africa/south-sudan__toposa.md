## Overview

The Toposa are a pastoral people living in the semi-arid Greater Kapoeta region, where they herd cattle, sheep, goats and camels. Their economy, social life and culture centre entirely on livestock. Knowledge and history are carried orally through songs, dance, poems and folklore rather than in writing, and most community decisions are made by councils of male elders. The Toposa were major ivory traders in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

## Material culture

Toposa material culture reflects a herding economy, made from wood, metal, horn, hide and ivory, with few textiles and no recorded weaving tradition.

### Textile & pattern traditions

No Toposa weaving, dyeing or named pattern traditions are recorded.

### Clothing & dress

Few garments are described; status comes from cattle and firearms rather than dress.

- **Cane and raffia palm hat** — A hat woven from cane and raffia palm.
- **Wrist-knife** — A metal blade with leather-wrapped cutting edges worn on the arm.
- **Ivory thumb-ring** — A man's ring worn during marriage ceremonies.

### Architecture

House forms are not described; the settlement pattern follows seasonal herding movements.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Household objects are made of wood, metal, horn and hide, built around herding and portable life.

- **Throwing spear** — The traditional Toposa weapon, used with a shield.
- **Stool or neck-rest** — Portable wooden furniture, often paired with a sheathed knife and tobacco container tied by hide thongs.
- **Knobkerrie** — A wooden club.
- **Drinking-horn** — A vessel made from skin.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ornaments are made from seed, fibre, cane, metal, brass and ivory, with ivory carrying ritual weight.

- **Seed and fibre neck ornament** — Neck ornaments woven from seed and plant fibre.
- **Brass nose-ring** — A brass ornament worn in the nose.
- **Ivory pendant or charm** — An ivory ornament combining metal and ivory, possibly with ritual use in marriage or as a charm.
- **Feather and metal ornament** — An ornament combining feather and metal.

## Music & performance

Music is one of the main carriers of culture, using metal, horn and skin instruments.

- **Metal and skin rattle** — A percussion instrument made by joining metal with skin.
- **Antelope horn trumpet** — A wind instrument made from animal horn, possibly antelope.
- **Cow-bell** — A bell of horn, cane and leather that hangs on herds and is part of the sound-world of cattle.

## Dance & theatre

Dance is listed as a carrier of culture but no individual dance or theatre tradition is named.

## Festivals & rituals

Ritual authority lies with elders; life-cycle transitions and conflict resolution are the main ceremonial occasions.

- **Coming of age** — Boys first tend goats and sheep, then graduate to herding cattle when they come of age, travelling far for water and pasture.
- **Marriage ceremony** — A man wears an ivory thumb-ring during the marriage rite.
- **Dry-season herding protocol** — The Toposa used to drive cattle to the Didinga Hills for water and pasture by agreement with the Didinga, with a gift in exchange.
- **Women's peace-making** — Women cross ethnic boundaries to initiate peace talks; a Didinga-Turkana-Toposa-Nyangatom women's workshop in February 2000 worked to discourage raiding.
- **Loyooro River ritual centre** — A sacred place where ritual authority over rain and drought is held.

## Foodways

Food comes from herds (cattle, sheep, goats for milk, blood and meat) and limited cultivation of sorghum in river valleys.

- **Sorghum** — The main crop, grown by women on fertile clay in river valleys, with yields depending on unpredictable rainfall.
- **Chewing tobacco** — A substance prepared in horn-and-hide containers, kept as part of a traveller's kit.

## Oral tradition & literature

Culture is entirely orally transmitted; written literacy was perhaps 5% of the population as of 2000.

- **Nakwuge sky narrative** (*Nakwuge*) — A cosmological story: men originally lived with Nakwuge in the sky and many slid down a rope to earth; the rope then broke.
- **Migration narrative** — The Toposa say they came from the Losolia Mountains in Uganda and moved during a severe drought, settling at Losilang before drifting north to Kapoeta by 1830.

## Language & religion

Toposa is a Nilotic language with vowel harmony and grammatical tone; belief centres on a supreme being and ancestral spirits.

- **Toposa language** (*Toposa (Akara, Kare, Kumi, Taposa, Topotha)*) — A Nilo-Saharan language mutually intelligible with Turkana and closely related to Karamojong, Nyangatom, Jiye, Jie and Dodos.
- **Vowel harmony** — Toposa has two sets of vowels (advanced tongue root and not) with vowel harmony; all nine vowels also occur devoiced.
- **Grammatical tone** — Tone in Toposa marks case in nouns and tense in verbs, not word meaning.
- **Roman Catholic Diocese of Torit** — The Catholic church has proselytized among the Toposa and helped resolve conflicts with neighbouring peoples.

## Glossary

- *Toposa* — Also called Akara, Kare, Kumi, Taposa, Topotha; a Nilotic people of South Sudan
- *Nilotic* — Relating to the Nile region; a branch of Nilo-Saharan languages
- *Eastern Sudanic* — A language family within Nilo-Saharan, containing Nilotic languages
- *Nilo-Saharan* — A language family spanning Africa from the Nile to the Sahel
- *Loyooro River* — A ritual centre held sacred by the Toposa
- *Kapoeta* — Main Toposa settlement in Eastern Equatoria state
- *wrist-knife* — A metal blade with leather-wrapped edges worn on the arm
- *knobkerrie* — A wooden club
- *Nakwuge* — A supreme being in Toposa cosmology who once lived in the sky with early humans
- *vowel harmony* — A linguistic pattern where vowels in a word must belong to the same set
- *advanced tongue root* — A vowel feature involving the forward position of the tongue base
- *Didinga* — A neighbouring people with whom Toposa made seasonal herding agreements
- *sorghum* — A grain crop grown by Toposa women in river valleys
- *Turkana* — A neighbouring Nilotic people whose language is mutually intelligible with Toposa
- *Nyangatom* — A Toposa section that moved east; also called Dongiro
- *Losolia Mountains* — Mountains in Uganda from which the Toposa say they originated
- *Karamojong* — A Nilotic people of Uganda, closely related linguistically to Toposa
