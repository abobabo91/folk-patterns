## Overview

The Mundang are an agricultural people in West Africa. They live mainly in northern Cameroon and nearby parts of Chad and Nigeria. Their material culture includes cotton work, pottery, iron tools, fishing gear, and vernacular buildings. Music includes the billim drum, an end-blown trumpet, and a gourd rattle used with dance.

## Material culture

Mundang material culture combines farming, cotton work, pottery, iron objects, wooden tools, fishing gear, and mud buildings.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The profile names cotton thread, loom equipment, and indigo-dyed cloth, but no named textile tradition or motif.

- **Loom parts** — Mundang looms used wooden and cane parts, including a heddle pulley, shuttle, reed, reed beater, and wooden implement.
- **Canoe-shaped shuttle** — One shuttle held cane and a wooden cylinder wrapped with white cotton thread.
- **Cotton thread** — Cotton thread was recorded as white spun thread and as thread wrapped around a wooden stick.
- **Indigo-dyed cotton cloth** — Indigo-dyed cotton cloth was used on an arm-dagger sheath made from split cane.

### Clothing & dress

The profile does not document Mundang everyday or ceremonial clothing.

### Architecture

Mundang builders made mud houses with straight roofs and circular granaries entered through the roof.

- **Mud houses** — Mundang people in Léré built mud houses with straight roofs and polished interior walls.
- **Circular granaries** — They built circular corn silos or granaries that were accessed through the roof.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The profile records pottery, iron tools and weapons, wooden clubs, and fishing equipment.

- **Pottery lamp** — A recorded lamp had a round base, a changing inward and outward shape, and a flared rim.
- **Decorated vase** — One vase had a shoulder decorated with bands of alternating diagonal lines.
- **Iron weapons and tools** — Recorded iron objects included knife blades, a sword, a comb-razor, a razor blade, and a double fish-hook.
- **Throwing-clubs** — The collection includes wooden throwing-clubs and ceremonial clubs.
- **Fish trap** — A fish trap was made of fibre tied to a wooden frame and included a gourd at the base of the net.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The profile does not document Mundang jewelry or body adornment.

## Music & performance

Mundang music uses the billim drum with an end-blown trumpet and a gourd rattle to accompany dance.

- **Double-headed drum** (*billim*) — The billim is a cylindrical drum with two laced membranes, played by hand on both sides while it rests on the ground.
- **End-blown trumpet** — An end-blown trumpet plays alongside the billim drum for dance rhythm.
- **Gourd rattle** — A gourd-vessel rattle accompanies the drum and trumpet.
- **Griot musicians** (*griot*) — Western writers call some Mundang families of musicians griots.
- **Modern Mundang influence** — Sona Jobarteh acknowledges her Mundang heritage and its influence on the bluesy sound of her music.

## Dance & theatre

The billim drum accompanies dance, but the profile names no dance or theatre tradition.

- **Dance accompaniment** — The billim drum is used rhythmically with dance.

## Festivals & rituals

The profile does not document Mundang festivals, ritual calendars, or life-cycle rites.

## Foodways

Mundang foodways grew from farming, livestock keeping, and millet beer brewing.

- **Peas and beans** — At the beginning of the 20th century, the Mundang grew and harvested peas and beans.
- **Potatoes and nuts** — They also grew and harvested potatoes and nuts.
- **Durra** — They grew and harvested durra.
- **Millet beer** — Mundang communities brewed beer from millet.
- **Cattle and goats** — They raised cattle and goats.

## Oral tradition & literature

The profile names musician families called griots but gives no account of their oral repertory.

- **Griot families** (*griot*) — Western writers identify some families of Mundang musicians as griots.

## Language & religion

Mundang is an Mbum language spoken across northern Cameroon and nearby parts of Chad, while religious traditions are not documented.

- **Mundang language** (*Mundang*) — Mundang is an Mbum language spoken by the Mundang people.
- **Mbum language group** (*Mbum*) — Mundang belongs to the Mbum language group.
- **Gelama dialect** (*Gelama*) — The Gelama dialect of Cameroon may be a separate language.
- **Léré Mundang** (*Léré*) — Mundang of Léré in Chad is highly similar to Mundang of Cameroon.
- **Cameroon Mundang** (*Kaélé*) — Mundang in Cameroon is centered in Lara and Kaélé and is highly similar to the variety in Léré.
