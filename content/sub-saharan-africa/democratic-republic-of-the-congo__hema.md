## Overview

The Hema people, also called *Bahema*, are a Bantu ethnic group concentrated in parts of Ituri Province in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. There are generally thought to be 160,000 people who consider themselves Hema, mostly concentrated in Ituri Province. Historically pastoralists, they migrated into Ituri from modern-day Uganda in the early 19th century and were among the last groups to settle in the region. The Hema are usually divided into Northern Hema, or Gegere, and Southern Hema, or Nyoro. Northern Hema are concentrated in Djugu Territory, while Southern Hema live mostly in Irumu Territory. Most Hema are Christians. Their history has also been shaped by relations with the Lendu and by the Ituri conflict.

## Language & religion

Hema is a Bantu language and one of three languages spoken by the Hema people of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It is sometimes called Southern Hema, in contrast to the unrelated language Lendu, which is also known as Northern Hema. Northern Hema, or Gegere, speak the Kilendu or Batha languages and are concentrated in Djugu Territory. Southern Hema, or Nyoro, speak Kihema or Kinyoro languages and live mostly in Irumu Territory. Most Hema are Christians.
