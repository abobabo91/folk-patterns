## Overview

The Otuho people, also known as the Lotuko, are a Nilotic ethnic group whose traditional home is Eastern Equatoria state of South Sudan. They speak the Otuho language, an Eastern Nilotic language. Their region includes ranges and mountain spurs such as Imatong mountain, described as the highest mountain in South Sudan, with an altitude of 10,453 feet above sea level. The Otuho are bordered by the Lopit to the north, the Bari to the west, the Acholi and Madi to the southwest, and the Didinga and Boya to the east. The supplied sources document agriculture, landholding, religion, mythology, and language.

## Festivals & rituals

The supplied sources describe an ethnic religion based on nature and ancestor worship and state that it is deeply rooted in Otuho ethnic identity. The chief god is called *Ajok*, who is generally regarded as kind and benevolent but can be angered. According to Otuho mythology, Ajok once answered a woman’s prayer for the resurrection of her son. Her husband re-killed the child, after which Ajok swore never to resurrect an Otuho again; death was consequently said to have become permanent.

## Foodways

The Otuho practise subsistence agriculture. Their main crops in the plains are sorghum, ground nuts, simsim (sesame), and maize. In the hills they grow telebun, dukhn, sweet potatoes, and tobacco.

## Oral tradition & literature

The supplied sources document an Otuho myth concerning Ajok, a woman’s prayer for the resurrection of her son, and the permanence of death. The Golden Bough, written by James Frazer, records an account concerning Latuka kings: when crops failed and attempts to bring rain proved fruitless, people were said to attack the king at night, rob him, drive him away, or kill him.

## Language & religion

Otuho, also known as Lotuko or Lotuxo, is the language of the Otuho people. It is an Eastern Nilotic language with several other Otuho-speaking dialect groups. Dongotono is related, and the sources also list Logir, Ifoto, and Imatong as other related varieties. The Otuho religious system is described as an ethnic religion based on nature and ancestor worship. Conversion to another religion is said to essentially equate to cultural assimilation.
