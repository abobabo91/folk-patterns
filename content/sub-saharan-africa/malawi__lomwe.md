## Overview

The Lomwe are a Bantu-speaking people living in Mozambique and Malawi, speaking Elomwe, a language also known as Western Makua. In Malawi, they speak a mutually unintelligible form called Malawi Lomwe. The Lomwe have been prominent in Malawian public life, including producing two presidents of the Republic of Malawi.

## Material culture

Lomwe material culture is documented through objects held by the British Museum, including textiles, iron tools, ceramic pipes, and ornaments of seeds and metal.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Lomwe weavers made cotton waist-bands and worked with barkcloth, using simple patterns of brown-dyed lines, checks, and black stripes.

- **Cotton waist-band** — A long woven band of cotton with brown-dyed warp lines along outer sides and hand-stitched check or striped borders finished with loose warp-thread fringes.
- **Barkcloth** — A sample of barkcloth is recorded in the collection, though details of how it was beaten, dyed or worn are not documented.

### Clothing & dress

Clothing is documented mainly through woven cotton waist-bands and barkcloth, worn with ornaments of iron, brass, seeds and palm seed-pods.

- **Cotton waist-band** — A long fringed strip of woven cotton, made in both check-bordered and striped versions for wearing around the waist.
- **Dance ornaments** — Rattles of seed pods strung on cord were worn as sounding ornaments by dancers, indicating dress assembled specifically for dancing.

### Architecture

No information on Lomwe house form, building materials, granaries or other elements of the built environment is available.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Iron tools dominate the record, with black earthenware pipes, and carved wooden objects including a powder flask and a decorated lime-box.

- **Iron axes** — Two axes each consist of an iron blade passed through a wooden handle.
- **Knives** — Knives appear with short wooden handles, bamboo handles and brass handles.
- **Lime-box** — A small box of hard dark wood shaped like a water-bottle, hollowed out and banded with a broad vertical zig-zag, held lime to be chewed with tobacco.
- **Powder flask** — A carved wooden powder flask shaped like a horn, decorated with carved designs and fitted with a long cord.
- **Smoking pipes** — Each pipe combines a bamboo stem with a bowl of black earthenware or pottery, perforated for the cord that tethers bowl to stem.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ornaments were made from plant material like seeds and palm seed-pods, and from worked metal—iron bangles, brass bracelets, and iron ankle-rings.

- **Seed necklaces** — Necklaces made from small dark brown seeds, or brown and black seeds long enough to form about ten coils around the neck.
- **Palm seed-pod necklaces** — Necklaces of brown palm seed-pods, some strung with pink beads and finished at the ends with two buck horns.
- **Iron bangle** — A strip of iron bent nearly into a circle, worn as a simple metal ornament.
- **Brass bangle** — A bracelet carrying a longitudinal ornament of paired hollowed semi-circles separated by a vertical raised bar.
- **Iron ankle-ring** — A thin iron bar beaten to a square section and bent almost closed, worn around the ankle.

## Music & performance

Lomwe musicians played pan-pipes and flutes of bamboo, decorated with black gum and inlaid red seeds, along with seed-pod rattles for dance.

- **Pan-pipes** — Sets of five or eight bamboo pipes ranging from 3 to 5¾ inches, lashed with fibre bands, coated with black gum, and inlaid with red seeds.
- **Flute** — A single complete joint of bamboo closed at both ends, pierced with three holes, decorated with diagonal zig-zag and herringbone patterns worked in the bamboo skin.
- **Dance rattles** — Seed pods strung on cord, worn as dance ornaments to supply percussion for dancing.

## Dance & theatre

The only evidence of dance is seed-pod rattles worn as dance ornaments, indicating dancers wore sounding ornaments, with no named dance or dramatic form recorded.

- **Seed-pod rattles** — Seed pods strung on cord and worn as sounding ornaments by dancers.

## Festivals & rituals

No annual festivals or life-cycle rites are documented in the available sources.

## Foodways

Smoking and tobacco-chewing with lime are the only consumption practices documented in detail.

- **Tobacco-chewing with lime** — Lime was chewed together with tobacco, held in a carved wooden lime-box hung with beads.
- **Smoking pipes** — Pipes combining bamboo stems with black earthenware or pottery bowls, decorated with lozenge, herringbone and incised line patterns.

## Oral tradition & literature

The documented literary activity is scriptural translation, with Lewis Mataka Bandawe and E. D. Bowman producing a New Testament translation into Lomwe.

- **New Testament translation** — Lewis Mataka Bandawe and E. D. Bowman translated the New Testament into Lomwe, with the four gospels and Acts published by 1928 through the Mihecani Mission.

## Language & religion

Elomwe is a Bantu language grouped with Makua, featuring distinctive phonetic differences and an unusual infinitive prefix of o- rather than ku-.

- **Elomwe language** (*Elomwe*) — Also called Lomwe or Lowe and known as Western Makua, a Bantu language of Mozambique where it uses ch where Makhuwa has tt.
- **Malawi Lomwe** — A mutually unintelligible form of Lomwe spoken in Malawi, carrying elements of the Mozambican language but standing apart from it.
- **Infinitive prefix** (*o-*) — Elomwe unusually takes an o- prefix for the infinitive rather than the typical ku- found in other Bantu languages.

## Glossary

- *Elomwe* — Language of the Lomwe people, also called Lomwe or Lowe, known as Western Makua
- *Makhuwa* — Related Bantu language of northern Mozambique, differing from Lomwe in phonetics
- *Malawi Lomwe* — Mutually unintelligible form of Lomwe spoken in Malawi
- *Mihecani Mission* — Mission led by Lewis Mataka Bandawe until 1928, where New Testament was translated into Lomwe
- *Waist-band* — Long woven strip of cotton worn around the waist, with check or stripe patterns
- *Barkcloth* — Non-woven cloth made from beaten bark
- *Pan-pipes* — Musical instrument of graded bamboo pipes lashed together, decorated with black gum and red seeds
- *Lime-box* — Small wooden container shaped like a water-bottle, holding lime for chewing with tobacco
- *Dance rattles* — Seed pods strung on cord, worn as sounding ornaments during dancing
- *Smoking pipes* — Bamboo stem with black earthenware or pottery bowl, decorated with incised patterns
- *Iron ankle-ring* — Thin iron bar beaten to square section and bent nearly closed, worn around the ankle
- *Brass bangle* — Metal bracelet with paired hollowed semi-circles separated by a vertical raised bar
- *Buck horns* — Horns used as finishing elements on seed-pod necklaces
- *Herringbone* — Incised pattern of zigzagging lines, used on pipes and flutes
