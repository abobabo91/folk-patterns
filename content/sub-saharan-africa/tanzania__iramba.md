## Overview

The Anilamba, also called Nyilamba, are a Bantu ethnic and linguistic group based in Kishapu District of Shinyanga Region and Iramba District of Singida Region in central Tanzania. In 1987, the Nilamba population was estimated at 400,000, with 50,000 other speakers of the Nilamba language. A 2013 estimate placed the total population at over 450,000. The available sources identify the group primarily through its ethnic and linguistic distribution.

## Language & religion

Iramba, also known as Nilamba, is a Bantu language spoken by the Nilamba and Iambi people of Iramba District in Singida Region of Tanzania. The 50,000 Iambi speak a slightly divergent dialect, sometimes listed as a distinct language. Isanzu is sometimes included as a dialect. The sources list forms of the language name with and without the prefixes *ni-* and *i-*, and with *iki-* or Swahili *ki-* as a noun-class prefix for “language.” Recorded variants include Nilamba, Niramba, Nilyamba, Nyilamba, Ikinilamba, Ikiniramba, Ilamba, Iramba, Kinilamba, Kiniramba, and Nilambari. There is no distinction between [ɾ] and [l].
