## Overview

The Tandroy are the fifth-largest ethnic group in Madagascar, living in the dry southern region whose name means 'place of the thorns'. They are traditionally pastoral, herding zebu cattle, and farm drought-resistant crops like maize and manioc. They stand out from other Malagasy peoples through their distinctive wooden architecture, sacred cotton textiles, and elaborate funeral rituals centered on zebu sacrifice.

## Material culture

Tandroy material culture reflects their arid homeland and pastoral economy, with wood, metal, raffia, and zebu byproducts forming the basis of houses, tools, containers, and ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Women hand-spin cotton and weave it into cloth, with the yarn holding spiritual power before it is finished.

- **Living yarn** (*fole velo*) — Hand-spun thread imbued with spiritual power before stiffening, used in circumcision ceremonies and tied around the wrist by healers for protection.
- **Lamba wrapper** (*lamba*) — Woven cotton wrapper, off-white with geometric designs in blue and red silk on the short ends and fringed.
- **Raw silk** (*landy*) — Raw silk used for clothing and as patterning yarn on cotton loincloths.

### Clothing & dress

Tandroy dress is minimal because of the hot climate, centered on woven cotton wrappers and loincloths with metal ornaments.

- **Woven cotton loincloth** — Off-white cotton with blue and red silk geometric designs on short ends and fringe.
- **Basketry hat** — Hat woven of basketry worn as part of daily dress.
- **Goat-hair hat-and-wig** — Hat combined with wig made of goat hair as hair adornment.
- **Hide sandal** — Sandal made of cattle hide, worn in pairs.

### Architecture

The Tandroy build distinctive square houses of wooden planks on stilts, with doors assigned by gender and family role.

- **Plank house** — Square house with vertically hung planks on a wooden frame, raised on low stilts, peaked roof, no windows, and three gendered doors—front for women, rear for children, third for men.
- **Tomb** (*fanesy*) — Large rectangular structure sized to the dead man's wealth, painted in colors, with stones or towers on either side and zebu skulls on the roof.
- **Yard fence** (*raketa*) — Fence of prickly-pear cactus or cut indigenous succulents from the spiny forest.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Tandroy are renowned ironsmiths, making weapons and tools, while containers and utensils are carved from wood or woven from raffia and gourd.

- **Wood-and-metal spear** — Spear combining wood and metal, a weapon and object of prestige.
- **Iron axe** — Axe with wooden handle and iron blade.
- **Gourd milk container** — Gourd used to store and serve zebu milk and curd, dietary staples.
- **Raffia basket** — Lidded basket woven of raffia for holding jewelry, medicinal plants, or money.
- **Carved wooden spoon** — Spoon carved with a figure.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Metal ornaments, especially silver, are the primary form of adornment, worn in multiples with goat-hair wigs and sacred protective thread.

- **Silver necklace** — Necklace of silver, a prestige metal and sign of wealth.
- **Metal bracelet** — Bracelets worn in multiples as adornment.
- **Metal hair-pin** — Pin used to dress and secure hair.
- **Sacred protective thread** (*fole velo*) — Living yarn tied around the wrist by healers for protection and ritual purposes.

## Music & performance

Stringed instruments dominate Tandroy music, accompanied by shrill whistles and fipple flutes in dances and ritual contexts.

- **Box zither** (*marovany*) — Box zither built from pine planks and strung with unwound bicycle cables.
- **Chordophone with frets** (*mandolina or gitara*) — Popular southern chordophone strung with nylon fishing line with five or seven movable frets to shift tuning.
- **Bowed fiddle** (*lokanga*) — Bowed instrument with carved gourd resonator making a three-stringed fiddle.
- **Trance state** (*bilo*) — State in which the living commune with and are possessed by ancestors.

## Dance & theatre

The Tandroy are known across Madagascar for distinctive spear dances accompanied by shrill whistles, with special performances at funerals.

- **Spear dance** — Distinctive dance performed with spears, accompanied by shrill whistles and fipple flutes.
- **Funeral coffin ceremony** — Celebratory performance at some funerals where men lift the coffin and run with it into the sea.

## Festivals & rituals

Tandroy ritual life centers on life-cycle rites and ancestor veneration, with circumcision and funerals as the major ceremonies.

- **Circumcision ceremony** — Central male rite in which the foreskin may be fired from a gun barrel and fole velo thread is used for protection.
- **Funeral feast and zebu sacrifice** — Communal gathering where zebu are killed and meat distributed to the community, repeated at coffining and tomb completion.
- **Tomb completion ceremony** — Two-day mourning ritual during fanesy tomb construction with ritual wailing and possible zebu sacrifice.
- **House burning** — The deceased's house is burned to close funeral rites, and the family does not visit the tomb again.
- **Prohibitions** (*fady*) — Rules set by elders and ancestors that govern daily life, including that women cannot milk zebu and the radiated tortoise cannot be killed.

## Foodways

The arid climate means the Tandroy eat less rice than other Malagasy, depending instead on drought-resistant crops and zebu products.

- **Staple grains and tubers** — Maize, sweet potato, manioc, yams, taro root, and millet, generally boiled and sometimes served in milk or with peanuts.
- **Zebu milk and curd** — Dairy products that are dietary staples and stored in gourd containers.
- **Honey** — Major Tandroy product from the region.
- **Ceremonial beef** — Zebu meat from sacrificed animals at funerals and other rituals, distributed to the community.

## Oral tradition & literature

Oral history splits by clan, with some groups claiming ancient settlement and others tracing refugee origins, and descent narratives define clan identity.

- **Karimbola clan tradition** — Western Tandroy clan with no migration story, implying they were always in place.
- **Mahandrovato tradition** — Eastern Tandroy original population gradually outnumbered by Antanosy and Bara refugees.
- **Zafindravoay descent** — Clan claiming descent from a union of a Tandroy woman and a crocodile.
- **Spirit being** (*kokolampo*) — Spirit understood to act for good or ill in a person's life.

## Language & religion

The Tandroy speak a Malagasy dialect related to Borneo languages and practice ancestor veneration combined with Lutheran Christianity.

- **Creator god and ancestor cult** — Religion combines belief in a creator god with veneration of ancestors who live on, intervene, and must be respected.
- **Trance communion** (*bilo*) — State in which the living commune with and are possessed by ancestors.
- **Soothsayer** (*mpisoro*) — Village figure with recognized authority in traditional communities.
- **Healer** (*ambiasa*) — Practitioner who works with consecrated fole velo thread for protection and healing.
- **Lutheranism** — Dominant Christian form among the Tandroy since late 19th century, coexisting with older ancestral beliefs.

## Glossary

- *fole velo* — Living yarn, hand-spun thread imbued with spiritual power before stiffening
- *lamba* — Woven cotton wrapper garment
- *landy* — Raw silk used for clothing and patterning
- *fanesy* — Tomb or eternal place of the dead
- *raketa* — Prickly-pear cactus used for yard fences
- *marovany* — Box zither stringed with bicycle cables
- *mandolina* — Chordophone with movable frets, strung with fishing line
- *gitara* — Another name for mandolina chordophone
- *lokanga* — Bowed three-stringed fiddle with carved gourd resonator
- *bilo* — Trance state in which the living commune with ancestors
- *fady* — Prohibition or taboo set by elders and ancestors
- *mpisoro* — Soothsayer with recognized authority in village society
- *ambiasa* — Healer who works with sacred thread and protective amulets
- *kokolampo* — Spirit that acts for good or ill in a person's life
- *zebu* — Humped cattle, central to Tandroy economy and ritual
- *Tandroy* — People of the thorns, from the spiny forest of southern Madagascar
- *Androy Region* — Arid southern region of Madagascar between Amboasary and Beloha
- *Malagasy* — Language of Madagascar, Malayo-Polynesian family from Borneo
