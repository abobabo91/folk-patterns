## Overview

Luba communities live across a broad part of south-central Congo. The historical Luba state grew in the southeastern savanna and influenced neighboring courts through alliance, trade, and conquest. Its leadership combined sacred kingship with councils and associations that held historical knowledge. “Luba” names both people and a far-reaching artistic tradition: a staff or figure made in a neighboring polity is not automatically the work of a Luba maker. This collection follows specific museum attributions and leaves out objects labeled only “Luba style,” attributed to multiple peoples, or marked uncertain by the museum.

## Material culture

The selected objects include carved seats, headrests, staffs, figures, divination equipment, metal ornaments, musical instruments, clothing, and a small number of textiles. Many belonged to chiefly or ritual settings; they should not stand in for every Luba household. Museum records document the forms more fully than the individual makers or original occasions of use.

### Textile & pattern traditions

- **Raffia and dress** — A woven raffia textile and recorded headwear show fiber work alongside the better-known carvings. The collection cannot establish a single textile pattern shared by all Luba communities.
- **Beads and metal** — Glass beads, copper, and other metals embellish some seats, staffs, and ornaments. Their placement is part of an object's design and, in court contexts, may also speak to status.
- **Lukasa** — A memory board may carry beads, shells, incisions, and carved forms. Trained members of the *mbudye* association interpret its relationships to history, political authority, and place; the marks are not a simple alphabet that a viewer can decode by sight.

### Clothing & adornment

The selected headdresses, cap, armlets, bracelet, and pendant show several forms of adornment. One cannot infer a universal rank or ritual meaning from the presence of beads or metal alone. Brooklyn Museum identifies a carved hippopotamus-tooth pendant as Luba and connects its female form with remembrance of honored ancestors.

### Woodwork & objects of authority

- **Lupona** — A carved royal seat could embody the legitimacy of a ruler. The Met explains that royal stools were kept as sacred insignia rather than used for sitting; some are supported by female figures.
- **Kibango** — A ceremonial staff of office could record a titleholder's lineage and authority over a territory. Its sculpted forms required knowledge and authority to interpret.
- **Musamo** — A carved headrest supported its owner's head while protecting an elaborate hairstyle. Some are also finely composed figure sculptures.
- **Bowls and figures** — The selected wooden bowls, cups, and carved figures include domestic and ceremonial forms. Their exact uses vary by object and should be read from each museum record.

### Metalwork & weapons

The collection includes an axe, sword, bow, arrow rest, bangles, and metal fittings. The Met describes carefully worked iron and copper as materials of Luba leadership as well as exchange. A ceremonial blade is evidence of skilled making and political symbolism, not a picture of everyday warfare.

## Music & performance

A *sansa* (thumb piano), drum, and bullroarer appear in the selected objects. A raffia dance skirt and masks in museum collections point to performance settings, but the records do not establish one repertoire or ceremony for every Luba community. The pictured *kifwebe* mask is identified as Luba in its museum photograph; related mask forms also occur among neighboring Songye makers, so form alone is insufficient for attribution.

## Memory, history & belief

The *mbudye* association sustained and interpreted political histories, and *lukasa* boards helped authorized readers recall them. The Met records a dynastic account in which Kalala Ilunga overthrows Nkongolo. Royal seats and staffs connect such histories with claims to office. Female figures appear repeatedly in Luba leadership art, where they can express the spiritual foundations of kingship; the meaning of any particular figure still depends on its record and setting. These court traditions are a documented part of Luba history, not a complete account of present-day religious life.

## Language & community

Luba is a broad name. Kiluba is associated especially with the southeastern Katanga region; Tshiluba with Kasaï. They should not be collapsed into one language or one uniform community. The objects here are mostly older museum holdings, while Luba people and their cultural practices continue today.

## Glossary

- *balopwe* — Sacred kingship in accounts of the historical Luba state.
- *kibango* — Ceremonial staff of office.
- *lukasa* — Memory board interpreted by trained keepers of historical knowledge.
- *lupona* — Royal seat or stool.
- *mbudye* — Association involved in preserving and interpreting Luba political history.
- *musamo* — Headrest.
- *sansa* — Thumb piano or lamellophone.
