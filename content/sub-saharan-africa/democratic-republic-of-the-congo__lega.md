## Overview

The Lega are a forest people of eastern Congo living in the Elila and Ulindi valleys. They are known above all for the Bwami association, a graded membership society that governs social and political life. The association teaches wisdom through proverbs carried by dance, poetry, song, and small carved masks and figures. Objects and ornament were not separate categories — sculptures gain their appearance from being handled or worn on the body.

## Material culture

Lega material culture centers on carved ivory and wood, woven raffia, and ornaments like cowrie shells, all tied closely to the Bwami association and its teaching.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Raffia cloth is the only named weaving tradition, made from palm fiber in sections with dyed and pompom-trimmed borders.

- **Raffia cloth** — A large skirt woven from raffia palm fiber in seven sections stitched together, with borders of hand-sewn raffia strips trimmed with red cotton, dyed purple with dark brown triangles, and finished with blue-purple strips and tiny pompoms.

### Clothing & dress

The sources name only a skin cap and raffia skirt, leaving most daily dress undocumented.

- **Skin cap** — Made of skin with hair left on, its edge finished with a decorative band of woven fiber and cowrie shells, fitted with a woven fiber strap also set with cowrie shells.

### Architecture

Villages are small without central authority, with cooking outdoors in good weather and indoors in the long houses during rain.

- **Long house** — A large house with an interior kitchen where women and children cook and eat during rain.
- **Men's house** — A separate space where men eat together as a group.
- **Outdoor hearth** — Built from slow-burning logs arranged to support a pot, used for cooking when weather allows.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ivory and bone carving dominate Lega objects, alongside iron swords and wooden hunting whistles.

- **Ivory spoon** — Cut from elephant ivory with a leaf-shaped bowl and openwork or raised-oval handle bearing circular motifs.
- **Spiked bone implement** — Carved from elephant bone and topped with two human heads.
- **Mugusu** — A sword or bill-hook with a shaped steel blade and wooden handle wrapped in reptile skin, held by an iron ring and leather thongs.
- **Hunting whistle** — Made of wood with three sound holes, bound in woven vegetable fiber coated with reddish pigment, with a hide carrying strap and cane or iron binding near the base.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Cowrie shells are the main ornament, and body marking is recorded on carved objects through scarification patterns.

- **Cowrie shells** — Set in rows along the edge and strap of the skin cap.
- **Facial scarification** — Recorded on ivory masks as triangular marks at the bridge of the nose and linear marks across both cheeks, showing how faces were marked.

## Music & performance

Hunting whistles are the only named instruments; song and poetry are central to Bwami teaching, delivered alongside dance and proverbs to members at graded levels.

- **Hunting whistle** — Wooden with three sound holes, wrapped in fiber and slung from a hide strap, used in the hunt.
- **Bwami song and poetry** — Central to the association's graded initiation, teaching wisdom to members at seven male and four female levels.

## Dance & theatre

Dance is one of three media through which Bwami proverbs are conveyed to initiates; masks serve as badges of rank and initiation objects.

- **Initiation dance** — One of the three media—along with poetry and song—through which Bwami proverbs are delivered to members.

## Festivals & rituals

Ritual life runs through the Bwami association rather than an annual calendar, with graded initiation rites and masked performances marking advancement.

- **Bwami** — A graded membership association open to all, with seven levels for men and four for women, governing social and political life through instruction, payment, and initiation.
- **Kindi** — A member who reaches the top Bwami grade and is recognized as a social leader of great moral authority.
- **Initiation rite** — Graded ceremonies where masks and figures are brought out and their meaning is carried by attached proverbs, with restricted pieces reserved for the highest grades.

## Foodways

The Lega lived by hunting and gathering in an abundant forest, with roasting preferred and cooking done by women in segregated eating spaces.

- **Forest hunting** — The traditional food economy, hunting monkeys, chimpanzees, leopards, buffaloes, elephants, and antelopes in the abundant upper Ulindi valley.
- **Roasting** — The preferred cooking method whenever possible.
- **Hunting whistle** — A wooden tool integral to the hunting and food economy.

## Oral tradition & literature

The proverb is the main oral form, inseparable from Bwami sculpture—each carved figure carries proverbs that teach moral and social values.

- **Bwami proverb** — Each Bwami mask and figure is associated with proverbs carried by dance, poetry, and song, teaching wisdom to members.

## Language & religion

Lega is a Bantu language with two major varieties; religion is polytheistic with four main gods and some Catholic and Protestant missionary influence since the 1910s.

- **Shabunda Lega** — The majority Lega language variety.
- **Mwenga Lega** — Spoken by about 10% of Lega speakers and difficult for Shabunda speakers to understand.
- **Kalaga** — A Lega god known as the promiser.
- **Kenkunga** — A Lega god known as the reassembler.
- **Ombe** — A Lega god known as the hidden.

- *mugusu* — A sword or bill-hook with a shaped steel blade and wooden handle
