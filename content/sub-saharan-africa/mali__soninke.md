## Overview

The Soninke are a Mande-speaking people spread across several West African countries. Their history is linked to the Ghana, or Wagadu, Empire, trade networks, migration, and agriculture. Islam shapes marriage, circumcision, and other rites. Their documented material culture includes indigo cotton textiles, embroidered gowns, and gold adornment.

## Material culture

The profile documents indigo cotton clothing, embroidered gowns, and gold jewelry, but gives little information about buildings, pottery, tools, or household goods.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Soninke textiles in the records are hand-sewn cotton pieces dyed indigo and decorated with geometric and linear patterns.

- **Indigo stitch-resist-dyed cotton cloth** — This cloth is made from five or six cotton strips sewn together, then dyed indigo with bars, dots, geometric designs, and fringed borders.
- **Indigo tie-dyed and stitch-resist-dyed shawls** — These cotton shawls use indigo and black stripes, dot and line patterns, deep indigo borders, and loose fringes.
- **Indigo checkerboard shawl** — This shawl has alternating indigo and natural cotton bands arranged like a checkerboard, with a narrow red stripe in each band.
- **Indigo herringbone-patterned skirt** — This skirt uses eight narrow cotton strips with light indigo herringbone designs and zig-zags at one end.
- **Indigo-dyed embroidered gown** (*tobe*) — This indigo gown is sewn from four cotton sheets and decorated with hand embroidery in many colors.

### Clothing & dress

Documented clothing includes women’s indigo skirts and shawls and a man’s embroidered rectangular gown.

- **Embroidered rectangular gown** (*boubou; tobe*) — A man’s gown has square sleeves, a pocket, and embroidery on the front and back.
- **Indigo cotton skirt** — Women’s skirts are made from sewn strips of indigo-dyed cotton.
- **Indigo cotton shawl** — Women’s shawls are made from sewn cotton strips with indigo patterns and fringes.
- **Head covering** — Photographs show women wearing head coverings, sometimes with gold rings attached.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Soninke architecture or building traditions.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources give little information about everyday objects but mention pottery workers and gold ornaments.

- **Pottery work** (*tage; numu*) — Pottery workers belong to the hereditary caste category called the tage or numu.
- **Gold earrings** — Museum photographs document gold earrings worn by Soninke women.
- **Gold head-covering rings** — Museum photographs document gold rings attached to women’s head coverings.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Gold earrings and rings mark women’s marital status in the documented photographs.

- **Large married women’s earrings** — Large, heavy gold earrings show that a woman is married.
- **Small unmarried women’s earrings** — Unmarried women wear much smaller gold earrings.
- **Marriage rings on head coverings** — Small gold rings may be attached at the forehead or sides of a head covering after marriage.
- **Shaved heads at circumcision** — Photographs record boys with shaved heads during a circumcision ceremony.

## Music & performance

Music appears in circumcision rites and in hereditary roles such as griots and bards.

- **Circumcision tam-tams** (*tam-tams*) — Boys circumcised the previous year organize tam-tams for the new boys to prepare them mentally.
- **Circumcision drum** (*daïné*) — During the ceremony, boys sit around a drum called the daïné.
- **Circumcision scarves** (*disa sing*) — The boys are surrounded by scarves called disa sing.
- **Bard** (*fune*) — The fune is a hereditary social role described as a bard.
- **Griot and singer** (*gesere; jeli*) — The gesere or jeli are described as griots and singers.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not document named dances or theatre traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

Marriage and circumcision are important Soninke rites shaped by Islam and social customs.

- **Engagement** (*i na tamma laga*) — After families agree, the couple becomes engaged in a mosque.
- **Bridewealth dower** (*nakhafa*) — Each month after engagement, the man pays the woman’s family a dower called nakhafa.
- **Marriage** (*futtu*) — The marriage is completed through a contract that names the dower and includes the wedding event called karikompe.
- **Marriage advisers** (*khoussoumanta-yougo; khoussoumanta-yakhare*) — The man and woman each have an adviser with a separate title.
- **Circumcision** (*birou*) — Boys’ circumcision is called birou and includes drums, scarves, and group gatherings.

## Foodways

Soninke foodways center on millet, rice, peanuts, corn, milk, sugar, and salt.

- **Millet breakfast porridge** (*fonde*) — Fonde is made from millet, sugar, milk, and salt.
- **Mixed-grain porridge** (*sombi*) — Sombi is made from rice, millet, or corn.
- **Rice and peanut dishes** (*demba tere; takhaya*) — Demba tere and takhaya both contain rice and peanuts.
- **Millet and bean stew** (*dere*) — Dere is a stew made from millet and beans.
- **Rainy-season farming** — During the rainy season, men and women cultivate crops, while women usually cook and care for children.

## Oral tradition & literature

Soninke oral traditions connect the people to Dinga, the Wagadu kingdom, migration stories, proverbs, and epic storytelling.

- **Dinga ancestry story** (*Dinga*) — Dinga is identified as the ancestor of the Soninke.
- **Wagadu founding story** (*Dyabe Sisse; Kumbi*) — Dyabe Sisse, one of Dinga’s sons, is described as the founder of Wagadu, whose capital was Kumbi.
- **Aswan migration tradition** (*Aswan*) — One tradition says the Soninke migrated from Aswan in Egypt.
- **Cousin proverb** — A traditional proverb says, “Cousins are made for each other.”
- **Blacksmith epic storytelling** — The bibliography mentions epic storytelling among Soninke blacksmiths.

## Language & religion

Soninke is a widely spoken Mande language, and most Soninke follow Sunni Islam in the Maliki tradition.

- **Soninke language** (*Sooninkanxanne*) — The language is called Sooninkanxanne in Soninke and is also known as Serakhulle, Azer, or Maraka.
- **Azer dialect** (*Azer*) — Azer is a dialect described as influenced by Berber.
- **Long-vowel spelling** (*aa; ee; ii; oo; uu*) — Long vowels are written with doubled letters.
- **Maliki Sunni Islam** (*Maliki*) — Most Soninke are Muslims who typically follow the Maliki school of Sunni Islam.
- **Islamic rites** — Islam shapes marriage, circumcision, and other rites of passage.
