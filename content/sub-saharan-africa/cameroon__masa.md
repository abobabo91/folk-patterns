## Overview

The Masa live mainly in Cameroon and also in Chad. Cattle, fishing, sorghum farming, weaving, warfare, marriage exchanges, and religion are important in society. They speak the Massa language, also called Masa or Masana. Museum collections include fishing equipment, armour, clothing, musical instruments, jewellery, and other objects.

## Material culture

Masa material culture includes woven fibre goods, fishing equipment, hide armour, reed shields, metal jewellery, rattles, and simple musical instruments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources describe Masa weaving and fibre work, but name no patterns or motifs.

- **Fishing devices** — Masa weavers developed many fishing devices and techniques, including reed fish traps and fibre nets on wooden frames.
- **Woman’s dress** — A museum record lists a woman’s dress made of fibre string.
- **Basketry** — Recorded basketry objects include a cane-and-leaf greave and a leaf basket.
- **Loin-cloth** — A museum record lists a hide loin-cloth with metal fittings.
- **Cuirass** — A recorded cuirass, or body armour, is made of hide.

### Clothing & dress

Known Masa clothing records include fibre-string dress, hide clothing, armour, helmets, shields, and greaves.

- **Fibre-string dress** — A woman’s dress in the British Museum catalogue is made of fibre string.
- **Hide loin-cloth** — A loin-cloth is made of hide with metal fittings.
- **War helmet** — Men who take part in formalised fighting may wear bark-fibre war helmets.
- **Reed shield** — Recorded shields are made of reed, or of reed, wood, and hide.
- **Greave** — A recorded greave is made in basketry from cane and leaf.

### Architecture

The sources give no information about Masa houses, settlements, or named buildings.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded everyday objects include baskets, fishing gear, rattles, metal objects, and a harp.

- **Wooden harp** — A recorded harp is made of wood with wire strings.
- **Leaf basket** — A museum record lists a basket made of leaf.
- **Reed fish trap** — A fish trap is made of reeds bound with vegetable-fibre string.
- **Fishing net** — A fishing net is made of fibre netting fastened to a wooden frame.
- **Rattles** — Recorded rattles are made of wicker, gourd, leather, and beads, while dance-rattles are made of leather with seed pods.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The documented Masa adornments are metal anklets, collars, bracelets, and tin lip-plugs.

- **Engraved iron anklet** — A recorded anklet is made of iron with engraved decoration.
- **Iron collar** — A museum record lists a collar made of iron.
- **Iron bracelet** — A museum record lists a bracelet made of iron.
- **Tin lip-plugs** — Recorded circular lip-plugs are made of tin.

## Music & performance

Museum records show several instruments, but the sources do not give their Masa names or performance settings.

- **Harp** — A harp is made of wood with wire strings.
- **Rattles** — A pair of rattles is made of wicker, gourd, leather, and beads.
- **Dance-rattles** — A pair of dance-rattles is made of leather with seed pods attached.

## Dance & theatre

The sources name no Masa dances or theatre traditions, but record dance-rattles.

- **Dance-rattles** — Leather rattles with attached seed pods are recorded, but their dances and settings are not described.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources give no festival calendar, but describe cattle exchanges, cow cures, and formalised fighting.

- **Cattle lending** (*golla*) — The Masa practise cattle lending called golla.
- **Cow-fattening cures** (*gurna*) — Traditional cow-fattening cures are called gurna.
- **Bride-price cattle** — Cattle ownership is central to social life, and a wife may cost a man cows.
- **Formalised fighting** — Fighting may follow cow theft or adultery, and it has rules about wounded men, fallen men, and ending at dusk.
- **Women’s fighting participation** — Women who take part in formalised fighting gain prestige in Masa society.

## Foodways

Masa foodways centre on sorghum, river fishing, rice, groundnuts, and fish sauces with vegetables.

- **Early red sorghum** (*Sorghum caudatum*) — Early red sorghum, identified as Sorghum caudatum, is a staple crop.
- **Logone fishing** — The Masa fish throughout the year in the Logone River and its affluent.
- **Rice cultivation** — Rice became an important cash crop and is grown mainly by northern Masa.
- **Groundnuts** — Groundnuts are also grown.
- **Fish sauces** — Fish sauces are served with sorrel, okra, and false sesame.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources record oral accounts of migration, ancestry, and the origins of related peoples.

- **Migration from Sudan** — An oral historical tradition says the Massa left Sudan and reached northern Cameroon before the Tikar.
- **Massa and Tikar account** — The account says the Massa stayed when Muslims entered to convert local people, while the Tikar fled south.
- **Tikar and Bamileke relationship** — The sources say the Massa are said to be related to the Tikar and Bamileke peoples.
- **Musgum origin account** — The Musgum claim a Massa origin through a union between a Kotoko prince and a Massa woman.

## Language & religion

Massa is a Chadic language with several dialects, and the sources describe large Muslim and Christian populations.

- **Massa language** (*Massa*) — Massa, also called Masa or Masana, is spoken in southern Chad and northern Cameroon by about 200,000 people.
- **Masa dialects** (*Bongor, Bugudum, Domo, Gizay, Gumay, Ham, Walia, Wina, Yagwa*) — Listed dialects include Bongor, Bugudum or Budugum, Domo, Gizay, Gumay, Ham, Walia, Wina or Viri, and Yagwa.
- **Muzuk** — Muzuk is identified as a Masa dialect, and its speakers are bilingual in Muzuk and Munjuk.
- **Muslim population** — The sources state that 45% of the population is Muslim.
- **Christian population** — The sources state that 45% is Christian, including mainline Catholics, Protestants, and Evangelical Christians.
