## Overview

The Dawsahak people, also called Idaksahak, are pastoralist Berbers centered on Ménaka and Inékar town in Ménaka Region and Talataye in Ansongo Cercle of the Gao Region of northeastern Mali. They speak Tadaksahak, a Northern Songhai language, and many also speak Western Tawallammat Tamajaq, the Tuareg language of southern Gao. Their pastoral life includes cattle, goats, and camels, with transhumance extending through Mali, southern Algeria, and northwest Niger. In 2001, the Malian population of Idaksahak was estimated at 30,000. Their history includes relations with local Tuareg Iwellemmeden, religious expertise, livestock tending, and participation in rebellions against the Malian government.

## Festivals & rituals

The Idaksahak are Muslim, although many maintain pre-Islamic beliefs and practices.

## Language & religion

The Idaksahak speak Tadaksahak, a Northern Songhai language. Many also speak Western Tawallammat Tamajaq, described as the Tuareg language of southern Gao. The Idaksahak are Muslim, while many maintain pre-Islamic beliefs and practices. The sources state that they took their language from the Songhay Empire and that their name *i-dáksahak* means “sons of Issac.” They share with Tuareg a three-part caste system of “free masters” (*i-dáksahak*), “craftspeople” (*ʒeem-án*), and “captives/slaves” (*ṭaam-én*).
