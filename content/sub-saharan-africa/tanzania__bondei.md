## Overview

The Bondei live mainly in Pangani District and also in eastern Muheza District. They speak Kibondei and are culturally related to the Shambaa and Zigua. Their history includes conquest, tribute, slave raiding, population movement, and resistance. The Kiva insurrection of 1869 helped the Bondei reclaim their freedom.

## Material culture

The profile records patterned grass and palm-leaf mats, wooden household objects, pottery smoking pipes, and a wooden stringed instrument.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Bondei matmaking uses grass, fibre, and palm leaf to create striped and geometric patterns.

- **Moa mats** (*Moa*) — These mats have rounded corners, slight fringes, and patterns of stripes, lozenges, zigzags, and dark interwoven bands.
- **Grass-plaited mats** — These mats are made from narrow grass strips sewn or woven together, often with black-and-white geometric designs.
- **Moa mat pattern samples** (*Moa*) — The museum holds brown, red, and green grass-weaving samples, as well as black-and-white samples, made for Moa mats.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded Bondei objects include pottery smoking pipes and simple wooden utensils.

- **Black pottery smoking pipes** — The pipes have bowls made from black pottery, and one has a wooden stem.
- **Wooden spoons** — The collection includes wooden spoons, including one with an oval bowl.
- **Incised wooden cup** — A wooden cup is decorated with incised geometric designs.
- **Lute-like instrument** — This wooden instrument has a skin membrane and four strings tuned with pegs.

## Music & performance

The only recorded Bondei musical object is a four-stringed instrument similar to a lute.

- **Lute-like stringed instrument** — It is made of wood, has a skin membrane, and has four strings tuned by pegs.

## Festivals & rituals

The profile describes political history rather than festivals or ritual practices.

- **Kiva insurrection** (*Kiva*) — The 1869 insurrection helped the Bondei reclaim their freedom and was linked with the restoration of the kingdom.
- **Tribute system** — Under Shambaa rule, people had to give food and livestock to local chiefs, who sent part of it to the king.
- **Jumbe Mkuu election** (*jumbe Mkuu*) — In November 1925, Geldart Mhina and John Juma contested the first election for a jumbe Mkuu, and most elders and headmen chose John Juma.

## Foodways

The profile links Bondei food life mainly to small-scale agriculture and tribute.

- **Small-scale agriculture** — Most Bondei live in Pangani District, where they engage in different activities, especially small-scale agriculture.
- **Food and livestock tribute** — Food and livestock were collected as tribute under Shambaa rule.

## Oral tradition & literature

The profile records political memory and historical statements but no wider oral-literary tradition.

- **Kiva political memory** (*Kiva*) — Followers of Geldart Mhina said they remembered the Kilindi killings during Kiva whenever they saw a Kilindi on the throne.
- **Political claims from the past** — Bondei developed the skill of presenting political assertions in terms of the past.

## Language & religion

Bondei identity is tied to Kibondei and to cultural links with nearby peoples.

- **Kibondei** — Kibondei is a Northeast Coast Bantu language of Tanzania, closely related to Shambala.
- **Wabondei** — Wabondei is the Swahili form of Bondei.
- **Shambaa connection** (*Shambaa*) — The Bondei are culturally related to the Shambaa.
- **Zigua connection** (*Zigua*) — The Bondei are culturally related to the Zigua.
