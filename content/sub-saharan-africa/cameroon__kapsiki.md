## Overview

The Kapsiki have lived around the Mandara Mountains for five centuries. They live on both sides of the Cameroon–Nigeria border and have kept an isolated cultural setting. Their communities are linked to villages, livestock farming, agriculture, and tourism in Rumsiki. The supplied sources focus mainly on settlement history, language, beaded aprons, and resistance to Islamisation.

## Material culture

The supplied material culture is best documented through beaded pubic aprons made with glass beads, cotton thread, cowrie shells, and possible seeds or nut shells.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Kapsiki beadwork includes multicoloured pubic aprons with rows, pairs, lozenges, animals, birds, and plants.

- **Beaded pubic aprons** — These aprons use multicoloured glass beads on cotton string, with some also carrying plastic or glass beads, cowrie shells, cut seeds, or nut shells.
- **Lozenge-patterned apron** — This apron uses red, yellow, green, and blue glass beads in paired lozenge patterns, with lower-edge beads and cowrie shells.
- **Horned-quadruped apron** — This apron uses rows of black, brown, yellow, white, and green beads around a central horned animal design.
- **Bird-and-plant apron** — This apron has a central design of a bird in front of a tree or plant, with lower-edge beads ending in cut seeds or nut shells.
- **Triangular beaded apron** — This triangular apron uses multicoloured beads in lozenge patterns, with cotton strings ending in glass beads.

## Language & religion

Kapsiki languages have many names and dialects, and the people are described in Cameroon as resisting Islamisation.

- **Psekiye** — Psekiye is a Chadic language also called Psikye, Psikya, or Kapsiki.
- **Kamwe** — Kamwe is a name for the language and is used for the Kapsiki in Nigeria.
- **Psikyɛ** — Psikyɛ is a language variety with a spelling system used in the 1988 Bible translation.
- **Zləngə** — Zləngə is one of the language varieties spoken in the area.
- **Kirdi** — Kirdi means pagan in the description of the Kapsiki in Cameroon.
