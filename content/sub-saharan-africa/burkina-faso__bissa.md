## Overview

The Bissa, also called Bisa, Bisan, or Bissanno, are a Mande ethnic group living in south-central Burkina Faso, northeastern Ghana, and the northernmost tip of Togo. Some Bissa live in Ivory Coast. In Burkina Faso, they are associated especially with the Boulgou, Koulpélogo, and Kouritenga Provinces of the Centre-Est Region, including Garango and Tenkodogo, as well as areas of the Centre–Sud and Plateau-Central Regions. The Bissa language is a Mande language and belongs to the Eastern Mande group. The Bissa are known for cultivating peanuts, and their social organization includes language groups, clans, clan appellations, burial customs, and the Lingani clan’s political and mystical authority. The sources state that Bissa make up 2/5th of the Burkina Faso population.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Bissa burial practices include graves constructed in the shape of traditional dwellings. These graves are built underground and have a small opening for the body’s entry. A designated individual receives the body and lays it inside; the entrance is sealed with a clay vessel that can be removed for future burials.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention a clay vessel used to seal the entrance of a Bissa grave. They also mention an ancestral hunting spear associated with the Lingani clan near Tangaré Mountain. No further ceramics, metalwork, wooden objects, tools, or household goods are described.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe several social and ritual practices. A Bissa man who wants to court a Bissa girl traditionally works in her mother’s peanut field and must be able to provide the girl with her own peanut field if they marry. Within Bissa society, the Lingani clan holds political and mystical power. The person who wears the crown does not possess authority independently; authority rests with the person who bestows it. No one can assume power or wear the crown without first undergoing mystical preparation by the Lingani clan in Tangaré, a village in Garango, Boulgou Province, Burkina Faso.

Bissa burial practices honor ancestors by placing deceased family members in front of their homes. Several family members may be interred in the same underground grave, which is built in the form of a traditional dwelling. The sources state that Barso, an ancestor in Bissa tradition, was a hunter.

## Foodways

The sources identify peanut cultivation as an important Bissa activity. They state that a Bissa man traditionally works in his prospective mother-in-law’s peanut field during courtship and must provide a peanut field for the woman if they marry.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention traditions concerning the origins and ancestry of Bissa and Mossi ruling lines. Daniel McFarland’s *Historical Dictionary of Upper Volta* reports a tradition that describes the Bissa as Mande people who settled along the White Volta below Tenkodogo by 1300. It also records that, according to some traditions, Rialle, progenitor of the Nakomse line of Mossi rulers, was Busansi. Bissa tradition identifies Barso as a hunter.

## Language & religion

Bissa, also called Bisa, has the endonym *Bɩsa*. It is a Mande language and belongs to the Eastern Mande group, which includes Boko, Busa, Samo, and Bokobaru. Bissa is spoken in Burkina Faso, Ghana, and Togo, with some speakers in Ivory Coast. In Burkina Faso, it is spoken in Boulgou, Koulpélogo, and Kouritenga Provinces, in Bazaga and Zoundwéogo Provinces, and in the Boudry Department of Ganzargou Province. The language has four dialects: *Barka* or *Baraka*, *Gormine* or *Gourmyne*, *Lebre* or *Zeba*, and *Leere*. The most widely spoken dialects are Barka and Lebre. High tone is marked with an acute accent and low tone with a grave accent in the writing system described for Bissa.

The Bissa language is one of the official languages of Burkina Faso alongside Mooré, Dyula, and Fula. Most Bissa people are Muslim.
