## Overview

The Tooro people, also known as Batooro or Toro people, are a Bantu ethnic group native to the Tooro Kingdom, a subnational constitutional monarchy within Uganda. The Tooro Kingdom comprises Kabarole District, Kamwenge District, Kyegegwa District and Kyenjojo District; in the 2002 national population census, these districts had a combined population of about 1 million people. Fort Portal Tourism city is the headquarters of the kingdom. The Tooro language, also called Rutooro or Orutooro, is a Bantu language spoken mainly by the Tooro people in western Uganda. Tooro culture includes individual clans with spiritually representative totems, the Kinyege dance, the Orunyege-Ntogoro courtship dance, and the oral traditions of Empaako and Koogere.

## Music & performance

They state that the Tooro cultural dance is called *Kinyege* and that *Orunyege-Ntogoro* is another important dance, described as a courtship dance. The Tooro people’s culture is also associated with the oral traditions of *Empaako* and *Koogere*, which the Tooro people article describes in connection with Fort Portal Tourism city and the Tooro Kingdom.

## Dance & theatre

*Kinyege* is identified as the Tooro cultural dance. *Orunyege-Ntogoro* is another important dance and is described as a courtship dance.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that most Tooro clans have a totem which spiritually represents them. The sources also associate Tooro culture with the oral traditions of *Empaako* and *Koogere*. The UNESCO material supplied here lists no inscriptions for Uganda.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Tooro cultural material includes the oral traditions of *Empaako* and *Koogere*. The Tooro people article states that these are inscribed elements on UNESCO’s list of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding, while the UNESCO source supplied for this profile states that there are no UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscriptions for Uganda. The Tooro language article identifies Tooro as a Bantu language and gives linguistic examples, but contains no Tooro literary corpus.

## Language & religion

Tooro, also called Rutooro or Orutooro, is a Bantu language spoken mainly by the Tooro people from the Tooro Kingdom in western Uganda. It is mainly used in Kabarole District, Kyenjojo District and Kyegegwa District, and is most closely related to Runyoro. The language lacks lexical tone, has five short vowels and five corresponding long vowels, and uses the same orthography as Nyoro. The language has noun classes and is heavily agglutinative; verbs agree with tense, mood, subject and object in class and number.

According to the 2002 Census of Uganda, 48.8% of Batoro are Roman Catholic, 30.8% Anglican or members of the Church of Uganda, 7.3% follow other religions, 5.4% are Muslim and 5.2% are Pentecostal.
