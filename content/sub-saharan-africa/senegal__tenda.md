## Overview

The Tenda or Tanda are an ethnolinguistic group living in southern Senegal, northeastern Guinea-Bissau, and northern Guinea. The group comprises the Bassari, Konyagui, Bedik, and Badiaranke. Historically, the Tenda occupied much of what is now southeastern Senegal, especially the stretch of the Gambia River between the Nieriko and Niokolo Koba. Their region contained many fords and served as a crossing place for caravans moving along trade routes linking Kaabu, the Futa Jallon, the states of Senegambia, the gold fields of Bambouk and Bure, and the great Niger River cities such as Djenné. The Tenda languages belong to the Senegambian language family.

## Language & religion

The Tenda languages are described as part of the Senegambian language family. The sources identify the Tenda as an ethnolinguistic group and explain that *Tenda* is the Mandinka word for a landing place or wharf, reflecting the region’s historical importance as a river crossing.
