## Overview

The Mende are mostly farmers and hunters living in villages in southern and eastern Sierra Leone. Rice farming, village labor, secret societies, masked performances, weaving, wood carving, body adornment, and oral literature shape cultural life. Mende is spoken in Sierra Leone and nearby Liberia and Guinea. Sande and Poro guide initiation, leadership, ritual knowledge, and community life.

## Material culture

Mende material culture includes woven cloth, raffia masks, wooden tools and weapons, musical objects, vessels, jewelry, and decorated body and hair styles.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Mende weavers make cotton cloth, hammocks, blankets, and display fabrics with strong colors and geometric patterns.

- **Mende woven fabrics** — These fabrics use cotton, sometimes mixed with synthetic fibres, and may include indigo, white, red, brown, blue, green, cream, and buff designs.
- **Hammock** (*Kpoikpoi*) — A kpoikpoi is made from hand-spun cotton strips that are woven and sewn together, often with colored patterns and fringes.
- **Country cloth** — This cotton cloth is made from stitched panels with nested borders, hair-comb motifs, and geometric designs.
- **Display cloth** (*Kpoikpoi*) — A display cloth may join ten cotton strips with indigo stripes and diamond patterns.
- **Mende loom weaving** — Mende looms use wood, reed, cotton, string, cane, and cardboard, with wooden shuttles that may have carved or cross-hatched designs.

### Clothing & dress

Mende clothing includes traditional cloth garments, special fibre clothing, and covered ceremonial dress for Sande performances.

- **Vegetable-fibre shirt** — This shirt has knotted openwork, yellow-orange ochre dye, and ruffs at the neck, waist, and wrists.
- **Wrapped head-dress** — Women may wear plain cloth with wrapped head-dresses.
- **Tunics and trousers** — Men may wear tunics and trousers or be draped in cloth.
- **Sande ceremonial covering** — Sande performers cover their bodies with raffia or black cloth and must not expose any part of the body.
- **Sande head-dress** — Sande ceremonial head-dresses include fibre forms, woven basketry domes, painted cotton coverings, feather coronas, beads, and fibre fringes.

### Architecture

Mende villages usually contain 70 to 250 people and have circular or single-storey buildings with thatched roofs.

- **Circular thatched building** — Museum photographs show circular buildings with thatched roofs.
- **Thatched-roof house** — Some single-storey buildings have thatched roofs and wooden shutters.
- **Supported thatched roof** — Some buildings have extended thatched roofs held up by wooden supports.
- **White-washed house** — One photograph shows a white-washed building with a thatched roof and wooden shutters.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Mende objects include wooden tools, weapons, weaving equipment, game boards, vessels, and ritual items, but the sources do not document ceramics.

- **Leopard society axe** — This wooden axe has a flat metal blade and curved spikes.
- **Iron spear** — The spear has a central geometric design.
- **Iron sword** — The sword has a curved blade and a wooden hilt.
- **Mancala board** — One mancala board contains 49 white bean counters.
- **Palm-wine vessel** — This vessel combines a calabash with a wooden neck.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Jewelry, bells, hair, scarification, and neck rings show beauty, status, community ties, and spiritual meaning.

- **Chief’s armlets** — A pair of chief’s armlets is made from elephant hide inlaid with wire.
- **Hunter’s bell** — Mende hunters often wear one bell that can be silenced when stealth is needed.
- **Women’s bells** — Women often wear several bells, whose sounds are linked to community.
- **Cowrie-shell rattle** — Cowrie shells are used in body adornment and rattles.
- **Sowo neck rings** — Neck rings represent health, prosperity, wholesome living, and the ripples linked with the Sowo spirit.

## Music & performance

Mende music uses songs, drums, rattles, bells, and performances connected with Poro and Sande.

- **A waka** — This Mende song survived in a Gullah family in coastal Georgia and was still sung in a Mende village in Sierra Leone in the 1990s.
- **Circumcision drum** — These wooden drums have skin or hide stretched over their openings and held with rope, leather, or hide strings.
- **Gourd rattle** — Rattles may be made from gourds, cotton string, cowrie shells, seeds, bone, nut-shells, and colored glass beads.
- **Bell necklace** — Bell necklaces are linked with communication between ancestors and the living.
- **The Language You Cry In** — This film documents the story of the Mende song A waka.

## Dance & theatre

Mende masked performances use dance, covered bodies, and spirits to express community ideals and guide Sande events.

- **Sande guardian mask** — Sande masks embody a guardian spirit linked with water and rivers and represent female morality and physical beauty.
- **Ndoli jowei** — Ndoli jowei is the main Sande spirit for celebration and also directs dance training.
- **Yaya gbegbi** — At yaya gbegbi, Ndoli jowei comes into town with Sande women but does not yet dance.
- **Kpete gbula yombo le** — This minor feast announces the date of the gani celebration.
- **Gonde** — Gonde is a dance instructor who offers comic relief, friendship, and encouragement.

## Festivals & rituals

Sande and Poro initiation rituals teach adulthood, responsibility, leadership, protection, and community service.

- **Sande initiation** (*Sande*) — Girls begin Sande initiation at puberty and learn modesty, hard work, adult duties, and proper relations with elders.
- **White clay marking** (*Hojo*) — Initiates are marked with white Hojo clay to show Sande protection and control.
- **Poro initiation** (*Poro*) — Boys undergo a seven-year initiation into manhood, leadership, wisdom, responsibility, and power.
- **Poro masked ritual** — Poro rituals include masked dancing, secret language, passwords, work, training, and service.
- **Chief installation mask** — Masks may appear at funerals, arbitrations, and the installation of chiefs.

## Foodways

Rice is the main crop, and farming depends on seasonal work groups in which men and women have different tasks.

- **Rice** — Rice is grown for local use and is the principal crop named in the sources.
- **Cash crops** — Coffee, cocoa, and ginger are grown as cash crops.
- **Local crops** — Pepper, groundnuts, beniseed, and palm oil are grown for local consumption.
- **Farm labor groups** — Labor groups move from farm to farm, with men clearing land and women cleaning and pounding rice, fishing, and weeding.
- **Palm wine** — A recorded palm-wine vessel is made from a calabash with a wooden neck.

## Oral tradition & literature

Mende oral tradition, stories, songs, and proverbs preserve ideas about origins, migration, war, and community life.

- **Mende origin story** — The tradition says the Mende formed when hunter-fishers mixed with later leaders and the native Bullom people.
- **Mende stories** — F.W.H. Migeod published 17 Mende stories with English translations in 1908.
- **Royal Antelope and Spider: West African Mende Tales** — This 1976 book contains 100 Mende folktales in Mende and English.
- **Mende songs** — Migeod’s 1908 publication includes 13 Mende songs.
- **Mεnde proverbs** — M. Mary Senior published a collection called “Some Mεnde Proverbs” in 1947.

## Language & religion

Mende is a tonal Mande language, while Sande and Poro organize spiritual, moral, social, and political life.

- **Mende language** (*Mende*) — Mende is spoken in Sierra Leone, Liberia, and Guinea and is a regional lingua franca in southern Sierra Leone.
- **Kikakui syllabary** (*Kikakui*) — Kisimi Kamara is credited in one account with inventing the Kikakui syllabary in 1921, though another dates it to 1917 and names Muhamad Turay as inventor.
- **Mende Bible** — The Mende Bible was translated and published in Latin script in 1959.
- **Sande society** (*Sande*) — Sande is associated with women, water, rivers, white clay, protection, justice, love, and harmony.
- **Poro society** (*Poro*) — Poro is associated with men, initiation, leadership, responsibility, and secret knowledge.
