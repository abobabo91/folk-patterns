## Overview

The Lebou, also called Lebu or Lébou, are an ethnic group of Senegal, in West Africa, living on the peninsula of Cap-Vert, where Dakar is situated. They are primarily a fishing community and also have substantial business activities in construction supplies and real estate. Their language is Lebu Wolof, closely related to Wolof proper but not intelligible with it. Lebu society emphasizes piety and respect for elders, and Lebu families include living people together with associated ancestral spirits. Their political and spiritual capital is Layene, in the Yoff neighborhood of northern Dakar, where the Layene religious sect and theocracy is headquartered. Other Lebu centres include Ouakam, Cambérène and Ngor.

## Language & religion

The Lebu speak Lebu Wolof, which is closely related to Wolof proper but is not intelligible with it.

Most Lebu are adherents of Islam. Their adherence is not homogeneous: a small segment of the population follows the Layene sect, while an overwhelming majority either follows another Sufi branch or is not affiliated with any Sufi practice. The Layene is a largely Lebu religious sect and theocracy headquartered at Layene in Yoff. Lebu society emphasizes piety and respect for elders, and Lebu families include associated ancestral spirits as well as living people. Public exorcism dances and rituals are also associated with Lebou religious and cultural life.

The Lebu identity, separate from neighboring Wolof and Serer communities, goes back at least as far as the early 15th century. The sources state that Lebu traditions place their origins, like those of the Wolof and Serer, north of the Senegal River, while noting that this is a frequent subject of debate. There were Lebu at Lake Guiers by the 16th century, and by 1700 they had moved into the Cap Vert peninsula.
