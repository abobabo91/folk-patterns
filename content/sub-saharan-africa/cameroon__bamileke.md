## Overview

The Bamileke are a Central African people of the Cameroon grassfields who trace their origins to the Nile region and arrived in western Cameroon in multiple waves. They built a sophisticated court art centered on the chiefdom system, ruled by a fon (chief), and are known for distinctive beadwork, indigo-dyed cloth, and masquerade traditions. Their culture blends ancestral veneration with royal ceremony and skilled craft production.

## Material culture

Bamileke material culture emphasizes decorated cloth, beadwork, carved wood, and metalwork produced for court use and ritual performance.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Ndop is a hand-woven indigo cloth decorated with stitch-resist patterns and used for both display and garments.

- **Ndop** — Stitch-resist indigo cloth woven in white cotton strips, hand-sewn together, and decorated with geometric patterns; made into display rolls, tunics with V-shaped or round necklines, and dance skirts.
- **Beadwork over sculpture** — Beads worked over cloth and wooden sculpture in colors that catch the eye; a technique unique to the Cameroon grasslands where no two pieces are alike and bead type can indicate status.
- **Leopard symbol** — Isosceles triangles represent the leopard and appear as geometric patterns in beadwork on elephant masks.

### Clothing & dress

Ceremonial dress is made of ndop cloth in tunic and skirt forms, often paired with elaborate beaded headgear.

- **V-neck tunic** — Wide rectangular tunic woven in narrow ndop strips with a V-shaped neck opening, lined inside with sackcloth.
- **Round-neck tunic** — Narrower rectangular tunic woven in wide ndop strips with a round neck, short capped sleeves and pockets.
- **Ndop dance-skirt** — Strip-woven ndop with red wool baize cloth strips sewn on at both ends.
- **Beaded basketry hat** — Basketry hat worn with the elephant mask, with its whole surface covered in dyed cotton cloth and intricate glass beadwork.
- **Fibre head-cape** — Circular woven fibre cape for covering the head, adorned with cowrie shells and feathers, worn by a witch doctor.

### Architecture

Traditional homes use a raffia-pole frame stuffed with grass and covered in mud with thatched cone roofs; modern houses use brick and metal sheeting.

- **Traditional raffia-pole house** — Square-walled frame of raffia poles with grass-stuffed holes, mud covering, and tall cone-shaped thatched roof; now mostly used for barns and storage.
- **Chiefdom settlement pattern** (*fon*) — Well-organized villages centered on the fon (chief) with ward heads responsible for sections; family houses grouped together around small fields.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Black pottery vessels and functional metalwork in iron and brass serve both household and ceremonial needs.

- **Black pottery vase** — Flat-bottomed vase with rounded sides tapering to a flared rim, a looped handle, and rouletted diagonal lines on the bottom third.
- **Bell-mouth vase** — Pottery vase with a bell-shaped mouth and two lifting lugs.
- **Openwork pedestal bowl** — Black pottery bowl raised on a cylindrical openwork pedestal with concentric circles, geometric rouletting, and three evenly-spaced holes near the top.
- **Ceremonial sword** — Iron sword with a hilt surmounted by a cat-like animal.
- **Chief's throne** — Carved wooden throne whose back represents a human figure and whose seat is supported by six carved figures.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Body adornment uses shells, feathers, and bead strings.

- **Cowrie shells and feathers** — Shells and feathers adorning a fibre head-cape worn by a witch doctor.
- **Bead strings on sansa** — Clear plastic and red glass bead strings wrapped around the metal rod of a sansa lamellophone.

## Music & performance

Bamileke instruments are mostly idiophones and drums; the tsamassi musical style was popularized by André-Marie Tala.

- **Sansa** — Lamellophone in two contrasting builds: one a rectangular wooden box with fifteen cane tongues and one made of two bamboo pieces bound with fibre with nine teeth over a bridge.
- **Bamboo flutes** — Two bamboo flutes, each with two stops.
- **Iron gongs** — Two iron gongs joined by a handle covered with reed.
- **Slit drum** — Drum carved in human form.
- **Tsamassi** — The Bamileke's own musical style, popularized by André-Marie Tala.

## Dance & theatre

Masquerades with beaded masks are central to Bamileke ritual and ceremony, enforcing royal authority and marking important occasions.

- **Elephant mask** (*mbap mteng*) — Mask with protruding circular ears, human-like face, and decorative front and back panels hanging to the knees, covered in geometric beadwork; used in dance ceremonies and funerals to show the importance of the deceased.
- **Buffalo mask** — Popular mask at most Grassland functions representing power, strength and bravery, associated with the fon.
- **Leopard skin costume** — Worn with the elephant mask by those permitted by the fon to perform, as a statement of wealth, status and power.

## Festivals & rituals

Funerary practice and ancestral veneration are the dominant rituals, with masquerades marking death and other royal ceremonies.

- **Totem oath at death** (*totem*) — Family members step up to the totem and swear they were not involved in a death; if the murderer is present, the totem traps their spirit forever.
- **Libation ritual** — Special ritual of pouring libation performed by the person believed to be responsible for the death, then by the family during burial.
- **Ancestral skull veneration** — The oldest male in the family keeps the skulls of both male and female ancestors in a blessed dwelling; a special libation ritual must be performed if a skull is not well preserved.
- **Bride price** — Payment made to the bride's family at marriage; polygyny is practised and some important individuals have hundreds of wives.

## Foodways

Staple crops are cocoyams, groundnuts and maize, with men clearing fields and women doing most of the work.

## Oral tradition & literature

Oral traditions trace Bamileke origins to the Nile region and arrival in western Cameroon, with narratives about founding figures and the transformative powers of the fon.

- **Origin narrative** — Stories collected by Alexis Maxime Feyou de Happy and his son Joseph describe arrival in western Cameroon in multiple waves from the Lake Tchad and Nile Valley area, and from around the Cross River in Nigeria.
- **Wouten (Betaka) tradition** (*Wouten*) — Some traditions hold the Bamileke to descend from an Mbum princess named Wouten, also called Betaka, who helped establish the Tikar fondom in the 13th century after being expelled from Ngan Ha.
- **Fon transformation narrative** — Oral tradition proclaims that the fon may transform into an elephant or a leopard whenever he chooses.
- **Sculptor as intermediary** — Sculpture is understood as artists creating statues and reliefs that represent life forces safeguarding intangible attributes of death; sculptors act as intermediaries realizing God's vision through symbols and signs.

## Language & religion

Bamileke languages form part of the Niger-Congo family; ancestral veneration is the dominant worship, with some Christianity and Islam.

- **Western Bamileke languages** — Comprises Mengaka, Ngombale, Ngomba, and dialect clusters including Yemba, Ngyembooŋ, Mmuock and Ŋwe.
- **Eastern Bamileke languages** — Comprises Feʼfeʼ, Ghomáláʼ, Kwaʼ, Ndaʼndaʼ and Medumba.
- **Ancestral veneration** — The dominant form of worship centered on veneration of ancestors and the keeping of ancestral skulls in blessed dwellings.
- **Diviner blessing** (*diviner*) — Diviners bless the dwellings in which ancestral skulls are kept.

## Glossary

- *ndop* — Stitch-resist indigo cloth made of hand-woven cotton strips
- *fon* — The chief or ruler who heads a Bamileke chiefdom
- *mbap mteng* — Elephant mask used in dance ceremonies and funerals
- *sansa* — Lamellophone, a stringed percussion instrument with cane or bamboo tongues
- *totem* — Object or emblem that traps the spirit of a murderer in death rituals
- *Wouten* — Mbum princess credited with helping establish the Tikar fondom in the 13th century
- *Tsamassi* — The Bamileke's distinctive musical style
- *Beadwork* — Decoration technique using beads over cloth and wooden sculpture
- *libation* — Ritual pouring of liquid as an offering to ancestors
- *diviner* — Spiritual practitioner who blesses dwellings and performs rituals
- *masquerade* — Performance wearing elaborate beaded masks at ceremonies and funerals
- *bride price* — Payment made to the bride's family at marriage
- *polygyny* — Practice of having multiple wives
- *rouletting* — Technique of creating decorative grooved patterns on pottery
- *isosceles triangle* — Geometric pattern representing the leopard
- *grassfields* — The Western High Plateau region of Cameroon where Bamileke live
- *cowrie shells* — Sea shells used as adornment and historical trade currency
- *raffia pole* — Plant fiber pole used as framework for traditional houses
- *cocoyam* — Tropical root crop, a staple food of the Bamileke
