## Overview

The Ahanta, also called Ayinda, are an Akan people living in the Western Region of Ghana, north and east of the Nzema. Their territory includes Busua, Kojokrom, Apowa, Agona Nkwanta, Dixcove, Princess Town, also known as Pokesu, and other coastal settlements. Ahanta land has historically been described as one of the richest areas on the coast of what is now Ghana. The Ahanta formed a confederacy of chiefdoms and came into early contact with European nations trading on the Gold Coast. The Ahanta language is described as a Guan language spoken along Ghana’s southwest coast between Takoradi and Kasoa.

## Music & performance

The Ahanta celebrate **Kundum**, a harvest festival involving dancing, drumming, and feasting. The sources state that Kundum was originally a religious festival used to expel evil spirits from the town.

## Dance & theatre

Dancing forms part of the Kundum harvest festival.

## Festivals & rituals

**Kundum** is an Ahanta harvest festival involving dancing, drumming, and feasting. It was originally a religious festival intended to expel evil spirits from the town and is now celebrated as a way to preserve the culture of the Ahanta and neighboring Nzema. The festival was formerly a month long but has been reduced to eight days. The history source states that some indigenous cultural practices regarded the birth of twins as a taboo, while Ahanta’s welcoming of the birth of twins was associated in one account with the designation “land of twins.”

## Foodways

The sources mention feasting as part of Kundum. No further Ahanta foodways are covered by the sources used.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources record several accounts concerning Ahanta origins and migration. One account says that Nana Badu Bonso and his descendants came from the mouth of a whale; another connects Ahanta migration with movement from the Pra river and settlement at Busua, which was described as an abode of whales. The sources also state that the land was already inhabited by indigenes who were probably Guans and that the present Ahanta people include descendants of Guans, people who migrated from the Bono kingdom, and people from other vassal states. The royal title for Ahantahene is described as having possibly been “beduru bonso,” later corrupted to Badu Bonso.

## Language & religion

Ahanta is described as a Guan language spoken along the southwest coast of Ghana between Takoradi and Kasoa. The language has two tones, high and low, and its vowels that can be nasalized are /ĩ/, /ã/, /ũ/, /ɪ̃/, and /ʊ̃/. The sources also place Ahanta among Congo-Niger and Kwa languages and state that the language faces possible extinction because Ahanta people scarcely speak or teach it. Ahantas practise traditional African religion, Christianity, and Islam to a lesser extent.
