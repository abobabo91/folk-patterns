## Overview

The Ibibio are an early-arriving coastal people of southern Nigeria with a tonal language in the Niger-Congo family. They are renowned for the Ekpo masking societies, whose masks are considered the greatest works of Ibibio art, and for sophisticated body art traditions including painting and intentional body modification. Their material culture spans textiles, pottery, woodcarving and metalwork, with strong traditions in visual pattern-making and decorative practice.

## Material culture

Ibibio material culture emphasizes decoration and visual pattern across textiles, pottery, wood, calabash and body surfaces, with masking and body art at its center.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Ibibio textiles combine plain weave and supplementary-weft techniques with bold geometric and applied designs.

- **Raffia mats** — Mats woven in plain hand-woven raffia with bands of supplementary-weft geometric decoration dyed dark brown, finished with unworked warp fringe.
- **Crocheted masquerade costumes** — Full-body garments of crocheted vegetable-fibre string striped in black, red, or natural colour, with raffia fringes at wrists and ankles.
- **Bast cloth** (*basttyg*) — Multicoloured bast textile lengths, often indigo-dyed and worn as hip cloths.
- **Shrine cloth** — Composite textile combining dyed and printed cotton panels with appliqué human figures holding a parasol and sword, topped by rows of triangles.

### Clothing & dress

Everyday dress is simple, but ceremonial dress for Ekpo performers distinguishes male and female through wilderness materials versus village cloth, with elaborate head ornaments.

- **Ekpo mask costume** (*Ekpo*) — A face mask with waist-length raffia, secured by rope and worn with a knee-length raffia skirt, with charcoal-painted lower legs, arms and hands.
- **Head-rings** (*Idiong*) — Leather-bound and flattened rings, hide circlets with projecting knobs, or hide and silk combinations, some carved from wood and covered with skin.
- **Plaited string jacket** — A sleeved jacket with fibre fringe along lower edge and sleeve ends.
- **Dance cap** — A composite headdress combining a plaited raffia cap, leather face-piece with white button eye-rings, and shock of raffia hair and fringe.

### Architecture

The sources describe the sacred landscape rather than dwellings, centered on forest lands set apart for shrines, burial, and secret societies.

- **Sacred forest lands** (*akai*) — Forest areas scattered through villages reserved for shrines, burial grounds, and secret society sites, forbidden to non-members for cultivation, hunting or gathering.
- **Secret society grounds** (*Ekpo Onyoho, Ekoong, Idiong, Ekong*) — Sacred sites where the masking and other societies held their spaces, with masks stored off-season in family or village shrines.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Ibibio are skilled in pottery, woodcarving and calabash work, creating vessels and functional objects with geometric and figural decoration.

- **Rouletted bowls** — Spherical pottery bowls decorated with impressed circular and rouletted patterns.
- **Bottle vases** — Pottery vessels with grooved necks, ridges and raised knobs, often with relief S-motifs.
- **Calabash work** — Gourds decorated with cut-and-burnt trefoil patterns, floral pokerwork, or carved stoppers, used for bowls, ladles and powder containers.
- **Carved wooden objects** — Spoons, quadrangular stools with incised flower and leaf motifs, and fretwork boxes with engraved decoration.
- **Decorated coffins** — Highly decorative coffins with ornamental painted metal motifs, coloured plastic sheets and glass side panels.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Body modification is central to Ibibio visual tradition, through temporary painting and permanent fattening, governed by rhythm and nature.

- **Odung body painting** (*Odung*) — Body painting for marriage, childbirth, coming-of-age and death, marking status in Ekpo society, applied by professionals and lasting up to three months.
- **Iduot fertility marking** (*Iduot*) — Red pigment made from crushed camwood applied to palms, feet, legs and faces, producing a light complexion over time.
- **Leather bracelets** — Leather bracelets stitched around the perimeter and bound at three points, with a protuberance possibly containing medicine.

## Music & performance

Ibibio instruments are percussion, idiophones and horns, carved and cast with decorative detail.

- **Composite drum** — A drum with carved human-head base on four-legged stool and skin head lashed with vegetable fibre.
- **Double rattle** — A carved wood rattle joining two open-ended boxes in dumb-bell form, containing wood slivers suspended on wire.
- **Leaded-bronze bell** — A lost-wax cast inverted tulip-form bell with looped strap handle and low-relief segmented vertical and X-shaped lines separated by tightly coiled spirals.
- **Musical bow** — A wooden bow with cane string wound at both ends and ornamental feather tufts bound at intervals.

## Dance & theatre

Masking is the central performance tradition, with Ekpo masks used to elicit fear and execute social control through assumed ancestral identity.

- **Ekpo mask** (*Ekpo*) — A carved mask representing ancestors, village heroes, or mythic forces, with feminine masks in light colours with delicate features and masculine masks in dark colours with deliberately ugly features.
- **Ukot wood masks** (*ukot*) — Lightweight palm wine tree wood carved into masks, easier to wear and move in, often with a horizontal wooden bar for the wearer to bite.
- **Ogbom dance crest** (*Ogbom*) — A dance headdress or crest form in the collections, related to performance tradition.
- **Articulated jaw puppets** — Puppet figures with moveable jaws operated by rods, clothed in cotton jackets, indicating a figure-performance tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

The Ekpo season from June to December structures the year, with masked society members enforcing community law as messengers of the spirits.

- **Ekpo season** (*Ekpo*) — The period from June through December when Ekpo society plays a large role, with farming and water-fetching prohibited on days masks appear and crimes carrying heavier punishment.
- **Ekpo membership and grades** (*Ekpo*) — Open to all Ibibio males but requiring wealth to reach politically influential grades, with code words and dance steps taught to initiates.
- **Worship of Abasi deities** (*Abasi Enyong, Abasi Isong, Abasi Ibom*) — Religious practice centres on libation, sacrifice, worship and invocation of the God of Heaven, God of the Earth, and the Supreme Being.
- **Mbia Idiong divination** (*Mbia Idiong*) — Diviners diagnose what a person did in a previous incarnation and prescribe sacrifices to reverse destiny.
- **Mme Ndem spiritual entities** (*mme Ndem*) — Spiritual entities of Ibibio divisions served by temple chief priests and priestesses.

## Foodways

Forest foods gathered from sacred lands are named, but staples, cooking methods and dishes are not described in the sources.

- **Forest gathered foods** (*mkpook, afang, odusa*) — Fruits such as mkpook, vegetables such as afang and odusa, and snails gathered from the akai sacred forest lands.

## Oral tradition & literature

Proverbs are the documented verbal genre, with naming itself a literary practice encoding birth circumstances, family history and parental hopes.

- **Ibibio proverbs** — Recorded in Anietie Akpabio's 1899 collection, including 'Eto keet isikabake akai' (one tree does not make a forest) and 'Idop, idop ewa, enye ata ɔkpɔ unam' (it is a quiet dog that eats the fattest bone).
- **Birth-order names** (*Akpan, Adiaha*) — Names given for birth order: Akpan for the first son and Adiaha for the first daughter.
- **Circumstantial names** (*Iniobong, Emem*) — Names given for circumstances of birth and qualities parents hope to impart, such as Iniobong meaning 'God's time' and Emem meaning 'peace'.

## Language & religion

Ibibio is a tonal Niger-Congo language with five phonemic tones, complex grammar and a base-20 numeral system, historically written in Nsibidi ideograms.

- **Ùsèm Ìbìbìò** — The Ibibio language with five phonemic tones (high, mid, rising, falling, low), SVO word order, rich pronoun markers and complex tense/aspect conjugation.
- **Nsibidi ideograms** (*Nsibidi*) — An ancient writing system used by Ibibio and related peoples, carved on wooden boards, still preserved in collections.
- **Qua Iboe Church** — Established by missionary Samuel Bill at Ibeno in the nineteenth century, representing Christian arrival among the Ibibio.
- **Ibibio Bible** (*Nwed Abasi ke Iko Ibibio Ayo Mfin*) — The first Ibibio-language Bible presented at Ibom Hall in Uyo on 27 August 2020.
