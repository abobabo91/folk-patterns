## Overview

The Antaisaka are a Malagasy people whose traditional homeland is on Madagascar’s south-eastern coast. Their culture is especially documented through marriage taboos, funeral customs, rice farming, fishing, and local plant materials. Oral history traces their clan to Andriamandresy. Their funeral rites include drying the dead, crying and dancing, and burial in a communal house in a sacred forest.

## Material culture

Antaisaka material culture uses local plant materials for houses, baskets, traps, trays, and some clothing items.

### Clothing & dress

The sources describe no named everyday or ceremonial clothing, but record one woven woman’s belt.

- **Woman’s woven belt** — A British Museum record describes an off-white cotton belt folded into layers, with a vegetable-fibre wallet sewn at the centre.

### Architecture

Antaisaka villages have simple one-room houses, while the dead are placed in a communal burial house in a sacred forest.

- **One-room house** — These rectangular houses are made from local plant material and have an extra eastern door used only to remove a corpse.
- **Communal burial house** (*kibory*) — The kibory is a communal burial house hidden in a sacred forest.
- **Sacred forest** (*ala fady*) — The ala fady is a sacred forest restricted to men and contains the kibory.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded Antaisaka objects include plant-fibre containers, wooden tools, a trap, and a metal-and-wood bill-hook.

- **Wooden shield** — A museum catalogue records a wooden shield associated with Antaisaka material culture.
- **Raffia basket** — A raffia basket is among the recorded Antaisaka objects.
- **Carrying radio basket** — A carrying radio basket made of raffia is recorded in the museum catalogue.
- **Crab trap** — A wooden and fibre crab trap is among the documented objects.
- **Bark winnowing tray** — A bark winnowing tray is recorded as an everyday object.

## Music & performance

Music is documented mainly in funeral rites, with children dancing outside to drum music until morning.

- **Funeral drum music** — Village children dance to drum music outside the corpse house until morning.
- **Funeral money offering** — Men take turns visiting the corpse house and attach money to the deceased with a specified oil.

## Dance & theatre

Dance forms part of the funeral ceremony for women and children.

- **Women’s funeral dance** — Women begin dancing after crying together beside the corpse.
- **Children’s funeral dance** — Children dance outside the corpse house to drum music until morning.

## Festivals & rituals

The documented rites center on marriage taboos, twin taboos, and a long funeral process.

- **Taboos** (*fady*) — Family life and marriage are regulated by numerous fady, or taboos.
- **Funeral transfer rite** (*tranondonaky*) — Tranondonaky is performed before a dried corpse is moved to the kibory.
- **Twin taboo** (*fady*) — Twins are regarded as taboo, and although killing or abandoning twins has been outlawed, the practice persists in some traditional communities.
- **Communal burial** (*kibory*) — After two to three years of drying, a corpse is transferred to the communal kibory.
- **Last words in the forest** (*ala fady*) — Men carry the body to the ala fady and privately speak their last words to the deceased.

## Foodways

Antaisaka foodways center on growing coffee, rice, and bananas, along with coastal fishing.

- **Rice cultivation** — Rice is a principal crop, and women are primarily responsible for the harvest.
- **Coffee cultivation** — Coffee is one of the main crops grown by the Antaisaka.
- **Banana cultivation** — Bananas are one of the principal crops documented in the sources.
- **Coastal fishing** — People living along the coast often rely on fishing as a main source of income.

## Oral tradition & literature

Antesaka history is preserved through oral accounts of clan founders and royal struggles.

- **Andriamandresy’s life story** (*Andriamandresy*) — Oral history says the clan founder was born Repila, became Ihazorango, and finally took the name Andriamandresiarivo before expecting to become king.
- **Ratongalaza’s rise to power** (*Ratongalaza*) — Oral history says Ratongalaza killed or exiled his brothers to secure the throne.

## Language & religion

Antesaka speak Malagasy and follow a ritual framework involving taboos, sacred forests, and funeral practices.

- **Malagasy dialect** (*Malagasy*) — Antesaka speak a dialect of Malagasy, a Malayo-Polynesian language derived from Barito languages of southern Borneo.
- **Taboo system** (*fady*) — Fady are taboos that shape family life, marriage, and spiritual practice.
- **Sacred forest** (*ala fady*) — The ala fady is restricted to men and contains the communal burial house.
- **Communal burial house** (*kibory*) — The kibory is the sacred forest’s communal burial house for dried corpses.
- **Funeral rite** (*tranondonaky*) — Tranondonaky includes drying the corpse, crying and dancing, attaching money with oil, and speaking final words.
