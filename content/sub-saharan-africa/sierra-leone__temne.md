## Overview

The Temne are mainly found in Sierra Leone, especially in the north and around Freetown. They speak Temne, a tonal language related to Baga. Farming, secret societies, masks, weaving, beadwork, oral literature, and religious practices are important parts of the profile. Most Temne are Muslim, while some retain or combine traditional beliefs.

## Material culture

Temne material culture includes woven and beaded textiles, masks, wooden figures, calabash objects, cowrie shells, raffia, and metal details.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Temne textile work includes batik, beadwork, and tools for weaving.

- **Brown and green batik cloth** (*batik*) — This cloth shows two women playing drums and a dancing woman, with wax-reserved designs dyed brown with kola.
- **Beaded apron** — This rectangular glass-bead apron has black and beige triangles, blue bead bands, and a twisted fibre fastening cord.
- **Boat-shaped shuttle** — This wooden shuttle carries the weft thread through the woven cloth.
- **Weaving comb** — This comb beats the weft into the cloth and has narrow sticks held in a frame with braided basket bands.

### Clothing & dress

The sources describe a Temne hat, a beaded apron, and chief’s regalia, but give little detail about full dress.

- **Bast hat** (*hatt*) — This top-shaped hat is made from natural-coloured bast covered in yellow and black bast, and is worn more often by Temne than by Mendi.
- **Chief’s regalia** — The chief and the Sanko appeared together and carried the same regalia.

### Architecture

The sources give little information about Temne buildings, but they mention villages, courts, and burial inside houses.

- **House burial** — Historical descriptions say that the dead were buried inside the house with gold ornaments.
- **Freetown houses** — By 1798, Freetown houses had stone foundations and wooden superstructures, but the sources do not call this a specifically Temne tradition.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Temne objects include wooden masks and figures, weaving tools, and medicine-related objects.

- **Medicine bowl** — This round calabash bowl has cowrie shells, cotton lining, metal bells, glass bottles, and string-wrapped charms.
- **Cane switch** — This medicine-man object is made of cane sticks wrapped in cotton and decorated with rows of cowrie shells.
- **Buffalo mask** — This wooden bush-cow mask is painted blue, fitted with wire, and decorated with raffia.
- **Sowei mask** (*Sowei*) — This black-stained wooden mask has a human face, a helmet, and holes for a grass fringe.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment in the records uses beads, cowries, cotton, raffia, and metal.

- **Beaded earrings** — One wooden female figure has red and orange glass beads attached to each earlobe with green thread.
- **Metal eyes** — Another wooden figure has metal nails inserted as eyes.
- **Metal waist string** — One figure has a string wrapped in metal around the waist.
- **Neck rings** — A female figure has six carved rings around the neck and a blue-striped cotton cloth tied around the waist.

## Music & performance

The sources mention drums, marriage dances, secret societies, and Temne stories.

- **Drumming scene** — A batik shows two women playing drums beside a dancing woman.
- **Marriage dances** — Historical descriptions mention dances connected with marriage.
- **Trickster spider stories** (*panis*) — Many Temne stories concern panis, a trickster spider also found in Vai, Mende, and Limba storytelling.

## Dance & theatre

Temne performance includes marriage dances, masks, masquerading, and secret-society ceremonies.

- **Chiefdom spirit mask** (*kärfi*) — The chiefdom spirit is represented by a distinct mask.
- **Chiefdom spirit masquerader** (*Sanko*) — The Sanko performs as the chiefdom spirit’s masquerader and appears publicly with the chief.
- **Poro** — Poro is a men’s secret society with initiation ceremonies.
- **Bondo** — Bondo is a women’s secret society with initiation ceremonies.
- **Ragbenle or Ramena** (*Ragbenle; Ramena*) — Ragbenle or Ramena is another secret society named in the sources.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe secret initiations, spirit sacrifices, chief succession rites, marriage dances, and funerary burial, but no annual festival calendar.

- **Secret initiation** — Poro, Bondo, and Ragbenle or Ramena conduct secret rites of passage for young boys and girls.
- **Ancestral sacrifice** — Traditional religion includes sacrifices to patrilineal ancestral spirits and other spirits.
- **Chief succession rite** — Historical descriptions mention chief succession rituals involving goat blood and rice flour.
- **Funerary house burial** — Historical descriptions mention burial inside the house with gold ornaments.

## Foodways

The Temne are traditionally farmers who grow rice, cassava, millet, and kola nut.

- **Rice** (*nack maloo*) — Portuguese records mention “nack maloo” as a Temne word for rice borrowed from Mandinka.
- **Cassava** — Cassava is one of the crops traditionally grown by the Temne.
- **Millet** — Millet is one of the crops traditionally grown by the Temne.
- **Kola nut** — Kola nut is grown by the Temne and was also used to dye a batik cloth brown.
- **Groundnuts** — Groundnuts were among the agricultural products traded in the historical sources.

## Oral tradition & literature

Temne oral literature includes fables, proverbs, stories, songs, and language materials recorded in written collections.

- **Temne fables and proverbs** — C. F. Schlenker published Temne fables and proverbs with facing English translations in 1861.
- **Temne-English dictionary** — Northcote Thomas published a Temne-English dictionary in 1916.
- **Twenty-seven Temne stories** — Thomas’s 1916 report included 27 Temne stories with interlinear English translations.
- **Panis stories** (*panis*) — Many recorded stories feature panis, the trickster spider.

## Language & religion

Temne is a tonal Mel language, and Temne religion includes Islam alongside older beliefs and secret societies.

- **Temne language** (*Temne*) — Temne is spoken mainly in Sierra Leone and belongs to the Mel branch of the Niger–Congo family.
- **Kuru Masaba** — Traditional Temne religion names Kuru Masaba as the Supreme Being and Creator.
- **Ancestral spirits** — Patrilineal ancestral spirits act as intermediaries, and sacrifices may be offered to them.
- **Poro** — Poro is one of the secret societies named in Temne religious and social life.
- **Bondo** — Bondo is one of the secret societies named in Temne religious and social life.
