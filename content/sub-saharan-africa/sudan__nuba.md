## Overview

The Nuba are indigenous people of the Nuba Mountains in southern Sudan. They live in remote mountain villages and towns. Their culture includes farming, herding, wrestling, body painting, storytelling, and ritual practices. Their languages and religions vary across the region.

## Material culture

Nuba material culture includes round mud huts, decorated bowls, weapons, musical instruments, initiation objects, beads, shells, and body adornment.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not describe named Nuba textile or pattern traditions in enough detail for separate entries.

### Clothing & dress

Dress includes sarongs, beads, cloaks, braided hair, scarification, and special initiation and wrestling garments.

- **Sarong** — Muslim men wear a sarong, and older and young women wrap one over their legs.
- **Initiation skirt** — An initiation skirt is made of grass.
- **Bull-tail initiation girdle** — An initiation girdle is made of a bull’s tail, rope, and fibre.
- **Leather-fringe girdle** — A leather-fringe girdle has glass beadwork, cowrie shells, and snailshells.
- **Horn head-dress** — A head-dress is made of two cow horns joined by cow skin.

### Architecture

Nuba family compounds use round mud huts, fences, yards, granaries, and gardens.

- **Two-hut compound** (*shal*) — A shal is a rectangular enclosure with two round mud huts facing each other.
- **Main yard** (*tog*) — The tog is the larger yard in front of the shal, where goats, chickens, and donkeys are kept.
- **Conical granary** (*durs*) — Tall conical durs stand on one side of the tog.
- **Round mud hut** — The huts are round, made of mud, and thatched with sorghum stalks.
- **Lafofa homestead** — A Lafofa homestead under construction has circular clay buildings, thatched roofs, impressed decoration, and a possible granary.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Everyday objects include decorated bowls, weapons, tools, shields, snuff-boxes, stamps, and cow bells.

- **Decorated bowl** — Bowls are made of earth and gum and decorated with ochre, gypsum, and charcoal.
- **Iron-bladed spear** — Iron-bladed spears are among the recorded Nuba weapons.
- **Hide shield** — Shields are made from hide and have notched edges, a conical boss, a stitched hide handle, and a twisted leather lanyard.
- **Nut snuff-box** — A snuff-box is made from a nut with a gourd stopper.
- **Baobab-nut cow bell** — Cow bells are made from baobab nuts with wooden clappers.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment includes beads, shells, bracelets, belts, scarification, braided hair, and painted bodies.

- **Glass beadwork** — Glass beadwork appears in green, blue, saffron, and white, with cowrie shells and snailshells.
- **Cow-tail bracelet** — Initiation and ceremonial objects include bracelets made from a cow’s tail.
- **Brass-and-tortoise belt** — Some belts are ornamented with brass rings and the carapace of a tortoise.
- **Body painting** — Young men spend hours painting their bodies with complex patterns and decorations.
- **Beaded braids** — Women and girls braid their hair in strands and string it with beads.

## Music & performance

Nuba instruments include stringed instruments, horns, rattles, and bells used in music and initiation performance.

- **Lyre** — A lyre has a wooden U-shaped frame, a gourd, cow skin, and twisted fibre strings.
- **Roan-antelope trumpet** — A trumpet is made from the horn of a roan antelope.
- **Gourd musical horn** — A musical horn is made from a gourd.
- **Kudu-and-wax trumpet** — A composite trumpet is made from a kudu horn and a wax extension decorated with abrus seeds.
- **Initiation leg rattles** — Initiation leg rattles are palm-leaf rattles attached to a two-ply twisted palm-fibre string.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not name Nuba dances or dramatic traditions, but they describe wrestling and fighting contests.

- **Traditional wrestling** — Strong young men compete with athletes from other villages to promote personal and village pride and strength.
- **Club-fighting contest** — In some villages older men take part in club-fighting contests.
- **Spear-fighting contest** — In some villages older men take part in spear-fighting contests.

## Festivals & rituals

Ritual life includes initiation, scarification, circumcision, rain control, crop-success rites, shrines, and spiritual possession.

- **Initiation ceremony** — Initiation-related objects include a skirt, girdle, whip, horn, bracelets, and leg rattles.
- **Rain-control ritual** — Traditional ritual specialists and priests conduct rituals intended to control rain.
- **Crop-success ritual** — Traditional ritual specialists and priests conduct rituals intended to ensure successful crops.
- **Shrine guardianship** — Some ritual specialists guard shrines containing objects used to secure positive ritual outcomes.
- **Scarification and circumcision** — The sources describe scarification and circumcision for boys and female genital mutilation for girls.

## Foodways

The Nuba farm millet, maize, vegetables, and peanuts, and also keep cattle, chickens, and other domestic animals.

- **Red millet** — Fields farther up the hills produce quickly growing red millet without irrigation.
- **White millet** — More distant farms produce white millet and other crops.
- **Home gardens** — Gardens near homes grow onions, peppers, and beans.
- **Compound crops** — Maize, pumpkin, beans, and peanuts are grown behind the main enclosure.
- **Domestic animals** — Nuba households keep cattle, chickens, and other domestic animals.

## Oral tradition & literature

Families tell stories around the fire, while films, photographs, and television also represent Nuba life.

- **Family storytelling** — Families tell stories and oral traditions around the fire in the shal.
- **Nuba Conversations** — Nuba Conversations (2000) is a documentary and ethnographic film directed by Arthur Howes.
- **The Last of the Nuba** — The Last of the Nuba (1973) is one of Leni Riefenstahl’s photographic collections.
- **The People of Kau** — The People of Kau (1976) is one of Leni Riefenstahl’s photographic collections.
- **Bisha TV** — Bisha TV is a satirical muppet show popular throughout the Nuba lands and an example of comedy used to deal with authoritarian rule.

## Language & religion

Nuba languages vary widely, and Islam, Christianity, and traditional animistic beliefs are all present.

- **Kordofanian languages** (*Kordofanian*) — Most Nuba speak languages in the geographic Kordofanian group, primarily within the Niger–Congo family.
- **Nilo-Saharan languages** (*Nilo-Saharan*) — Several Nuba languages belong to the Nilo-Saharan family.
- **Sudanese Arabic** — Many Nuba also speak Sudanese Arabic, the common language of Sudan.
- **Islam** — The majority of Nuba living in the eastern, western, and northern parts of the mountains are Muslims.
- **Traditional animistic religions** (*animistic*) — Nuba living to the south are described as Christians or practitioners of traditional animistic religions.
