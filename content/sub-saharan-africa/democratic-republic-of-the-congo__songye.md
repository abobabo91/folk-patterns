## Overview

The Songye are organized into thirty-four separate chiefdoms led by chiefs advised by councils of elders and nobles. They are known chiefly for two sculptural complexes: the striated kifwebe mask and the charged nkishi power figure, both produced within a framework of blacksmithing, chiefly investiture and mystical practice. Their artistic traditions concentrate on wood, skin and metal rather than woven cloth, and their ceremonial life is keyed to the life cycle and political succession.

## Material culture

Songye pattern and decoration live primarily on wood, skin and metal through carving, scarification, and metal sheathing rather than on cloth.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Raffia weaving is plain compared to Kuba work, with pattern coming from warp and weft contrast rather than pile.

- **Mbuki raffia cloth** (*mbuki*) — Panels woven from softened raffia palm fibre on a single-heddle loom, used for wrappers, masquerade skirting and funerary payment.
- **Bifwebe striation** (*bifwebe*) — Dense parallel incised lines covering the entire face of a kifwebe mask, filled alternately with white kaolin, red camwood and black soot to express a colour argument about order and danger.
- **Kilonda scarification** (*kilonda*) — Raised keloid patterning cut into skin of the abdomen, temples and back, reproduced on nkishi figures to show the owner's or lineage's identity marks.
- **Kalengula copper sheathing** (*kalengula*) — Copper sheet and strip beaten over carved wood, most often on the face and temples of a large communal nkishi, serving as both protective skin and statement of wealth.
- **Lukasa-style beadwork** (*lukasa*) — Glass-bead and cowrie work applied to regalia, belts and figure attachments, shared with neighbouring Luba traditions.

### Clothing & dress

Everyday dress is a raffia wrapper, but ceremonial masqueraders are covered completely in wooden mask, knotted raffia netting, bark fibres and goat skin.

- **Kifwebe masquerader costume** (*kifwebe*) — A complete body covering of wooden mask, full-body suit and sleeves of knotted raffia netting, long black fibres from tree bark and roots, and a goat skin at the waist, ensuring no skin is visible during performance.
- **Chiefly regalia** (*bwalelo*) — Investiture dress assembling leopard skin, copper ornament, beaded belts, a fly-whisk, ceremonial adze and staff worn by the supreme chief.
- **Kutomboka musanga dance dress** (*kutomboka musanga*) — The regalia worn during the investiture dance that marks the new supreme chief as warrior and protector.

### Architecture

Villages are agricultural settlements of rectangular wattle-and-daub houses arranged around a central open space for assembly and masquerade.

- **Chief's compound** — A fenced court distinguished by the kabila stool and a shrine structure housing the large communal nkishi power figure.
- **Kabila stool** (*kabila*) — A carved chief's seat that materialises the office of supreme chief.
- **Blacksmith's forge** — A charged building tied to founding cultural heroes, where Songye axes and blades were produced and circulated widely enough to reach present-day Zimbabwe.
- **Lineage shrine** — A compound structure within family compounds that houses family-level power figures; smaller personal figures travel with their owners.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Metalwork is the craft on which Songye prestige rests, producing parade weapons and status objects with elaborate hafts.

- **Nsapo axe** (*nsapo*) — A parade and status weapon with openwork or inlaid copper hafts.
- **Mpungu iron blade** (*mpungu*) — A parade and status blade with inlaid copper work.
- **Songye ceremonial adze** — A prestige tool whose blade issues from a carved human head in the manner of Luba court adzes.
- **Kwilu and handa copper ingots** (*kwilu, handa*) — Currency ingots in croisette and X-shaped forms that functioned as wealth, bridewealth and raw material for kalengula sheathing.
- **Kiteki figure** (*kiteki*) — A carved wooden figure type in the Songye sculptural corpus.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment concentrates on copper, iron, cowrie and glass bead worn in quantity as accumulated wealth, while kilonda scarification marks lineage identity.

- **Bangles, anklets, neck rings and belt strings** — Copper and iron ornaments worn in accumulating quantity as displays of personal wealth rather than as single showpieces.
- **Kilonda keloid scarification** (*kilonda*) — Body marks cut at initiation and after childbirth on the abdomen, temples and back, reproduced on nkishi figures to identify their lineage.
- **Shankadi coiffure** (*shankadi*) — High built hairstyles of cascading stepped or terraced arrangements held with oil and clay, echoed in the carved coiffures of figures and masks.
- **Nganga charms** (*nganga*) — Small horns, packets and studded pendants worn on the body, made by mystical specialists on the same principles as large power figures.

## Music & performance

Music is responsorial and organised by occasion rather than concert genre, accompanying masquerade and announcing assemblies.

- **Slit drums and membrane drums** — Tuned drums that accompany masquerade and announce assemblies.
- **Lamellophone and xylophone** — Pitched instruments shared with the wider Luba–Songye region.
- **Mukanda songs** (*mukanda*) — Initiation and circumcision songs performed in responsorial form.
- **Bala musician tradition** (*Bala*) — Documented through Alan P. Merriam's field work, musicians are recognised as specialists but regarded with ambivalence as valued yet mistrusted.

## Dance & theatre

The kifwebe masquerade of the bwadi bwa kifwebe society is the core performance tradition, demonstrating moral instruction through contrasting male and female masks.

- **Kifwebe masquerade** (*kifwebe*) — Male masks with crests and harsh striations carry aggressive, unpredictable movement at initiation, circumcision, enthronement and chiefly funerals; female masks are whitened, rounder, and move gently at public ceremonies.
- **Bwadi bwa kifwebe society** (*bwadi bwa kifwebe*) — The masquerade society whose dancers (all men) perform the choreographic contrast of aggressive and controlled movement as moral instruction.
- **Kutomboka musanga dance** (*kutomboka musanga*) — The chiefly investiture dance that enacts the shedding of blood and the chief's double nature as warrior and protector.

## Festivals & rituals

Ceremonial life is keyed to the life cycle and political succession, centred on boys' initiation, chiefly investiture and funerals.

- **Mukanda initiation** (*mukanda*) — Boys' circumcision held for an age cohort during which kifwebe masks appear and initiates are taught conduct and obligation.
- **Bwadi bwa kifwebe initiation** (*bwadi bwa kifwebe*) — Entry into the masquerade society demands learning an esoteric secret language and undergoing a deliberately violent and frightening ordeal.
- **Chiefly investiture** — The largest public event with elaborate ritual and the kutomboka musanga dance, performed by the new supreme chief.
- **Chiefly hunts** — Ritual as much as subsistence, staged by the chief to display the power conferred on him by the founding cultural hero.
- **Nganga consultation** (*nganga*) — The community-sanctioned mystical specialist commissions each nkishi, compounds its bijimba charge and later adds shells, horns, skins and studs to raise its potency.

## Foodways

Cassava is the staple, pounded and cooked into a stiff porridge eaten with a relish of leaves, beans, fish or meat.

- **Cassava porridge** — The primary staple food made from cassava root pounded and cooked stiffly, eaten with relishes of leaves, beans, fish or meat.
- **Supplementary crops** — Maize, plantain, groundnut, palm oil and cassava leaf round out the everyday diet.
- **Chiefly hunts** — Organised game hunting at village or chiefly level supplies meat for the community.
- **Palm wine** — The drink of assembly, poured at judgments, funerals and masquerade; libation to ancestors precedes consumption.

## Oral tradition & literature

Oral tradition centres on dynastic origin narrative and proverb, with a founding account tracing the people to Tshimbale and Kongolo.

- **Founding origin narrative** — The dynastic charter traces Songye ancestry to Tshimbale and Kongolo, figures who established the Luba kingdom, legitimising chiefly authority as inherited from the founding cultural hero.
- **Proverb tradition** — Proverb is a formal genre; Muepu Mibanga's Songye: livre des proverbes collects 277 pages of them.
- **Bwadi bwa kifwebe secret language** — Initiates of the masquerade society must master an esoteric language unintelligible to non-members.
- **Twentieth-century documentation** — Leo Frobenius's 1905–06 field notes and Alan P. Merriam's village monograph; Congolese authors have written from within the community since the 1980s.

## Language & religion

Songe is a Niger-Congo Bantu language with no indigenous script; the older cosmology of a supreme creator, ancestors and spirits remains the frame for ritual practice despite widespread Christianity.

- **Songe language** (*Songe*) — A Bantu language of the Niger-Congo family spoken across the Sankuru–Lualaba interfluve with dialect variation tracking political divisions.
- **Efile Mukulu** — The single supreme creator who brought into being sun, moon, star, water, earth, fire, wind and rainbow.
- **Kikudi** — An individualised mental force originating from a star and distributed by the wind to each person.
- **Ancestral spirits** — Benevolent ancestral spirits guard the living; rivers, waterfalls and particular trees are the points at which the living reach the ancestors.
- **Mikishi wandering spirits** (*mikishi*) — Malevolent spirits that wander in the landscape.

- *bijimba* — Mystical material packed into the cavities of nkishi power figures

- *bwalelo* — Chiefly regalia including leopard skin, copper, beads and ceremonial objects
- *Efile Mukulu* — The supreme creator god in Songye cosmology
- *kabila* — The carved chief's stool that materialises the office of supreme chief

- *mikishi* — Malevolent wandering spirits in Songye cosmology

- *nganga* — A community-sanctioned specialist in mystical power who commissions and charges nkishi figures
- *nkishi* — A charged power figure housing mystical force, commissioned by an nganga and accreted with shells, horns and skins
