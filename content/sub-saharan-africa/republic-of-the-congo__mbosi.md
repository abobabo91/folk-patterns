## Overview

The Mbosi, also called Mbochi or M’Boshi, are a Bantu ethnic group in Central Africa. Their population is concentrated in the northern region of the Republic of the Congo. The sources describe their language as originating in the regions of the African Great Lakes, from which the group undertook a long migration eastward toward the centre until 1850. Mbochi is spoken in northern la Cuvette, including Boundji, Ngoko, Owando, Oyo, Bokouélé, Tongo, Tchikapika and Mossaka, and in the Plateaux region, including Olombo, Abala, Allembé and Ogogni. Denis Sassou-Nguesso and many senior government officials belong to this group.

## Language & religion

Mbosi or Mbochi, also called Mboshi, is a Bantu language spoken by the Mbosi people in the Republic of the Congo. The sources state that Mbochi is traditionally spoken in northern regions of la Cuvette and in parts of the Plateaux region, listing Boundji, Ngoko, Owando, Oyo, Bokouélé, Tongo, Tchikapika, Mossaka, Olombo, Abala, Allembé and Ogogni.
