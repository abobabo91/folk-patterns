## Overview

The Kipsigis live mainly in the Kenyan highlands of Kericho and Bomet. They are known for herding, farming, oral traditions, initiation rites, clan systems, and age sets. Their material culture includes spears, shields, snuff-containers, leather objects, beads, and animal-skin clothing. Kipsigis belief combines traditional accounts with modern religions.

## Material culture

Kipsigis material culture includes iron, wood, horn, gourd, leather, animal hide, vegetable fibre, and glass beads.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not document Kipsigis textiles or named patterns.

- **Beaded leather objects** — Leather objects and containers were decorated with glass beads, chains, and other details.
- **Animal-hide shield** — A Lumbwa boy was recorded holding a shield made of animal hide.
- **Monkey-skin cap** — An old man’s cap was made of monkey skin and had a leather strap.

### Clothing & dress

The sources give only a few clothing details and do not describe a general dress system.

- **Monkey-skin cap** — An old man wore a cap made of monkey skin with a leather strap.
- **Braided hairstyles** — The Lumbwa were known for braided hairstyles.
- **Neck ornaments** — A Lumbwa boy was shown wearing neck ornaments.

### Architecture

The sources do not document Kipsigis house forms or named buildings.

- **Settlement cluster** (*kokwet*) — A kokwet is a geographic cluster of settlement similar to a village.
- **Village council** (*kok*) — The kok is the village council and local authority for arbitration and conflict resolution.
- **Geographic and political unit** (*emet*) — Emet is named as one of the Kipsigis geographic and political units.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Kipsigis objects used iron, wood, horn, gourd, leather, fibre, and glass beads.

- **Iron-and-wood spears** — The sources record spears made from iron and wood, including one of Masai type and one with a counter-poise.
- **Feathered spear-head sheath** — A spear-head sheath was made of untanned leather and covered at the top with black feathers.
- **Beaded horn bottles** — Horn bottles were decorated with iron chains and glass beads.
- **Composite snuff-boxes** — Snuff-boxes were made from gourd, horn, iron wire, leather, and chains.
- **Wooden snuff-box** — One wooden snuff-box had a lid, chain, and tweezers.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Beads, leather, iron chains, ornaments, and braided hair appear in the records.

- **Semi-cylindrical ear ornament** — An ear ornament was made of leather, iron chain, and glass beads.
- **Neck ornaments** — A Lumbwa boy was recorded wearing neck ornaments.
- **Beaded horn vessels** — Horn vessels carried white beads or arrangements of red and white beads.
- **Braided hairstyles** — The Lumbwa were known for braided hairstyles.

## Music & performance

Kipsigis oral tradition has many songs, but the sources do not name instruments or song genres.

- **Oral songs** — Kipsigis oral tradition is described as having a rich background in songs.
- **Chemosi** — Chemosi is a monstrous ape-like creature with basic intelligence that appears in many oral traditions.
- **Dingonek** — Dingonek was described as a strange water creature in the Maggori River.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not document Kipsigis dances, theatre, or named dramatic traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

Kipsigis ritual life includes initiation rites and harvest ceremonies tied to the yearly calendar.

- **Boys’ initiation** (*tumdo*) — Tumdo involved circumcision and seclusion, where boys learned community values and the duty to defend their people.
- **Year** (*Kenyit*) — Kenyit began in February and was divided into twelve months.
- **Months** (*arawet; arawek*) — The months were called arawet, with arawek as the plural form.
- **Wet season** (*olt-ap-iwot; iwotet*) — The wet season ran from March to August.
- **Harvest ceremonies** (*kipsunde; kipsunde oieng*) — These ceremonies were held in September and October to mark the change of seasons.

## Foodways

Kipsigis food centers on thick cooked paste, milk products, meat, and gathered mushrooms.

- **Thick cooked paste** — Meals mainly consist of a cooked, thick paste made from elusine flour, vegetables, or meat.
- **Milk and cow blood** — A blend of milk and cow blood is a recorded Kipsigis drink.
- **Fermented milk** (*Mursiik*) — Mursiik is milk fermented in gourds with powdered popcorn flower tree cinders.
- **Nubian gin** — The Kipsigis brewed Nubian gin, reserved for men and women in and past middle age.
- **Gathered mushrooms** (*puunereek*) — The Kipsigis gather Termitomyces tylerianus, Termitomyces umkowaan, and Termitomyces microcarpus, also called puunereek.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kipsigis oral tradition preserves songs, migrations, clan histories, beliefs, military history, and stories of extraordinary beings.

- **Pharaoh’s army** — This tradition tells of ancestors who separated from the Pharaoh’s army and migrated south into East Africa.
- **Chemosi tradition** (*Chemosi*) — The Chemosi tradition concerns a monstrous ape-like creature also known as the Nandi bear.
- **Dingonek account** (*Dingonek*) — The Dingonek account concerns a water creature linked to the Maggori River.
- **Lumbwa traditions** (*Lumbwa*) — Oral traditions recall the Lumbwa.
- **Agumba traditions** (*Agumba*) — The Agumba are generally remembered as hunters who lived with the Lumbwa.

## Language & religion

Kipsigis is a Kalenjin language, and Kipsigis belief includes supreme deities, natural harmony, and moral consequences.

- **Kipsigis language** (*Kipsigis; Kipsikii; Kipsikiis*) — Kipsigis is a Southern Nilotic variety spoken mainly in Kericho and Bomet counties.
- **Related languages** (*Nandi; Keiyo; Keyo; Elgeyo; South Tugen; Tuken; Cherangany*) — Kipsigis is closely related to several other Kalenjin languages.
- **Supreme deity** (*Asis*) — Asis is identified as a supreme deity whose main manifestation is the sun.
- **Major deity** (*Tororot*) — Tororot is described as a major deity in the wider Kalenjin belief system.
- **Spiritual harmony** — Kipsigis spiritual life is linked to harmony, the connectedness of nature, and the results of good and bad actions.
