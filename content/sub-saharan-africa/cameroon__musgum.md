## Overview

The Musgum, or Mulwi, are a Chadic ethnic group in Cameroon and Chad. In Cameroon they live in the Maga and Kai-Kai sub-divisions of Mayo-Danay Division in the Far North Province; in Chad they live in Bongor Subprefecture, Guelendeng, Katoa, Wadang, N'Djaména Subprefecture and areas including Ngueli and Sukkabir. Their territory lies between the Chari and Logone rivers, and Musgum communities also occur in northern Nigeria, especially Borno State. They speak Musgu, a Chadic language of the Biu–Mandara subgroup. The sources report 61,500 speakers in Cameroon in 1982 and 24,408 in Chad in 1993. Fishing, agriculture, Islam and continuing traditional practices are documented aspects of Musgum life.

## Material culture

### Architecture

 They mention that Waza, a national park in Cameroon, was founded on Musgum territory, and that *Waza* derives from a Musgum word meaning “my house, or my homeland,”.

## Music & performance

 They identify *Laba*, or Labana, as a traditional rite for fighting and state that Musgum people are known as its initiators.

## Dance & theatre

 They mention *Laba*, or Labana, as a traditional rite for fighting, but provide no further description of its performance.

## Festivals & rituals

 They document *Laba*, or Labana, as a traditional rite for fighting. Most Musgums profess Islam, while traditional beliefs and practices remain influential.

## Foodways

 They state that fishing is an important activity during the dry and rainy season, when the Logone River floods. They also report that many Musgums engage in agriculture, with ground nuts and cotton among the staple crops; cotton is sold for commercial use.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They record several Musgum-derived place-name explanations: *Waza* means “my house, or my homeland”; *Moulvoudaye* means “I buy people”; *Mindif* literally means “the mouth of man”; and *Bogo* comes from a Musgum word meaning “noise” and refers to the site of a battle.

## Language & religion

Musgu is a cluster of closely related language varieties in the Biu–Mandara subgroup of the Chadic languages, spoken in Cameroon and Chad. The endonym is *Mulwi*. *Muzuk* is another name for the language, while *Mousgoum* is not used by the speakers themselves. The sources distinguish the Munjuk, Muzuk, Beege, Mpus and Vulum varieties and state that Blench classified three varieties as separate languages. Vulum is found mainly in Chad, while Beege and Mpus occur in areas associated with the Logone River and neighbouring departments. Most Musgums profess Islam, although traditional beliefs and practices remain influential. No script is documented.
