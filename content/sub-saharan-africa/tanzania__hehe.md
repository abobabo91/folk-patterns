## Overview

The Hehe live in south-central Tanzania, mainly between the Great Ruaha and Kilombero rivers. Their land includes mountains, grasslands, woodland, rainforest, and dry plains. They were agricultural people who grew maize and sweet potatoes and kept some cattle and goats. Their history is strongly linked to warfare, the spear, and resistance led by Mkwawa.

## Material culture

Hehe material culture includes cotton cloth, skin clothing, pottery, wooden household objects, baskets, iron tools, weapons, beads, and metal ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources record cotton cloth with printed designs and bead decoration but no named Hehe textile tradition.

- **Lattice head-cloth** — This was yellow cotton with a block-printed lattice design.
- **Beaded square cloth** — This cotton cloth had four tails on one side, one on the other, and squares of coloured glass beads.
- **Geometric waist cloth** — This waist cloth was made of yellow-dyed cotton with black-and-white geometric patterns.

### Clothing & dress

Recorded clothing uses leopard skin, goat skin, feathers, otter skin, cotton, and beads.

- **Leopard-skin apron** — This apron was made of leopard skin with knobs of black goat skin.
- **Feather-rosette apron** — This apron had a clipped feather rosette sewn onto a skin base.
- **Feathered head-dress** — This head-dress used feathers on a skin foundation.
- **Leopard-skin head-dress** — This annular head-dress used leopard skin with hanging strips of otter skin.

### Architecture

Hehe homesteads were usually surrounded by fields, while wartime villages became more closely packed.

- **Field-surrounded homestead** — A homestead was ideally built farther from others and surrounded by its own maize fields.
- **Wartime village** — During warfare, villages became more consolidated and houses were built closer together.
- **Large courtyard house** — A larger house for many wives could be surrounded by an open courtyard.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Hehe everyday objects included pottery, woodwork, basketry, iron tools, and spears.

- **Porridge bowl** — This pottery bowl was used for cooking porridge.
- **Gourd water pipe** — This object combined a pottery bowl with a water pipe made from a gourd and reed stems.
- **Milking pot** — This pot was carved from a single piece of wood.
- **Three-legged stool** — This was a carved wooden stool with three legs.
- **Spear** — The spear was the chief weapon and used a wooden shaft, an iron head, hide, and wood.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Beads, iron bells, skins, vegetable fibre, horns, feathers, and animal skins were used for adornment.

- **Fringed bead necklet** — This necklet was made of beads with a strung fringe.
- **Iron-bell anklet** — These anklets had iron bells or iron rattle bells.
- **Colobus-skin armlet** — This armlet was made from colobus monkey skin.
- **Horn-pendant necklace** — This necklace used vegetable-fibre braid, two horn pendants, and coloured glass beads.
- **Three-colour bead necklace** — This necklace consisted of strings of white, black, and red beads.

## Music & performance

The sources do not identify Hehe musical genres or performance traditions, but they record a drum and bells.

- **Hour-glass drum** — This drum was carved from wood and had a hide membrane stretched with pegs.
- **Iron bell** — This bell had a hide sling.
- **Rattle anklet** — This anklet had iron rattle bells.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not document Hehe dances, theatre, masked performances, or named entertainment traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe family customs, marriage rules, bride-wealth, and open legal hearings but no named festivals.

- **Praise name** (*Kihehe*) — A child received a family name described as the praise name.
- **Forbidden foods** — A child inherited forbidden foods from the father.
- **Cross-cousin marriage** (*Wahehe*) — There was a preference for marrying cross cousins, while some same-name and female-line relationships were forbidden.
- **Bride-wealth cattle** — Two cows and a bull were important parts of bride-wealth for a wife.
- **Open court hearing** — Cases were presented orally and openly, with different witness numbers required for different cases.

## Foodways

Hehe foodways centered on farming maize and sweet potatoes, with some cattle and goats.

- **Maize fields** (*Uhehe*) — Scattered homesteads were normally surrounded by large maize fields by 1800.
- **Sweet potatoes** (*Mkwawa*) — Sweet potatoes were mentioned, including at Mkwawa’s camp.
- **Cattle and goats** — Some people kept a limited number of cattle and goats.
- **Porridge** — The sources record a pottery bowl for cooking porridge but do not identify its grain or preparation.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not document Hehe folktales, epics, proverbs, riddles, praise poetry, or named storytelling genres.

- **Lugalo ambush** (*Lugalo*) — Historical accounts preserve descriptions of the Hehe ambush at Lugalo.
- **Mkwawa’s resistance** (*Mkwawa*) — Historical sources describe Mkwawa’s resistance, his wars, and his death in 1898.
- **Mkwawa as national hero** (*Mkwawa*) — Mkwawa later became a national hero in Tanzania.

## Language & religion

Kihehe is a tonal Bantu language with four main dialects, while the profile gives no details about Hehe religion.

- **Hehe language** (*Kihehe*) — Kihehe is spoken by the Hehe people in the Iringa region south of the Great Ruaha River.
- **Kalenga dialect** (*Kalenga*) — Kalenga is one of the four main Hehe dialects.
