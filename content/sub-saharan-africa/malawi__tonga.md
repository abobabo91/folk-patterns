## Overview

The Tonga, also called Nyasa Tonga, are an ethnic group living in northern Malawi, specifically in Nkhata Bay District. They are part of the larger Tumbuka people group and are described as remnants of the Tumbuka people. According to the 2018 census, nearly 170,000 people spoke the Malawi Tonga language. The Nyasa Tongas speak Chitonga, a dialect of Chitumbuka. Their language belongs to the Bantu family through its relationship with Chitumbuka, while the Tonga languages of Zambia and Zimbabwe belong to different branches and are not related. The sources provide information about the group’s history, location, population and language.

## Language & religion

The Nyasa Tongas speak Chitonga, a dialect of Chitumbuka, and the Malawi Tonga language is described as a dialect of the Tumbuka language. According to the 2018 census, there were nearly 170,000 speakers of the Malawi Tonga language. During colonial times, Tongas learned Chitumbuka in school because it was one of the official languages of Malawi. The sources state that the Tonga language of Malawi and the Tonga languages of Zambia and Zimbabwe belong to different branches of the Bantu family and are not related.
