## Overview

The Konkomba are the second-largest ethnic group in Ghana's Northern Region, living as subsistence farmers and herders. Their material culture is dominated by elaborate headgear decorated with cowries and beads, and by ironwork including daggers and fighting claws. They have a strong oral tradition that disputes neighboring accounts of their history, and now have a written literature including a complete Bible translation.

## Material culture

Konkomba material culture centers on cowrie-encrusted headgear in gourd and fiber and on hand-worn iron weapons.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Patterns are built on the surface of headgear and ornaments rather than woven into cloth.

- **Cowrie-covered gourd helmet** — A hollowed gourd or calabash stitched over its whole surface with cowrie shells in concentric bands, interspersed with small colored glass beads and hung with pendant cowrie strings from the rim.
- **Coiled fiber hat** — A round hat worked by coiling technique, some with a knob at the top and thick rim, others with ear flaps and covered completely with sewn-together hide pieces.
- **Twined and stitched surface work** — Some gourd hats carry twined string covering over cowrie work, and helmet crests of animal skin held by triple bands of cotton stitching.

### Clothing & dress

Headgear is the main documented piece, made from hollowed gourds or calabashes sewn with white cowries and colored glass beads.

- **Gourd helmet with horns** — An inverted semicircle of hollowed gourd with two long antelope horns tied to the crown, often with a tail of cowrie shells hanging between them.
- **Crested helmet** — A gourd piece with a crest of animal skin with hair still attached standing 9 cm high, fitted with a leather chin strap.
- **Plaited fiber head-band** — A head-band made of plaited vegetable fiber ornamented with cowries and ending in a tuft of hair, worn as a set with limb ornaments.

### Architecture

No architectural documentation is available in the sources.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Iron weapons made to be worn on the body are the best-documented material in the collection.

- **Iron knife with loop handle** — An iron knife with a loop handle for wearing on the hand, decorated with incised ornament and brass wire at the base of the blade.
- **Loop dagger** — A plain iron dagger with a loop for wearing.
- **Fighting-claw** — A claw-shaped iron blade terminating in a ring for wearing on the finger.
- **Barbed arrow** — An arrow with a barbed iron head on a reed shaft bound with vegetable fiber, representing archery.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Limb ornaments work in plaited fiber and leather, often coordinating with headgear to form a set.

- **Fiber armlet or anklet** — Plaited vegetable fiber ornamented with cowrie shells and finished with a tuft of hair, matching the head-band to form a coordinated set.
- **Leather ring armlet** — A cylindrical armlet assembled from separate rings sewn together with looped thongs attached.
- **Leather leglet** — A cylindrical leg ornament assembled from separate rings sewn together with looped thongs attached.

## Foodways

The Konkomba are primarily subsistence farmers, but no specific cuisine, staple grains or ceremonial foods are named in the sources.

- **Subsistence farming** — Farming and animal husbandry were the primary occupations, with raising of poultry, small ruminants and cattle.

## Oral tradition & literature

Konkomba oral tradition disputes neighboring accounts of their history, and a written literature now exists alongside oral transmission.

- **Ndewura Jakpa** — A Gonja leader who, according to Konkomba oral tradition, defeated the Dagomba under Ya Na Dariziogo.
- **Konkomba Bible translation** — A complete Bible in Konkomba produced through GILLBT, GIL and RILADEP, begun by Mary Steele in 1962 with Wycliffe Bible Translators.
- **Likpakpaani Dictionary** — A dictionary of the Konkomba language produced by GILLBT.

## Language & religion

Konkomba is a Gur language with several dialects, and the Bible has been fully translated into it.

- **Likpakpaanl language** (*Likpakpaanl*) — A Gur language of the Gurma subgroup, related to Bimoba, Moba, and Bassari, spoken across northern Ghana and Togo.
- **Lichaboil dialect** (*Lichaboil*) — The written standard variety of Konkomba, using Latin orthography with digraphs gb, kp, ny and special letters ŋ and ɔ.

## Glossary

- *Bikpakpaam* — The Konkomba's own name for themselves
- *Likpakpaanl* — The Konkomba language; also spelled Likpakpaanl
- *Lichaboil* — The written standard variety of Konkomba
- *Ndewura Jakpa* — A Gonja leader who defeated the Dagomba in Konkomba oral tradition
- *Ya Na Dariziogo* — A Dagomba leader defeated by Ndewura Jakpa in Konkomba tradition
- *GILLBT* — Ghana Institute of Linguistics, Literacy and Bible Translation
- *RILADEP* — The Konkomba Literacy and Development Project, formerly KOLADEP
