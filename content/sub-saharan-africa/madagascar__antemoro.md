## Overview

The Antemoro live on Madagascar's southeastern coast and trace their ancestry to both East African Bantu and Indonesian Austronesian speakers, with some clans claiming Arab and Persian descent. They developed sorabe, an Arabic-script writing system unique among Malagasy peoples, used to record sacred knowledge and astrological teachings. Their culture blends East African, Arab, and Islamic influences in dress, ritual, and daily life. Rice farming and fishing form the economic foundation of their coastal communities.

## Material culture

Antemoro material culture relies heavily on plant materials—woven reeds, raffia, coconut shell, and wood—for clothing, containers, and household objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Antemoro textiles are made from reeds, barkcloth, and raffia-cotton weaving, sewn into garments and household goods.

- **Reed-mat jackets and tunics** (*tafitsihy*) — Plaited grass or reed mats sewn together and fitted with folded sections for collar and armholes to form sleeveless garments.
- **Barkcloth loincloth** (*fanto*) — Beaten barkcloth worn beneath reed ensembles.
- **Striped raffia-cotton skirt** — Two rectangles of raffia and cotton woven in beige with red, off-white and brown stripes, sewn into a tube.
- **Antemoro paper** — Handmade sheet material decorated with pressed fresh flowers and leaves, traditionally used to record sorabe writings and now sold to tourists.

### Clothing & dress

Men wore turbans or fez hats with long robes and reed-mat jackets over barkcloth loincloths; women wore sleeveless reed-mat dresses, traditionally forbidden to wear finer cloth.

- **Men's turban or fez hat** — Headwear worn with long loose robes of Muslim style.
- **Reed-mat tunic** (*tafitsihy*) — Long-sleeved (for older men) or sleeveless jackets woven from harefo reeds and sewn together.
- **Women's sleeveless sheath dress** — Made of two or three reed mats sewn together, belted at the waist or drawn over one shoulder, sometimes with a mat bandeau or halter top.
- **Wife's red cotton shirt** — Later cotton dress with white lace at cuffs, collar and neck, and six clear plastic buttons.
- **Wife's head-band** — Red cotton backed in white with a white tassel at each end for tying.

### Architecture

Antemoro villages are clustered near the coast, with the Anakara clan historically isolating themselves behind fortified palisades.

- **Spiked wooden palisade** — Defensive structure surrounding Anakara clan villages to prevent entry without permission and maintain their separation from other Antemoro.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Antemoro household items are made from wood, cane, coconut shell, and plant fibre rather than pottery or metal.

- **Cylindrical wooden rice container** — Wood vessel with circular wooden lid for storing the staple food.
- **Cane fruit basket** — Woven cane container for fruits.
- **Vegetable-fibre grain filter** — Plant-material tool for processing grain.
- **Wood-and-coconut-shell spoon** — Eating utensil combining wood with coconut shell.
- **Fish harpoon** — Fishing tool combining wood with a metal tip, reflecting the coastal economy.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Antemoro wear amulets inscribed with Qur'anic verses in sorabe script, made and sold as both devotional objects and livelihood.

- **Qur'anic amulet** — Charm inscribed with verses of the Qur'an in sorabe script, worn for protection and spiritual power.

## Festivals & rituals

Antemoro ritual life centers on animal sacrifice for ancestors, divination, clan endogamy, and fady taboos that structure both social life and politics.

- **Animal sacrifice for ancestors** (*sombili (sombily)*) — Ritual sacrifice controlled by the ruling Zafikasimambo clan to honor ancestors and regulate regional politics and economy.
- **Astrological divination** (*ombiasy*) — Astrologer-sages predicted the future from lunar phases and offered charms, spells and divinations across Madagascar.
- **Week-long community mourning** — When someone dies, the entire community mourns for seven days without washing or changing clothes; on the eighth day everyone bathes and dresses fresh.
- **Widow's mourning period** — A widow continues mourning until the parents of the deceased declare it over.
- **Widower's seclusion** — A widower stays shut in his house for one or two weeks while the parents of the deceased send a female relative to care for him.

## Foodways

Rice and fish are the dietary foundation, with clan-based commensality rules and Islamic prohibitions shaping what is eaten and who eats together.

- **Rice** — The staple crop and historical export that made the Antemoro major traders by the early 18th century.
- **Coffee** — Grown in the southeastern homeland and generates income for many Antemoro families.
- **Sea salt** — Historically produced and traded by the Ampanira clan as a principal livelihood.
- **Pork prohibition** — Islamic dietary rule that survives as a fady even where Islamic practice has lapsed.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Antemoro are unique among Malagasy peoples for their extensive tradition of sorabe manuscripts written in Arabic script, containing astrology, history, and sacred knowledge.

- **Arabic-Malagasy manuscripts** (*sorabe*) — Writings in Arabic script ranging from astrology instruction to historical records, produced mainly in the nineteenth century though some copies are much older.
- **Scribe specialists** (*katibo*) — Those who master sorabe writing and are believed to possess unusual power.
- **Historical genealogies** — Oral and written accounts of Antemoro ancestry and the Antemoro–Zafiraminia conflict, told by other Malagasy as a battle between two giants.
- **Secret religious language** (*Kalamo (kalamon' Antesitesy)*) — Arabic vocabulary carried on Malagasy grammar, spoken by Anakara and other religious specialists to preserve secret knowledge.

## Language & religion

Antemoro speak a Malagasy dialect traditionally written in sorabe script and practice a layered religion mixing ancestor veneration, Islam, and pre-Islamic beliefs.

- **Malagasy dialect** — A Malayo-Polynesian language of the Barito group from southern Borneo, with older Antemoro vocabulary containing far more Arabic words than today.
- **Arabic-script writing** (*sorabe*) — Unique Antemoro adaptation of Arabic script to write Malagasy, used for sacred texts and records.
- **Ancestor veneration** — Central practice honoring deceased family members through ritual and observance of their established taboos.
- **Creator god and intermediary spirits** — Pre-Islamic belief system in which the creator was largely forgotten and spirits raised to deities by the time of Islamic conversion.
- **Spirit communication** (*ziny*) — Genies with whom the Anakara were believed to communicate.

## Glossary

- *sorabe* — Arabic-script writing of Malagasy, unique to Antemoro, used for sacred and historical texts
- *ombiasy* — Astrologer-sage who predicts the future and offers charms and divinations
- *tafitsihy* — Reed-mat jackets or tunics woven from harefo and sewn together
- *fanto* — Beaten barkcloth loincloth worn under reed garments
- *harefo* — Reeds used for weaving mats into clothing and household items
- *fady* — Taboo or prohibition established by a ruler or ancestral tradition
- *sombili* — Ritual animal sacrifice performed for the ancestors
- *katibo* — Master of sorabe script writing, believed to possess unusual power
- *Kalamo* — Secret religious language mixing Arabic vocabulary with Malagasy grammar
- *ziny* — Genies or spirits that religious specialists communicate with
- *Anakara* — Clan of guardians of sacred idols, texts, and religious knowledge
- *Zafikasimambo* — Ruling clan that absorbed the power to perform animal sacrifice around 1550
- *Zafiraminia* — Clan that originally held the prerogative of animal sacrifice for ancestors
- *Ampanira* — Clan historically principal in sea salt production and trade
