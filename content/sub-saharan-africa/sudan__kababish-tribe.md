## Overview

The Kababish are a nomadic tribe of the northern Kordofan region of Sudan. They comprise about 19 different groups, all led by a single *nazir*, or chief. Their main occupation is camel herding, an activity that gives them a high standing in Arabic society because camels are highly prized and valued. The Kababish speak a form of Sudanese Arabic and follow Sunni Islam. They descend from Arab forefathers associated with the Arabian tribe of Juhaynah. Their material life is shaped by mobility, camel herding, desert conditions, and the need to protect valuable stock. Since the famine in the 1980s, many have moved towards cities or adopted a more semi-nomadic life. Their estimated population is between 70,000 and 350,000.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Women classically wear a long blue cloth wrapped several times around the body. Men wear long white tunics, loose white pants, and white turbans. Most men carry a dagger or sword and perhaps a rifle or shotgun, which the source connects with the harshness of desert life and the threat of banditry associated with valuable livestock.

### Architecture

A Kababish home is described as a simple place with canvas or cloth walls and roofs made of camel hair and hides. Inside are a few ornaments and a large bed raised off the ground and held together with leather straps.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They mention only a raised bed bound together with leather straps and the weapons carried by many men, including daggers, swords, rifles, and shotguns.

### Jewelry & body adornment

 They mention women’s blue body wrapping and men’s white turbans as elements of dress.

## Festivals & rituals

 The sources identify Islam, specifically Sunni Islam, as the group’s main religion but provide no further description of religious festivals or rituals.

## Foodways

The Kababish diet consists of meat, berries, and whatever can be traded. Spiced tea is described as an Arabic staple in their home life.

## Language & religion

The Kababish speak a form of Sudanese Arabic. Their main religion is Islam, and they adhere to the Sunni denomination.
