## Overview

The Ana, also known as Ifè, Ife, Ifɛ, Ana-Ifé, Anago, Baate, or Ede Ife, are an ethnic group living in Togo and Benin; the Ana language is also spoken in Ghana. In Togo, they are concentrated around Atakpame, especially in the Gnagna (Ñaña) and Djama (Jama) quarters, and along the land strip between Glei and Sokode and the Togo-Benin border. In Benin, communities are found mainly in the commune of Savalou and also in parts of Bante and Djidja. Ethnologists identify the Ana as the most western of the Yoruba subgroups, tracing their origins to Ife. One source describes more than 400,000 speakers of Ifè, while another gives 271,000 ethnic Ana-Ife people.

## Music & performance

The Ife language is used on local radio based in Tchetti, including Radio FM Ore Ọ̀fẹ́, which broadcasts at 102.1 Mhz to the surrounding region.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do record written works in Ifè beginning in the 1980s, published by the Comité Provisoire de Langue Ifɛ̀ and SIL. An Ifè–French dictionary, *Oŋù-afɔ ŋa nfɛ̀ òŋu òkpi-ŋà ŋa nfãrãsé*, edited by Mary Gardner and Elizabeth Graveling, was produced in 2000.

## Language & religion

Ana speech is Ifè, also called Ife, Ifɛ, Ana, Ana-Ifé, Anago, Baate, or Ede Ife, a Yoruboid language and the language of the westernmost Yoruba subgroup identified by the sources. In Togo, the sources distinguish varieties associated with Atakpame, Dadja, and Kamina; in Benin, the language is based on the dialect of Tchetti. Ife has three tones—High, Mid, and Low—and many speakers are bilingual in Ewe in southern Togo and Standardized Yoruba in the North. Ede Ife is also used as a second language by speakers of Bago-Kusuntu and Kpessi in Togo, Anii in Benin, and Northern Nago and Aguna in both countries.
