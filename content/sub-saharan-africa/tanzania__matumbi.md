## Overview

The Matumbi are a Bantu ethnolinguistic group native to Kilwa District in Lindi Region, southern Tanzania. They also inhabit the Songosongo island archipelago, while their homeland extends south of the Rufiji delta in southern Pwani Region, in Rufiji District. In 1978, their population was estimated at 72,000. They are the largest ethnic group in Kilwa District, and the Matumbi Highlands are named after them. The Matumbi speak Matumbi, also called Matuumbi, Kimatuumbi, or Kimatumbi. Their language is Bantu and is classified as P13 in Guthrie’s classification. Matumbi livelihoods include farming, fishing, livestock raising, and the cultivation of rice and coconuts.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources identify Islam as the religion of the Matumbi.

## Foodways

Matumbi are mainly farmers, and crops grown by them include rice and coconuts. Those who settled on the coast and the Songosongo Islands are fishermen.

## Language & religion

Matumbi, also known as Matuumbi, Kimatuumbi, and Kimatumbi, is spoken in Tanzania in the Kipatimu region of Kilwa District, south of the Rufiji river. It is a Bantu language, classified as P13 in Guthrie’s classification, and is closely related to the Ngindo, Rufiji, and Ndengereko languages. It is spoken by about 70,000 people according to the Ethnologue. *Matuumbi* is the augmentative plural of the Kimatuumbi word for “hills”; the singular form is *kituumbi*. The prefix *ki-* is a Bantu noun-class prefix attached to nouns in the class that includes languages. The Matumbi are adherents of Islam.
