## Overview

The Bari are sedentary agropastoralists living along the White Nile in South Sudan, combining subsistence farming with livestock held mainly as social and financial capital. They speak Bari, a Nilotic tone language shared in dialects with neighbouring peoples. Their material culture emphasizes iron, brass, hide, and plant fibre in clothing, ornaments, and everyday objects. Most Bari today are Christian, though many retain older religious practices alongside their faith.

## Material culture

Bari material culture centres on hide, plant fibre, and metalwork, with decorative work appearing especially in textiles, body ornaments, and weapons.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Bari textiles, documented mainly through museum objects, combine skin, plaited hide, and vegetable fibre.

- **Fibre-fringe aprons** — Girls' aprons made of plant fibre with a hanging fringe and leather straps, sometimes finished with hide and red beads.
- **Beaded hide garments** — Red-stained leather buttock garments repaired in multiple places with glass beads so the mending became part of the surface decoration.
- **Chain-hung leather skirt** (*rahat*) — A petticoat or skirt formed from a broad band of leather with iron chains hanging free.
- **Spun cotton** — Cotton thread wound on wooden spindles and used for beaded purses carried on cotton girdles.
- **Initiation scarifications** — Girls received arrow-shaped or flower-shaped scarifications on the belly, flank, back and temple.

### Clothing & dress

Bari dress combines hide, plant fibre, and metal into aprons, skirts, sandals, and elaborate headwear.

- **Fibre-fringed aprons** — Worn by women and girls on leather straps, made of plant fibre with hanging fringe.
- **Red leather sandals** — Pairs in red-coloured leather with a strap and button fastening.
- **Conical basketwork head-dress** — A fitted head-dress four and a quarter inches across, topped with split black feathers.
- **Feathered head-dress ornaments** — Plumes mounted on bamboo with string and weaver-bird feathers, forming separable parts of larger head-dresses.
- **Leopard-skin mask** — The only mask recorded in the collection, made of leopard skin.

### Architecture

Bari oral tradition describes villages once strung along the Nile horizon, now reduced by the slave trade.

- **Nile-flanking villages** — Bari oral tradition holds that villages once stretched to the horizon along the Nile, confirmed by explorer Samuel Baker in the 1860s.
- **Woven-stick fence** — Fencing made of woven sticks, documented in museum photographs.
- **Shelter with entrance** — Simple dwellings with an entrance where people worked with pottery, visible in museum photographs.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Bari ceramics, woodwork, and metalwork emphasize functional forms and weapons, with pottery and wooden vessels at the centre of daily life.

- **Pottery** — Pots made by hand and dried, visible in photographs of women working at shelters.
- **Wooden head-rests** — Carved with forked legs and cross-supports bored with holes, some combining wood and hide.
- **Red clay pipe-bowl** — Smoking equipment with incised ornament and a stem of cane and metal.
- **Giraffe-skin shield** — Shields stiffened with a central stick bound in hide strips.
- **Iron-bladed spear** — Wooden shafts banded with brass, or spears combining iron and bamboo.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Bari ornament is dominated by iron and brass, worn on wrists, ankles, neck and head, with ritual body modification at initiation.

- **Iron armlet with central knob** — An armlet of iron carrying a central knob bound with brass.
- **Iron head-band** — A head-band of iron that closes at the back with a chain.
- **Brass-banded leglet** — A leglet worn below the knee, cut into seven strips and decorated with brass bands.
- **Rattle-bracelet** — Worn on the right wrist as functional ornament, with a rattle held inside a projecting central flange.
- **Mourning necklace** — Made of twisted strands of vegetable-fibre cord, worn during mourning.

## Music & performance

Bari musicians play strings, winds and percussion, from lyres and antelope-horn whistles to dancing bells.

- **Lyre** — Built on a tortoise carapace covered with crocodile skin, with wooden supports and five fibre strings.
- **Drum** — A cylindrical wooden body tapering towards the base, with a skin membrane and hide tension thongs.
- **Antelope-horn whistle** — Partly covered with skin and sewn with fibre thread, with finger holes for playing.
- **Musical horn for dancing** — A cow's horn capped with hide on a sling of twisted hide, used to accompany dancing.
- **Dancing bells** — Thirty bells mounted on plaited hide thongs and worn or swung during dancing.

## Dance & theatre

Dancing is attached to marriage blessings and inter-tribal visiting ceremonies, accompanied by specific musical instruments.

- **Marriage blessing dance** — Dancing follows the blessing of a marriage by both families after bride-price terms are agreed.
- **Inter-tribal visiting ceremony** — Participants sang and chanted while swinging back and forth on hammocks, with men's hammocks slung closer to the ceiling and women's hung lower, followed by gift exchange within each gender.
- **Dancing with bells** — Thirty bells mounted on hide thongs were worn or carried during dancing.

## Festivals & rituals

Bari life-cycle ritual centres on marriage negotiation and payment in livestock, with initiation involving tooth removal and scarification.

- **Marriage declaration** (*nyara*) — A suitor declares his intent by presenting himself at the girl's parents' house with close relatives and friends.
- **Bride-price negotiation** — Families meet months before the wedding to negotiate the bride price, traditionally two dozen cattle and forty head of sheep and goats, with payment in livestock or cash.
- **Wedding** (*budu*) — The bride arrives about two weeks after the bridegroom returns home alone following the feast, to take charge of her house.
- **Initiation rite** — Both boys and girls had the lower front teeth removed; girls additionally received arrow-shaped or flower-shaped scarifications on the belly, flank, back and temple.
- **Death rites** — Mourning necklaces of twisted fibre cord are worn, and small animal sacrifices are made to the spirits of the dead; livestock are sacrificed at celebrations and funerals.

## Foodways

The Bari economy rests on subsistence mixed farming with livestock raised mainly as socio-economic and financial investment.

## Oral tradition & literature

Bari folklore preserves accounts of northern origins, fifteenth-century history, and arrival in their present homeland in the 1650s or early eighteenth century.

- **Origin narrative** — Bari folklore holds that they came from the north, with history reaching back to the fifteenth century.
- **Settlement story** — The Bari arrived in their present homeland in the 1650s or the early eighteenth century, where they found and drove away the Pari people.
- **Memory of Chief Gubek lo Gore** — Oral history preserves the killing of Chief Gubek lo Gore of the Dung Kaliri clan and his son Doggale lo Gubek at Rejaf East, beheaded by the Turkish slaving army after Gubek sent his villagers into the bush.
- **Horizonal villages** — A remembered image of a land whose Nile-flanking villages once stretched to the horizon, recorded as accurate by explorer Samuel Baker in Ismailia (1874).
- **Cultural hero** (*Sabaseba*) — Known as Old Wind, a cultural hero in Bari tradition.

## Language & religion

Bari is a Nilotic tone language with vowel harmony, spoken across Central Equatoria state in South Sudan; most Bari today are Christian, many blending this with older religious practice.

- **Bari language dialects** — Bari proper (Beri), Pöjulu, Kakwa, Nyangbara, Mandari, Kuku, Nyepu and Ligo.
- **Sky and earth god** — A nameless двух-sided god, half living in the sky and sending rain, half living in the ground and quickening agriculture.
- **Supernatural power of trees** (*Nun*) — A supernatural power inhabiting big strong trees; bark was stuffed with tobacco leaves and sometimes anointed with animal blood and fat, visited at night to feast.
- **Spirit of abundance** (*Nun-lukak*) — The spirit of food, prayed to for a plentiful season, with sacrifice following if prayer fails.
- **Forgiving spirit** (*Nun-loki*) — The most forgiving of the spirits, ending the life of one judged to have lived long enough.
