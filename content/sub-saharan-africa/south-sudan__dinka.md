## Overview

The Dinka are a Nilotic people of South Sudan whose life follows the annual flood cycle, dividing their year between rainy-season villages on high ground and dry-season camps in the grasslands. They are agro-pastoralists who rely on sorghum and other crops for most of their calories, though cattle remain central to their wealth, marriage, and ritual life. Their material world—beaded corsets, leaf-bladed spears, parrying shields, and ceremonial mounds—reflects their close ties to cattle and the river. Religious practice centers on sacrifice, sky gods, and totems inherited from both parents.

## Material culture

Dinka material culture is built from cattle products, imported glass and shells, and locally worked wood and iron, with beadwork and cattle-based ornament dominating the decorative arts.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Dinka beadwork rather than weaving creates pattern and ornament, using strung glass and shell to build elaborate garments.

- **Beaded corset** — A man's bodice built as a corset with a hide spine down the back and strings of predominantly blue glass beads that encircle the torso and meet at the front in a panel of red, yellow, black and white beads finished with six brass tassels.
- **Beaded collar** — Seventeen strings of faceted blue glass beads with a central front panel of spotted beads and three pendants made from brass cartridge cases, bound at the back with hide and edged with white beads.
- **Ostrich-eggshell breast-ornament** — Fifteen strings of ostrich shell beads plus one string of faceted blue glass beads, attached to two woven hide bands decorated with coiled metal, cowrie shells, and hide thongs.

### Clothing & dress

Men's dress is ornamental and occasional rather than continuous, featuring beaded corsets, ostrich-eggshell belts, and woven straw hats with feathers.

- **Ostrich-eggshell belt** — Belts made of light brown goat skin or strands of white ostrich eggshell beads, worn at the waist as part of the ornamental system.
- **Straw hat with ostrich feathers** — A conical hat woven from straw and finished with a plume of brown ostrich feathers.
- **Human-hair hat** — A hat made of human hair built over a palm-leaf frame with cotton material.
- **Plaited hide neck-band** — Neck-bands made from thongs of plaited cow hide worn around the neck.

### Architecture

Dinka settlements move with the flood, using mud and thatch structures raised slightly above flood level in the rainy season and semi-permanent dwellings in the grasslands during the dry season.

- **Luak** — A cattle byre built in rainy-season villages to shelter livestock.
- **Toic grassland dwellings** (*toic*) — Semi-permanent rectangular thatched shelters with mud walls built in the seasonally flooded grasslands from December to June for cattle grazing.
- **Alel** — A shrine-mound at Makuac in Western Luaic territory entombing the Eastern Twic priest Kuol Alel, raised in a cone from cattle ashes and debris.
- **Yik Ayuong** — A shrine-mound at Rukcuk built over the priest Ayuong Dit, who was shut into his cattle byre with his wife and eight bulls by his own order.
- **Luak Deng** — A palisade shrine on high ground by a pool ringed with ardeiba and suba trees, holding the shrine of the deity Deng Dit and called the Mecca of the Dinka and Nuer.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Dinka everyday objects are made of wood, iron, hide and plant fibre, with weapons dominating the catalogued collection.

- **Leaf-bladed spear** — Iron spear-heads with a leaf shape and a cylinder at the base for fitting to the shaft.
- **Parrying shield** — A narrow shield carved from a single piece of wood with a central boss, hollowed-out handle, two tapering ends and a deep groove down the back.
- **Wooden club** — Straight wooden clubs, one knobbed with a pointed butt, one grooved and served with lizard skin at the grip.
- **Beer strainer** — A household tool of plaited vegetable fibre used for straining beer during brewing.
- **Iron cow-bell** — A bell with iron ring, clapper and hide thong used to mark cattle.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Dinka ornament uses ivory, ostrich eggshell, and imported glass, with elaborate beaded necklaces and armlets marking adult status.

- **Ivory armlet** — Bracelets made of ivory, including pieces made from two joined sections.
- **Glass-bead necklace with ivory pendant** — A necklace of small coloured glass beads topped with an ivory pendant worn around the neck.
- **Green glass-bead necklet** — A man's necklet of three strings of green glass beads carrying a square ivory charm.
- **Forehead marking** — Young men pass into adulthood through initiation that includes marking the forehead with a sharp object, after which they acquire a second cow-colour name.
- **Cow-hide tassel armlet** — A pair of armlets made of cow-hide tassels on leather thongs worn on the arms.

## Music & performance

Dinka musical practice is embedded in religious ceremony and prayer, using traditional instruments and vocal hymns to accompany sacrifice.

- **Lyre** — A wooden-framed instrument with five strings of vegetable fibre and a resonator made from a tortoise carapace with a hide cover laced into position.
- **Reed pipe** — A pipe made from reed with six small holes running its length and a wax seal at each end.
- **Ox-songs** — Hymns of honour sung by the congregation during stages of sacrificial prayer, used to offer praise and expel misfortune onto the sacrificial animal.
- **Prayer chanting** — During sacrifice, those leading prayers hold a fishing spear and chant short phrases naming the need while thrusting the spear at the animal, with participants repeating the leader's words.

## Festivals & rituals

The ritual year is set by the flood and the herd, with annual celebrations at shrine-mounds and life-cycle rites marking initiation and marriage.

- **Alel celebration** (*Alel*) — An annual celebration held at the pyramid of Alel north of the Paliang region in Bahr el-Ghazal in honour of Kuol Alel, a rite dating back at least two centuries.
- **Yairunka baiet** — A harvest season ceremony at Yik Ayuong where the mound is cleared of grass, built up with earth, and smoothed by women drawing stripes with durra flour in preparation for communal offerings and the sacrifice of eight bulls to make women fertile.
- **Masters of the fishing spear** (*beny bith*) — At least partly hereditary ritual chiefs of otherwise independent clans who lead the central rite of Dinka religion, the sacrifice of oxen.
- **Male initiation** — Young men pass through an initiation that includes forehead marking with a sharp object after which they acquire a second cow-colour name.
- **Marriage bridewealth** — Marriage rites where grain and cattle serve as bridewealth; cattle bought with surplus grain are held in higher honour because that wealth is not returnable and counts as pure property.

## Foodways

Sorghum is the most important Dinka crop, with crops supplying 87% of calories and livestock supplying 13%, making the Dinka agro-pastoralists rather than pastoralists.

- **Caudatum sorghum** — A drought-resistant, productive sorghum variety introduced by the Dinka into southern Sudan during their southward migrations, not grown by existing peoples during the great droughts of the 1300s to 1600s.
- **Sorghum beer ritual** — Beer-drinking ceremonies accompanying the sowing and harvesting of sorghum, a practice resembling Nubian custom noted by Ibn Selim el-Assouani.
- **Okra, sesame, and cow peas** — Secondary crops grown alongside sorghum, including pumpkin, maize, cassava, groundnuts, beans, watermelon, tobacco and millet.
- **Milk feeding** — Milk is prioritized for children when supply is low; children are fed milk from nine to twelve months and start on solid food and porridge after about a year.
- **Beer strainer** — A plaited vegetable fibre tool used in beer brewing, a drink-making practice tied to crop ritual and communal work.

## Oral tradition & literature

Dinka oral history carries accounts of migrations, battles, and culture heroes, with creation narratives centring on the first humans and the sky rope.

- **Ayuel Longar** — A culture hero who reached the Nile and Sobat and ordered the building of the great monument at Pwom Ayuel, with labourers' bodies adding to the rising edifice.
- **Garang and Abuk** (*Garang, Abuk*) — The first humans, molded from the clay of Sudan; Abuk's disobedience in planting more than one grain caused Nhialic to cut the rope connecting Heaven and Earth.
- **Wathal Jook** — The Dinka name for Orion, the dogs who are hunters.
- **Cuur** — The Dinka name for the constellation of the scorpion, a fish that resembles a scorpion.
- **Refugee literature** — Recent Dinka literature dominated by the refugee experience, including John Bul Dau's *God Grew Tired of Us* and *They Poured Fire On Us From The Sky* by Alephonsion Deng, Benson Deng and Benjamin Ajak.

## Language & religion

Dinka is a Nilotic dialect cluster with 13 contrastive vowels and heavy use of tone; traditional religion is polytheistic under creator god Nhialic, with most Dinka converting to Christianity after 1983.

- **Dinka language** (*Thuɔŋjäŋ*) — A Nilotic dialect cluster with main varieties including Padang, Rek, Agaar, Ciec, Malual, Aliab, Bor, Hol, Nyarweng, Twic East and Twic Mayardit, with 13 contrastive vowels and heavy grammatical use of tone.
- **Nhialic** — The creator god of Dinka religion, god of sky and rain.
- **Deng** — The sky god of rain and fertility in Dinka religion.
- **Abuk** — The patron goddess of gardening and of all women in Dinka religion, represented by a snake.
- **Totem (Kuar)** (*kuar*) — Inherited from both parents and spoken of as relatives rather than worshipped in Dinka religious practice.
