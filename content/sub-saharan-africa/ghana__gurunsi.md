## Overview

The Gurunsi are not a single people but related ethnic groups spread across northern Ghana and Burkina Faso, speaking languages from the Gur family. They organized themselves as autonomous village communities built as defensive strongholds against centuries of Mossi slave-raiding. Their material culture—masks, brass ornaments, woven textiles, and archery equipment—shows the distinctive practices of these fiercely independent communities.

## Material culture

Gurunsi material culture centers on archery equipment, cast brass ornaments, carved masks, and hand-made textiles worked in fiber and leather.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Gurunsi textiles use structural patterning from twining, braiding, and contrasting materials rather than applied dye.

- **Fibre aprons** — Vegetable fibre aprons using twined, braided and plain techniques, some embroidered with strips of black and white leather.
- **Woman's front apron** — Hand-spun, hand-woven cotton garment with a narrow woven band and hanging fringe in red and blue vertical stripes.

### Clothing & dress

Gurunsi dress emphasizes headgear and aprons, combining practical wear with display pieces featuring shells, feathers, and skin.

- **Coiled-leaf basketry hat** — Basketry made from coiled leaf strip with a plume of white hair set in the centre.
- **Monkey-skin cap** — Fitted with leather ornament of buttons and projecting horns at front, metal chain pendant, skin chin strap and cloth lining.
- **Cowrie-shell band** — Braided vegetable-fibre band covered in horizontal rows of stitched cowrie shells with white feathers massed at the front.

### Architecture

Gurunsi settlements were built as defensive strongholds, with the Kassena notable for women decorating house exteriors with geometric patterns.

- **Kassena patterned clay houses** — Men build clay houses while women decorate the exteriors with geometric patterns.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Archery equipment dominates the everyday material record, with hide quivers, cane and iron arrows, and carved wooden whistles.

- **Hide quivers** — Made of hide, bound with vegetable fibre and slung from plaited leather carrying straps, holding poisoned arrows in sets of five, eight and sixteen.
- **Poisoned arrows** — Combine cane or reed shafts, iron heads and vegetable-fibre bindings, sometimes bundled together in fibre cloth.
- **Carved wooden whistles** — Two types documented: one with terminal mouthpiece and metal strips attached; another tubular with wide angled mouth-hole and incised line decoration.
- **Bamboo and wood bows** — Strung with cane or hide, with one wooden bow having its cane string ornamented with bands of leaf and string binding.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Cast brass anklets are the signature ornament, featuring geometric patterns and worn as pairs.

- **Cast brass anklets** — Penannular open rings with flaring triangular terminals, decorated with applied circular bosses, striated triangles, and chevron and angle patterns.
- **Cowrie-shell adornment** — Braided fibre band sewn with horizontal courses of cowrie shells and fronted with white feathers.
- **Metal chain pendant** — Hanging ornament attached to monkey-skin caps, worn at the side of the head.

## Music & performance

Only carved wooden whistles are documented in the record; song genres and performance occasions are not named.

- **Metal-stripped whistle** — Carved wooden whistle with terminal mouthpiece and strips of metal attached to the body.
- **Notched tubular whistle** — Wooden instrument with wide angled mouth-hole, openwork triangular form, suspension hole for carrying on a cord, and incised line decoration.

## Dance & theatre

Masking is central to Gurunsi performance, with surviving masks overwhelmingly zoomorphic in form.

- **Animal head mask** — Semicircular crown with two long ears projecting backwards and long snout thrust forward, serrated ridge carved down centre, sides incised with squares, muzzle with diamonds, ears painted red.
- **Bull mask** — Large mask with tall yellow-painted horns over a black face striped in red and white, open snout with white teeth, and mirrors set as eyes.
- **Crocodile-jaw mask** — Painted white, red and black with crocodile jaws and a fibre fringe hanging below the face.

## Festivals & rituals

Festival calendar and life-cycle rites are not documented, but a ritual belief ties the group's name to protection against iron.

- **Babatu's iron-proof battalion** (*guru-si*) — During late 19th-century Djerma invasions, the jihadist leader Babatu recruited local men who, after consuming traditional medicines, were said to be invulnerable to iron.

## Foodways

Gurunsi staples, dishes and ceremonial food are not documented in the sources.

## Oral tradition & literature

Gurunsi oral tradition preserves the group's migration history from the western Sudan through the Sahel.

- **Migration narrative** — Oral tradition holds that the Gurunsi came out of the western Sudan and passed through the Sahel to reach their present lands, though the tradition does not date the movement.

## Language & religion

The Grũsi languages form a branch of Gur with roughly twenty related languages; current religious landscape is not described.

- **Kabiye language** — The largest Gurunsi language, spoken by about 1.2 million people in central Togo, 550,000 of them as a first language.
- **Eastern Gurunsi languages** — Lukpa, Kabiyé, Tem, Lama, Delo, Bago-Kusuntu, Chala, showing Kwa influence on some languages.
- **Northern Gurunsi languages** — Lyélé, Nuni, Kalamsé, Pana, Kasem spoken in the northern region.

## Glossary

- *Gurunsi* — Umbrella term used by Mossi kingdoms for related ethnic groups sharing Gur languages
- *Grũsi* — The Gurunsi languages; also called East Mabia languages
- *guru-si* — Djerma words meaning 'iron does not penetrate,' from which Gurunsi name is derived
- *penannular* — Open ring form, as in brass anklets
- *Mossi* — Kingdoms and people to whose states Gurunsi communities were historically subject
- *Djerma* — People whose jihadist leader Babatu invaded Gurunsiland in late 19th century
- *Babatu* — Jihadist leader known for recruiting local battalion said to be iron-proof
- *Kassena* — One of the largest Gurunsi groups, known for patterned clay houses
- *Frafra* — One of the largest Gurunsi groups in Ghana
- *Talensi* — One of the largest Gurunsi groups in Ghana
- *Kabiyé* — Largest Gurunsi language, spoken mainly in central Togo
- *Lyélé* — Northern Gurunsi language spoken in Burkina Faso
- *Nuni* — Northern Gurunsi language spoken in the region
- *Kasem* — Northern Gurunsi language spoken by Kassena people
- *Sisaala* — Western Gurunsi language spoken in Burkina Faso
