## Overview

The Soga, also called Basoga, are a Bantu ethnic group native to the kingdom of Busoga in eastern Uganda. They live in Bugiri, Iganga, Jinja, Kamuli, and Mayuge, as well as later districts including Luuka, Kaliro, Namayingo, Bugweri, Namutunba, and Buyende. Busoga lies immediately north of the equator and is bounded by Lake Kyoga, the Victoria Nile, the Mpologoma River, and Lake Victoria. The region covers 8,920 square kilometres, with a length of about 160 km and a width of a little over 80 km. Soga, or Lusoga, is a Bantu language spoken in the Busoga region. The sources describe Lusoga language, literature, dialects, greetings, vocabulary, and cultural terminology.

## Music & performance

 The Soga language source does mention literature in Lusoga, including story books, but gives no account of musical performance.

## Festivals & rituals

 The language source records *kisoga* as describing religious tradition or culture common to the Soga people.

## Foodways

The Soga language source records the following Lusoga food-related vocabulary: *emmere* means “food”; *sukaali*, “sugar”; *kaawa*, “coffee”; *amata*, “milk”; and *enkoko*, “chicken”. Household terms include *kajiiko*, “teaspoon”; *sowaani*, “plate”; *sefuliya*, “saucepan”; and *kyikopo*, “cup”.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Soga language source states that the Lusoga Language Authority, abbreviated LULA, has researched the language and published literature in Lusoga. Its publications include a Lusoga Bible, grammar books, riddles, proverbs, story books, and dictionaries, including *Eibwanio English/Lusoga – Lusoga/English dictionary*. Publications can be found at the Busoga Cultural Centre Offices library in the Nile garden in Jinja, Uganda, and others are available in bookshops throughout Busoga and in major bookstores in Kampala and other parts of the country.

The same source records that *Kodheyo* was a Soga news publication in Uganda, printed between 1997 and 1998. Cornelius Wambi Gulere founded the paper and served as its editor-in-chief; it mainly reported on events in the Busoga sub-region. Published under Kodheyo Publications Limited, the paper folded in 1997.

## Language & religion

Soga, or Lusoga, is a Bantu language spoken by the Soga people in eastern Uganda. It has over three million speakers and is one of Uganda’s major languages after English, Swahili, and Luganda. Lusoga is tonal and is mainly spoken within Busoga, bounded by Lake Victoria to the south, Lake Kyoga to the north, the Nile river to the west, and the Mpologoma, or “Lion,” river to the east of Namutumba district.

Lusoga resembles neighbouring Luganda and Gwere because all three descend from Proto-North Nyanza. Its written form appeared after the arrival of Arab and European traders and missionaries, with printing beginning in the second half of the nineteenth century. Lusoga is used in some primary schools, taught in secondary schools, and offered at Busoga University, Kyambogo University, and Makerere University. The dialects include Lulamogi, Lupakoyo, and Lutenga. According to the 2002 Census of Uganda, 16.2% of Basoga were Roman Catholic, 46.2% Anglican, 30.6% Muslim, and 3.7% Pentecostal.
