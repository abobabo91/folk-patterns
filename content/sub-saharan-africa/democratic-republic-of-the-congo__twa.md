## Overview

The Twa are indigenous hunter-gatherer peoples of the Great Lakes Region in Central Africa, recognized as some of the earliest inhabitants of the area. In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Twa populations live in several environments, including the dense forests under the Ruwenzoris, the swamp forest around Lake Tumba, the forest–savanna swamps of Kasai, and the savanna swamps of Katanga. The island of Idjwi has a native population of approximately 7,000 Batwa, while the Lake Tumba area has about 14,000 Twa. The term Batwa covers different cultural groups, and many Twa in parts of the country call themselves Bambuti; the Congolese variant of the name is Cwa in Mongo, Kasai, and Katanga. The various Twa populations speak Bantu languages. Great Lakes Twa speak Rundi and Kiga, while other Congolese Twa speak varieties of Mongo, Bushong, Luba-Katanga, Hemba, Songe, or Taabwa.

## Foodways

The sources describe hunting, gathering, and exchange. The Twa spend part of the year in regions where they hunt game and trade for agricultural products with farmers. The Batwa of Uganda are described as having lived by gathering and hunting as their main source of food. The forest economy associated with African hunter-gatherer peoples includes trade in forest products such as meat and honey for agricultural and iron products. No further foodways of Twa communities in the Democratic Republic of the Congo are covered by the sources used.

## Language & religion

The various Twa populations speak Bantu languages. The Great Lakes Twa of Rwanda, Burundi, eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, and southern Uganda speak Rundi and Kiga. The Mongo Twa or Ntomba Twa of Lake Tumba and Lake Mai-Ndombe speak varieties of Mongo, including Konda, Ntomba, and Lia. The Kasai Twa or Kuba Twa speak Bushong; the Upemba Twa or Luba Twa speak Luba-Katanga, Hemba, Songe, and Taabwa; and the Mbote Twa speak a D20 language. The Bangweulu Twa speak Bemba, the Lukanga Twa speak Lenje, and the Kafwe Twa speak Tonga.
