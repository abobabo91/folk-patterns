## Overview

The Kinga live in Tanzania’s Kipengere Range. They farm millet, beans, bananas, wheat and potatoes, and keep cattle, sheep and goats. Their culture includes mountain settlements, skilled ironworking and bamboo beer production. Kinga is spoken mainly at home, while most people also know some Swahili.

## Material culture

Kinga material culture includes thorn-hedged homesteads, iron tools and weapons, and grass, bamboo and wood objects.

### Architecture

Kinga homesteads had cylindrical and conical huts around shared public spaces.

- **Common meeting space** — Kinga structures were built around a shared space for public meetings.
- **Cylindrical huts** — Kinga homesteads included cylindrical-shaped huts.
- **Conical huts** — Kinga homesteads included conical-shaped huts.
- **Thorn hedges** — Kinga homesteads were surrounded by hedges of thorn.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Kinga smiths made demanding iron tools and weapons, while basket makers produced beer containers and other objects.

- **Iron-tipped spear** — Kinga smiths made iron-tipped spears through a long process of heating and forging iron.
- **Hoe** — Kinga smiths made hoes from forged iron.
- **Knife** — Kinga smiths made knives from forged iron.
- **Iron axe** — A museum catalogue records an iron axe with a wooden handle.
- **Beer basket** — Grass-plaited baskets were used for beer, including close-weave, openwork and natural-colour forms.

## Festivals & rituals

Kinga religious life in the sources centers on priests, sacred water and yearly pilgrimages to Lwenbe’s shrine.

- **Priests** — Kinga priests claimed an ancient heritage older than their chiefs.
- **Water interpretation** — The priests seemed to interpret movement of water below the ground.
- **Pilgrimage to Lwenbe’s shrine** — Pilgrims descended the mountain paths each year toward Lwenbe’s shrine.

## Foodways

- **Millet** — The Kinga cultivate millet.
- **Beans** — The Kinga cultivate beans.
- **Bamboo beer** — The Kinga cultivate bamboo for a strong and pleasing beer.
- **Bananas** — The Kinga cultivate bananas.

## Language & religion

Kinga is a Bantu language used mainly at home, and Kinga religious accounts include priests and pilgrimages to Lwenbe’s shrine.

- **Kinga language** (*Kinga*) — Kinga is a Bantu language closely related to Magoma, but speakers have low mutual understanding with Magoma speakers.
- **Magoma** — Magoma is the language most closely related to Kinga.
- **Swahili** — Kinga speakers usually know Swahili but are not fluent.
- **Kinga priests** — Kinga priests claimed an ancient heritage older than their chiefs.
- **Lwenbe’s shrine** — Annual pilgrimages traveled toward Lwenbe’s shrine.
