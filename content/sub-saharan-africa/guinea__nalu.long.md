## Overview

The Nalu, also called Nalo, Nanum, or Nanu, are a West African ethnic group found in Guinea and Guinea-Bissau. They speak Nalu, an Atlantic language traditionally classified within Niger-Congo, Atlantic-Congo, and Mbulugish-Nalu, although Güldemann classifies it as unclassified within Niger-Congo. The Nalu settled in the region before the arrival of the Mandé or Mandinka peoples, a history that has led to their description as “pre-Mandingas.” Nalu speakers live predominantly along the littorals of Guinea and Guinea-Bissau. In Guinea, most live north of the Nuñez River, on the Tristão islands and in the sub-prefecture of Kanfarandé, in the prefecture of Boké. In Guinea-Bissau, most live in the Cacine estuary in the Tombali region. Estimates place the number of Nalu speakers between 10,000 and 25,000, while Wilson reports around 12,000.

## Language & religion

Nalu, also spelled Nalou and referred to as nalɛ or nul, is an Atlantic language of Guinea and Guinea-Bissau. It is spoken predominantly by adults and is considered endangered because its speaker population is dwindling. The language has six dialects: three spoken in Guinea-Conakry and three in Guinée-Bissau, although the relationship between the dialects is unknown. Most Nalu speakers in Guinea live north of the Nuñez River, on the Tristão islands and around Kanfarandé; in Guinea-Bissau, most live in the Cacine estuary in the Tombali region. Nalu speakers are shifting toward Susu in Guinea, while the language continues to be passed to the next generation in a few remote villages around Katoufoura. The sources do not describe Nalu religious beliefs or practices beyond identifying the Simo as a secret society active among the Nalu and related groups.
