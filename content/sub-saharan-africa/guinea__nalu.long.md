## Overview

The Nalu, also called Nalo, Nanum, or Nanu, are a West African ethnic group found in Guinea and Guinea-Bissau. They speak Nalu, an Atlantic language traditionally classified within Niger-Congo, Atlantic-Congo, and Mbulugish-Nalu, although Güldemann classifies it as unclassified within Niger-Congo. The Nalu settled in the region before the arrival of the Mandé or Mandinka peoples, a history that has led to their description as “pre-Mandingas.” Nalu speakers live predominantly along the littorals of Guinea and Guinea-Bissau. In Guinea, most live north of the Nuñez River, on the Tristão islands and in the sub-prefecture of Kanfarandé, in the prefecture of Boké. In Guinea-Bissau, most live in the Cacine estuary in the Tombali region. Estimates place the number of Nalu speakers between 10,000 and 25,000, while Wilson reports around 12,000.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources record a basket and a storage basket made from vegetable fibre, straw, and bamboo.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name crocodile imagery on a wooden mask: the mask is carved in the form of a crocodile with two long horns and has white painted decorations along the top of the snout.

## Music & performance

The sources identify the Simo as a West African secret society active among the Nalu and related groups. No Nalu musical instruments, song genres, musical scales, or performance occasions are named in the sources.

## Oral tradition & literature

Walter Rodney’s description places the Nalu alongside the Landuma, the Baga, and the Temne as peoples described as “pre-Mandingas,” but the sources provide no Nalu oral narrative connected with this history.

## Language & religion

Nalu, also spelled Nalou and referred to as nalɛ or nul, is an Atlantic language of Guinea and Guinea-Bissau. It is spoken predominantly by adults and is considered endangered because its speaker population is dwindling. The language has six dialects: three spoken in Guinea-Conakry and three in Guinée-Bissau, although the relationship between the dialects is unknown. Most Nalu speakers in Guinea live north of the Nuñez River, on the Tristão islands and around Kanfarandé; in Guinea-Bissau, most live in the Cacine estuary in the Tombali region. Nalu speakers are shifting toward Susu in Guinea, while the language continues to be passed to the next generation in a few remote villages around Katoufoura.
