## Overview

The Buduma are fishers and cattle-herders who live on many islands of Lake Chad. They prefer to be called Yedina, while neighboring peoples call them Buduma, meaning “people of the grass (or reeds).” Their material culture centers on water, reeds, fishing, cattle, and movement across the lake. They traditionally claim descent from the Sao civilization and Kanem-Bornu Empire.

## Material culture

Buduma material culture uses reeds, grass, fibre, wood, bone, iron, brass, pottery, and cotton for fishing, herding, transport, shelter, and household work.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not describe named Buduma textile patterns, but they record many reed, grass, fibre, and basketry objects.

- **Reed and grass basketry** — Reed and grass were used for strainers, milk vessels, baskets, coiled dishes, and mats.
- **Raffia tray** — A museum catalogue records a tray made of raffia.
- **Milk vessel and carrying sling** — Some milk vessels had grass containers, conical lids, and slings made from twisted and knotted fibre.
- **Marea board** (*Marea*) — A Marea board was used for pressing cotton seed.

### Clothing & dress

The sources record sandals and bracelets.

- **Ambatch sandals** — A pair of sandals was made from ambatch wood with straps of vegetal fibre.
- **Brass and iron bracelets** — The catalogue records bracelets made of brass and iron, without identifying their dress or ceremonial use.

### Architecture

Buduma use papyrus reeds to build light huts that can be moved when the lake rises.

- **Movable papyrus-reed huts** — Lightweight huts made from papyrus reeds can be moved to higher ground if the lake rises.
- **Reed passenger canoe** — A museum record describes a model passenger canoe made of reed.
- **Rush-bundle boat** — A model boat was made from two bundles of rushes tied with twisted cotton cord and bent upward at the tapered end.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded Buduma objects include pottery, tools, weapons, fishing gear, baskets, strainers, dishes, and mats.

- **Pottery water-pots** — The museum catalogue records pottery water-pots.
- **Pottery jug or mug** — A pottery jug or mug had a handle at the side.
- **Bifid iron spear** — An iron spear had a fork-like split point and a wooden shaft.
- **Iron hoe** — A socketed iron hoe had a wooden haft.
- **Bone fishing implement** — A fishing implement was made from a sickle-shaped bone.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources describe brass and iron bracelets but give no ritual or body-adornment traditions beyond them.

- **Penannular iron bracelet** — One iron bracelet was half-twisted and had coiled terminals.
- **Looped iron bracelet** — Another iron bracelet was twisted with three loops around its circumference.
- **Brass bracelets** — The catalogue also lists additional bracelets made of brass.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not name Buduma festivals or rituals, but they note that Islamic practice includes many older beliefs and practices.

- **Islamic practice with traditional beliefs** — The Buduma are Muslims and still incorporate many traditional beliefs and practices into their Islamic practices.

## Foodways

Buduma foodways center on fish, cow milk, water lily roots, and other regional foods, while cattle are rarely eaten.

- **Fish** — Fish is a staple food, caught for family use or for commercial fishing.
- **Cow milk** — Cow milk is a staple food.
- **Water lily root flour** — Water lily roots are ground into flour.
- **Cattle products** — The Buduma use or consume many cattle products but do not commonly kill and eat their cattle.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not describe Buduma stories or literary traditions, though the people claim descent from the Sao and Kanem-Bornu peoples.

- **Sao ancestry claim** (*Sao*) — The Buduma traditionally claim descent from the peoples of the Sao civilization.
- **Kanem-Bornu ancestry claim** (*Kanem-Bornu*) — The Buduma traditionally claim descent from the peoples of the Kanem-Bornu Empire.

## Language & religion

Yedina is the Buduma language, and the Buduma are Muslims who retain older beliefs and practices within Islam.

- **Yedina language** (*Yedina*) — Yedina is a Chadic language of the Biu–Mandara branch spoken around Lake Chad.
- **Buduma name** (*Buduma*) — Buduma is the name used by neighboring peoples and means “people of the grass (or reeds).”
- **Boudouma name** (*Boudouma*) — Boudouma is another name for the Yedina language.
- **Islam** — The Buduma were converted by Islamic missionaries during the era of French colonialism in Chad.

## Glossary

- *Yedina* — The name the Buduma prefer
- *Buduma* — Neighboring name meaning people of the grass or reeds
- *Boudouma* — Another name for the Yedina language
- *Marea* — A board used for pressing cotton seed
- *Sao* — A civilization linked to Buduma ancestry claims
- *Kanem-Bornu* — An empire linked to Buduma ancestry claims
