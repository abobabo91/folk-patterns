## Overview

The Jiji live in Kigoma Region, Tanzania. They were traditionally organized into the kingdom of Bujiji, also called Ujiji in Swahili. Bujiji was part of the wider Buha or Uha territory. Most Jiji people are Muslim, while a minority are Christian.

## Material culture

The profile records cotton and barkcloth, iron spears, red wooden containers, and a polished boar-tusk neck ornament.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources record cotton cloth with striped borders and a square of iron-red barkcloth.

- **Striped cotton cloth** — This oblong cotton cloth has broad side borders of narrow dark brown lines, narrower middle stripes, and loose warp ends.
- **Iron-red barkcloth** — This square of barkcloth is coloured iron red.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The recorded objects include two iron spears and small red wooden containers.

- **Socketed iron spear** — This spear has a well-made socketed iron head, iron-wire binding, a yellow-wood shaft, and a socketed iron point at the butt.

## Language & religion

The Jiji are described as a Bantu group, but the sources do not establish a separate recognized Jiji language.

- **Bujiji kingdom** (*Bujiji*) — The Jiji were traditionally organized into the separate kingdom of Bujiji, called Ujiji in Swahili.
- **Buha territory** (*Buha*) — Bujiji formed part of Buha, also called Uha or Ha territory, with several other kingdoms.
- **Jiji language entry** (*JIJ*) — The language article says Jiji was removed from Ethnologue in 2005 and lists it with the code JIJ among spurious and uncertain languages.
- **Islam** — Most Jiji people are Muslim.
- **Christianity** — A minority of Jiji people are Christian.
