## Overview

The Pokot live mainly in western Kenya and eastern Uganda. They speak Pökoot, a northern Kalenjin language. Their cultural record includes pastoral and agricultural life, age groups, cattle raiding, oral art, and distinctive clothing and adornment. Museum collections show a wide range of leather, bead, wood, gourd, basket, and metal objects.

## Material culture

Pokot material culture uses leather, skin, beads, cowrie shells, hair, feathers, gourds, wood, reeds, fibre, and metal.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not name Pokot textile traditions, but they record many bead-decorated garments and accessories.

- **Beaded loin cloth** — A leather loin cloth has rows of beads and cowrie shells, with beaded straps tied around the waist.
- **Beaded women’s belt** — A skin belt has a broad central band, tie thongs, cowrie shells, cotton thread, and rows of colored plastic beads.
- **Beaded women’s bag** — A rectangular cow-skin bag has hair attached, a fold-over top, and a strap decorated with cowrie shells and plastic beads.
- **Beaded head ornament** — Four strings of red, green, yellow, and black beads are twisted together and tied around the forehead.
- **Beaded collar** — Collars use several strings of colored plastic beads arranged in bands on metal wire or twisted vegetable fibre.

### Clothing & dress

Pokot clothing and dress accessories use leather, skin, beads, hair, feathers, and red ochre.

- **Man’s pubic cover** — A leather pubic cover is decorated with iron beads and coated with red ochre.
- **Red leather cap** — A red cap is made from folded strips of oxhide.
- **Feathered hair head-dress** — A felted human-hair head-dress is decorated with four large feathers.
- **War head-dress** — A tightly fitting head-dress of woven human hair has a crest of black and white ostrich plumes.
- **Hair helmet** — A wig-like helmet of matted human hair has side-flaps, a gourd pendant, and ostrich feathers in cane settings.

### Architecture

The supplied sources do not cover Pokot houses, roofs, settlements, or built decoration.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pokot everyday objects include pottery, gourds, wooden vessels, baskets, knives, bells, and a war-horn.

- **Handled pottery vessel** — A brownish pottery vessel has a rounded bottom, two handles, and black scorch marks.
- **Gourd milk container** — A long slender gourd container has a lid, skin strap, beads, and palm-stalk and palm-fibre repairs.
- **Wooden water bowl** — An elliptical wooden bowl has a rounded bottom, a wire repair, a blackened interior base, and a plaited leather loop.
- **Grain basket** — A composite reed basket with a leather handle is used for grain.
- **Iron finger-knife** — An iron finger-knife has a kidney-shaped or other blade, sometimes bound with copper wire.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Pokot adornment combines beads, shells, glass, bone, hardwood, feathers, hair, wire, and fibre.

- **Man’s ear ornament** — A man may wear several ear ornaments in the same ear.
- **Banana-seed necklace** — One necklace is made from banana seeds.
- **Bone armlet** — One recorded armlet is made from bone.
- **Young women’s collar** — A banded bead collar is described as worn by young married women or unmarried girls.
- **Beaded neck ornament** — A neck ornament uses strips of hardwood and white and blue glass beads strung on fibre thread.

## Music & performance

The sources give little information about music, but they record a side-blown war-horn and a strong verbal performance tradition.

- **War-horn** — A side-blown horn is made of wood covered with strips of hide.
- **Elder proverb speaking** — At gatherings of elders, speakers use proverbs to show skill in speaking and make a point.
- **Lökoi** — Lökoi is speech about news from other places.
- **Chiran** — Chiran is speech about events in the neighborhood.
- **Kokwö** — Kokwö is serious, business-like conversation.

## Dance & theatre

The supplied sources do not cover named Pokot dances, theatre, masked performance, shadow puppetry, or other dramatic traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe age groups, circumcision, and initiation, but no annual festival calendar.

- **Karachna** — Karachna is the social grouping for boys.
- **Muren** — Muren is the social grouping for circumcised men.
- **Poi** — Poi is the social grouping for old men.
- **Age-set joining** — Boys joined an age-set after circumcision.
- **Girls’ circumcision controversy** — In 2014, pictures of the circumcision of young Pokot girls caused public outrage abroad, and Kenya had legally banned the practice.

## Foodways

The sources do not name Pokot dishes or cooking traditions, but they record containers for milk, honey, sheep fat, and grain.

- **Milk gourd** — A long gourd container is used to store milk.
- **Honey and sheep-fat container** — A hollowed wooden container with a leather bottom and lid is used for sheep fat or honey, especially honey.
- **Grain basket** — A composite basket is used for grain.
- **Cattle bell** — A cattle-bell is used on a bull.

## Oral tradition & literature

Pokot verbal art uses proverbs, riddles, and tales to teach, argue, entertain, and show speaking skill.

- **Moral proverbs** — Proverbs teach younger people the consequences of leaving the moral path.
- **Louwialan clan tale** (*Louwialan*) — A popular tale about the Louwialan clan warns against pride.
- **Blind girl tale** — Another common tale concerns a blind girl who returns from death.
- **Riddles** (*Tyangoi*) — Tyangoi riddles sharpen children’s wits and hold their attention during storytelling.
- **Old-times talk** (*Ng'öliontoköny*) — Ng'öliontoköny is talk about olden times.

## Language & religion

Pökoot is a northern Kalenjin language spoken in western Kenya and eastern Uganda, while the supplied sources give no detailed account of religion.

- **Pökoot language** (*Pökoot*) — Pökoot, also called Pokot, Päkot, Pökot, or Suk in older literature, is spoken in western Kenya and eastern Uganda.
- **Kimukon** — Other Kalenjin peoples usually call the Pökoot Kimukon.
- **Kalenjin language family** — Pökoot belongs to the northern branch of the Kalenjin languages found in Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania.
- **Neighboring language area** — The language area borders Karimojong, Turkana, Maa languages, Tugen, and Markweta.
