## Overview

The Loko are an indigenous ethnic group in Sierra Leone. *Landogo* is used as an endonym for the people and language, while other groups refer to them as Loko. Most Loko live in Sierra Leone’s Northern Province, particularly in Bombali District, and around Freetown, including communities such as Regent. Regional towns associated with Loko settlement include Tambiama, Kalangba, Kagbere, Batkanu, and Gbendembu, although Mandingo, Fula, and Temne communities also live in these places. The Loko belong to the larger group of Mande peoples of West Africa. They are mostly farmers and hunters, and their social and spiritual life includes secret societies such as Kpangbani and Bondo.

## Language & religion

Loko, or *Landogo*, is a Southwestern Mande language spoken by the Loko people, who primarily live in Northern Sierra Leone. The sources identify two mutually intelligible dialects, Landogo and Logo. Ethnic Loko outnumber native Loko speakers because of the linguistic encroachment of Temne and Krio and because of urbanization to Freetown. In Freetown, Loko is described as having low prestige both internally and externally.

The Loko belong to the wider Mande peoples of West Africa. Most Loko are Muslim; the sources connect their conversion with the growing influence of Muslim merchants during the British colonial era. Loko beliefs also give secret societies an important role, stating that most humanistic and scientific power is passed down through societies such as Kpangbani. The Bondo secret society concerns adulthood in girls, fertility, morality, proper sexual comportment, and the well-being of members throughout their lives.
