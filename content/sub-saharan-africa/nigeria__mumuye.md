## Overview

The Mumuye live mainly in Taraba State and nearby Adamawa State in northeastern Nigeria. Their communities are divided into small independent groups that speak related dialects and share traditions. Villages are governed by elders and an elected leader. Important cultural features include Iagalagana figures, masquerades, scarification, rainmaking and the Vabong secret society.

## Material culture

Mumuye material culture includes carved figures, masks, wooden clubs, hide aprons, textiles, rattles and body ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The named textile tradition is a handspun cotton cloth called langtang.

- **Handspun cotton textile** (*langtang*) — This cloth has striped borders, a loosely woven center and long twisted cotton tassels at both ends.

### Clothing & dress

The sources name hide aprons and a handspun cotton textile but do not describe complete everyday or ceremonial dress.

- **Hide apron** — Men’s aprons were made of hide, including aprons with fringe.
- **Crested hat and bead necklace** — A wooden male figure is shown wearing a crested hat and a necklace of beads.

### Architecture

Mumuye people live in small villages of extended families, while Iagalagana figures are kept in a separate hut.

- **Village group** (*dola*) — A dola is a social group of extended families living in a small village.
- **Figure hut** — Iagalagana figures are kept in a separate hut by someone thought to have magical powers.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Named objects include wooden clubs, rattles, rods and ornaments, while the sources do not document pottery.

- **Wooden club** — Clubs were made of wood with leather or vegetable fibre, and some had elaborate or curved heads.
- **Leg rattle** — This rattle has four sticks with gourds joined by string or fur and a bast cord for tying it to the leg.
- **Copper-bound rods** — Two wooden rods were bound with copper, iron and aluminium wire.
- **Boar’s tusk ornament** — A boar’s tusk was cased in leather and made into an ornament.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Mumuye adornment includes tusk and fibre ornaments, bead necklaces, ear discs and scarification.

- **Boar’s tusk ornament** — The tusk has a leather casing and an attachment loop.
- **Wire-bound ornament** — Men wore an ornament made from wooden rods bound with copper, iron and aluminium wire.
- **Braided fibre necklace** — The museum catalogue records a necklace made from braided fibre.
- **Ear discs** — Large ear lobes are linked to the tradition of inserting discs into the ears.
- **Facial scarification** — Some scarification consists of three horizontal rows of cuts dividing the face into thirds, and women undergo scarification before marriage.

## Music & performance

Mumuye performance is documented mainly through masquerades, ceremonies and ritual masks rather than songs or instruments.

- **Funeral masquerade** (*Vabon*) — The Vabon is a funeral masquerade that involves the whole community.
- **Twice-yearly ceremony** (*Ushavuko*) — The Ushavuko ceremony takes place twice a year.
- **Secret society initiation** (*Vabong*) — Initiation into the Vabong includes explanations of masks and other objects used for divination.
- **Rainmaking mask** (*Sukwava*) — Sukwava masks were worn before war and are now used for healing and rainmaking.
- **Rainmaking mask** (*Sukuru*) — Sukuru masks may be worn during rainmaking rites.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not name Mumuye dances or dramatic genres, but they describe ritual masquerade performance.

- **Funeral masquerade** (*Vabon*) — The Vabon funeral masquerade encompasses the whole community.
- **Long-necked mask** (*Sukwava*) — The Sukwava mask has a long neck and large ears.
- **Initiation face mask** — This face mask has hollowed-out eyes, animal-like features and a skeletal appearance.

## Festivals & rituals

Mumuye ritual life includes scarification, secret-society initiation, divination, healing and rainmaking.

- **Twice-yearly ceremony** (*Ushavuko*) — The Ushavuko occurs twice a year.
- **Funeral masquerade** (*Vabon*) — The Vabon is a funeral masquerade involving the whole community.
- **Secret society** (*Vabong*) — The Vabong has seven levels, and initiation can involve intense beating and instruction about divination objects.
- **Rainmaking rods** — Z-shaped rods are linked with lightning, snakes, fertility of the land and fertility of the people.
- **Divination figure** (*Iagalagana*) — The Iagalagana is used for divination, good luck, protection from evil and rainmaking.

## Language & religion

Mumuye spiritual and social life centers on the Vabong society, village leadership, the land and Iagalagana figures.

- **Mumuye language** (*Mumuye*) — Mumuye belongs to the Leko–Nimbari branch of Savanna languages and has many dialects.
- **Secret society** (*Vabong*) — The Vabong governs spiritual life and has seven levels.
- **Village group** (*dola*) — Mumuye communities are divided into village groups called dola.
- **Divination figure** (*Iagalagana*) — Iagalagana figures are used for divination, good luck, protection from evil and rainmaking.
- **Rainmakers** — Rainmakers protect the community and are considered powerful in helping it thrive.
