## Overview

The Rendille, also known as Rendile, Randile, Rendili, Reendile, Randali, Reerdiid, and Randille, are Somali people inhabiting the Eastern Province of Kenya. They live in the climatically harsh regions between Marsabit County and Lake Turkana and are concentrated in the Kaisut Desert and Mount Marsabit in northern Kenya’s Eastern Province. According to Ethnologue, there were approximately 94,700 Rendille speakers in 2006. Traditionally nomadic pastoralists, they tend camels, sheep, goats, and cattle; camels are generally kept in the northern parts of their territory and cattle in the southern section. Their language belongs to the Cushitic branch of the Afroasiatic family.

## Language & religion

Rendille, also known as Rendile or Randile, is an Afro-Asiatic language belonging to the Cushitic branch. The Rendille speak it as a mother tongue; some use English or Swahili as working languages for communication with other populations. The Ariaal sub-group, described as of mixed Nilotic and Cushitic descent, speaks the Eastern Nilotic Samburu language. The Rendille alphabet has 26 letters; long vowels are written doubled, and tone is phonemic. In religion, traditional practices center on the worship of Waaq, described as a monotheistic archaic pre-Abrahamic religion adhered to by Cushitic groups. More recently, Christianity has spread through missionary work, and an increasing number of Rendille practice Islam.
