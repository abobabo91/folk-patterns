## Overview

The Diola, also called Jola, are an ethnic group associated with the Casamance region of southern Senegal. The supplied sources describe the region as extending into neighboring countries and identify the group with the Jola languages. The language is spoken by 475,000 people in the Casamance region of Senegal and neighboring countries. The sources also use the names Joola, Jola-Fonyi, Diola-Fogny, and Kujamataak. Their documented folk-culture profile in the supplied material includes a rice-based dish, a range of hunting, fishing, storage, cooking, and domestic objects, and a photograph identified as showing a woman and child of the Diola people.

## Foodways

Thiebou Diola, also spelled Ceebu Diola and described as “C'est Bon,” is a traditional rice-based dish associated with the Diola ethnic group in the Casamance region of southern Senegal. The source characterizes it as a lesser-known variant of Thieboudienne, with ingredients and flavours specific to the Diola culinary tradition. ## Language & religion

Jola, also called Diola, Joola, Jola-Fonyi, Diola-Fogny, and Kujamataak, is spoken in the Casamance region of Senegal and neighboring countries. Jola-Fonyi is identified as one of several closely related Jola languages spoken in the area. The source records phonemic vowel length and describes an official orthography created in Senegal by decree No. 2005-981. Long vowels are represented by doubling the letter, as in aa, ee, ii, oo, and uu. An acute accent over a vowel indicates the advanced and retracted tongue root for that vowel and the other vowels of the words by vowel harmony. The supplied sources do not describe Diola religious beliefs, institutions, spiritual practices, or the relationship between religion and folk culture.
