## Overview

The Mandja, also known as Mandjia, Mandija, or Manja, are an ethnic group found in the central region of the Central African Republic. They number approximately 250,000 and are related to the Gbaya people. Their language is called Manza, Mānzā, or Mandja and belongs to the Ubangian language group. It is spoken by the Mandja people of the Central African Republic and is closely related to Ngbaka; the languages may be to some extent mutually intelligible. ## Language & religion

Manza, also written Mānzā or Mandja, is a Ubangian language spoken by the Mandja people of the Central African Republic. It is closely related to Ngbaka and may be to some extent mutually intelligible with it. The supplied description of its phonology notes that the sounds /ɾ/ and /ⱱ/ are very rare in word-initial position; /ⁿz/ can occur in free variation as the prenasal affricate [ⁿd͡ʒ]; [l] occurs only in free variation of /j/; and /j/ can be heard as [ɲ] before a nasal vowel. The vowel /a/ can have the allophone [ɐ] in complementary distribution, while the nasalization of /ɛ̃/ may also be heard lower as [æ̃] in free variation.
