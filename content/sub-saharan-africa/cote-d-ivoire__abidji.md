## Overview

The Abidji are an Akan people who live in the Ivory Coast. Their language, Abidji, is also known as Abiji and Ambidji. It has an uncertain classification within the Kwa branch of the Niger–Congo family and is spoken in Ivory Coast. Abidji has two dialects, *enyembe* and *ogbru*. Members of these Abidji-speaking ethnic groups use these names to refer to themselves, while the name Ambidji was given to the language by neighboring groups. ## Language & religion

Abidji is a language of uncertain classification within the Kwa branch of the Niger–Congo family. It is spoken in Ivory Coast and has two dialects, *enyembe* and *ogbru*. Abidji is written with a Latin alphabet using the graphemes of the practical writing for the languages of the Ivory Coast. The letter upsilon ⟨Ʊ, ʊ⟩ is often replaced with the V with hook ⟨Ʋ, ʋ⟩. Nasal vowels are written with ⟨n⟩, or with ⟨m⟩ before ⟨p⟩ and ⟨b⟩. The language has nine vowels, contrastive nasal forms for all vowels except /e/, vocal harmony, open syllables apart from the first syllable of *darsɛ*, and two phonemic tones, high and low. Its grammar includes four verb groups distinguished by tonal and syllabic arrangements.
