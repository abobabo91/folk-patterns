## Overview

The Efik live around the lower Cross River in southern Nigeria and nearby western Cameroon. Their culture is known through trade, migration, Old Calabar history, brass objects, songs, proverbs, and oral literature. Religious life included Abasi, Ndem spirits, ancestral rites, cleansing ceremonies, and the Ekpe society. The sources give limited information about ordinary clothing, houses, daily food, and jewelry.

## Material culture

Efik material culture is especially known for engraved brass trays, dishes, and basins, along with raffia robes, decorated houses, sacred objects, and musical instruments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Named textile evidence is limited, but the sources mention a woven raffia robe and geometric house designs.

- **Woven raffia robe** (*Ikpaya*) — Ikpaya was a woven raffia robe linked with royal emblems associated with Uruan.
- **Geometric house designs** — Efik women decorated houses with geometric designs.
- **Engraved brass plates** (*Akpangkpang*) — Akpangkpang were brass plates with engravings connected with Efik beliefs in Ndem and Ekpe.

### Clothing & dress

The sources describe little ordinary clothing but record a loincloth used during libation and elite burial objects.

- **Libation loincloth** — The oldest man conducting libation wore a loincloth wrapped around the hips and flowing to the ground, with a shirt above it.
- **Royal raffia robe** (*Ikpaya*) — Ikpaya was a woven raffia robe used as a royal emblem.
- **Burial crowns and umbrellas** — Crowns, umbrellas, swords, and ornaments were placed with nobles and kings at burial.

### Architecture

The sources give few details about Efik buildings but mention decorated houses, shrines, and the Iron Palace.

- **Abasi shrine hut** (*Isu Abasi*) — Isu Abasi was a little mud hut with an altar in the middle of the yard where plates and bones were placed.
- **Iron Palace** — The Iron Palace of Eyamba V was destroyed during the reign of King Archibong II.
- **Decorated houses** — Efik women used geometric designs to decorate houses.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Brass work is a major feature of the material culture described in the sources.

- **Brass trays, dishes, and basins** — Efik women transferred designs from fragile calabashes to brass objects such as trays, dishes, and basins.
- **Feather-design circular tray** — One brass tray had punched feather designs, dotted squares, and four central figures with ornate headdresses.
- **Wooden thumb piano** (*Mbira*) — The Metropolitan Museum catalogue records a late-19th-century Mbira made from kapok wood.
- **Wooden and rattan instrument** (*Zanze*) — The late-19th-century Zanze was made from kapok wood and rattan.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources do not document named Efik jewelry or detailed body-adornment practices.

- **Crowns** — Crowns were among the personal objects associated with nobles and kings at burial.
- **Burial ornaments** — Ornaments and other personal objects were placed with nobles and kings at burial.

## Music & performance

Songs, bardic poetry, and special instruments preserved Efik history, religion, myths, and legends.

- **Religious songs** — Religious songs praised different Ndem and named figures such as Anansa, Afia Añwan, and Atakpọr Uruan.
- **Bardic poetry** (*Uto*) — Uto was Efik bardic poetry recited by knowledgeable bards in the Efik language.
- **Leopard-roar instrument** (*mbɔkɔ*) — The mbɔkɔ made a sound like a leopard’s roar and was central to legends about Ekpe.
- **Ekpe procession names** — Ekpe processions used names including Eyo Ema, Esien Ekpe, Asibong Ekondo, Mutaka, and Nkok Ofuta.

## Dance & theatre

Efik performance included moonlight plays, secret dances, and a midnight postwar dance.

- **Moonlight plays** (*Mbre Ọffiọñ*) — Mbre Ọffiọñ were Efik moonlight plays, and myths were first narrated under moonlight.
- **Women’s cleansing dance** (*Ekpa*) — Ekpa was a private night dance performed nude by elderly women before the Obong’s coronation.
- **Postwar midnight dance** (*Ankọ Ebekpa*) — Ankọ Ebekpa included a dance at midnight after war while non-members stayed indoors.

## Festivals & rituals

Efik ritual life included purgation festivals, war cleansing, coronation dances, offerings, and historic funeral rites.

- **Purgation festival** (*Ndọk*) — Ndọk was a biennial festival around December that drove evil spirits from Efik country.
- **Doorway effigies** (*Nabikim*) — Nabikim were rough figures made from sticks, grass, bamboo matting, and cloth, then thrown into the river after Ndọk.
- **Good Friday effigy rite** (*mbre judas*) — During mbre judas, young people beat Nabikim figures with sticks and shouted that Judas killed Christ.
- **War-cleansing ritual** (*Ankọ Ebekpa*) — Ankọ Ebekpa cleansed pollution caused by bloodshed and war medicines.
- **Libation and offerings** — Libation and offerings were used at weddings, naming ceremonies, coming-of-age ceremonies, welcomes, send-offs, and other social events.

## Foodways

The sources mainly describe foods and drinks used in offerings and sacrifice, not everyday Efik meals.

- **Offering basket** (*Ekete*) — Offerings to Ndem were placed in an Ekete woven basket.
- **Sacrificial yam meal** (*Iwewe*) — Iwewe was boiled yam mashed with a little palm oil and stirred with a spoon.
- **Palm wine** (*Mmịn Efịk*) — Mmịn Efịk was the palm wine formerly used for libation.
- **White animal offerings** — Offerings could include white fowl, white goats, cows, and tortoises, with white animals especially valued.

## Oral tradition & literature

Efik oral literature preserves myths, history, folktales, riddles, proverbs, and commemorative poetry.

- **Myths, legends, and history stories** (*Mbụk*) — Mbụk is the Efik term for myths, legends, and historical stories.
- **Epic poetry** (*Uto*) — Uto denotes Efik bardic or epic poetry.
- **Commemorative poetry** (*Ase*) — Ase is a form of commemorative poetry.
- **Folktales and proverbs** (*Nke*) — Nke includes folktales, riddles, and proverbs.
- **Akpa Uyok story** — One folktale tells of Akpa Uyok, an Efik chief who lived so long that people tried to abandon him in a canoe.

## Language & religion

Efik is a Lower Cross language, and traditional religion centers on Abasi, ancestors, Ndem, cleansing rites, and Ekpe.

- **Supreme deity** (*Abasi Ibom*) — Abasi or Abasi Ibom is part of traditional Efik religious belief.
- **Water spirits** (*Ndem*) — Ndem are water spirits or deities, and the singular form is Idem.
- **Secret society** (*Ekpe*) — The Ekpe society maintained law and order and was linked with esoteric rituals.
- **Efik language** — Efik is spoken in Cross River State, Akwa Ibom State, and north-western Cameroon and is mutually intelligible with several Lower Cross languages.
- **First written Efik** — Chief Eyo Nsa, also known as Willy Eyo Honesty, first put Efik into writing in 1812.
