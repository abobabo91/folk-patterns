## Overview

The Kapsiki, also called Ka-Tsepkye, are a people living on both sides of the border between North Cameroon and Northeast Nigeria. They are called Kapsiki in Cameroon and Kamwe, or Higi, in Nigeria, and together amount to about 120,000 people. In Cameroon they live on a plateau in the Far North Province, in the center of the Mandara Mountains; in Nigeria they live on mountain slopes and the western plain. They have lived in the area for five centuries and have maintained an isolated cultural setting. Their language, Psekiye or Kamwe, belongs to the Chadic language family. The Kapsiki are associated with village life, livestock farming, agriculture, and, particularly in Rumsiki, tourism. Their folk-cultural significance in the supplied sources is chiefly visible in their settlement history, language diversity, clothing objects, and resistance to Islamisation.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Beaded pubic aprons** — The supplied British Museum catalogue records describe pubic aprons made from multicoloured glass beads arranged in rows, pairs, or lozenge patterns on cotton string. The objects have cotton-thread waistbands and ties; some include larger plastic or glass beads, cowrie shells, cut seeds, nut shells, or large white glass beads suspended from the lower edge.

**Lozenge-patterned apron** — One catalogue record describes an apron made predominantly from red, yellow, green, and blue glass beads arranged in pairs to form lozenge patterns. Its lower edge has larger plastic and glass beads suspended on cotton-thread strings terminating in cowrie shells.

**Horned-quadruped apron** — Another recorded apron uses predominantly black, brown, yellow, white, and green glass beads arranged in rows. Its central design is a horned quadruped, with larger uncertain plastic or glass beads suspended from the lower edge.

**Bird-and-plant apron** — A further apron has multicoloured glass beads arranged in pairs in rows and a central blue, black, red, and yellow design of a bird in front of a tree or plant. The lower edge has larger plastic beads suspended on twisted cotton-thread strings terminating in cut seeds or nut shells.

**Triangular beaded apron** — One catalogue record identifies a triangular apron made from multicoloured glass beads arranged in lozenge patterns on cotton string. Cotton strings terminating in glass beads run around two sides.

**Motif vocabulary.** Lozenge patterns; horned quadruped; bird in front of a tree or plant.

## Language & religion

Psekiye or Kamwe is a Chadic language with eleven dialects, including Nkafa, Sina, Ghye, Humsi, Dakwa, and Tilli. The language is also called Psikye, Psikya, or Kapsiki; varieties include Psikyɛ and Zləngə. In Cameroon, Psikya speakers use Margi for their own language and its three varieties, while in Nigeria it is called Higi. Psikyá is spoken in parts of Mokolo and Mogodé, including Roumzou, Mogode, and Roumsiki; Zléŋé and Wula are spoken in two neighborhoods of Oula. A Psikyɛ spelling developed by the Biblical Alliance of Cameroon uses additional letters and appears in the 1988 Bible translation *Ghena ta Shala*. In Cameroon, the Kapsiki are described as a Kirdi, or pagan, ethnic group because of their resistance to Islamisation during the Fulani jihad of Modibo Adama and Hama Yaji.
