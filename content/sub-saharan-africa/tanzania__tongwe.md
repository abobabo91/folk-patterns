## Overview

The Tongwe live in western Tanzania, along the eastern shore of Lake Tanganyika. Their population was estimated at 31,551 in 2000. Tongwe and Bende are related Bantu languages in Zone F.10. The supplied sources give little information about Tongwe folk culture beyond a few museum objects and language details.

## Material culture

The documented material culture includes carved wooden ladles with burned black stains and iron adzes with wooden handles.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Museum records describe carved wooden ladles and iron adzes.

- **Carved wooden ladle** — The ladles have incised carving on their handles, with black stains in some parts made by burning.
- **Iron adze** — The adze has an iron blade and a wooden handle.

## Language & religion

Tongwe and Bende are related Bantu languages classified in Zone F.10, while Tongwe religion is not documented.

- **Tongwe language** (*Sitongwe*) — Tongwe, also called Sitongwe, belongs to a Bantu language clade that includes Bende.
- **Bende language** (*Sibende*) — Bende, also called Sibende, is closely related to Tongwe and may be a dialect of the same language at 90% lexical similarity.

## Glossary

- *Sitongwe* — Another name for Tongwe
- *Sibende* — Another name for Bende
