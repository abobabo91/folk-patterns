## Overview

The Tama are an ethnic group living in eastern Chad and western Sudan, especially in Dar Tama in northeastern Chad and in Darfur, Sudan. They number 793,000 and speak Tama, a Nilo-Saharan language; Tama is also described as a Taman language belonging to the Eastern Sudanic branch of the Nilo-Saharan language family. Their traditional home is Dar Tama, while the Gimr and Mileri live near Saref Omra and Kebkabiya in Sudan. Many Tama are subsistence farmers and traders living in permanent settlements, and some raise livestock. The sources describe Tama society through farming, livestock raising, Islam, sultans, and conflicts with the Zaghawa.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources state that many Tama live in permanent settlements.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources identify Islam as the religion practiced by the Tama and state that the majority are Muslims, while also noting some animistic beliefs.

## Foodways

Many Tama are subsistence farmers who raise millet, beans, cucumbers, gumbo, and sesame. They also raise cattle, camels, and goats.

## Language & religion

Tama, or Tamok, is the primary language spoken by the Tama in Ouaddai, eastern Chad, and in Darfur, western Sudan. It is a Taman language belonging to the Eastern Sudanic branch of the Nilo-Saharan language family. Tama is spoken by 63,000 people in Dar Tama, a well-irrigated area near Guéréda extending from Kebkebiya village to nearby Sudan. The language has two nearly identical dialects: one spoken in the northern and central areas and another spoken in the south. Miisiirii is often considered a dialect, although the source states that it is not particularly close. The majority of Tama are Muslims, but they also have some animistic beliefs.
