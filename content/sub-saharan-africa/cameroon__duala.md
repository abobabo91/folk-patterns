## Overview

The Duala live mainly along the coast and just inland in Cameroon. Their traditional capital is Douala, near the Wouri estuary. They became important coastal traders by the 16th century. Trade, education, Christianity, city work, and property ownership have shaped their influence.

## Material culture

The supplied records show carved wood as the main form of Duala material culture, especially decorated stools and a small keyed instrument.

### Textile & pattern traditions

- **Carved wooden stools** — Stools have crescent-shaped seats, open-work bases, backrests, and carved figures, animals, or patterns.
- **Leopard motif** — Leopards appear on some stools, along with circles of burnt decoration.
- **Serpent motif** — One stool has two S-shaped serpents supporting its open-work base.

### Architecture

- **Douala property** — Many Duala live in and around Douala, and some own city property that provides rent and development income.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The supplied records mainly show carved wooden objects and one small instrument with iron keys.

- **Prestige seat** — The Metropolitan Museum records a wooden prestige seat from the mid-to-late 19th century.
- **Keyed plate instrument** (*sansatyp*) — A small instrument has a wooden plate, iron keys, and an ornamented gourd attached by a string.
- **Inscribed stool** — One stool has a bird, two leopards, and the inscription “KAMEFUN.”
- **Pigmented stool** — A pale carved wooden stool has pigment and letters on its seat.

## Music & performance

Duala performance traditions include coded drum messages, a small keyed instrument, and musical pirogue racing.

- **Drum language** — Until at least the German period, Duala men tapped coded messages on drums to send news over long distances.
- **Keyed plate instrument** (*sansatyp*) — A small instrument uses iron keys on a wooden plate and a gourd as its resonating bottom.
- **Pirogue racing** — Racing combines music, display, competition, and divination.

## Dance & theatre

- **Annual festivals** — Traditional annual festivals are described as visible expressions of pre-Christian beliefs.
- **Secret societies** (*Ekongolo, Jengu, Losango, Munji*) — The sources mention these secret societies.

## Festivals & rituals

Duala religious life combines Christianity with ancestor worship and belief in sea spirits.

- **Miengu water spirits** (*Miengu*) — These demi-human spirits live in the water and mediate between worshippers and God.
- **Jengu** — Jengu is the singular form of Miengu.
- **Ngondo** — Ngondo is an assembly of important chiefs, but its ceremonies are not described.
- **Pirogue race divination** — In the past, diviners read race results to predict the future, while a Christian priest presides today.
- **Jebale Island spirit help** — Until the late 1930s, a family on Jebale Island was said to summon Miengu to help favoured racers.

## Foodways

Fishing is the main rural trade.

- **Fishing** — Rural Duala mainly work as fishermen and farmers, usually at subsistence level.
- **Successful fishing** — A museum photograph shows people from Duala after a successful fishing trip.

## Oral tradition & literature

Duala history is preserved through stories about migration and descent.

- **Mbedi ancestry** (*Mbedi*) — The Duala trace their ancestry to Mbedi, who lived in Bakota in what is now Gabon or the Republic of the Congo.
- **Ewale and Dibongo migration** (*Ewale, Dibongo*) — Mbedi’s sons moved north to Pitti, then separated after a quarrel and led different migrations.
- **Duala and Limba origins** — Ewale’s followers became the Duala, while Dibongo’s followers became the Limba.
- **Written Duala** — Early missionaries developed a written form of Duala partly to translate the Bible.

## Language & religion

Duala is a tonal Bantu language shaped by trade, missionary work, and changing colonial school policies.

- **Duala language cluster** (*Duala, Bodiman, Oli, Ewodi, Wuri, Pongo, Mongo*) — The cluster includes several related speech forms used by the Duala and Mungo peoples.
- **Jo pidgin** (*Jo*) — Maho records a Duala-based pidgin.
- **Duala Bible** — Alfred Saker completed the first Bible translation into Duala in 1870.
- **Trade language** — Duala spread as a trade language among neighbouring Wovea and Isubu communities.
- **Christianity and older beliefs** (*Miengu, Jengu*) — Most Duala have been Christian since the 1930s, while ancestor worship and belief in water spirits remain documented.

## Glossary

- *Duala* — The people and their language
- *Miengu* — Demi-human water spirits
- *Jengu* — Singular form of Miengu
- *Ngondo* — Assembly of important chiefs
- *Ekongolo* — A secret society
- *Losango* — A secret society
- *Munji* — A secret society
- *Jo* — Duala-based pidgin
- *Mbedi* — Ancestor in Duala origin traditions
- *Ewale* — Mbedi’s son and Duala ancestor
- *Dibongo* — Mbedi’s son and Limba ancestor
- *Bakota* — Area where Mbedi lived
- *Pitti* — Place where Ewale and Dibongo separated
- *sansatyp* — Type of small keyed instrument
- *Wouri* — River at the centre of Duala country
- *Mungo* — River and related people
