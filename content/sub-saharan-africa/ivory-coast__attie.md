## Overview

The Attié, also spelled Akyé, Akié, Atié, or Atyé, are an Akan ethnic group native to southeastern Côte d’Ivoire. They primarily inhabit Adzopé, Akoupé, Alépé, Yakassé-Attobrou, and the northern outskirts of Abidjan, particularly around Anyama. The Attié language belongs to the Kwa branch of the Niger-Congo language family and is spoken by perhaps half a million people in Ivory Coast. According to Attié oral tradition, their ancestors originated in present-day Ghana and migrated westward during the period of Akan migrations. Their folk-culture profile includes age-grade social organization, traditional festivals, music, wood carving, agriculture, and customary community institutions.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention iron tools, crafted goods, and wood carving.

## Music & performance

Music is identified as part of Attié cultural identity, and traditional festivals are mentioned as an important cultural practice.

## Festivals & rituals

The Attié maintain traditional festivals as part of their distinct cultural identity. Chiefs, lineage elders, and ritual authorities traditionally oversaw important ceremonies, managed communal lands, and regulated disputes. Villages shared religious traditions with other Akan groups and sometimes formed temporary alliances for mutual defense, trade, or resolving regional disputes.

## Foodways

Agriculture remains the foundation of the Attié economy. Principal crops include cocoa, coffee, cassava, plantains, and yams. Before European colonization, exchanges of agricultural products and forest resources connected Attié villages to regional trade networks extending toward the coast. Colonial administration encouraged the cultivation of cocoa and coffee as export crops, changing local agricultural practices and integrating Attié communities into the colonial economy.

## Oral tradition & literature

Attié early history is preserved through oral traditions because written records from the pre-colonial period are limited. According to Akyé traditions, the ancestors of the Attié originated in present-day Ghana before migrating westward into Ivory Coast. These traditions connect Attié history with wider Akan migrations and with related Akan groups such as Agni and Boulé.

## Language & religion

Attié, also known as Akie, Akye, Atche, Atie, or Atshe, is a language of uncertain classification within the Kwa branch of the Niger-Congo family. It is spoken in Ivory Coast by perhaps half a million people. The writing system indicates nasalisation by a vowel followed by `<n>`, while tones are marked with a diacritic before or after the syllable. Traditional religious beliefs continue to influence aspects of cultural life. The majority of Attié today identify as Christians, with smaller communities practicing Islam or indigenous religions.
