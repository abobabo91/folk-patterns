## Overview

The Rapa Nui are the easternmost Polynesian culture. Most island residents live in Hanga Roa, and many Rapa Nui also live in mainland Chile. The culture is known for moai, carving, tattooing, music, food, and oral traditions. Rapa Nui remains important, but Spanish is now widely spoken and most children grow up speaking Spanish.

## Material culture

Rapa Nui material culture includes stone and wood carving, feather headdresses, bark cloth, tattoo tools, dance paddles, and large stone platforms for moai.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Rapa Nui arts include feather headdresses, bark cloth, plaited bags, and carvings in wood and stone.

- **Plait bag** (*Flätad påse*) — A museum record identifies this Easter Island bag as a plaited object made from Scirpus ripar var. paschalis.
- **Wood carvings** — Wood carvings were completed with adzes described as blunt round stones.
- **Stone carvings** — Museum records document stone sculptures from Easter Island.
- **Human figure** (*moai*) — The moai motif shows human figures.
- **Lizardman figure** (*Moko*) — Moko is a lizardman figure motif.

### Clothing & dress

The sources give little information about clothing but describe feather headdresses and traditional tattooing.

- **Feather headdress** — Feather headdresses were historically made by the Rapa Nui.
- **Tattoo comb** (*Uhi*) — Uhi were combs made from bird or fish bones and used in tattooing.
- **Ti leaf ink** (*Ti*) — Ti leaves were burned to help make tattoo ink.

### Architecture

Rapa Nui architecture includes stone houses and large coastal platforms that held moai.

- **Stone house** — Museum records identify houses of stone on Easter Island.
- **Moai platform** (*ahu*) — Ahu are large stone platforms on which moai rested.
- **Ahu Tongariki** — Ahu Tongariki is described as the largest ahu.
- **Ahu Vinapu** — Ahu Vinapu is another named moai platform.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources do not describe ceramics or metalwork, but they record stone tools, paddles, bags, gorgets, and carved figures.

- **Axe** (*toki*) — Toki means axe in a Mapuche/Rapa Nui cognate.
- **Dance paddle** (*Rapa*) — Rapa are wooden dance paddles recorded in museum collections.
- **Gorget** (*rei miro*) — Wooden rei miro gorgets are recorded in museum collections.
- **Stone adze** — Adzes made from blunt round stones were used to complete stone images and wood carvings.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Tattoos were spiritual signs of strength and social class, while body painting and wooden gorgets were also recorded.

- **Spiritual power** (*mana*) — Tattoos could be seen as a receptor for divine strength or mana.
- **Wooden gorget** (*rei miro*) — A rei miro is a wooden gorget.
- **Tattoo designs** — Traditional tattoo designs combined human-like and animal-like images.

## Music & performance

Traditional Rapa Nui music centers on choral singing and chanting, with shells, percussion, and newer musical styles.

- **Jaw-bone percussion** (*kauaha*) — Kauaha is a percussion instrument made from a horse jaw bone.
- **Rapa Nui tango** — Modern Rapanui music includes a Rapa Nui style of tango.
- **Matato'a** — Matato'a is a famous island music group that promotes traditional dance and music.
- **Conch shell trumpet** — Conch shell trumpets are named among Rapa Nui musical instruments.
- **Percussive dancers** — Percussive dancers are named among Rapa Nui performance traditions.

## Dance & theatre

Rapa Nui dance is linked with traditional music, and wooden paddles were used as dance objects.

- **Dance paddle** (*Rapa*) — The Rapa is a wooden dance paddle recorded from the early 19th century.
- **Dance group** (*Matato'a*) — Matato'a promotes traditional styles of Rapa Nui dance and music.
- **Clubs and staffs** — Museum records include clubs and staffs from Easter Island, although their identification is uncertain.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe the bird-man cult and offerings to ancestors but give no annual festival calendar.

- **Bird-man cult** (*Tangata manu*) — Tangata manu was a competition involving eggs that selected a birdman who remained sacred for five months.
- **Bird-man deity** (*Make-make*) — Make-make was the chief deity of the bird-man cult.
- **Ancestor offerings** — The living offered support to deified ancestors to improve the dead's place in the spirit world.

## Foodways

Rapa Nui food combines Indigenous Polynesian and American influences and uses fish, seafood, fruits, and tubers.

- **Earth-oven meal** (*umu*) — Umu is meat, fish, vegetables, and fruit wrapped in banana leaves and roasted in an earth oven.
- **Earth oven** (*umu pae*) — Umu pae is the earth oven used to roast traditional food.
- **Banana and pumpkin pudding** (*Po'e*) — Po'e is a pudding made from mashed bananas, pumpkin, and flour and baked in the umu pae.
- **Fish grilled on stones** (*tunu ahi*) — Tunu ahi is fish grilled on hot stones.
- **Easter Island curanto** (*Umu Rapa Nui*) — Umu Rapa Nui is the Easter Island version of curanto.

## Oral tradition & literature

Rapa Nui oral tradition includes settlement stories, gods, spirits, clan ancestors, and the unresolved Rongorongo glyph system.

- **First settler** (*Hotu Matu'a*) — Hotu Matu'a is regarded as the legendary first settler and supreme chief.
- **Supreme chief** (*ariki mau*) — Ariki mau means supreme chief.
- **Bird-man** (*Tangata manu*) — Tangata manu appears in Rapa Nui mythology as the bird-man figure and cult.
- **Epic battle** (*Hanau epe*) — The Hanau Epe fought an epic battle with the Hanau Momoko.
- **Glyph system** (*Rongorongo*) — Rongorongo is a system of glyphs whose meaning and purpose remain uncertain.

## Language & religion

Rapa Nui is an Eastern Polynesian language, while traditional beliefs include creator gods, spirits, ancestors, and a later Catholic influence.

- **Rapa Nui language** (*Rapa Nui*) — Rapa Nui is an Eastern Polynesian language spoken on Easter Island and written in the Latin script.
- **Pascuan language** (*Pascuan*) — Pascuan is another name for the Rapa Nui language.
- **Creator god** (*Make-make*) — Make-make is the creator god in Rapa Nui mythology.
- **Spirits of the dead** (*Aku-Aku*) — Aku-Aku are spirits of the dead.
- **Ancestor spirits** — Deified ancestors were believed to provide health, fertility, and fortune in return for support from the living.
