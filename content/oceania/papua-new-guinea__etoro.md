## Overview

The Etoro, also called Edolo, are a tribe and ethnic group of Papua New Guinea. Their territory lies on the southern slopes of Mt. Sisa, along the southern edge of the central mountain range of New Guinea, near the Papuan Plateau. In 2009, the National Geographic Society estimated that fewer than 1668 people spoke the Etoro/Edolo language. Edolo is a Papuan language belonging to the Bosavi branch of the Trans–New Guinea language family; as of 2015, there were 300 monolingual speakers. The Etoro are particularly known among anthropologists for ritual practices involving young boys and adult men, including beliefs about the ingestion of elders’ semen as part of male maturation.

## Festivals & rituals

The Etoro are known among anthropologists for ritual acts practiced between young boys and men. The sources state that Etoro believe young males must ingest the semen of their elders to achieve adult male status and properly mature and grow strong. O’Neil and Kottak report that most men marry and have heterosexual relations with their wives. The sources state that fear that heterosexual sex causes earlier death, together with the belief that homosexual sex prolongs life, focuses heterosexual relations toward reproduction.

## Language & religion

Edolo, or Etoro, is a Papuan language of Papua New Guinea spoken by the Etoro people. It belongs to the Bosavi branch of the Trans–New Guinea language family. As of 2015, there were 300 monolingual speakers. The sources describe Etoro beliefs concerning male maturation and sexual relations.
