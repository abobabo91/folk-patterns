## Overview

The Motu live on the southern coast of Papua New Guinea. With the Koitabu people, they are the original inhabitants and landowners of the Port Moresby area. Their culture includes pottery, fishing gear, body adornment, tattooing, music, dance, and the annual hiri trading voyages. Hanuabada is their largest village.

## Material culture

Motu material culture includes pottery, fibre items, fishing gear, tobacco pipes, and shell, wood, and turtle-shell ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Motu fibre work includes skirts, armlets, fishing nets, leaf streamers, and decorated bamboo pipes.

- **Coconut-fibre skirt** — An infant’s skirt is made of plain brown coconut fibre.
- **Plaited vegetable-fibre armlet** — A finely twisted vegetable-fibre cord is plaited into a zigzag armlet.
- **Rattan-fastened fishing net** — A hand net is made from cord and a loop of cane fastened with rattan, with seeds at its base.
- **Leaf streamers** — Red-stained palm leaves and strips of leaf are attached as streamers to badges and pendants.
- **Pyroincised tobacco pipe** — A bamboo tobacco pipe is decorated by burning bands, lines, curved forms, letters, triangles, and other shapes into it.

Motu dress includes fibre skirts, waist coverings, ornaments, headwear, flowers, and body bands.

## Festivals & rituals

The annual hiri combined trading voyages and pottery-making, while mourning included body blackening among old women.

- **Hiri trading voyages** (*hiri*) — Community members made annual trading voyages through the Gulf of Papua, and women made pottery for sale.
- **Bridewealth** — The Motu observed bridewealth.
- **Mourning body blackening** — Old women blackened their bodies with a kind of earth as a sign of mourning.

## Language & religion

Motu is a vowel-based language, and Hiri Motu developed as a regional trade language; religious beliefs are not described.

- **Motu language** (*Motu*) — Motu is spoken by the Motuans and is commonly used around Port Moresby.
- **Hiri Motu** — Hiri Motu is a simplified trade language that developed in the Papuan region and was first known as Police Motu.
- **Police Motu** — Police Motu was the earlier name for the simplified trade form of Motu.
- **Vowel-based sound system** — Every Motu syllable ends in a vowel sound, there are no consonant clusters, and the language has five vowel sounds.
