## Overview

The Rotumans (Rotuman: *Rotuạm*; Fijian: *Ro*) are a Polynesian ethnic group native to Rotuma, an island group forming part of Fiji. Rotuma is described as a cultural meeting point of the Micronesian, Melanesian and Polynesian divisions of the Pacific Ocean, and Rotuman culture shares aspects with neighboring Pacific cultures through long-standing seafaring connections. The sources identify Rotuman as an Austronesian language and describe Polynesian influence, including borrowing associated with Samoan and Tongan migrations. They also emphasize communal activities, district organization, chanting traditions and distinctive linguistic features.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They state only that men traditionally kept their hair shoulder length or longer, a practice viewed unfavorably by post-colonial Rotumans.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention communal property associated with agriculture.

## Music & performance

The sources describe an earlier Rotuman musical tradition as consisting primarily of chanting, with styles compared to traditional Tahitian or Maori forms. They name **Tautoga** and **Himene** in this connection. They also state that Rotuman musical tradition was shaped by European and Central Polynesian influence. The Rotuman language article gives a distinction between ordinary conversation and song, poetry and chant when discussing grammatical forms, indicating that these performance modes have a distinct linguistic context. No catalogue records or UNESCO inscriptions supplied for this profile document Rotuman instruments, recordings or performance objects.

## Dance & theatre

 **Tautoga** and **Himene** are mentioned in connection with chanting and musical tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that Rotuma was regarded by people from Tahiti Nui, the Marquesas Islands and Rapa Nui as a royal burial ground for kings and queens, and that the island was formerly known as *Siria*. The same account says that indigenous peoples of Tahiti Nui and Rapa Nui prayed to *Tagaroa Siria*, meaning “God of Siria,” because the name referred to the star directly above the island’s location. A species of seaweed was adopted as a symbol of blood ties between Tahiti Nui and Rotuma; the sources say it grows only in Tahiti and Rotuma, is a regional delicacy, and was given by a princess from Bora Bora.

## Foodways

They mention one species of seaweed as a regional delicacy and as a symbol of the relationship between Tahiti Nui and Rotuma. They also describe agriculture as a field of communal activity and communal property.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Wikipedia material states that Rotuman mythology commonly attributes Tahiti, Samoa and Tonga as true parent civilizations, and it connects earlier Rotuman musical tradition with chanting.

## Language & religion

Rotuman, also called Rotunan, Rutuman or *Fäeag Rotuạm*, is identified as an Austronesian language spoken by the Indigenous Rotuman people in the South Pacific. It has undergone significant Polynesian borrowing, especially following Samoan and Tongan migrations into Rotuma, and it was incorporated as a dependency into the Colony of Fiji in 1881. The language is noted for metathesis, which inverts the ultimate vowel with the immediately preceding consonant, and it is generally classified as an AVO language. The sources describe prayer to *Tagaroa Siria* in the context of Rotuma’s former identity as *Siria*.
