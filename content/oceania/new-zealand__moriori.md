## Overview

The Moriori were the first settlers of the Chatham Islands. Their culture developed separately from mainland Māori during long isolation. They became known for pacifism, marine-based subsistence, tree carvings, and a distinct language. Their population was recorded as 1,806 in 2023.

## Material culture

Moriori material culture adapted to an island environment with little greenstone and limited timber, using bone, schist, whale materials, and tree trunks.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not document Moriori textiles, but they describe tree carvings and albatross feathers as distinctive visual traditions.

- **Tree carvings** (*rakau momori*) — Moriori carved human forms, fish, and birds into tree trunks.
- **Albatross feathers** — Some Moriori wear albatross feathers in their hair as a sign of peace.

### Clothing & dress

The sources give only limited information about Moriori clothing.

- **Seal clothing** — Seals supplied food and clothing before European contact.
- **Peace feathers** — Some Moriori wear albatross feathers in their hair as a sign of peace.

### Architecture

Moriori architecture is represented mainly by marae and meeting houses.

- **Kōpinga marae** — The new Kōpinga marae became associated with the renewal of the Covenant of Peace in January 2005.
- **Kopinga meeting place** — The Kopinga meeting place is in Waitangi on Chatham Island.
- **Hokomenetai meeting house** — The Hokomenetai meeting house is in Waitangi on Chatham Island.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources record no ceramics or metalwork, but they describe schist clubs and a bone bird-spear point.

- **Oblong-bladed schist club** (*pohatu taharua*) — This short schist club has an oblong blade, convex sides, concave edges, marked shoulders, and a rounded butt.
- **Bill-hook schist club** (*okewa*) — This short schist club has a bill-hook-shaped blade, a curved handle, and a pointed butt.
- **Barbed bird-spear point** (*uata*) — This bone point, possibly made from albatross wing-bone, has five sharp barbs on one side.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Moriori adornment included whale-related pendants and albatross feathers worn in the hair.

- **Whale-tooth pendant** (*rei*) — One whale-tooth pendant is perforated, deeply undercut near the hole, and strongly curved.
- **Whale-tooth pendant** (*rei*) — A second whale-tooth pendant has a large hole, abrasions near its curved point, and a worn or broken tip.
- **Whalebone pendant** (*rei*) — A curved, blunt-tipped whalebone pendant is also called rei or a cloak pin.
- **Peace feathers** — Some Moriori wear albatross feathers in their hair as a sign of peace.

## Music & performance

The sources provide little information about Moriori music, but efforts have preserved Moriori vocabulary and songs.

- **Moriori songs** — Work began in 2001 to preserve Moriori songs as part of cultural and language revival.
- **Pipes of the fire god** — Moriori oral tradition remembered the crew of HMS Chatham as containing the god of fire because they smoked pipes.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not document Moriori dance, theatre, or named entertainment traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

The central Moriori ritual principle was the law of Nunuku, which prohibited warfare and cannibalism and promoted peace.

- **Law of Nunuku** — The law of Nunuku replaced armed conflict with ritual fighting and conciliation.
- **Ritual fighting** — Men could strike each other with a rod, but the contest ended when skin was abraded or blood first appeared.
- **Covenant of Peace renewal** — The Covenant of Peace was renewed at Kōpinga marae in January 2005.
- **Moriori council** (*hui*) — A Moriori council at Te Awapatiki in 1835 declared that the law of Nunuku was a moral imperative.

## Foodways

Moriori foodways were based almost entirely on marine foods because the Chatham Islands were unsuitable for most Polynesian crops.

- **Fish** — Fish provided protein and fat in the Moriori diet.
- **Fur seals** — Fur seals were valued for food and clothing and had religious significance.
- **Young seabirds** — The fatty young of seabirds provided protein and fat.
- **Stranded whales** — Stranded whales were regarded as special delicacies.
- **Bird hunting** (*uata*) — The uata was equipment used for hunting birds.

## Oral tradition & literature

Moriori knowledge was passed through oral tradition, genealogy, written records, and modern language-revival work.

- **European landing tradition** — Moriori tradition remembered the crew of HMS Chatham as including the god of fire because of their smoking pipes.
- **Elders’ petition** — Surviving Moriori elders wrote a manuscript petition in 1862.
- **Moriori vocabulary** — Samuel Deighton collected a Moriori vocabulary that was published in 1887.
- **Moriori texts** — Alexander Shand collected Moriori texts that were published in 1911.
- **Ta Rē Moriori app** — The Ta Rē Moriori app was launched in 2021 to teach the language.

## Language & religion

Moriori was a distinct Eastern Polynesian language, and its religious and ethical life centered on peace, seals, and carved trees.

- **Moriori language** (*Ta Rē Moriori*) — Moriori was closely related to Māori and Cook Island Māori and shared about 70% of its vocabulary with Māori.
- **Law of Nunuku** — The law of Nunuku banned warfare and cannibalism.
- **Seals** — Seals had religious significance in Moriori life.
- **Tree carvings** (*rakau momori*) — Tree carvings had ritual importance.
- **Johann Friedrich Wilhelm Baucke** — Baucke, who lived from 1848 to 1931, was the last man who could speak Moriori.

## Glossary

- *Rēkohu* — Moriori name for the Chatham Islands
- *Wharekauri* — Māori name for the Chatham Islands
- *rakau momori* — Moriori tree carvings
- *pohatu taharua* — Oblong-bladed schist club
- *okewa* — Bill-hook-shaped schist club
- *uata* — Bone bird-spear point or bird-hunting equipment
- *rei* — Whale-tooth or whalebone pendant
- *hui* — Council or meeting
- *Ta Rē Moriori* — Moriori language-teaching app
