## Overview

The Chuukese, previously spelled Trukese, are a Micronesian-speaking ethnic group indigenous to the island of Chuuk and its surrounding islands and atolls. They constitute almost 49% of the population of the Federated States of Micronesia. Their language belongs to the Chuukic branch of Micronesian languages and, more broadly, to the Malayo-Polynesian subdivision of the Austronesian language family. The sources describe Chuukese culture through fishing, palm-thatch housing, family relationships, traditional marriage practices, and continuing beliefs in spirit possession by the dead. A 2010 census showed that the Chuukese were the fastest-growing group of Pacific Islanders during the 2000s decade in the United States, increasing from about 700 people in 2000 to more than 4,000 by the 2010 census.

Traditional Chuukese homes were built using palm thatch. The sources state that families maintained strong intimate bonds in these homes. No additional architectural traditions are covered by the sources.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe traditional marriage practices. Before a man could consider marriage, he needed experience in farming, fishing, and boat construction and had to be able to build his own house. He would then inform his parents that he was ready for marriage, after which they searched for a suitable young woman.

When the families had decided on a girl, the man’s parents visited the girl’s parents, introduced themselves and the purpose of their visit, and discussed a possible marriage. If the marriage was agreed upon, the young man stayed with the girl’s parents while the girl resided with the young man’s parents until the wedding day. Before the marriage, both families prepared a feast attended by the leaders of their respective families. According to custom, the girl’s family provided enough food for the man’s family, and his family did the same for the girl’s.

The sources also state that marriage partners were valued for their ability to work. Although both the man and woman desired compatible sex partners, they looked even more for good workers. A person incapable of work was unlikely to be successful at marriage in Chuuk, and people selected partners according to their knowledge of character and abilities within the community.

## Foodways

They state that fishing is the traditional subsistence of the Chuukese and remains an important source of income for families in modern times. Both men and women engage in fishing. The marriage account also describes feasts prepared by both families before a wedding.

## Oral tradition & literature

They mention that traditional beliefs in spirit possession by the dead continue to exist.

## Language & religion

Chuukese, also rendered Trukese, is a Chuukic language of the Austronesian language family spoken primarily on the islands of Chuuk in the Caroline Islands in Micronesia. Communities of speakers exist on Pohnpei and Guam, and estimates in the supplied source give about 45,900 speakers in Micronesia. Chuukese is an Austronesian language of the Micronesian subbranch and is one of the dialects making up the Chuukic subgroup, together with close relatives such as Woleaian and Carolinian.

The language is written in a modified Latin alphabet. Its orthography permits word-initial double consonants, and “Truk” and “Chuuk” differ in orthography, with older ⟨tr⟩ and current ⟨ch⟩ representing the sound [ʈʂ]. The sources state that the Chuukese are overwhelmingly Christian, while traditional beliefs in spirit possession by the dead continue. These spirits are said to possess women overwhelmingly, usually as a result of family conflicts, and to speak through the women while admonishing family members to treat one another better.
