## Overview

The Arrernte live around Alice Springs and across nearby Central Australian lands. Their cultural life includes oral history, ceremonies, songs and religious objects. Eastern and Central Arrernte has about 4,100 speakers, while other dialects are threatened or extinct. Arrernte culture also includes a highly developed sign language.

## Material culture

Documented material culture includes wooden weapons, stone tools, resin objects, hair-string girdles, seed necklaces, bone nose ornaments and ochre.

### Clothing & dress

Documented dress includes hair-string girdles, a seed necklace and bone nose ornaments.

- **Human-hair waist girdle** — A men’s waist girdle is made of human hair string.
- **Possum-fur waist girdle** — Another waist girdle uses reddish, slightly crimped possum-fur strings secured by short cross-bindings.
- **Seed necklace** — A woman’s necklace is made from red bean-tree seeds strung on vegetable fibre.
- **Bone nose ornament** — Men wore sharpened bone nose ornaments through a hole in the nasal septum, sometimes with gum knobs and ochre.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Documented everyday objects are mainly wooden weapons and stone tools.

- **Boomerang** — Boomerangs are made of hard wood, grooved, polished or ochred.
- **Shield** — A wooden shield made from bean tree has an oval shape, a solid handle and red ochre covering.
- **Stone and resin knife** — A knife is made of stone and resin identified as porcupine grass.
- **Axe** — An axe consists of stone and wood.
- **Throwing stick** (*weet-weet*) — A weet-weet is a thin flexible stick with a pointed bulb at one end, coated with red ochre.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Body adornment includes seed necklaces, bone nose ornaments, hair string and ochre.

- **Red seed necklace** — The necklace uses red bean-tree seeds and vegetable fibre string.
- **Bone nose ornament** — Men’s nose ornaments are sharpened bone, sometimes fitted with gum knobs and rubbed with ochre.
- **Human-hair string ball** — Museum records include an ovoid ball made of dark brown human hair string.

## Music & performance

Songs and ceremonies are important parts of Arrernte cultural life, though the sources name no traditional instruments or genres.

- **Rites of Passage** — Peter Sculthorpe’s music theatre work is written partly in Arrernte and partly in Latin.
- **Journey to Horseshoe Bend** — This work used Western and Southern Arrernte in parts of its libretto.

## Dance & theatre

The sources mention ceremonies but give no details of named Arrernte dances or a separate theatre tradition.

- **Rites of Passage** — Peter Sculthorpe’s music theatre work is partly in Arrernte and partly in Latin.
- **Journey to Horseshoe Bend** — The production used Western and Southern Arrernte in parts of its libretto.

## Festivals & rituals

Arrernte spirituality connects the landscape with Altyerre, ancestral beings and sacred objects.

- **The Dreaming** (*Altyerre*) — Altyerre is the Arrernte name for the Dreaming, which centers on the landscape.
- **Creator being** (*Altjira*) — Altjira is described as the creator being of the Inapertwa that became all living creatures.
- **Religious objects** (*Tjurunga*) — Tjurunga are objects of religious significance.
- **Eastern MacDonnell ancestor** (*Ayepe-arenye*) — Ayepe-arenye is an ancestral caterpillar-being associated with forming the eastern MacDonnell Ranges.
- **Western MacDonnell ancestor** (*Ntyarlke*) — Ntyarlke is an ancestral caterpillar-being associated with forming the western MacDonnell Ranges.

## Oral tradition & literature

Arrernte oral history describes Alice Springs and the surrounding landscape as shaped by ancestral caterpillar-beings.

- **Ayepe-arenye** — Ayepe-arenye, identified as Hyles livornicoides, is linked with the eastern MacDonnell Ranges.
- **Ntyarlke** — Ntyarlke, identified as Hippotion celerio, is linked with the western MacDonnell Ranges.
- **Utnerrengatye** — Utnerrengatye, identified as Coenotes eremophilae, is another ancestral caterpillar-being in Arrernte oral history.
- **Living Archive of Aboriginal Languages** — The Living Archive of Aboriginal Languages is part of language-preservation work involving Arrernte.

## Language & religion

Arrernte is an Arandic dialect cluster with several threatened dialects and a highly developed sign language.

- **Central Arrernte** (*Eastern/Central Arrernte*) — Eastern/Central Arrernte has about 4,100 speakers and is used in schools, universities, media and local government.
- **Eastern Arrernte varieties** (*Akarre, Antekerrepenh, Ikngerripenhe and Mparntwe Arrernte*) — Eastern Arrernte, also called Central Arrernte, includes Akarre, Antekerrepenh, Ikngerripenhe and Mparntwe Arrernte.
- **Alyawarre** — Alyawarre is the second biggest dialect in the group.
- **Arrernte sign language** (*Iltyeme-iltyeme*) — Iltyeme-iltyeme is the Arrernte highly developed sign language.
- **Religious life** (*Altyerre*) — Arrernte religious life focuses on the landscape and Altyerre, with Altjira, Inapertwa and Tjurunga also named.
