## Overview

The Angu, also called Änga and previously called Kukukuku, are a small group living mainly in the high, mountainous region of southern Eastern Highlands Province, south-western Morobe Province, and north-eastern Gulf Province of Papua New Guinea. They speak several related languages. The Angu are known in the sources for their historical raids on villages in lower valleys, their limited clothing in a cold, high-altitude environment, and the smoked mummies found at sites around Aseki in Hamtai territory. The largest tribe, the Hamtai, has a population of 45,000. Four Änga languages are described as almost extinct.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources describe no documented Angu pattern-bearing textile traditions.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no motifs.

### Clothing & dress

The sources state that, despite the high altitude and cold climate of their homeland, the Änga wore limited clothing, including grass skirts, a piece similar to a sporran, and cloaks made from beaten bark called *mals*.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe mummification among Hamtai communities in the Aseki area. Three famous mummy sites are located around Aseki in Hamtai territory. The sources state that it is unclear when the practice began and that it ended in 1949 with the arrival of missionaries. No annual festivals, seasonal calendar, birth rites, coming-of-age ceremonies, wedding customs, or funeral practices are described beyond this reference to mummification.

## Foodways

The account of the Toulambi says that two members of the group tasted store food after being taken to a government station, but it provides no further information about Angu foodways.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention J. K. McCarthy’s account of early contact in *Patrol into Yesterday: My New Guinea Years*, published in 1963.

## Language & religion

The Angu speak several related languages. Four Änga languages are described as almost extinct, while the largest tribe, the Hamtai, is described as thriving.
