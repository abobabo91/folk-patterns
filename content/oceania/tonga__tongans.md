## Overview

Tongans or Tongan people are a Polynesian ethnic group native to Tonga, a Polynesian archipelago in the Pacific Ocean. They represent more than 98% of the inhabitants of Tonga; the remainder includes European, mixed European, and other Pacific Islanders, as well as several hundred Chinese. Almost two-thirds of the population live on Tongatapu, Tonga’s main island. An increasing number have moved to Nukuʻalofa, the country’s only urban and commercial center, where European and Indigenous cultural and living patterns have blended. Village life and kinship ties remain important throughout the country. Everyday life is strongly influenced by Polynesian traditions and the Christian faith.

## Festivals & rituals

Everyday life in Tonga is heavily influenced by the Christian faith. Commerce and entertainment activities cease from midnight Saturday until midnight Sunday, and the constitution declares the Sabbath to be sacred, forever.

## Language & religion

Tongan is the official language of Tonga, along with English. It is a Polynesian language of the Tongic branch and is closely related to Niuean, Uvean, Futunan, Samoan, and Tokelauan. It is more distantly related to Cook Islands Māori, Hawaiian, New Zealand Māori, and Tahitian. Important Christian denominations include Methodists (Free Wesleyan), Catholics, and Latter-day Saints.
