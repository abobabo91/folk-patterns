## Overview

The Urarina are an Indigenous people of the Peruvian Amazon Basin, in Loreto, inhabiting the valleys of the Chambira, Urituyacu, and Corrientes Rivers. Archaeological and historical sources place their residence in the Chambira Basin of contemporary northeastern Peru for centuries. The Urarina call themselves *Kachá*, meaning “person,” while “Urarina” is the ethnonym used by ethnologists. Their population is estimated at around 2,000 in one account, while the Urarina language article describes 2,000–3,000 members and around 3,000 speakers in Urarinas District. Urarina society combines semi-mobile hunting and horticulture with woven palm-fiber production, animistic cosmology, ayahuasca shamanism, elaborate mythology, and continuing political and educational mobilization.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Palm-fiber bast mats* are woven by Urarina women and are identified in the sources as a characteristic form of women’s craftsmanship. The sources specify the material and woven technique.

*Palm-fiber hammocks* are another product of Urarina women’s craftsmanship. The sources identify them as woven objects but provide no named designs, motifs, or construction details.

*Net-bags* are produced by Urarina women as part of their recognized craftwork.

No other documented Urarina textile or pattern traditions are covered by the sources used here.

### Architecture

Urarina settlements are composed of multiple longhouse groups. These settlements are located on high ground, called *restingas*, or on embankments along flood-free margins of the Chambira Basin’s rivers and streams. The embankments are bounded by low-lying territories called *tahuampa* and *bajiales*, which are susceptible to flooding during the annual rainy season, roughly November–May.

## Music & performance

 They do state that the Urarina have an elaborate animistic cosmological system based on ayahuasca shamanism and that men are esteemed for shamanic skills.

The sources mention a cassava-beer festival in an Urarina deluge myth. In that story, a deluge was produced by the urination of the daughter of the ayahuasca-god, giving rise to the chthonic world of spirits. This is presented as mythological narrative rather than as an account of a documented musical or festival performance.

## Festivals & rituals

 One Urarina deluge myth takes place on the occasion of a cassava-beer festival.

Urarina cosmology includes ayahuasca shamanism and the profoundly ritualized consumption of *Brugmansia suaveolens*. The sources also state that the Urarina have an elaborate animistic cosmological system. The sources mention brideservice, uxorilocal patterns of post-nuptial residence, debt peonage, and sororal polygyny as customary social practices.

## Foodways

 They mention cassava beer in connection with an Urarina deluge myth and identify horticulture as part of Urarina subsistence.

The Urarina are described as a semi-mobile hunting and horticultural society. The sources therefore identify hunting and horticulture as important subsistence practices.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Urarina continue to tell elaborate myths and stories about the violence they have experienced from outsiders. Historically, these experiences included forced-labor conscription, rape, disease, concubinage, and abusive treatment at the hands of outsiders.

One deluge myth tells of a man who saved himself by climbing a *cudí*, identified as an amasiza tree and as *Erythrina elei*. His wife was transformed into a termites’ nest clinging to the tree, while their two sons became birds. The man later acquired another wife after a woman unsuccessfully attempted to evade him by summoning a pit viper, a spider, and a giant biting ant.

A second deluge myth attributes the flood to the urination of the daughter of the ayahuasca-god during a cassava-beer festival. The event gives rise to the chthonic world of spirits. The sources also identify a deluge-myth tradition and cite Bartholomew Dean’s work on Urarina cosmogony and historical consciousness. Portions of the Bible were first published in Urarina in 1973, but the complete Bible has not been published.

## Language & religion

Urarina is a language isolate or an unclassified language. It is spoken in Peru, specifically in the Loreto Region of Northwest Peru, by the Urarina people, with around 3,000 speakers in Urarinas District along the Chambira River. The language uses the Latin script and is also known as *Itucali*, *Simacu*, or *Shimacu*. It has object–verb–subject word order and is described as one of the strictest languages using this order in speech. There are four dialect zones: Zone A, Zone B, Zone C, and Zone D.

Urarina religion is described through an elaborate animistic cosmological system and ayahuasca shamanism, including ritualized consumption of *Brugmansia suaveolens*. The language is considered potentially endangered as bilingualism and use of Spanish increase. A bilingual education system exists, although most bilingual schools almost exclusively use Spanish.
