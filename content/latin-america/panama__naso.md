## Overview

The Naso, also known as the Teribe or *Tjër Di*, are an Indigenous people of Panama and Costa Rica. They primarily live in northwestern Panama, in Bocas del Toro Province and the Naso Tjër Di Comarca, and also in southern Costa Rica, in Puntarenas Province; individuals are also found in Limón Province. There are roughly 3,500 Naso people. Their ancestral territory consists of mountainous jungle regions along the river known in the Spanish-speaking world as the Teribe and in Naso as Tjër Di. Naso belongs to the Chibchan language family and the Talamanca subgroup. The people are notable in the sources for their continuing connection to their territory, their subsistence economy, their bilingualism, and their political organization as one of the few Indigenous peoples governed through a monarchy.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources state that many Naso wear Western clothing.

### Architecture

Most Naso live in elevated wooden houses with thatched or zinc-coated roofing. In Costa Rica, Naso communities have received United Nations financial support for tourism facilities, including hostel and cabin housing with plumbing, as well as improvements to trails.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do mention dugout canoes and rafts as means of transporting agricultural products, handicrafts, and other goods to Changuinola.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do describe sacred places and religious traditions. The Diquís Dam project in Costa Rica was described as threatening important sacred sites, while the traditional God is identified as Sibö, a supreme God and creator. Tjër, described as the mythical “Grand-Mother,” was endowed by God with the secrets of botanical medicine. No further ritual procedures or ceremonial occasions are given.

## Foodways

The Naso are described primarily as subsistence farmers. They supplement their earnings through the sale of agricultural products including cocoa, oranges, and plantains, as well as pigs, chickens, and ducks.

The sources identify botanical medicine as part of the significance of Tjër, the mythical “Grand-Mother,”.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Naso language sources describe oral tradition as preserving an account of a group of Teribes who were lost while attempting to cross over to the Pacific. The historical material also connects the name Tjër Di with Tjër, the mythical “Grand-Mother,” and explains that *Di* means “water.” These are the only named elements of Naso oral tradition in the supplied sources.

The sources list *Narraciones Teribes: Nasoga Laiwãk*, a volume of sacred texts, and state that a language policy requires linguists to obtain a permit from the king and distribute published works to the community.

## Language & religion

Naso, also known as Teribe, is a Chibchan language of the Talamanca subgroup and belongs to the Chibchan language family. The Naso people refer to their language as Naso. The term Teribe is also used, while Térraba refers to the language as spoken in Térraba, Costa Rica, where the language is endonymously known as Brörán. The language is spoken primarily in the Naso Tjër Di Comarca and Bocas del Toro Province, with speakers also present in Costa Rica.

There are fewer than 1,000 speakers as of 2018, of whom only 500 are fluent. The sources state that nearly all known speakers are bilingual in Spanish to some degree. In Panama, the language has been preserved more strongly, while in Costa Rica it is near language death. In 2000, only three fluent speakers and “half a dozen” semispeakers remained among the Térrabas. As of 2018, the vast majority of Naso children did not speak Teribe.

Most inhabitants in Panama speak the native language, although the majority also know Spanish. Many Naso have converted to evangelical Protestant Christianity. Very few adhere to Roman Catholicism, while the Seventh-day Adventist Church is described as very important. The traditional God is Sibö, a supreme God and creator.
