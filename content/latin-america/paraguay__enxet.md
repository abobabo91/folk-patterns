## Overview

The Enxet are an Indigenous people living in the Gran Chaco region of western Paraguay, including Presidente Hayes Department. The source describes their population as about 17,000, while another section states that it is currently about 8,200 and has not yet recovered from the Chaco War period. Originally hunter-gatherers, many Enxet now supplement their livelihoods as laborers on cattle ranches that have encroached upon their dwindling natural forest habitat. Most live in small settlements sponsored by missionary organizations, although a handful maintain a traditional way of life. Their languages remain vigorous. Enxet is part of the Enlhet-Enenlhet, also called Mascoian, language family.

## Language & religion

Enxet, also known as Enxet Sur or Southern Lengua, is spoken by the Indigenous southern Enxet people of Presidente Hayes Department, Paraguay. It is one of twenty languages spoken by the wider Gran Chaco Amerindians of South America. Enxet belongs to the Enlhet-Enenlhet, also called Mascoian, language family, and is most closely related to Enlhet according to preliminary analysis. Enxet and Enlhet were once considered dialects of a single language known as Lengua; they later diverged as extensive differences became apparent. The language was first documented in the late nineteenth century by explorers from Spain. It has three phonemic vowel qualities, /e, a, o/, whose lengths help maximize distinction.
