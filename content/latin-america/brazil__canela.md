## Overview

The Canela are a group of multiple Indigenous peoples of Northeastern Brazil who speak the Canela language. The peoples historically grouped under the label have included the Ramkokamekrá, Apanyekra, and Kenkateye. Canela is spoken in Maranhão, Brazil, by the Apànjêkra, also called Apaniêkrá, and by the Mẽmõrtũmre, also called Ràmkôkãmẽkra or Ramkokamekrá. Canela is a dialect of the Canela-Krahô language, a Timbira variety of the Northern Jê language group within Jê and Macro-Jê. Until their pacification and resettlement between 1814 and 1840, the Canela were primarily hunter-gatherers, with some cultivation of garden foods, estimated to be 20% of their subsistence.

## Language & religion

Canela is a dialect of the Canela-Krahô language, a Timbira variety of the Northern Jê language group, within Jê and Macro-Jê. It is spoken by the Apànjêkra or Apaniêkrá and by the Mẽmõrtũmre, also called Ràmkôkãmẽkra or Ramkokamekrá, in Maranhão, Brazil. The Kenkateye dialect has been extinct since 1913 because of the massacre of the tribe by cattle ranchers. Canela is a head-final language. Its finite and nonfinite verb forms differ in several grammatical contexts, including matrix and subordinate clauses, and its clauses include split-S, ergative–absolutive, and nominative–absolutive alignment patterns. The language has absolutive, accusative, nominative, and ergative grammatical forms, including person prefixes and postpositional phrases.
