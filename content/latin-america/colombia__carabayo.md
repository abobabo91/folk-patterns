## Overview

The Carabayo, also known as the Aroje or Yuri people, are an uncontacted people of Colombia. They live in at least three long houses, known as *malokas*, along the Rio Puré, now the Río Puré National Park, in the southeastern corner of the country. Their territory is in the Amazonas Department of the Colombian Amazon rainforest, near the border with Brazil. They share the protected National Park with the Passé and Jumana people. In the last 400 years, intermittent contact with outsiders, including violent attacks by slave traders and rubber extractors, contributed to their retreat from outside groups and increased isolation. Their language appears to belong to the Tikuna–Yuri family.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Carabayo language is poorly documented. During the captivity of a family taken in a violent encounter, a priest recorded words overheard or used in exchanges, sometimes with contextual indications of meaning. Fifty words were collected; excluding Spanish words acquired during captivity and two words from the old lingua franca Nheengatu, 25 words were recorded with a gloss or context.

## Language & religion

The Carabayo language, also called Caraballo, is spoken by the Carabayo people and appears to be a member of the Tikuna–Yuri family. The people are also known as Yuri and Aroje, and their self-designation has been reported as Yacumo. The name “Carabayo” comes from the mock name “Bernardo Caraballo,” given to a Carabayo man during his captivity in the Capuchin mission at La Pedrera in 1969, after the Colombian boxer. The Bora people know the Carabayo as the Aroje. Maku and Macusa are pejorative Arawak terms meaning “without speech” applied to many local languages and are not specific to Carabayo.
