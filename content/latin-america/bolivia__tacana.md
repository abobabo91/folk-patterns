## Overview

The Tacana are an ethnic group living in Bolivia’s Beni department. The Tacana language article describes Tacana as a Western Tacanan language spoken by people living in the forest along the Beni and Madre de Dios rivers in the north of La Paz Department. The Tacana people article records 18,535 Tacana in 2012, including 559 native speakers of Tacana, while the language article gives an ethnic population of 5,000 and approximately 1,800 speakers. Their documented cultural life includes agriculture, hunting, fishing, gathering, crafts with rubber products, patrilineal descent, political institutions, and religious ceremonies conducted by shamans in jungle worship houses.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources state that Tacana residence may be with one or the other of the in-laws or in an independent house, forming a western-style nuclear family. Ceremonies involving shamans take place in secret houses of worship in the jungle.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They state that crafts with rubber products form part of Tacana economic activity.

## Music & performance

 They state that shamans celebrate traditional ceremonies on key dates of the agricultural calendar and that traditional religious beliefs and practices continue to influence daily life.

## Festivals & rituals

Traditional religious beliefs and practices continue to exert an important influence on Tacana daily life. Shamans celebrate traditional ceremonies on key dates of the agricultural calendar. The *baba tcuai* or *tata janana*, identified in the source as shamans, are healers and guardians of the welfare of the community and the universe. The ceremonies in which they participate are held in secret houses of worship in the jungle. In some Tacana communities in Pando, the shaman is known as *Baba Cuana*.

## Foodways

Agriculture is the predominant Tacana economic activity. In each productive unit, composed of one or two families, people cultivate 1ha. of rice, 1ha. of corn, 1/2 ha. of banana, and 1/4 ha. of cassava. Some products are commercialized to obtain complementary foods or other items. Hunting, fishing, and gathering also form part of Tacana economic activities.

## Language & religion

Tacana is identified as a Western Tacanan language. It is spoken in Bolivia by people living in the forest along the Beni and Madre de Dios rivers in the north of La Paz Department. The sources list numerous dialects attributed to Tacana, including Ayaychuna, Babayana, Chiliuvo, Chivamona, Idiama (Ixiama), Pamaino, Pasaramona, Saparuna, Siliama, Tumupasa (Maracani, “Tupamasa”), Uchupiamona, Yabaypura, and Yubamona; the dialects are described as now extinct. They document shamans, secret jungle worship houses, healing, guardianship of the community and universe, and ceremonies linked to the agricultural calendar.
