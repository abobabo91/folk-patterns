## Overview

The Cubeo are an ethnic group of the Vaupés Department in Colombia, near the center of the Northwestern Cultural Area of the Amazon. Their villages are distributed along the median course of the capital Mitú and alongside the Cuduyari and Querary rivers. Reports of their population range between 3,000 and 5,000 individuals; the national census of 1985 recorded 4,368 inhabitants. “Cubeo” is a generic name used in local Spanish and in literature for a social and linguistic group. The Cubeo refer to themselves by this name in interactions with others, while *pâmiwâ* means “people” and *jiwa* means “my people.” Their language belongs to the Tukanoan languages and is notable for its unusual word-order descriptions.

## Language & religion

Cubeo is spoken in the Vaupés Department, along the Cuduyari and Querarí Rivers and their tributaries in Colombia, and in Brazil and Venezuela. It is a member of the central branch of the Tucanoan languages; it has also been classified as Eastern Tukano, while a reclassification to Middle Tukano has been suggested. Its word order has been described as subject–object–verb or object–verb–subject, with the latter being very rare cross-linguistically. The language has six oral vowels and six nasal vowels, and its writing system uses the bowl-struck form of Đ, approximately ⟨ꟈ⟩.
