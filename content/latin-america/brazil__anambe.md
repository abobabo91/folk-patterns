## Overview

The Anambé are an Indigenous people of Brazil who live in the state of Pará, including on the Cairari River. They have been in contact with non-Natives since 1842. Their population was 60 in the 1940s, later decreasing because of measles and marriages outside the tribe, before increasing since the 1960s. The Anambé language belongs to the Tupi language family and the Tupi–Guarani language family, Subgroup VIII.

## Language & religion

The Anambé speak Anambé, a Tupi language belonging to the Tupi–Guarani language family, Subgroup VIII. Anambé, more specifically Anambe of Cairari, is spoken in Pará on the Cairari River and is being supplanted by Portuguese. Its ISO 639-3 language code is “aan”; only approximately seven people still speak Anambé today.
