## Overview

The Pira-tapuya, also called Pira-Tapuia, Piratapuyo, Tapuya, and *Wa’îkɨ̃hɨ*, are an Indigenous people of the Amazon regions. They live along the Vaupés River and its tributaries in Colombia and Brazil, including the state of Amazonas. They call themselves *Waíkana* and speak Piratapuyo, one of the Eastern Tucanoan languages. Their principal settlements include Mitú, capital of the Vaupés Department in Colombia, and Iaraueté, a district seat in the municipality of São Gabriel da Cachoeira. As of 2014, Siasi/Sesai estimated 1,325 Pira-tapuya in Amazonas; as of 1988, an estimated 400 lived in Colombia. Their cultural life is situated within the Uaupés/Pira-Paraná socio-cultural complex, whose peoples intermarry, trade, and participate in shared rituals.

## Language & religion

The Pira-tapuya call themselves *Waíkana*. They speak Piratapuyo, an Eastern Tucanoan language. Other ethnic groups in the region also speak Eastern Tucanoan languages, apart from the Tariana people, who originally spoke an Arawakan language. Tucano is the lingua franca of the region and has around 20,000 speakers.
