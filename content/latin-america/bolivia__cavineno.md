## Overview

The Cavineño, also known as Cavina, Cavineña, Cavinenyo, Cavinya, and Kaviña, are an ethnic group in Bolivia. They mainly live along the Beni and Madidi rivers, in several communities near the Beni River, with the nearest towns identified as Reyes and Riberalta. In 2012, there were 3,884 Cavineño, of whom 1,173 spoke Cavineña natively. Almost all also speak Spanish. The Cavineño and Araona are closely intermixed with other Takanan-speaking peoples. Their collective territories are shared with the Tacanas in Beni and with the Ese Ejja and Tacanas in Pando.

## Language & religion

Cavineña is an indigenous language spoken on the Amazonian plains of northern Bolivia by over 1,000 Cavineño people. It is still spoken and learned by some children but is endangered; Guillaume states that about 1200 people speak it, out of a population of around 1700. Nearly all Cavineña speakers are bilingual in Spanish. Cavineña is described as a Takanan language or dialect, and the Cavineño people live in communities near the Beni River.
