## Overview

The Cumanagoto are an Indigenous people of South America whose territory originally extended across the ancient province of Nueva Andalucía, including Cumaná and Barcelona in eastern Venezuela. Their descendants live in the north of Anzoátegui State. At the time of the Spanish incursion, they lived in northeastern Venezuela, but since the 17th century they have not existed as a tribal or governmental unit. Their language belongs to the Carib language family and is nearly extinct. The Cumanagoto cultivated maize, manioc, sweet potatoes, and other native crops, gathered wild foods, and hunted. Their material and spiritual life included defensive village palisades, feather and shell ornaments, agriculture, hunting, and worship of the sun and moon.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no textile motifs.

### Clothing & dress

Cumanagoto dress was minimal. It consisted of a small genital covering and decorative ornaments made from feathers, pearls, gold, shell, clay beads, coral beads, bones, teeth, or flowers. The sources do not distinguish men's and women's clothing, identify named garments, or describe head coverings, belts, footwear, or separate ceremonial dress.

### Architecture

Cumanagoto villages often had wooden palisades for defense.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention clay beads, gold, shell, coral beads, bones, teeth, and flowers as materials for decorative ornaments.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Cumanagoto decorative adornment used feathers, pearls, gold, shell, clay beads, coral beads, bones, teeth, and flowers.

## Festivals & rituals

Cumanagoto religion centered on worship of the sun and moon. Frogs were also valued because the Cumanagotos regarded the animal as the god of waters; they did not kill frogs and instead kept them as pets, but whipped them during an extended winter or a period of little rainfall.

## Foodways

The Cumanagoto were agriculturalists who grew maize, manioc, sweet potatoes, and other native crops. They also gathered wild foods, and hunting was important. Domesticated animals were uncommon, except for turkeys.

## Language & religion

Cumanagoto is a nearly extinct Cariban language of eastern coastal Venezuela and is the language of the Cumanagoto people and other nations. It is also identified as *Cumanogota*, *Cumaná*, *Kumaná*, *choto maimuru*, *Itoto Majun*, *Palank*, *Pariagoto*, and *Tamanaku*. Extinct dialects include *Palenque*, *Piritu*, and *Avaricoto*. Colonial sources reported a distinctive linguistic homogeneity across parts of Venezuela, with only minor dialectal variations among different Indigenous peoples. In 2012, 49 people were reported to speak the language, and a language revitalization program had started. Cumanagoto religion centered on the sun and moon, while frogs were associated with the god of waters.
