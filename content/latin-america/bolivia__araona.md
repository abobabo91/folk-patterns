## Overview

The Araona are an ethnic group in Bolivia who live in the headwaters of the Manupari river in northwest Bolivia. Their population was 228 according to the 2012 census. The Araona language is spoken by 711 people, although many Araona also speak Spanish; a separate language source reports that about 90% of the 90 Araona people are fluent in Araona. The Araona language is described as an indigenous language related to other Tacanan languages. Alfred Métraux observed that the Araona and Cavineño are so intermixed with other Takanan-speaking peoples that they can be difficult to treat separately. Their documented history includes displacement, enslavement in the rubber industry, settlement between the Manorimi and Monopare Rivers, and cooperation with SIL International missionaries in 1965.

## Festivals & rituals

 The historical sources mention Franciscan missionaries, Protestant Evangelical activists, and missionaries from SIL International.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They state that the Araona language has a dictionary and that portions of the Bible have been translated into Araona.

## Language & religion

Araona or Cavina is described as an indigenous language spoken by the Araona people. The language is considered vigorous, although knowledge of Spanish is increasing. Capachene and Machui are identified as dialects of either Araona or Cavineña. Araona has four vowels (/a/, /e/, /i/, /o/) and 19 consonants. Like other Tacanan languages, it has four periodic tense markers: diurnal *-tseiñe*, nocturnal *-sisa*, auroral *-huena*, and vesperal *-niapona*, with cognates in Cavineña.
