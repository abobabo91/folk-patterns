## Overview

The Carijona are a South American Indigenous group of Colombia whose language is Carijona, also called Karihona. The language is a moribund Cariban language, or a pair of languages, and was spoken by only 15 people as of 2015. Carijona numbered in the thousands in the 1840s, but war with the Witotoans and exploitation from the rubber industry led to virtual extinction. Some Carijona live among the Correguaje. Descendants of an Afro-Colombian named Salvador Perea and a Carijona woman also survived. The available sources place Carijona in Northwest Amazonia.

## Language & religion

Carijona, also called Karihona, is described as a moribund Cariban language of Colombia, or as a pair of languages. Derbyshire (1999) lists the varieties Hianacoto-Umaua and Carijona proper as separate languages.
