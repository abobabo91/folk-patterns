## Overview

The Pemon or Pemón (Pemong) are an Indigenous people living in areas of Venezuela, Brazil, and Guyana. They are part of the larger Cariban language family and include groups such as the Arekuna, Ingarikó, Kamarakoto, Tualipang, Mapoyo, and Macushi/Makushi. In Venezuela, Pemon communities live in the Gran Sabana grassland plateau of Bolívar State, a landscape marked by tabletop mountains and the Canaima National Park. In Brazil, they live in villages within the Terras Indígenas São Marcos and Raposa Serra do Sol. Their society is described as egalitarian and decentralized and based on trade. Tourism, Christianity, missionary activity, and historical events have strongly affected Pemon language, belief, and daily life.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe religious and mythological practices. Pemon have polytheistic beliefs, and figures including Makunaima, Kanaima, Iwarrika, and Sigu are important in their culture. The shaman plays an important role in religious practice and meets the god during hallucinogenic rituals in which tobacco and a specific diet are used.

Pemon mythology describes gods residing in the tabletop mountains of the grassland region, called tepui. These mountains are off-limits to the living because they are also home to ancestor spirits called mawari. Missionary work affected Pemon belief, and Jechikrai is described as the Pemon adaptation of Jesus Christ.

The sources also document the history of the Kueka stone. In 1999, Wolfgang Kraker von Schwarzenfeld arranged the transport of a red stone boulder weighing about 35 metric tons from Venezuela’s Canaima National Park to Berlin Tiergarten for a “global stone” project. A dispute then continued over the Pemon attempt to recover the stone, and on 16 April 2020 it was returned to Venezuela.

## Oral tradition & literature

Pemon mythology is described as a rich mythic tradition merged with present Christian faiths. Important myths concern the origins of the sun and moon, the creation of the tepui mountains, and the activities of the creator hero Makunaíma. Makunaíma is described either as an individual or as a group of brothers, sons of the sun Wei and a woman made of clay.

In the story, Makunaíma searches for his father, who has been held captive by malevolent spirits. After finding him, Makunaíma discovers the “tree of the world” and cuts it down. The stump becomes Mount Roraima. The falling tree causes a cataclysm involving floods and fire, after which humans are recreated by Makunaíma. Makunaíma also turns people and animals into stone, forming part of the Pemon description of the natural world.

The first non-native person identified in the sources as having seriously studied Pemon myths and language was the German ethnologist Theodor Koch-Grunberg, who visited Roraima in 1912.

## Language & religion

Pemon, called Pemón in Spanish, is an Indigenous language of the Cariban family. It is spoken mainly in Venezuela, particularly in the Gran Sabana region of Bolívar State, and also in southeastern Venezuela, the Roraima State of Brazil, and Guyana. The language includes dialects or varieties called Arecuna or Arekuna, Camaracota, Camaracoto, Ingariko or Ingarikó, and Taulipang or Taurepan. The Pemon article identifies Arekuna, Kamarakoto, and Taurepang as the three principal dialects.

Pemon was an oral language until the 20th century. Catholic missionaries, especially Armellada and Gutiérrez Salazar, worked on dictionaries and grammars. The Latin alphabet has been used, with diacritic signs representing phonemes not existing in Spanish. Pemon syntax is described as SOV with alternation to OVS.

Pemon belief combines polytheistic traditions with Christian influence. Makunaima is regarded as the most important god because he created the tribe, while Iwarrika is associated with flooding the earth. Many Pemon people became a Catholic majority population, while Chamanes continue to practice the ancestral religion, mostly for healthcare.
