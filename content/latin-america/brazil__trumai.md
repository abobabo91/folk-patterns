## Overview

The Trumai, also known as Trumaí, and using the native name *Ho kod ke*, are an Indigenous people of Brazil who currently reside within the Xingu Indigenous Park in Mato Grosso. They settled in the upper Xingu River in the 19th century after moving from the region between the Xingu and Araguaia Rivers following attacks from another people. In 2014 their population was 258; earlier figures were 97 in 2011, 120 in 2006, and 26 in 1966. They live in four villages—Terra Preta, Boa Esperança, Steinen, and Terra Nova—and some families live near the Leonardo Villas-Bôas Post and the Diauarum Indigenous Post. Their language is an isolate and is severely endangered. In folk-cultural terms, the Trumai are associated with farming, the jawari ritual, and ritual singing traditions.

## Music & performance

The Trumai are associated in the sources with ritual singing traditions. De Vienne conducted ethnographic studies focusing on language and communication in the community, including joking and ritual singing traditions.

The Trumai are also identified as the people who introduced the jawari ritual, called *hopep* in the Trumai language. Along with the kwarup, jawari is described as one of the most important inter-tribal festivals in the Upper Xingu cultural complex.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources describe joking and ritual singing traditions as subjects of ethnographic study.

Buell Quain spent four months with the Trumai and gathered information on many aspects of their culture and community. Later documentation included ethnographic work by De Vienne focused on language and communication, and linguistic documentation associated with the Documentation of Endangered Languages project. These sources concern research and preservation rather than a described body of Trumai literature.

## Language & religion

Trumai is an endangered language isolate spoken by the Trumai people in the Xingu reserve along the Upper Xingu River in central Brazil. It is highly divergent from other South American languages. Most Trumai are fluent in languages of wider communication, and children are not learning Trumai well as a first language. Children are becoming native speakers of Awetï, Suyá, or Portuguese, and Portuguese is popular among the local population.

The language has been described through work on grammar, phonology, morphology, syntax, and language documentation. Trumai has ergative–absolutive case assignment, with absolutive, ergative, and dative argument types. Its pronouns distinguish person, gender, number, and listener inclusion or exclusion in the first-person plural. Number includes singular, dual, and plural.
