## Overview

The Ye'kuana live in the Venezuelan states of Bolívar and Amazonas and across the border in Brazil. They speak a Cariban language with several dialects and alternative names. Their material culture centers on plaited cane, feather work, and wooden objects that carry their mythology. Cassava and hunting are central to their foodways.

## Material culture

Ye'kuana objects are made from cane, bamboo, feathers, wood and calabash, with designs using black stain, natural colors, and red, yellow and blue from birds.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Plaited split cane carries the group's figural design vocabulary, with humanoid and animal figures worked into geometric grounds.

- **Storage baskets** — Cylindrical baskets woven in double thickness with figures and herringbone patterns in black against natural cane.
- **Food trays** — Flat plaited trays for cassava, brown and black with geometric designs on the interior surface.
- **Dart quivers** (*madana*) — Bamboo cylinders wrapped in palm leaf, bound with brown cotton, holding split cane darts with cotton wadding tips.

### Clothing & dress

Men wear paired brown cotton armbands fitted with red drawstrings and separate feather ornaments mounted on them.

- **Cotton armbands** — Woven brown cotton bands with red drawstrings, worn as a pair by men.
- **Feather armband ornaments** — Bundles of toucan feathers in black-red, black-yellow, or all three colors, bound with fibre cord and mounted on armbands.
- **Macaw headdress** — Band of split cane mounted with trimmed macaw feathers in blues, greens, reds and yellows, with two tall red feathers rising from it.

### Architecture

House forms and settlement layout are not described in available sources, though ethnographic studies from the 1960s-70s addressed village formation.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ye'kuana everyday objects are made from wood, cane, bamboo and calabash, with no ceramics or metalwork recorded.

- **Calabash dipper** — Interior blackened with cadimani soot fixed with tree resin, with a fibre cord threaded through the handle for hanging.
- **Wooden paddle** — Carved from mototo hardwood with a semicircular handle, used for river transport.
- **Shaman's fork implement** — Fork-shaped ceremonial object with handle carved as a shaman in trance, finished with natural and black-stained plaiting.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment is made from forest birds and animals, combining teeth, feathers and fibre into composite pieces.

- **Peccary tooth necklace** — Peccary teeth strung on fibre cord with terminal tassels of truncated feathers and a bird's head.
- **Red and blue feather ornament** — Cluster of iridescent red and blue feathers bound with vegetable fibre, with small yellow, red and white flowers at the base.
- **Bird headdress** — Made from eleven gutted birds with wings and heads removed, showing black backs and vivid white, yellow and red breasts tied to a wooden bar.

## Music & performance

A bamboo flute with a long slit and two holes is the only instrument documented; broader musical and performance contexts are not described.

- **Bamboo flute** — Long slit with two holes near the top, lower end carved with zigzag designs.

## Dance & theatre

Dance and dramatic performance are not described, though feather headdresses were made for wearing in public contexts.

## Festivals & rituals

Ritual specialists use carved objects; mythological beings directly enter the object world through representation.

- **Shaman's stool** — Carved wooden bench with concave seat and pierced sides, decorated as a jaguar with red sides and black chevrons and dots.
- **Dede bat emblem** (*Dede*) — Mythological bat with folded wings, carved at the centre of a feather headdress.
- **Kushu storage basket** (*Kushu*) — Plaited basket panel including a manifestation of the major deity Kushu among frog, baby and monkey figures.

## Foodways

Cassava is the staple, served on plaited trays; hunting with blow-guns provides protein.

- **Cassava tray** — Split cane food tray plaited in brown and black with geometric designs on the interior surface where food rests.
- **Blow-gun darts** — Nineteen split cane darts with cotton wadding-wrapped tips, stored in a bamboo quiver.

## Oral tradition & literature

Ye'kuana oral tradition has been documented in publication, with mythology legible in objects.

- **Guss oral tradition study** — 1986 publication on Ye'kuana oral tradition including texts in the language.
- **Ethnographic mythology studies** — 1960s-70s ethnographic work documented mythology, political structure and village formation.

## Language & religion

Ye'kuana is a Cariban language with real dialect variation; shamanic practice and named mythological beings are attested.

- **Caura dialect** — Dialect differing in which nouns accept plural markers.
- **Auaris dialect** — Dialect distinguished by sound correspondences, with rhotic where other dialects have a glide.
- **Venezuelan Indigenous Languages Alphabet orthography** — Official orthography devised in the 1970s and widely adopted in Bolívar state.

## Glossary

- *madana* — blow-gun dart quiver
- *cadimani* — black soot coating for calabash dippers
- *Dede* — mythological bat
- *Kushu* — major deity
- *mototo* — hardwood used for paddles
- *Cariban* — language family
- *Roraima* — Brazilian state bordering Venezuela
- *Bolívar* — Venezuelan state where Ye'kuana live
- *Amazonas* — Venezuelan state where Ye'kuana live
- *Auaris* — area across the border in Brazil
- *plaiting* — interlacing cane strips in geometric or figural patterns
- *peccary* — small wild boar hunted for meat and teeth
- *toucan* — tropical bird with colorful feathers
