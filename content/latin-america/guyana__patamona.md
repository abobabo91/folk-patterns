## Overview

The Patamona are an Amerindian people native to the Pakaraima Mountains of Guyana and northern Brazil. They are considered a sub-group of Kapon people and speak Kapóng, a Cariban language. About 5,000 members of this and closely related ethnic groups live in Guyana; a 1990 estimate recorded 5500, while FUNASA recorded 120 Patamona in Brazil in 2010. In Guyana, Patamona communities include Paramakatoi, Campbelltown, Micobie, Monkey Mountain, Princeville, Kato, and Orinduik along the Maú River. Their culture is associated with animist beliefs, cassava cultivation, fermented drinks, fishing, hunting, pottery, food preparation, and the cultural importance of Kaieteur Falls.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pottery was traded for cassava graters made by other Carib groups up until the 20th century.

## Festivals & rituals

 They do describe an Indigenous system of beliefs among the Carib tribes that dates back to the 16th century. In the Patamona animist religion, Knaima is a spirit that possesses people and turns them into evil beings. Kaieteur Falls is an important cultural site, and the Patamona name it after Old Kaie, a member of the Patamona tribe.

## Foodways

Common agricultural products include bitter cassava, yam, sweet potato, sugarcane, chili pepper, and squash. Bitter cassava is used to make *kasiri* and *pajuaru*: kasiri is a lightly fermented cassava drink consumed at breakfast, while pajuaru undergoes a longer fermentation and is a festive drink. Fish are often eaten and can be dry smoked.

Patamona culinary practices include preparation of the Guyanese pepperpot, called *tuma* in Patamona. Food is also steamed by packaging it in leaves and setting it beside a fire. The Patamona gather wild mushrooms for consumption, including *amanita perphaea*, also called *pulutukwe*; *Cookeina*, called *agubana*; *Lentinula boryana*, called *kapiokwok*; and certain *clavulina*, also known as *kunmudlutse* and *tepurumeng*.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They state that the Patamona call themselves the People of the Heavens and that remnants of their culture survive in Paramakatoi and near Kaieteur Falls.

## Language & religion

Kapóng is a Cariban language spoken mainly in Guyana, especially in the region of the Upper Mazaruni. It is also spoken in lowland tropical South America, particularly in Guyana, Brazil, and Venezuela. In Guyana, Kapóng is spoken in forests by the Mazaruni River Basin. Population centers include Kamarang, Jawalla, Waramadong, and Kako.

Kapóng has the dialects Akawaio, Ingarikó, and Patamona. The Macushi name of the language is Ingarikó. The language has subject-object-verb as its preferred word order, although word order can be flexible. It has no gender distinctions in its personal pronoun systems and noun affixes. The language’s vocabulary reflects beliefs in a higher spirit or god in the sky. The sources give *Kapóng* as “Sky People,” *akwalo* as “the spirit,” *akwa* as “God’s place,” *Waica* as “warrior,” *Taemogoli* as “grandfather,” *Kapo* as “in the sky,” and *Iopotari akuru* as “chief spirit.”

The Patamona follow an animist religion in which Knaima is a spirit that possesses people and turns them into evil beings.
