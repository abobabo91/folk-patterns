## Overview

The Wichí are a people of the Chaco, a dry flat forest across four countries. Their material culture is built almost entirely from plant fiber, wood, and animal parts. Museum records show a repertoire of twined and plaited bags, fiber garments, carved pipes, and hunting equipment. The Chaco forest is being cleared for commercial plantations and its water poisoned.

## Material culture

Wichí material culture is built almost entirely from plant fiber, wood, and animal parts, with no ceramics or metalwork in the recorded collection.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Wichí bags and garments are twined or plaited from plant fiber and decorated with geometric patterns in earth tones and black.

- **Caraguata-fiber bags** (*caraguata*) — Bags twined or woven from caraguata fiber string, patterned with diamonds in beige, red-brown, and dark brown.
- **Corded carrying bags** — Bags woven of fiber cord with a carrying strap, patterned with lozenges in black on undyed ground; also used as baby-carriers.
- **Hexagon-patterned twined bag** — A bag twined of vegetable-fiber string with a pattern built from hexagons rather than the usual diamond grid.
- **Plaited fiber body garment** — A cylinder of plaited vegetable fiber with shoulder straps, ornamented with horizontal lines of black pattern.

### Clothing & dress

Wichí dress in these records consists of plaited fiber cylinders worn as body garments and war shirts, ornamented with geometric patterns.

- **Plaited body garment** — A sleeveless cylinder of plaited vegetable fiber held up by two shoulder straps, with horizontal black pattern lines on the surface.
- **War shirt** — A cylindrical plaited garment accented with dark brown stripes, worn for fighting.
- **Feather war head-dress** — A head covering made of feathers, part of the war outfit along with the war shirt and clubs.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Wichí everyday objects are made from wood, animal shell, and plant material; no ceramics or metalwork appear in the records.

- **Armadillo-shell water vessel** — A water container made from armadillo shell, used to carry water in the dry Chaco forest.
- **Carved wooden tobacco pipes** — Pipes carved from wood in several forms, ornamented with incised and painted diamonds and zigzags.
- **Hunting arrows** — Bundled arrows made of reed and wood, fletched with feathers and notched at the bottom, used with a bow.
- **War clubs** — Clubs carved from a single piece of wood, thicker at one end, used in fighting.
- **Wooden needle** — A needle used in bag-making, preserved with an unfinished bag showing the netting in progress.

### Jewelry & body adornment

- **Feather war head-dress** — A head covering made of feathers, part of the fighting outfit worn with the war shirt and clubs.

## Glossary

- *caraguata* — A plant whose fiber is twined or woven into bags
- *Wichí* — The people and their language; also used in museum accessioning
- *Chaco* — The dry, flat, arid forest region where the Wichí live
- *twined* — A weaving technique using twisted fibers or cords
- *plaited* — Woven by interlacing three or more strands in a regular pattern
- *lozenge* — A diamond-shaped geometric pattern
- *incised* — Cut or carved into a surface
- *war shirt* — A plaited fiber garment worn for fighting
- *war head-dress* — A feathered head covering worn in fighting
- *tobacco pipe* — A carved wooden instrument for smoking tobacco
