## Overview

The Chichimeca Jonaz are an Indigenous people of Mexico living in Guanajuato and San Luis Potosí. In Guanajuato, they live in a community in San Luis de la Paz municipality, at 2,070 m above sea level. They call this settlement *Rancho Úza* or *Misión Chichimeca*. The Chichimeca Jonaz are descendants of the Pame people, who fought in the Chichimeca War from 1550 to 1590 as part of the Chichimeca Confederation. The 2000 General Census recorded 1,641 people who named themselves as speakers of the Chichimeca Jonaz language: 1,433 lived in Guanajuato and 115 in San Luis Potosí. The 2020 census recorded 2,354 speakers in Mexico, while the Secretariat of Economy recorded 2,653 speakers in San Luis de la Paz.

## Language & religion

Chichimeca Jonaz, also called Chichimeca or Chichimeco, is an Indigenous language of Mexico spoken in several communities within the municipality of San Luis de la Paz, notably in Misión de Chichimecas. It forms its own branch of the Oto-Pamean languages within the Oto-Manguean language family. Its closest relative is the Pame language. Native speakers call themselves *éza’r* and their language *úza’*, also appearing as *uzá’* or *úzá’*. Natives also call themselves chichimeco, chichimeca, or meco.

The language is spoken in numerous towns and communities in San Luis de la Paz, Guanajuato, including Berlín, Buenavista, Cerro Blanco, Colonia Benito Juárez, El Desmonte, La Huerta, Misión de Chichimecas, San Antonio Primero, San Ignacio, and San Pedro de los Pozos. Earlier locations included Misión Arnedo, Misión de las Palmas, Misión de Santa Rosa, San Pedro Tolimán, and Villa Colón.

Chichimeca Jonaz uses an alphabet with 36 consonants, 6 vowels, and 6 nasal vowels. The language has high and low tones: the low tone is not marked, while the high tone is marked with an acute accent. The orthography uses a saltillo or glottal stop, and the letter ɇ represents /æ/.
