## Overview

The Karitiana, also called Caritiana, are an Indigenous people of Brazil whose reservation lies in the western Amazon. The Karitiana reserve is 95 kilometres south of Porto Velho, in the State of Rondônia. The sources give a population of 320 people, while the Karitiana language article states that 210 of them speak Karitiana and gives an alternative figure of 400 according to Cláudio Karitiana. The people subsist by farming, fishing and hunting and are described as having almost no contact with the outside world. Their language is Karitiana, also known as Caritiana or Yjxa, an Arikém language belonging to the Tupi stock. The sources provide linguistic and ethnographic documentation.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do document efforts to preserve knowledge of the language and culture: a literacy project in the 1990s made 24 students literate, and written documentation of the culture and audio recordings were created. The project ended in 1997 because of a lack of permanent funding. David Landin compiled a Karitiana dictionary, and later linguistic studies documented phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics, and other aspects of the language.

## Language & religion

Karitiana, also known as Caritiana or Yjxa, is spoken in the State of Rondônia, Brazil, and belongs to the Arikém language family of the Tupi stock. It is described as the only surviving language in that family after Kabixiâna and Arikém became extinct. The first recorded contact with the Karitiana dates to 1907, when a survey by Cândido Rondon indicated that they were already working for Bolivian rubber tappers. Systematic contact with Caucasians began in the 1950s through the intervention of ISA and Roman Catholic Salesian missionaries. The sources describe bilingualism in Karitiana and Portuguese, a vulnerable status assigned by UNESCO because of the low number of speakers and proximity to Porto Velho, and language lessons held by Indigenous teachers from 2005.

Karitiana has short and long vowels, oral and nasal vowels, and the consonants [tʃ] and [ʔ]. Its morphology includes agglutinative verbal affixes, a binary future/non-future tense suffix system, aspect suffixes, desiderative inflection, an optional evidentiality suffix, and a verb-focus system. The language has no pronoun distinction between male and female. Its noun phrases appear as bare nouns without inflection marking number or definiteness; the context determines whether they refer to one or more entities, definite or indefinite. Numerals receive the oblique suffix *-t*, and the numeral system consists of units from 1 to 5, with larger numbers expressed through combinations of these units.
