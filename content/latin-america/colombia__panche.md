## Overview

The Panche, also called the Tolima, were an Indigenous group in what is now Colombia. They inhabited the lower-altitude southwestern areas of Cundinamarca and the northeastern areas of Tolima, near the Magdalena River. Their territory was bounded by the Río Negro and Guarinó River to the north, and by the Coello and Fusagasugá Rivers to the south. At the time of the Spanish conquest, more than 30,000 Panche lived in what became the New Kingdom of Granada. They were organized in a loose confederation of subgroups, whose names remain associated with municipalities of Cundinamarca. Their language is extinct and unclassified. The Panche are documented through archaeology, warfare, body adornment, rock art, burial practices, and museum-held artefacts.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The Panche are described as walking partially naked and wearing earrings, feathers, and golden pieces. The sources do not distinguish everyday and ceremonial clothing, or provide names for garments, head coverings, belts, or footwear. No specific dress tradition is documented for men or women.

### Architecture

They mention *bohíos* only in connection with a report that conquered enemies’ heads were hung there; later research found no evidence for cannibalism and attributed cannibalistic ideas to Spanish conquistadores.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources identify golden pieces as part of Panche adornment and state that more than 2,000 artefacts from the Panche are stored in the Museo del Oro in Bogotá.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Panche adornment included earrings, feathers, and golden pieces. The sources also state that, like other Indigenous peoples of the Americas such as the Guane, the Panche performed cranial deformation. No further jewellery types, hair practices, tattoos, or ritual functions are documented.

## Festivals & rituals

They do document burial practices: unlike neighbouring groups, the dead were not oriented in a fixed position.

## Foodways

According to Pedro Simón, the Panche performed cannibalism on parts of conquered enemies, and some sources stated that they ate everything except the heads, which they hung in their *bohíos*. Later research by various researchers found no evidence for cannibalism and attributed these ideas to the Spanish conquistadores.

## Oral tradition & literature

Early knowledge about the Panche was compiled by the scholar Pedro Simón. The Panche language article states that, according to Pedro Simón, the word *panche* in their own language meant “cruel” and “murderer.”

## Language & religion

The Panche language is extinct and unclassified because of a lack of attestation.
