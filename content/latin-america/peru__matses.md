## Overview

The Matsés, also known as the Mayoruna, are an Indigenous people of the Peruvian and Brazilian Amazon. Their traditional homelands lie between the Javari and Galvez rivers, and Matsés communities are located along the Javari River basin, which forms part of the border between Brazil and Peru. Approximately 3,200 Matsés speak Matsés, a language belonging to the Panoan family. In the last thirty years, they have become largely settled in permanent forest settlements while continuing to rely mainly on hunting and gathering. Their culture is closely connected to detailed knowledge of rainforest plants and animals, hunting with bows and arrows, an animist worldview, and the continued use of the Matsés language in community life.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

### Architecture

They state that the Matsés have become largely settled in permanent forest settlements and that each village has its own chief, but provide no further account of the built environment.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources do mention that Matsés people historically recovered firearms and metal tools during warfare, and that basic medical tools in their communities include microscopes, needles, and thermometers, but these references do not constitute descriptions of Matsés material traditions. Traditionally, the principal hunting technology described is the bow and arrow.

## Music & performance

The Matsés language is described as vigorous and spoken by all age groups in Matsés communities. Linguistic documentation includes collections of texts and word lists, and the work of Fields and Kneeland includes phonological and grammatical descriptions produced during approximately 1966 to 1981. These materials concern the language rather than a documented musical repertoire.

## Festivals & rituals

Marriage practices are described: Matsés families often practice polygamy, cross-cousin marriages are most common, and marriages are primarily between cousins, including a man marrying the daughter of his father’s sister.

In the Matsés worldview, there is no distinction between the physical and spiritual worlds, and spirits are present throughout the world. Animal spirits are believed to determine health and success in hunting. Matsés people are careful not to offend animal spirits and observe many taboos concerning the hunting of different animal species. Plants, especially trees, are associated with animal spirits; when a plant product is used as medicine, it is typically applied externally while the shaman talks to the associated animal spirit.

## Foodways

The sources used name one Matsés food: *chapo*, a sweet plantain beverage. They also state that the Matsés still rely on hunting and gathering for most of their subsistence and that their main source of income comes from selling peccary hides and meat.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do document linguistic and ethnographic preservation efforts. Missionaries from the Summer Institute of Linguistics produced the first descriptions of the Matsés language, while Fields and Kneeland published phonological descriptions, grammatical descriptions, collections of texts, and word lists. Kneeland developed an approximately 800-word Matsés-Spanish glossary with sample sentences, and Wise produced a Spanish-Matsés word list with approximately 150 entries.

Acaté Amazon Conservation is documenting and preserving Indigenous medicinal knowledge by completing the first traditional medicine encyclopedia written in the Matsés language. The sources also mention the ethnographic work of Steven Romanoff, Erikson, Matlock, Luis Calixto Méndez, Dan James Pantone, and Bjorn Svensson.

## Language & religion

Matsés, also referred to as Mayoruna in Brazil, is spoken by the Matsés in the border regions of Brazil and Peru. It belongs to the Panoan language family, within the larger Pano-Tacanan family. The sources identify Peruvian Matsés, Brazilian Matsés, and the extinct Paud Usunkid as dialects. Matsés is spoken by all age groups, and Brazilian Matsés communities identify themselves as monolingual because most children are nurtured and taught exclusively in Matsés. The sources state that Matsés has 21 distinctive segments, comprising 15 consonants and 6 vowels.

The sources describe an animist worldview in which physical and spiritual worlds are not distinguished. Animal spirits influence health and hunting success, and each plant is associated with an animal spirit. Shamans speak to the spirit associated with a medicinal plant.
