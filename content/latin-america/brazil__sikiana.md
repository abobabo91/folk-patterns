## Overview

The Sikiana are an Indigenous people living in Brazil, Suriname, and Venezuela. In Brazil, they live between the Cafuini River and the headwaters of the Turuna and Itapi, close to the border with Suriname. In Suriname, they live in Kwamalasamutu, while the group in Venezuela is probably extinct. The Sikiana language belongs to the Carib language family. The people in Suriname speak Tiriyó as a second language, and some Sikiana people in Venezuela speak Tiriyó. The language is also called Warikyana or Kashuyana and was spoken by about 12 people in Suriname in 2012.

## Language & religion

Sikiana, also called Warikyana or Kashuyana, is a Cariban language spoken by about 12 people in Suriname. The name Warikyana refers to Cariban-speaking groups living on the Trombetas River, called Kahuwini, meaning “sky-water” in Arawak. The “most influential” group of the Warikyana are the Kashuyana, who reside on the Cachorro River and call themselves Purehno. Other groups named in the sources include the Kahyana, Kahuyana, Yaskuriyana, Ingarüne, and Pawiyana, also called Pauxi or Pawitxi.
