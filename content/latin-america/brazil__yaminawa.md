## Overview

The Yaminawá—also called Iaminaua, Jaminawa, Yawanawa, and by other names—are an Indigenous people whose homeland is Acre, Brazil. They also live in Madre de Dios, Peru, and Pando, Bolivia. Linguists estimate that fewer than 1,600 people speak the Yaminawá language, which belongs to the Panoan language family. Yaminawá culture is documented in relation to language, community organization, rainforest protection, cultural revitalization, and relationships with the outside world. The Yawanawa community is led by Tashka and Laura Yawanawa, who have worked to increase Yawanawa territory, reinvigorate Yawanawa culture, and establish economically and socially empowering relationships with the outside world.

## Language & religion

Yaminawa is a Panoan language of western Amazonia spoken by the Yaminawá and some related peoples. It forms an extensive dialect cluster that includes Brazilian and Peruvian Yaminawa and several related varieties, including Yawanawá and Yora. Xinane Yura, described as a recently discovered variety, is spoken by a group contacted in the Kampa and Envira River Isolated Peoples Indigenous Territory, Acre, Brazil, during the 2010s. Yaminawa is polysynthetic and primarily suffixing, uses compounding, nasalization, and tone alternations, and exhibits split ergativity. Its ISO 639-3 code is YAA. Very few Yaminawá people speak Spanish or Portuguese, and their literacy rate is extremely low.
