## Overview

The Sanapaná are a nomadic people of the lower Gran Chaco of western Paraguay. Following the introduction of Mennonite settlements in the central Chaco in the 1930s, many nomadic groups became semi-settled near the Mennonites. The Sanapaná and Lengua were settled at La Esperanza mission, southeast of Filadelfia, near the Pan-American Highway. Sanapaná belongs to the Mascoian, or Enlhet-Enenlhet, language group. The Sanapaná call themselves *Nenhlet*, meaning “the people.” Their cultural documentation in the sources concerns mobility and settlement, multilingual interaction, and the continued transmission and use of their language.

## Language & religion

Sanapana is a Mascoian language of the Paraguayan Chaco spoken by the Sanapaná people. The language is classified within the Mascoian, or Enlhet-Enenlhet, languages, which are distributed in the Gran Chaco region and are considered to have developed from a dialect continuum. Around half of Sanapaná people speak Sanapana and call themselves *nenlhet*. The language continues to be transmitted to children in La Esperanza and Anaconda, where children grow up monolingual in Sanapana before attending school and learning Spanish. Sanapana is also reported in everyday use, including religious contexts. It uses the Latin alphabet and has three vowels and 13 consonants.
