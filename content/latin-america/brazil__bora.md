## Overview

The Bora are an Indigenous Amazonian people living in the western Amazon basin across three countries. Over the last forty years they have become a largely settled people in permanent forest settlements. They are known for their intimate knowledge of rainforest plants, especially trees, and for distinctive hand-crafted ornaments and wooden drums. The Bora suffered enslavement during the rubber boom and Putumayo genocide around the turn of the twentieth century, and their ancestral lands are now threatened by illegal logging.

## Material culture

Bora material culture is documented primarily through ornaments and wooden objects, with emphasis on hand-twisted cotton work and large wooden drums.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Bora used twisted cotton for ornament rather than woven cloth, a practice that distinguished them from neighbouring peoples.

- **Twisted-cotton feather headdress** — A headdress built on a base of coarse twisted cotton threads with yellow and red feathers set in a crescent around the head and a bunch of cotton threads hanging at the nape.

### Clothing & dress

The sources record Bora body ornament but not everyday or ceremonial clothing.

- **Necklaces** — Personal ornaments collected from northwestern Amazonas.
- **Wrist bands** — Made of snakeskin, collected from northwestern Amazonas.
- **Ear sticks** — Cane ornaments with designs burned into the material.

### Architecture

The Bora have lived in permanent forest settlements over roughly the last forty years.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Bora material culture includes wooden objects used for conflict and communication.

- **Bows and arrows** — The main weapons used in person-to-person conflict.
- **Wooden drums** — Large drums photographed on the Yaguasyacu river in 1941, made in at least two sizes with one identified as the male drum of a pair.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Bora body adornment consists of necklaces, wrist bands, ear ornaments and feathered headdresses, with no recorded tattooing or body paint.

- **Feather headdress** — Built on a base of coarse twisted cotton threads with yellow and red feathers in a crescent shape around the head.
- **Snakeskin wrist bands** — Paired wrist ornaments made from snakeskin.
- **Cane ear sticks** — Paired ear ornaments of cane with designs burned into the material.
- **Necklaces** — Personal ornaments worn around the neck.

## Music & performance

The Bora used large wooden drums, documented as objects but not as a musical repertoire or performance tradition.

- **Wooden drums** — Instruments made in at least two sizes, with one identified as the male drum by comparison with a companion pair.

## Dance & theatre

No sources document Bora dance or dramatic performance.

## Festivals & rituals

Bora social life is shaped by exogamy and an animist worldview that makes no distinction between the physical and spiritual world.

- **Exogamy** — Bora families marry outside the group.
- **Animist worldview** — Spirits are understood as present throughout the physical and spiritual world without distinction.
- **Rainforest plant knowledge** — Elaborate knowledge of plants and the complex importance of trees is treated as both practical and more than practical.

## Foodways

No sources document Bora food, cooking or drink.

## Oral tradition & literature

Bora has a written form developed by Wycliffe Bible Translators, and substantial documentation exists in published texts and annotated audio recordings.

- **Bora written form** — Developed by Wesley and Eva Thiesen with speakers in Brillo Nuevo on the Yaguasyacu river, including school books, New Testament translation, dictionary and grammar.
- **Bora folkloric texts** — A published collection of Bora folkloric texts exists.
- **Bora DoReCo corpus** — Audio recordings of narrative texts compiled by Frank Seifart with phone-level transcription, translations and morphological annotation.

## Language & religion

Bora is a tonal language belonging to the proposed Bora–Witoto family, with unique linguistic features including 350 classifiers—more than any other language so far described.

- **Bora language** (*Bora*) — Spoken in the western Amazon, it is tonal, contrasts vowel length, and has 350 classifiers.
- **Miraña** — Spoken along the Caquetá-Japurá river from Colombia into Brazil, it is 94 per cent mutually comprehensible with Bora proper.
- **Muinane** — Spoken on tributaries of the Caquetá in central Colombia, about 50 per cent mutually comprehensible with Bora.

## Glossary

- *tonal* — language that uses pitch to distinguish words
- *classifiers* — grammatical markers that categorize nouns
- *Bora–Witoto family* — proposed language family grouping Bora with Witoto
- *exogamy* — practice of marrying outside one's own group
- *animist* — worldview in which spirits are present throughout the physical and spiritual world
- *Yaguasyacu* — river in northeastern Peru where Bora live
- *Putumayo* — river in the region between Peru, Colombia and Brazil where Bora live
- *Caquetá* — river in the region where Bora and related peoples live
- *Brillo Nuevo* — village on the Yaguasyacu river where Bora language was written
- *Wycliffe Bible Translators* — organization that developed written Bora with Wesley and Eva Thiesen
- *Putumayo genocide* — atrocities and enslavement of Indigenous peoples during the rubber boom around the turn of the twentieth century
- *rubber boom* — period around the turn of the twentieth century when rubber extraction caused enslavement and abuse of Indigenous peoples
- *Miraña* — related language and people 94 per cent mutually comprehensible with Bora
- *Muinane* — related language 50 per cent mutually comprehensible with Bora
