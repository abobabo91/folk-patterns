## Overview

The Nukak people, also called Nukak-Makú, live between the Guaviare and Inírida rivers in the tropical humid forest on the fringe of the Amazon basin, in Guaviare Department, Colombia. They are nomadic hunter-gatherers with seasonal nomadic patterns and practice small-scale shifting horticulture. An estimated 210–250 Nukak live in provisional settlements at San José del Guaviare, while around the same number live nomadically in the Nukak Reservation. Their language is tonal and very closely related to Kakwa; its broader classification is uncertain, perhaps belonging to the Puinave-Maku family. Their material culture is closely connected with hunting, gathering, horticulture, mobility, and the use of forest plants.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Wopyi* — Nukak cover their encampments with leaves of *Phenakospermum guyannense* and palms.

Hammocks — Hammocks are made with fiber of the cumare palm, *Astrocaryum* sp.

Moorings — Moorings use *Heteropsis tenuispadix*, *Eschweilera* sp., and *Anthurium* sp.

Baskets — Baskets are made with *Heteropsis* spp.

Disposable bags — Disposable bags are made with *Ischnosiphon arouma* and *Heliconia* sp.

### Clothing & dress

The sources mention men’s loincloths made from *Couratari guianensis*. Body painting is associated with dyes made from *achiote* (*Bixa orellana*) and *carayurú* (*Arrabidaea chica*).

### Architecture

Nukak encampments are covered with leaves of *Phenakospermum guyannense* and palms. Their movements are seasonal, and groups form different settlements for harvesting and hunting according to climatic seasonal changes and the security situation.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Until 1990, the Nukak practiced small-scale pottery. They produced a small kind of pot to carry during travels and a larger kind to leave as supplies at camping sites; they now prefer metallic pots. They make blades with piranha teeth but have also adapted to metals. Their tools include blowguns, bows, darts, javelins, baskets, bowls, arrows, harpoons, and axe ends. Bowls may be made from *Crescentia cujete* and *Lagenaria siceraria*. When they lack matches or lighters, they use *Pausandra trianae* to produce fire.

### Jewelry & body adornment

They mention body painting using *achiote* (*Bixa orellana*) and *carayurú* (*Arrabidaea chica*) dyes, and perfumes made from *Myroxylon* sp. and *Justice pectoralis*.

## Dance & theatre

The Nukak practice *entiwat*, a ritual in which groups dance face to face, strike and verbally injure one another, and finally embrace while weeping, remembering their ancestors, and expressing affection.

## Festivals & rituals

They describe an initiation ritual through which a man must pass before seeking a partner; during it, he endures trials and consumes a hallucinogen, *Virola* sp. Tobacco (*Nicotiana tabacum*) is harvested for ritual uses. The *entiwat* ritual brings different territorial groups together, while the sources also describe marriage practices, pregnancy-related temporal polyandry, and the taboo against discussing dead people.

## Foodways

Nukak food acquisition combines hunting, fishing, gathering, and small-scale shifting cultivation. Men hunt with blowguns whose darts are coated with curare, called *manyi*, and use javelins made from *Socratea exorrhiza* palm wood. They hunt monkeys, birds, peccaries, and spectacled caimans; they consume caiman eggs. They also capture rodents, armadillos, tortoises, frogs, crabs, shrimps, snails, palm-weevil larvae called *mojojoy* or *mun*, and larvae of wasps and caterpillars.

Fishing uses cord and metal hooks, as well as bow and arrow, harpoons, traps, and baskets called *mei*, or water cages. The Nukak also use *nuún*, the root of a *Lonchocarpus* sp., dissolved in streams to stun fish.

Gathered foods include honey from twenty species of bees and many fruits, including palm fruits. Crops include sweet potatoes, taros, yams, manioc, peach palms, pineapple, chili pepper, bananas, sugar cane, and several fruit trees.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that Nukak speak a tonal language and that the language is very closely related to Kakwa. In 2006, one migrant, Pia-pe, was quoted expressing a wish to join the “white family” without forgetting the words of the Nukak.

## Language & religion

Nukak is a tonal language with six oral and six nasal vowels, eleven consonant phonemes, and two tonemes: high and rising. Its default word order is subject–object–verb; it is agglutinative, and nouns are marked for gender, number, and case. The language is very closely related to Kakwa and has an uncertain broader classification, perhaps as part of the Puinave-Maku family. They do document ritual uses of tobacco, an initiation involving a hallucinogen from *Virola* sp., ancestor remembrance during *entiwat*, and the taboo against discussing dead people.
