## Overview

The Huarpes or Warpes are an Indigenous people of Argentina who live in the Cuyo region. They settled in permanent villages beginning in the 5th century CE, with approximately 50 to 100 people in a village. Their territory extended from the Jáchal River in the north to the Diamante River in the south, and between the Andes and the Conlara Valley in San Luis. It included areas of the present provinces of San Luis, Mendoza, San Juan and the north of Neuquen Province. The Huarpe language family was historically associated with central Argentina and consisted of at least two closely related languages, Allentiac and Millcayac. Huarpe communities practiced agriculture, cultivated corn, beans, squash and quinoa, and adopted llama ranching and the Quechua language after 1480.

## Language & religion

Huarpe, also called Warpe, was a small, extinct language family of central Argentina, historically associated with Cuyo Province. It included at least two closely related languages, Allentiac, also called Alyentiyak or Huarpe, and Millcayac, also called Milykayak. Puntano of San Luis was a third language that was not documented before the languages became extinct. Varieties listed in the sources include Oico or Holcotian, Orcoyan or Oscollan, Chiquiyama, Tuluyame or Puelche algarrobero, Michilenge or Puntano, Olongasto and Comechingon. The sources describe no Huarpe religious system, spiritual practices, historical script or contemporary religious landscape.
