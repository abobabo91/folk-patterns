## Overview

The Itonama are an ethnic group in northeastern Bolivia, associated with the current Mamoré and Iténez Provinces in Beni Department. They numbered 16,158 in 2012, including 1,249 people who claimed to speak Itonama, although the language was considered extinct by 2023. The last speakers resided in Magdalena, on the western bank of the Itonama River, a tributary of the Iténez River. Itonama is an extinct language isolate once spoken in the Amazonian lowlands, on the Itonomas River and Lake. The sources provide limited information about Itonama material and intangible culture; they chiefly document the group’s location, population, language, linguistic classification, and the decline of the language.

## Language & religion

Itonama is an extinct language isolate. It was once spoken by the Itonama people in the Amazonian lowlands of north-eastern Bolivia, including the area around the Itonomas River and Lake. The language is polysynthetic, head-marking, and verb-initial, with an accusative alignment system and an inverse subsystem in independent clauses; dependent clauses have straightforward accusative alignment. Its nominal morphology lacks case declension and adpositions, while its verbal morphology includes body-part and location incorporation, directionals, evidentials, and verbal classifiers. The language was considered “virtually extinct” in 2023: only a few elderly people in Magdalena could remember a few words and phrases, although 1,249 people claimed to speak it in 2012. The sources state that the Itonama have been Christian for the past 300 years.
