## Overview

The Aparai or Apalai are an Indigenous people of Brazil who live in Amapá and Pará states. A small community is located in French Guiana, in Antécume-Pata. The main group lives along the East Paru River in Brazil, within Tumucumaque Indigenous Park and the East Paru Indigenous Land. Other groups live along the Maroni River in French Guiana and the Tapanahoni River in Suriname. The population numbered 450 in 1993 and 564 in 2014. The Aparai speak a Carib language, and most are multilingual. Historically, they were sedentary slash-and-burn farmers, hunters, and gatherers; in the 20th century, their subsistence shifted toward craftwork as they adapted to modern Brazil and the cash economy.

## Language & religion

Aparai is a Carib language and one of the Karib languages. Most Aparai people are multilingual and many speak Aparai, Wayana, Portuguese, and Tiriyó, as well as Wajãpi, Aluku, and Criollo.
