## Overview

The Mawé, also known as the Sateré or Sateré-Mawé, are an Indigenous people of Brazil living in the state of Amazonas. Their estimated population is about 16,312 according to Siasi/Sesai in 2020. The Sateré-Mawé were the first to domesticate and cultivate guaraná, a popular stimulant. Their name combines *Sateré*, meaning “caterpillar of fire,” and *Mawé*, meaning “intelligent and curious Parrot.” They are also called Maué, Mawé, Mabue, Maragua, Sataré, Andira, and Arapium. Their language belongs to the Tupian family. The available sources document language, guaraná cultivation, and initiation rites.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They mention only a glove made from cashew leaves, used during the Sateré-Mawé initiation rite. Hundreds of bullet ants are woven into the glove with their stingers facing inward, and the glove resembles a large oven mitt.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The initiation rite involves a glove made from cashew leaves and a natural sedative used to render the ants unconscious.

### Jewelry & body adornment

During initiation, charcoal is coated on the hands as the only described form of protection from the ants’ stinging.

## Festivals & rituals

The principal ritual described in the sources is the Sateré-Mawé initiation rite involving bullet ant stings. Men of the community gather the ants from their homes, while women and children gather cashew leaves. The ants are submerged in a natural sedative and then woven into a cashew-leaf glove. After the ants regain consciousness, a young male initiate places the glove on his hand and attempts to keep it on for ten minutes.

The rite is connected with becoming a warrior. The ant venom causes severe and intense pain, and the initiate’s hand and part of his arm become temporarily paralyzed. He may hallucinate and shake uncontrollably for days. Charcoal is applied to the hands, supposedly to confuse the ants and inhibit their stinging. To fully complete the initiation, boys must undergo the ordeal 20 times over several months or sometimes even years.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Wikipedia article lists works concerning Sateré-Mawé mythology, storytelling, kinship, ritual, and Christianity, but the supplied source material does not summarize their contents. A grammar book for the Sateré-Mawé language was developed in 1986.

## Language & religion

The Mawé speak the Sateré-Mawé language, also known as Sateré or Mabue. It belongs to the Tupian family. The language is spoken by 7,000 Mawé people, many of them monolingual. They do mention initiation rites and list a work on the modes and effects of Christianity among Indigenous peoples.
