## Overview

The Chorotí are an indigenous people of northwestern Argentina whose material culture is built from the dry Chaco environment: chaguar plant fiber, gourd, bone, wood, reed, leather, shell and feathers. They speak Chorote, a Matacoan language split into two distinct varieties by modern linguists. Museum collections document their textiles, clothing, musical instruments, and body ornaments, but little else about their practices or beliefs.

## Material culture

Chorotí material culture draws from locally available plants, animals and natural materials of the Chaco, combined into functional and ceremonial objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Chaguar plant fiber is the textile staple, worked into nets, tunics and cordage alongside wool woven in brown and beige stripes.

- **Chaguar net bags** — Bags worked from chaguar plant fiber, also used to make fishing nets.
- **Chaguar tunic** — A war shirt made of chaguar fiber used in ceremonial and male dress.
- **Striped wool cloak** — Two rectangles of wool woven with brown and beige stripes, sewn together lengthwise with hemmed edges.

### Clothing & dress

Chorotí dress combines chaguar fiber, wool, leather, cloth and shell into tunics, cloaks, belts and caps.

- **War shirt** (*chaguar tunic*) — A chaguar fiber tunic with ceremonial or martial function.
- **Striped wool cloak** — The outer garment, made from two striped wool panels joined lengthwise.
- **Leather belt with fringe** — A belt accessory made of leather and carrying a fringe.
- **Shell-and-cloth cap** — A cap combining cloth with shell material.

### Architecture

No information on Chorotí architecture is documented in the available sources.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pottery water vessels and gourd containers serve everyday needs, while bone and wood tools handle work and household tasks.

- **Pottery water vessels** — Bottle-jars of pottery combined with cord, made to be carried or suspended.
- **Gourd spoon** — A gourd carved into a spoon for eating.
- **Gourd water scoop** — A gourd shaped for drawing or carrying water.
- **Bone awl and tattooing tool** — A bone instrument that served as both a work awl and a body-marking tool.
- **Fish-jawbone lice comb** — A comb made from the lower jawbone of a fish.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ornaments are made from feathers, shell, wood, leaf, skin and imported glass beads, worn on the head, neck, wrists, ankles, ears and fingers.

- **Turkey feather head ornament** — Two turkey feathers joined at the shaft ends with the vanes cut away to leave triangles along the shaft.
- **Shell disc necklace** — Equal-sized circular shell discs strung on a single fiber strand.
- **Down and cloth anklets** — A pair of circlets made from down, cloth and vegetable fiber.
- **Wooden ear-plugs** — Pairs of wooden cylinders, each with a hole carved in one side.
- **Lizard-skin finger ring** — A ring made from lizard skin.

## Music & performance

Chorotí instruments are aerophones and rattles, with whistles of bone, reed and wood and gourd rattles for ritual use.

- **Bone whistle** — A bone whistle with a single hole carved near one end.
- **Reed whistle** — A reed whistle with a mouthpiece at the top, three holes on the lower half, and the top sealed with resin.
- **Breast whistle** — A wooden ovoid worn on the breast, incised with triangles, hung from a fiber band beaded with glass and shell.
- **Death dancer's rattle** — A gourd rattle incised with geometric designs, carried by a dancer in mortuary rites.

## Dance & theatre

Dance is documented only through death dancer's rattles used in mortuary performance.

## Festivals & rituals

The only documented ritual is a mortuary rite involving a death dancer who carries gourd rattles.

- **Death dancer mortuary rite** — A mortuary performance in which a death dancer carries incised gourd rattles.

## Foodways

Chorotí food practices are reflected in equipment for fishing, water transport and fire-making.

- **Plant-fiber fishing net** — A net made from spun chaguar plant-fiber string for catching fish.
- **Pottery water vessels with cord** — Bottle-jars rigged with cord for transport and storage of water.
- **Wooden fire-stick** — A stick used by men for making fire.

## Oral tradition & literature

No Chorotí folktales, epics, proverbs or storytelling traditions are documented in available sources.

## Language & religion

Chorote is a Matacoan language with two recognized varieties, spoken in northwestern Argentina and across borders into Paraguay and Bolivia; religious practice is not documented.

- **Chorote language** (*Chorote, Yofúaha, Tsoloti*) — A Matacoan language with two distinct varieties, closely related to Wichí and spoken across northwestern Argentina, Paraguay, and southeastern Bolivia.

## Glossary

- *chaguar* — A plant fiber used for nets, string, tunics and cordage
- *Chorote* — The language of the Chorotí people; also Yofúaha and Tsoloti
- *Yofúaha* — An alternative name for the Chorote language
- *Tsoloti* — An alternative name for the Chorote language
- *Matacoan* — A language family to which Chorote belongs
- *Wichí* — The language most closely related to Chorote within the Matacoan family
- *chaguar tunic* — A war shirt made of chaguar fiber
- *death dancer* — A ritual performer in Chorotí mortuary rites who carries gourd rattles
- *gourd rattle* — A sound-making instrument made from gourd, used in death dancer rituals
- *breast whistle* — A wooden whistle worn on the chest, decorated with incised triangles and beaded fiber band
- *bone awl* — A bone tool serving both utilitarian and body-marking functions
- *lice comb* — A grooming tool made from fish jawbone
