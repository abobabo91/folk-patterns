## Overview

The Xokleng, also called Aweikoma and sometimes botocudos, are an Indigenous tribe of Brazil. Their territory is located mainly in the state of Santa Catarina. They were among the original inhabitants of Misiones Province in Argentina and are also found on the Ibirama, Posto Velho, and Rio dos Pardos reservations. Their language, Xokleng or Laklãnõ, is a Southern Jê language within Jê and Macro-Jê, closely related to Kaingang.

## Language & religion

Xokleng or Laklãnõ is a Southern Jê language within Jê and Macro-Jê, spoken by the Xokleng people of Brazil and closely related to Kaingang. The sources list the alternate names Socré, Chocré, Xocren, Bugre, Botocudo, Aweicoma, Cauuba, Caahans, Caagua, and Caaigua. They describe vowel off-glides in word-final position and phonological alternations involving nasal sounds, /v/, /ð/, /j/, and /l̃/.
