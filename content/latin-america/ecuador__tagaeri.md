## Overview

The Tagaeri are an eastern Waorani people living in Yasuni National Park, in the Ecuadorian Amazon Basin. They are named after one of their members, Tagae. Nearby Kichwa communities sometimes refer to them as Awashiri, meaning “high-ground people.” The Tagaeri live by hunting and foraging and have resisted outside contact, making them one of the so-called uncontacted peoples of the world. Their kin, the Taromenane, are another eastern Waorani group living in the same area. It is estimated that perhaps only 20–30 surviving Tagaeri remain. The sources describe their language only as being like that of the western Waorani and do not identify its language family.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe the Tagaeri as having separated from Waorani families in 1968 after refusing missionary settlement and as having lived in voluntary isolation since then. Attempts at contact by outside peoples have often been violently rebuffed. The sources mention attacks, inter-clan violence, missionary contact, and the threats posed by foreign diseases, illegal loggers, smugglers, settlers, and oil companies.

## Foodways

The sources state that the Tagaeri live a hunting and foraging lifestyle. They also report that eastern Waorani groups moved westward in the 1990s, partly to escape petroleum exploration and logging activity and possibly because of reduced game stocks.

## Language & religion

The sources say that the Tagaeri speak a language like that of the western Waorani, according to Curaray Kichwa who occasionally see them but avoid interacting with them. The article states that Tagae and his followers separated from Waorani families in 1968 after refusing missionary settlement and have since lived in voluntary isolation.
