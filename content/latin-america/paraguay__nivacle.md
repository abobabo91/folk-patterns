## Overview

The Nivaclé are an Indigenous people of the Gran Chaco. An estimated 13,700 Nivaclé people live in the President Hayes and Boquerón Departments in Paraguay, while approximately 200 live in the Salta Province of Argentina; a very small number live in Tarija, Bolivia. Nivaclé is an autonym meaning “human.” The people are also known as the Ashlushlay, Axluslay, Chulupí, and Nivaklé. They speak Nivaclé, a Matacoan or Mataco-Guaicuru language with Forest Nivaclé and River Nivaclé dialects. Their language is notable for complex phonology, morphology, clitics, demonstratives, directional marking, and unusual grammatical patterns. In the last 50 years, 15,000 Mennonites from Canada, Russia, and Germany have settled in traditional Nivaclé territory.

## Foodways

 The language article states that Nivaclé has different genitive noun classifiers for possessed domestic animals and possessed prey, referring to hunted animals.

## Oral tradition & literature

 A dictionary has been published for Nivaclé, and the Bible was translated into Nivaclé in 1995. The sources also list the Colección Gutiérrez study on the Nivaclé language and documentation projects associated with the Archivo DILA, the Endangered Languages Archive, and ELAR.

## Language & religion

Nivaclé is a Matacoan language spoken in Paraguay and Argentina by the Nivaclé. It is also known as Chulupí and Ashluslay; older sources use Ashluslé, Suhin, Sujín, Chunupí, Churupí, Choropí, and other variant spellings. Speakers are found in the Chaco, including Presidente Hayes Department and Boquerón Department in Paraguay and Salta Province in Argentina. Nivaclé has 21 consonants and six vowel qualities, including glottalized stops and affricates and the phoneme /k͡l/. It has Forest Nivaclé and River Nivaclé dialects.
