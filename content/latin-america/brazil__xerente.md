## Overview

The Xerente, also known as Sherenté, Xerentes, and Xerénte, are an Indigenous people of Brazil living in Tocantins. They are a Central Jê people related to the Xavante. In 2007, the group numbered 1813 members, and use of the native language was universal; most members were monolingual until age 5. The Xerente language, also called Xerénte or Akwẽ-Xerénte, belongs to the Akuwẽ branch of the Central Jê languages, within Jê and Macro-Jê. It is spoken in Tocantins between Rio do Sono and Rio Tocantins. Xerente social organization includes exogamous moieties associated with mythic heroes embedded in the sun and moon. Their villages were traditionally built in a semi-circular fashion, although society has largely assimilated Brazilian standards of organization.

## Festivals & rituals

The Xerente creation myth is based on a duality of mythic heroes embedded in the sun and the moon. This duality is reflected in a division between two exogamous moieties: the sun moiety, called *Doí*, and the moon moiety, called *Wahirê*. Each moiety consists of three or four clans.

In 2010, following the impact of the Lajeado Dam and the operation of the Programa de Compensação Ambiental Xerente, also known as PROCAMBIX, the Xerente attempted a class action suit seeking additional compensation for the dam’s continuing impacts. After a lengthy court case, a judge decided in 2013 in favour of the dam’s builders, Investco.

## Language & religion

Xerénte, or Akwẽ-Xerénte, is an Akuwẽ language of the Central Jê family, within Jê and Macro-Jê. It is spoken in Brazil by the Xerente people in Tocantins, between Rio do Sono and Rio Tocantins. In unstressed syllables, the vowels /i, ɨ, u, a/ are realized as [ɪ, ɨ̞, ʊ, ɐ]. The language also has documented phonological alternations involving /k/, /d/, /s/, /h/, /n/, and /ɾ/.

The sources state that nominative and emphatic forms are free morphemes functioning as subjects of transitive or intransitive sentences.
