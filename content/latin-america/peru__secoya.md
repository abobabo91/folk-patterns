## Overview

The Secoya, also known as Angotero, Encabellado, Huajoya, Piojé, and Siekopai, are an Indigenous people living in the Ecuadorian and Peruvian Amazon. They speak Secoya, also called Sieko Coca, Paicoca, and Airo Pãi, a Western Tucanoan language. The Peruvian population numbers about 700, while the language status of the Siona-Secoya group is threatened, with 680 speakers in Peru. The Secoya have lived for centuries between the Putumayo and Napo in what is now Colombia, Ecuador, and Peru. Their culture includes animistic legends, distinctive grammatical categories, independent political organization, and contemporary artistic work. The sources describe them in relation to the Siona and record historical pressures from the rubber boom, missionary activity, petroleum development, colonization, and assimilation.

An important academic work identified in the sources is William Vickers’s *Cultural adaptation to Amazonian habitats: the Siona-Secoya of eastern Ecuador*, published in 1976; the source notes that it describes the Sionas and Secoyas as a single group.

## Language & religion

Secoya, also known as Sieko Coca, Paicoca, and Airo Pãi, is a Western Tucanoan language spoken by the Secoya people of Ecuador and Peru. The sources also record the names Angoteros, Encabellado, Pioje, Santa Maria, and Angutera in linguistic and ethnographic publications. Angoteros are described as part of the Secoya, with only dialectal differences and no other communicative obstacles. The Siona of the Eno River are described as linguistically different from the Siona of the Putumayo and as having significant dialectal differences from Secoya, although they are still considered part of them. The language status of the Siona-Secoya group is threatened. Secoya grammar includes distinctions of person, number, gender, space, time, modality, and grammatical case. The sources mention animistic legends.
