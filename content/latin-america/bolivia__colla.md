## Overview

The Colla, also written Qulla or Kolla, are an Indigenous people of western Bolivia, northern Chile, and the western portions of Jujuy and Salta provinces in Argentina. They moved freely between the borders of Argentina and Bolivia. Most live in arid highlands, while their easternmost lands extend into the yungas, altitude forests at the edge of the Amazon rainforest. The 2004 Complementary Indigenous Survey reported 53,019 Qulla households living in Argentina. Several groups made up the Qulla people, including the Zenta and Gispira. Their history includes links proposed with the pre-Incan Tiwanaku Polity, resistance to Spanish invasion, struggles over land, and continuing participation in Argentine public life. Their language is Northwest Jujuy Quechua or Qulla, a dialect of South Bolivian Quechua.

## Material culture

### Architecture

 They identify Sillustani in Peru as a prehistoric Qulla cemetery with elaborate stone chullpas, but provide no further architectural description.

## Music & performance

The sources identify Micaela Chauque as an Argentine musician and name no instrument, song genre, performance setting, or musical tradition. No further information about Colla music or performance is provided.

## Festivals & rituals

 They record several historically significant collective actions: representatives of Qulla communities in Jujuy and Salta sent a note to the National Agrarian Council on 31 August 1945 demanding restitution of their lands; Edelmiro Julián Farrell signed an expropriation decree on 17 January 1946; Qulla people joined the Malón de la Paz in 1946; and on 19 March 1997 they regained legal possession of the Santiago Estate.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention Qulla traditions and historians including Thérèse Bouysse-Cassagne and Teresa Gisbert, as well as linguist Alfredo Torero, in connection with a proposed link to the pre-Incan Tiwanaku Polity.

## Language & religion

The Qulla speak Northwest Jujuy Quechua or Qulla, described as a dialect of South Bolivian Quechua. South Bolivian Quechua is a variety of Southern Quechua, one of the Quechuan languages. The Qulla of the northern Altiplano near Titicaca appear to have originally spoken Puquina, which is also identified as the likely main language of the Tiwanaku culture during the Middle Horizon period. The separate article on Colla language states that “Colla” or “Kolla” can refer either to the Aymara language or to neighboring Quechua dialects such as South Bolivian Quechua.
