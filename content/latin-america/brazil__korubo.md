## Overview

The Korubo, also known as Korubu and by the endonym *Dslala*, are a Panoan-speaking Indigenous people of Brazil who live in the lower Vale do Javari in the western Amazon Basin. The Flecheiros live in the Vale do Javari Indigenous Territory, an area covering 83,000 square kilometres (32,000 sq mi). The main tribe’s population is unknown but has been estimated from aerial reconnaissance of houses to be a few hundred individuals. Much of what is known about the group comes from the research of Brazilian explorer Sydney Possuelo, who first contacted the tribe in October 1996, and journalist Paul Raffaele. The Korubo are notable for their voluntary isolation, communal malocas, club-based hunting, body painting, and cultivation of bananas, manioc and maize.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources state that both men and women wear little clothing.

### Architecture

The Korubo often live inside large, communal huts known as *malocas*.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They identify the club as the Korubo hunting and weapon of choice and state that, aside from poison darts, the group uses no other ranged weapons.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Both men and women paint themselves with a red dye from the roucou plant.

## Foodways

The Korubo grow bananas, manioc and maize in clearings. They hunt spider monkeys, peccary, birds and wild pig, among other species.

## Language & religion

Korubo is a nearly extinct Panoan language spoken by the Korubo people of Brazil. It has two dialects, Korubo itself and moribund Chankueshbo, and has six vowels: /a, e, i, ɨ, o, u/.
