## Overview

The Huave, who call themselves *Ikoots* or *Kunajts*, are an Indigenous people of Mexico living on the sandspits of the Pacific Ocean in the Isthmus of Tehuantepec. Their communities include San Mateo del Mar, Santa María del Mar, San Dionisio del Mar, and San Francisco del Mar. They have lived in the Isthmus for more than 3,000 years, preceding the Zapotec people in settling the area. According to the 2000 census, 13,687 people declared themselves Huave speakers, although many non-speakers continue to identify as Huaves or *Mareños*. Huave is a language isolate spoken in four coastal villages. The sources emphasize fishing, agriculture, marine-product exchange, community organization, and the continued use and endangerment of the Huave language.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Huave families live in homes with thatched roofs.

## Music & performance

 They do record that, as of 2011, Radio Ikoots in San Mateo del Mar was broadcasting in Huave.

## Festivals & rituals

Male members of each Huave village belong to the *escalafón*, a community organization concerned with civic and religious affairs.

## Foodways

Many Huave people work as fishermen and agriculturalists, and Huave communities trade marine products with inland neighbors.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They mention *Cuentos Huaves III*, published by the Instituto Lingüístico de Verano, as the source of a passage used as a text sample in the description of Huave. Universities in different countries have also carried out fieldwork and revitalization projects in Huave communities.

## Language & religion

Huave, also spelled Wabe, is a language isolate spoken on the Pacific coast of the Mexican state of Oaxaca. It is known as Huave, *ombeayiüts*, or *umbeyajts*, depending on the dialect. In San Mateo del Mar, the Huave people call themselves *Ikoots*, meaning “us,” and call their language *ombeayiiüts*, meaning “our language.” In San Francisco del Mar, the corresponding terms are *Kunajts*, meaning “us,” and *umbeyajts*, meaning “our language.” Huave is spoken in San Francisco del Mar, San Dionisio del Mar, San Mateo del Mar, and Santa Catarina del Mar; the sources also name Santa María del Mar in descriptions of the four varieties. The varieties are San Dionisio del Mar Huave, San Francisco del Mar Huave, San Mateo del Mar Huave, and San María del Mar Huave, and speakers also speak Spanish. Huave remains in use in most domains of social life in at least one village but is an endangered language. The sources mention civic and religious affairs through the *escalafón*.
