## Overview

The Dâw are an Indigenous people of Brazil who live on the right bank of Rio Negro, in the Amazon rainforest area commonly known as Alto Rio Negro. They share this region with other Nadahup peoples, including the Nadëb, Nukak, and Hup, as well as Arawakan and Tucanoan peoples such as the Barasana and Tucano. Dâw is a self-designation meaning “people”; in literature and Alto Rio Negro, the group is also called Kamã, a name considered very pejorative. In 1984, the number of Dâw was 56, following a period when the population was close to extinction. The situation has since improved. Dâw is a Nadahup language, and many Dâw also speak Nheengatu and Portuguese.

## Language & religion

Dâw is a Nadahup language spoken by about one hundred Dâw people in the northwestern part of Amazonas, Brazil, in the area commonly known as Alto Rio Negro. An extinct variety, Kurikuria(r)í, named after the Curicuriari River, was sociolinguistically distinct but at least partially intelligible with Dâw. Dâw has 15 vowels and 25 consonants. Its stress is fixed on the last syllable of a word, while its tone system is analyzed as having either three or four tones. The community’s orthography is based on the Latin alphabet, with some correspondences from the Tukano language.
