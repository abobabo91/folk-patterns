## Overview

The Aweti are Indigenous Brazilians living in the Xingu Indigenous Park, near the headwaters of the Xingu River in Brazil. Their two villages lie between the Curisevo and Tuatuarí rivers, which flow into the Xingu further upstream. The principal village, Tazu’jyretam, has been inhabited since at least the 19th century and includes a small port; the other village is unnamed. Their population was 196 in 2011, compared with 140 in 2006. The Aweti belong to a multilingual upper-Xingu environment shaped by the movement of Indigenous peoples and by relocation following European colonialism. Their culture includes distinctive village organization, ritual practices, inter-tribal exchange, Huka-huka wrestling, and a language of the Tupian family.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Hammocks** — The Aweti trade hammocks made from Burití palm trees with other upper-Xingu groups.

No other pattern-bearing textile tradition is documented in the sources used.

### Architecture

Aweti villages are arranged with several huts surrounding a central plaza. The plaza is used for rituals, including funeral rites, and this arrangement is characteristic of villages in the area around the Xingu headwaters. A men's hut stands at the center of the village. It holds ritual flutes that women are not allowed to see.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources identify vegetable salts and hammocks made from Burití palm trees as goods traded by the Aweti in inter-tribal exchange.

## Music & performance

The Aweti men's hut is used for holding ritual flutes. These flutes are not allowed to be seen by women, making their storage and restricted visibility part of the village’s ritual organization.

The Aweti participate in inter-tribal rituals among the peoples of the upper Xingu. These exchanges take place alongside trade and marriage connections between members of different upper-Xingu cultural groups.

The sources also identify Huka-huka wrestling as a popular intertribal sport.

## Festivals & rituals

Aweti villages contain a central plaza used for rituals, including funeral rites.

The sources describe inter-tribal rituals among the peoples of the upper Xingu as common. They also state that marriage between members of different tribes is frequent among upper-Xingu cultural groups. No detailed account is provided of Aweti birth ceremonies, coming-of-age rites, weddings, funerals beyond the use of the central plaza, or other life-cycle practices.

The men's hut has a specific ritual function: it holds flutes that women are not permitted to see.

## Foodways

The Aweti fish, hunt, and farm. Their primary crops are maize and manioc. These activities form the subsistence practices identified in the sources.

The Aweti also trade vegetable salts with other upper-Xingu groups. No dietary rules, named beverages, festive foods, or food-related prohibitions are documented.

## Language & religion

Aweti, also called Awetí, is a Tupian language of Central Brazil spoken by Indigenous Aweti people living along the Upper Xingu River. The language is described as endangered, with a declining 150 living speakers. It is unique and distinctive within the Tupian language family.

Most Aweti people are multilingual. Portuguese is the majority language of Brazil and is taught at an Aweti tribal school, especially affecting younger speakers. In the newer village, many people, particularly younger individuals, speak Kamayurá better than Aweti, and all residents speak at least one language other than Aweti. The Aweti language has also been documented under forms including Awety, Awetö, Aueto, Aueti, Auiti, and Auití.
