## Overview

The Chʼol are an Indigenous people of Mexico, mainly living in the northern Chiapas highlands in the state of Chiapas. They are one of the Maya peoples, and their Indigenous language belongs to the Mayan language family. According to the 2000 Census, Chiapas had 140,806 Chʼol speakers, including 40,000 monolingual speakers. The language is also called Chʼol, while *Lak tyʼañ* means “our speech.” The Chʼol are associated with Palenque, Tilá, Tumbalá, Sabanilla, Misjia, Limar, Chivalita, Vicente Guerrero, and Chivalito. Their cultural life combines Christianity with Mayan practices, including cave worship and ceremonies involving the Mayan cave god, Earth owner, and Christ.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe continuing cave ceremonies within Chʼol Christian religious practices. Cave worship has been legitimized throughout the region, and local curers alternate between churches and caves to gain their powers and carry out their functions. The sources explain that the Mayan cave god, Earth owner, and Christ have been reconciled, and that cave ceremonies have continued since the conversion of the population to Christianity. No annual festival calendar, festival dates, or life-cycle rites are documented.

## Foodways

The staple foods of the Chʼol are corn, livestock including chicken and turkey, beans, squash, bananas, greens, and other fruits. Some Chʼol earn income by selling livestock, including pigs, cows, and chicken, as well as fruits. This income is used to purchase soap, medicine, and other essential materials.

## Language & religion

The language called Chʼol in English is also referred to as *Lak tyʼañ*, meaning “our speech.” Chʼol belongs to the Mayan language family and consists of three branches: Sabanilla, Tilá, and Tumbalá. Although some linguists consider these three different languages, they are commonly known as dialects of the same Chʼol language; speakers of Tilá and Tumbalá can usually understand each other. The Chʼol practice Christianity, while Mayan traditions remain part of Christian religious practices. These include cave worship and ceremonies involving the Mayan cave god, Earth owner, and Christ. Some Chʼol speakers also speak Spanish in a variety called “Castia.”
