## Overview

The U'wa are an Indigenous people living in the cloud forests of northeastern Colombia, particularly in the departments of Arauca, Boyacá, Casanare, Santander and Northern Santander. Their ancestral homeland, known as *Kajka-Ika* or *Kera Chikara*, lies in the Sierra Nevada del Cocuy and covers more than 12,000 square kilometres, including the headwaters of the Orinoco River. Historically, their homeland extended across the Venezuela-Colombia border and the U'wa numbered as many as 20,000; approximately 7–8,000 are alive today. The U'wa speak Uw Cuwa, a language of the Chibchan family, and have passed down knowledge and customs primarily through song. Their cultural and political life became internationally visible through a 14-year-long struggle against oil drilling on their land.

## Music & performance

Song is central to the transmission of U'wa knowledge and customs. The U'wa have no written tradition and have passed down their knowledge primarily through song. Their religious tradition includes an obligation to gather in the summer months and “sing the world into being.” This practice is connected with maintaining equilibrium between the layers of the world, identified in the source as earth, water, oil, mountains and sky.

The sources do record that neighboring Indigenous peoples know the U'wa as “the thinking people” or “the people who speak well,” and that the U'wa language is called Uw Cuwa, meaning “people's tongue.” These descriptions connect speech, song and the preservation of knowledge.

## Festivals & rituals

The U'wa religious tradition includes an obligation to gather in the summer months and “sing the world into being.” They describe it as part of a wider responsibility to maintain equilibrium between the layers of the world: earth, water, oil, mountains and sky.

Purification rituals are also significant. The U'wa consider non-U'wa to be impure and place high importance on purification rituals, a practice that makes interaction with outsiders difficult.

The U'wa identification of petroleum with the blood of Mother Earth is expressed through the term *Ruiria*. This understanding shaped their resistance to oil extraction during the 1990s. During the conflict surrounding the Gibraltar 1 test site, the U'wa constructed a small village at the drillsite, established roadblocks and coordinated a regional social strike with neighboring campesinos and the Guahibo. The sources state that the U'wa had previously threatened to commit mass suicide if the oil extraction project went forward. The Colombian military later dislodged protesters from the site, but no commercially viable deposits were found.

## Oral tradition & literature

U'wa knowledge and customs have been transmitted primarily through song, and the language has no written tradition. The religious obligation to “sing the world into being” indicates that song is a vehicle for maintaining the order and equilibrium of the world.

They do, however, record the U'wa linguistic terms *Uw Cuwa*, meaning “people's tongue,” and *Ruiria*, their name for petroleum, which they identify with the blood of Mother Earth.

## Language & religion

The U'wa speak Uw Cuwa, commonly known as Tunebo, a Chibchan language spoken by between 1,800 and 3,600 U'wa people in Colombia. The sources state that the total U'wa population is about 7,000 in the language article, while the U'wa people article gives an estimate of approximately 7–8,000 alive today. Uw Cuwa has half a dozen known varieties, and communication between modern varieties can be difficult. Varieties and dialect names listed in the sources include Cobaría, Tegría, Agua Blanca, Barro Negro and the extinct Sínsiga, as well as additional names such as Tagrinuwa, Kubaruwa and Kaibaká.

Uwa is an ergative–absolutive language with subject-object-verb word order. It uses a base-10 decimal number system, has four case suffixes, and distinguishes four tenses: present, past, immediate past and immediate future. The sources state that the language has no written tradition.

U'wa religious tradition emphasizes gathering in the summer months, singing the world into being, maintaining equilibrium among earth, water, oil, mountains and sky, and carrying out purification rituals. The U'wa identify petroleum, or *Ruiria*, with the blood of Mother Earth.
