## Overview

The Caripuna, also known as the Karipuna do Amapá or Karipúna, are an Indigenous people living in the riverine areas of the Brazilian state of Amapá, particularly around the Caripi River. In 2014, their population was estimated at 2,922 people. Their history reflects several migrations and mixing among Amerindian, French Guianese, Saint Lucian Arab, Chinese, and non-Indigenous populations. They live across 16 settlements, including communities along the Caripi River and villages near the BR-156 highway. Their economy is mainly based on fishing and subsistence farming. Karipúna French Creole and Portuguese are the main languages spoken by the Karipuna.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They record settlements along the Caripi River and the BR-156 highway, an unpaved road from Manga to the BR-156, and migration to Oiapoque and the urban area around Cayenne.

## Language & religion

The Karipuna originally spoke Palikúr, an Arawakan language. They used to speak the now-extinct Karipúna do Uaçá language, but by 1900 Karipúna French Creole had taken over. The main languages spoken today are Karipúna French Creole and Portuguese; French Creole is also used as a common language with the Palikur and the Uaçá Galibi.
