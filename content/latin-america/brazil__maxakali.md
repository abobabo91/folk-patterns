## Overview

The Maxakali are an ethnic group of Indigenous people in Brazil. They live in the districts of Santa Helena de Minas, Bertópolis, Ladainha and Teófilo Otoni, in Minas Gerais. The Maxakalí language is a Maxakalían language spoken in four villages and is the primary language of the Maxakalí people. The four main communities are Pradinho, Água Boa, Aldeia Verde and Cachoeirinha. The sources give different population figures: one describes 400 members living in isolation and poverty, while the language article gives a total ethnic population of about 2,000 and says that Maxakalí is spoken by more than 2,000 people. The language has no known dialectal variation and is notable for its phonology, SOV word order and mood inflection.

## Music & performance

The sources mention a variety of the language used in Maxakalí ritual songs, called Ritual Maxakalí. Spoken Maxakalí differs from this ritual-song variety, although both are classified as Maxakalían languages.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe a creation story in which the creator Topa gave the people a tame otter for otter fishing. The otter instructed them to give him the three largest fish he caught; when the husband of the otter keeper’s granddaughter neglected this rule, the community was punished by heavy flooding. The language article also distinguishes Ritual Maxakalí from spoken Maxakalí.

## Foodways

The sources describe otter fishing in the Maxakali creation story. Topa gave the people a tame otter so that they would always have something to eat, provided that they gave him the three largest fish he caught.

## Oral tradition & literature

The principal oral tradition described in the sources is the Maxakali creation story involving Topa, a tame otter, otter fishing, the three largest fish and a heavy flooding that punished the community. The sources also identify Ritual Maxakalí as the language variety used in Maxakalí ritual songs.

## Language & religion

Maxakalí, also written Maxakalí: *Tikmũũn yĩy ax’* and *Mãxakani yĩy ax*, is a Maxakalían language spoken in four villages in Minas Gerais, Brazil. No dialectal differences are known within modern Maxakalí. The language typically follows Subject-Object-Verb order, and verbs inflect for mood; the realis mood is most common, while the irrealis mood is used in imperative and purpose clauses. Its phonology includes vowel lowering and backing, and the language has been analyzed as lacking contrastive fricative and nasal consonants except for the glottal continuant /h/, although the status of nasal consonants is described as changing through phonologization.
