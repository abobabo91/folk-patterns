## Overview

The Tenharim are an Indigenous people of Brazil who live in the state of Amazonas. Their self-denomination is *Kagwahiva*. They speak Tenharim, also called Tenharin, one of the dialects of the Kawahíva language, which belongs to the Tupian languages. The Tenharim reside in the Indigenous territories Tenharim/Marmelos, Tenharim of Igarapé Preto and Sepoti, including villages named Marmelos, Vila Nova, Bela Vista, Tracoá, Campinho, Taboca, Mafuí, Castanheira Jacuí, Pakyri and Caranaí. Tenharim is the only remaining Kawahíva dialect still transmitted to children.

## Oral tradition & literature

Tenharim was documented, along with other dialects of Kawahíva, by Helen Pease and LaVera Betts, missionaries with the Summer Institute of Linguistics, from the 1960s to the 1980s. They produced a pandialectal dictionary of Kawahíva and a number of texts, mainly in Tenharim.

## Language & religion

Tenharim, also called Tenharin, is a dialect of the Kawahíva language, one of the Tupian languages, spoken in western Brazil by the Tenharim. It is the only remaining dialect still transmitted to children. Only in Tenharim Marmelos is the language learned by children as a first language; children there attend school and are educated in Tenharim, although this education is restricted to elementary school.
