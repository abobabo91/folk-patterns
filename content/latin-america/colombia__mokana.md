## Overview

The Mokaná, also known as Mocaná, are an Indigenous people living in the Atlántico Department of Colombia. They are the only Indigenous community in that department. Their language belongs to the Malibu family of languages and is extinct; only 500 words have been preserved.

## Festivals & rituals

The Mokaná largely converted to Christianity in the 16th century.

## Language & religion

The Mokaná language is part of the Malibu family of languages and is extinct; only 500 words have been preserved. The sources state that the Mokaná largely converted to Christianity in the 16th century.

The first recorded contact between the Mokaná and Europeans occurred in 1529, when the Portuguese conquistador Jerónimo de Melo led an overland expedition from Santa Marta to Malambo. Malambo was a settlement on the Rio Magdalena named for the Mokaná cacique Pedro Malambo, who governed it at that time.

The Spanish Crown granted the Mokaná ownership of 17,500 hectares of land by royal decree in 1766. During the Spanish American wars of independence, Mokaná fought in the Magdalena Campaign of 1812 and the siege of Cartagena de Indias in 1821. The Government of Colombia officially recognized the Mokaná as an ethnic group on 2 May 1998.
