## Overview

The Sanumá, also called *Sanīma dībī*, are an Indigenous people of Brazil and Venezuela related to the Yanomami. In 1995, they numbered about 6,410, including about 3,200 living in Venezuela and between 900 and 1,000 living in Brazil. In Venezuela, they live in the Caura River and Ventuari River basins alongside the Ye'kuana. The Sanumá language is a Yanomaman language spoken in Venezuela and Brazil. Sanumá society makes decisions by consensus, and villages own land in common. Historically, Sanumá communities settled in villages that they moved every two to three years.

## Language & religion

Sanöma or Sanumá is a Yanomaman language spoken in Venezuela and Brazil. It is also known as Sanema, Sanima, Tsanuma, Samatari, Samatali, Xamatari, and Chirichano. Most Sanumá speakers in Venezuela also speak Ye'kuana, also known as Maquiritare, the language of the Ye'kuana people who live alongside them in the Caura River basin. Three dialects are spoken in Roraima, Brazil: Awaris, with 2,955 speakers; Aracaçá, with 29 speakers; and Hokomawä, with 180 speakers. In Sanöma, the subject is placed before the verb or predicate in subordinate and independent clauses.
