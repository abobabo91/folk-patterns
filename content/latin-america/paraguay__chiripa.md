## Overview

The Chiripá are a Guaraní indigenous people living mainly in Paraguay, in the area bounded by the Paraná River and the Acaray and Jejuí Rivers. They also live in Brazil, including Mato Grosso do Sul, Paraná and São Paulo, and in Argentina, where small groups live among the Mbyas in Misiones. Around 2002, Paraguay had 6918 people of this ethnic group and 1900 speakers of the language. The 2012 census recorded 17,697 Avá Guaranis, including communities in Canindeyú, Alto Paraná, San Pedro, Caaguazú, Asunción and the Central Department, and Concepción. The Chiripá maintain dialects and religious traditions despite high acculturation in some areas.

## Festivals & rituals

The Chiripá maintain religious traditions, and the Chiripá people in Argentina are described as retaining religious traditions while living among the Mbyas.

## Language & religion

Chiripá Guaraní, also called Tsiripá, Txiripá, Ava Guaraní, Nhandéva or Ñandeva, is a dialect of the Guarani language spoken in Paraguay, Brazil and Argentina. Nhandéva is closely connected to Mbyá Guaraní, and intermarriage between speakers of the two languages is common. Speakers generally live in mountainous areas of the Atlantic Forest, extending from eastern Paraguay through Misiones Province of Argentina to the southern Brazilian states of Paraná, Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul. The sources report approximately 4,900 speakers in Brazil and 7,000 in Paraguay.
