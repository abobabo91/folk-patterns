## Overview

The Yukpa are an Indigenous ethnic group living in the northeastern part of the Cesar Department in northern Colombia, in the Serranía del Perijá bordering Venezuela. Their territory includes the eastern areas of the municipalities of Robles La Paz, Codazzi and Becerril, in Indigenous reserves named Socorpa, Menkue and El Cozo Iroka, as well as smaller areas in Venezuela. According to an Inter Press Service story cited in the source, the Yukpa number nearly 10,000, with the majority living in Venezuela and other communities located across the border in Colombia. Yukpa is a Cariban language spoken by 3,000 people in Zulia State in Venezuela and 3,000 in Colombia. Its dialects include Río Casacará (Iroka), Río Maracas, Caño Padilla–La Laguna, Yrapa and Río Negro.

## Foodways

The source describes one food-related practice: the Yukpa have been known to consume certain nest-inhabiting wasp species, including *Polistes pacificus*. These wasps make paper nests that can be knocked from a tree directly into a fire, where the larvae are toasted.

## Language & religion

Yukpa, also known as Yuco, Yucpa, Yuko, Yupa, Carib Motilón, Macoíta, Northern Motilón and Manso, is a Cariban language. It is spoken in Zulia State in Venezuela and across the border in Colombia. Río Casacará (Iroka) and Río Maracas are the principal dialects described in the source, and they are different enough to possibly be considered separate languages. Caño Padilla–La Laguna is also identified as a dialect area. The Venezuelan dialects Yrapa and Río Negro are closer to Río Maracas than to Río Casacará. Similarity to Japrería, described as the other Yupka language, is slight.
