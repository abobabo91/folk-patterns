## Overview

The Mazatec are an Indigenous people living in the highlands of Oaxaca, Puebla, and Veracruz in southern Mexico. They are known for two distinctive cultural features: whistle speech, a tonal register that carries full conversations without words, and a shamanic tradition centered on cultivated entheogens. Their material culture is documented primarily through cotton textiles featuring woven brocade and cross-stitch embroidery. The Mazatecan languages are recognized as national languages of Mexico and remain vigorous in many communities.

## Material culture

Mazatec material culture is dominated by hand-woven and embroidered cotton textiles, with two main ornamental systems: gauze-weave brocade and counted-thread cross-stitch.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Mazatec weavers create two distinct ornamental systems—loom-figured weave and counted-thread embroidery—often on the same garment.

- **Gauze-weave and supplementary-weft brocade** — Hand-spun cotton is woven so open gauze bands alternate with denser ground, with colored or white supplementary wefts floated across to make geometric figures.
- **Cross-stitch on cuadrillé** (*cuadrillé*) — A white open-mesh embroidery cloth is folded, cut with a rounded neck opening, and covered in cross-stitch in cotton thread.
- **Two-panel huipil** (*huipil*) — Two gauze-woven panels of hand-spun cotton are joined lengthwise, with a square neck hole cut and geometric indigo designs on borders.
- **Tubular wrapped skirt** — Two rectangles of cotton are seamed and folded into a tube, decorated at the bottom edge with brocade or cross-stitch and a blue cotton hem binding.
- **Men's plaid shoulder cloth** — A rectangle of cotton in white and brown plaid, hemmed on opposing sides with no brocade or embroidery.

### Clothing & dress

Women's dress consists of the huipil and tubular skirt, with ornament concentrated on visible parts; men wear a girdle and shoulder cloth.

- **Huipil** — A woman's upper garment made either as two woven panels with a cut square neck or as an embroidered rectangle with a rounded neck, decorated on chest, shoulders, and lower border.
- **Square head cloth** — A white cotton cloth brocaded in red with stripes and fringed on two sides, worn by women.
- **Men's girdle** — An off-white cotton rectangle with sets of three red stripes and brocaded geometric bands at each end, finished with white tassels.
- **Striped and brocaded cotton bag** — A woven accessory with braided carrying loops sewn into its top corners.

## Music & performance

Mazatec culture is distinguished by whistle speech, a tonal register in which full conversations are conveyed through whistling without voiced words.

- **Whistle speech** — Young men hold complex conversations entirely by whistling the tonal contours of the language, a practice learned in childhood that serves to communicate across distance or avoid interrupting speech.
- **XEOJN radio station** — A CDI station broadcasting in Mazatecan language from San Lucas Ojitlán, Oaxaca.

## Festivals & rituals

Mazatec ritual practice centers on the cultivation of entheogens for spiritual and ceremonial use, a tradition associated with renowned shamans.

- **Psilocybin mushrooms** — Cultivated and used in Mazatec shamanic rituals for spiritual purposes.
- **Morning glory seeds** — Psychoactive seeds from species including Ipomoea tricolor and Ipomoea corymbosa, used in Mazatec shamanic ceremonies.
- **Salvia divinorum** (*ska María Pastora*) — A psychoactive plant called by Mazatec shamans ska María Pastora, a name invoking the Virgin Mary and marking the blend of indigenous practice with Catholic devotion.
- **María Sabina** — A renowned Mazatec healer and shaman from Huautla de Jiménez, known internationally for her work with entheogens.
- **Julieta Casimiro** — A Mazatec figure belonging to the entheogenic shamanic tradition.

## Oral tradition & literature

Whistle speech is the distinctive living oral register of Mazatec culture, while the written tradition includes Bible translations and Jehovah's Witness publications.

- **Whistle speech** — A verbal art and living oral register in which full conversations are sustained without voiced words, learned in childhood alongside speech.
- **New Testament in Mazatec** — Religious texts exist in several varieties of the Mazatec language, translated to account for local standards rather than a single norm.
- **Jehovah's Witnesses publications** — Bible-based literature and video content published in Mazatec by Jehovah's Witnesses.

## Language & religion

Mazatecan languages are recognized as national languages of Mexico, with nine varieties that divide into Highland and Lowland groups, and religion centers on shamanic entheogenic practice overlaid with Catholic tradition.

- **Huautla Mazatec** — The prestige variety of Mazatecan with about 50,000 speakers, spoken in the town of Huautla de Jiménez.
- **Nine Mazatecan varieties** — Chiquihuitlán, Huautla, Ayautla, Mazatlán, Eloxochitlán, Ixcatlán, Jalapa, Soyaltepec, and Puebla and Northeastern Mazatec.
- **Tonal language system** — All Mazatecan varieties are tonal with complex tone inventories and phonation contrasts including creaky and breathy voice.
- **Ska María Pastora** — The Mazatec shamanic name for Salvia divinorum, invoking the Virgin Mary and marking the overlay of Catholic devotion on indigenous practice.

## Glossary

- *huipil* — a woman's upper garment, either woven or embroidered
- *cuadrillé* — white open-mesh embroidery cloth
- *ska María Pastora* — Mazatec shamanic name for Salvia divinorum, literally 'Holy Mary the Shepherdess'
- *Mazatecan* — languages spoken by the Mazatec people, part of the Oto-Manguean family
- *Sierra Mazateca* — highland region in northern Oaxaca where Mazatec communities live
- *Oto-Manguean* — a language family of Mesoamerica
- *entheogen* — a plant or substance used in shamanic or spiritual rituals
- *whistle speech* — communication by whistling the tonal contours of language without voiced words
- *cross-stitch* — counted-thread embroidery technique creating X-shaped stitches
- *Ipomoea tricolor* — morning glory species with psychoactive seeds used in Mazatec ritual
- *Ipomoea corymbosa* — morning glory species with psychoactive seeds used in Mazatec ritual
- *psilocybin mushroom* — psychoactive fungus cultivated and used in Mazatec shamanic ceremonies
- *tonal language* — a language in which pitch or tone changes the meaning of words
- *breathy voice* — a phonation type with audible air passing through the vocal cords
- *prestige variety* — the most socially valued or widely recognized form of a language
