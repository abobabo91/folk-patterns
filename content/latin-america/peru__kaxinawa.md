## Overview

The Kaxinawá are an Indigenous people living in the western Amazon basin along the Curanja and Purus Rivers in Peru and in Acre, Brazil. They speak Hãtxa Kuĩ, a Panoan language spoken by about 1,600 people in Peru and 400 in Acre. Knowledge of the people comes chiefly from museum-held objects: woven cotton cloth, painted pottery, feather regalia, and initiation stools, along with linguistic research.

## Material culture

Cotton runs through nearly every textile and composite object, appearing as woven cloth and as cord and binding.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Cotton is woven on back-strap looms into cloth, bed covers, skirts, and belts, and dyed into striped cord for binding.

- **Back-strap loom** — A back-tensioned loom uses a cotton textile strap worn on the weaver's body to hold tension.
- **Woven bed cover** — A cotton bed cover is woven as an uneven rectangle, wider at the ends than at the middle, patterned in blue and white geometric designs.
- **Tube skirt** — Women's clothing made of cotton fibre, catalogued without decoration.
- **Striped cord binding** — Cotton string in blue, red and white stripes is wrapped around wooden handles as a finishing technique.
- **Plaited basketry** — A shallow basket with a round rim is plaited from vegetable fibre, with a string of white wool threaded through the rim.

### Clothing & dress

Dress consists of cotton garments worn with adornments of seeds, animal teeth, shells, and feathers.

- **Cotton shirt** — A garment of textile, probably cotton.
- **Cotton belt** — Cotton fibre belt worn as part of women's dress.
- **Chest-strap** — Body ornament made of seeds and tin, or seeds and animal teeth.
- **Leg-band** — Adornment made of cotton and snail shell, worn in pairs.
- **Feather head-dress** — Built on a cane ring with small red, yellow and blue feathers attached, bound with wool strips, and a larger ring of long feathers standing behind.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pottery bowls feature knobbed rims as a recurring formal trait, while woodwork includes clubs, stirrers, initiation stools and fishing nets.

- **Knobbed bowl** — Rounded ceramic vessels with three, four or more knobs spaced around the rim.
- **Wooden club** — Carved with a flat blade, a handle and a knob at the handle's end, the grip wrapped in striped cotton string.
- **Wooden stirrer** — A flat-bladed tool with paired protrusions on either side of the handle where it meets the blade, and three more on either side of the handle's end.
- **Initiation stool** — Carved from wood with two legs and a round top, sometimes with bark-wrapped legs and painted geometric designs.
- **Fishing net** — Made of wood and cotton for catching fish.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment is made from gathered animal and plant material: seeds, animal teeth, snail shells, cotton and feathers.

- **Seed chest-strap** — Strung from seeds with tin, or from seeds with animal teeth.
- **Shell leg-band** — Made of cotton and snail shell.
- **Back-rack** — Combines harpy eagle feathers with wood and cotton for ceremonial use.
- **Bamboo head-dress** — A length of bamboo folded into a circle, tied with yellow wool thread, with pink and black geometric designs painted on the outer face.
- **Feather back-rack** — Built on a cane ring with small red, yellow and blue feathers, bound with wool strips in various colors, and a larger ring of long feathers with tassels.

## Music & performance

No information about instruments, song genres or performance occasions is documented, though narrative audio has been recorded.

## Festivals & rituals

Initiation stools and feather regalia suggest ceremonial use, though details about rituals and their occasions are not documented.

- **Initiation stool** — A carved wooden seat with two legs and a round top, used in ritual contexts but without documented details about age, participants or sequence of events.

## Foodways

Staples, dishes and cooking are not documented, though fishing nets and wooden stirrers relate to food production and preparation.

- **Fishing net** — Made of wood and cotton for catching fish.
- **Wooden stirrer** — A carved tool with protrusions flanking the handle, used in food preparation.

## Oral tradition & literature

Narrative has been documented through audio recordings and linguistic studies, though no folktales, epics or proverbs are described in the sources.

- **Cashinahua DoReCo corpus** — Audio recordings of narrative texts with phone-aligned transcriptions, translations and partial morphological annotation, compiled by Sabine Reiter.
- **Animacy and mythology studies** — Research on animacy and mythology in Hãtxa Kuĩ conducted at the Department of Linguistics of the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology.
- **Participant Referencing in Cashinahua** — Richard Montag's 2008 study of how narrative keeps track of its characters, published by SIL International.

## Language & religion

Hãtxa Kuĩ is a Panoan language with three named dialects and two extinct related languages, with forty percent of speakers literate and twenty to thirty percent literate in Spanish.

- **Hãtxa Kuĩ** — A Panoan language of western South America spoken by about 1,600 people in Peru and 400 in Acre, Brazil.
- **Brazilian Kashinawa** — One of three named surviving dialects of Hãtxa Kuĩ.
- **Peruvian Kashinawa** — One of three named surviving dialects of Hãtxa Kuĩ.
- **Juruá Kapanawa** — An extinct dialect also known as Capanahua of the Juruá River.
- **Paranawa** — An extinct language related to the Panoan family.

## Glossary

- *Hãtxa Kuĩ* — Panoan language spoken by Kaxinawá, also written Hantxa Kuin
- *Kaxinawá* — Indigenous people of the western Amazon basin, also written Kashinawa, Kaxynawa, Caxinawa, Caxinawá, Cashinahua
- *Panoan* — language family of western South America
- *back-strap loom* — back-tensioned loom with cotton textile worn on weaver's body
- *tube skirt* — cylindrical garment of cotton fibre worn by women
- *chest-strap* — body ornament made from seeds, tin or animal teeth
- *leg-band* — adornment worn on legs, made of cotton and snail shell
- *feather head-dress* — ceremonial headwear with small feathers on cane ring and larger feathers standing behind
- *initiation stool* — two-legged carved wooden seat with round top, used in ritual contexts
- *harpy eagle* — large bird whose feathers are used in head-dresses and back-racks
- *Cashinahua DoReCo corpus* — audio recordings of narrative texts with transcriptions and translations compiled by Sabine Reiter
- *Brazilian Kashinawa* — dialect of Hãtxa Kuĩ spoken in Brazil
- *Peruvian Kashinawa* — dialect of Hãtxa Kuĩ spoken in Peru
- *Juruá Kapanawa* — extinct dialect also known as Capanahua of the Juruá River
- *Paranawa* — extinct Panoan language
- *Purus River* — river in Peru where Kaxinawá live
- *Acre* — Brazilian state where 400 Kaxinawá speakers live
