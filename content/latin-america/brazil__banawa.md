## Overview

The Banawá, also known as Banawa, Banavá, Jafí, Kitiya, and Banauá, are an Indigenous group living along the Banawá River in Amazonas State, Brazil. Their territory lies between the Juruá and Purus Rivers. Approximately 158 Banawá people live in one major village and two smaller settlements, each containing a single extended family. The Banawá call themselves Kitiya and speak Banawá, a dialect of the Madí language. Banawá is an Arawan language, and the wider Madí language is spoken by about 1,000 Jamamadi, Banawá, and Jarawara people scattered over Amazonas, Brazil. The sources document the Banawá especially through their territory, history, self-identification, and language.

## Language & religion

The Banawá speak Banawá, a dialect of Madí, an Arawan language. Madí has three extant dialects corresponding to three distinct tribal groups: Jamamadí, Jarawara, and Banawá. The dialects are mutually intelligible, although Madí speakers refer to them as separate languages, and dialectal differences are an important part of tribal self-identification. Banawá is spoken by about 120 people to the northeast within the wider Madí-speaking area. The Banawá call the river *Kitia* and themselves *Kitia-ka-mee*, “those who belong to the Kitia.” The term Banawá is the Brazilian-Portuguese name for the river on which the tribe now lives. The Banawá only recently migrated to the river area, in about 1950; before this they were known as the Munia.

Madí has no officially standardized orthography, and it was not written prior to Euro-American contact. A practical orthography devised by SIL for the Jamamadí dialect provides the basis for written representation among native and non-native speakers. Madí is grammatically agglutinative, primarily suffixing, and “verb-heavy”; it has a basic object-subject-verb word order and a fluid-S active-stative alignment system. The language distinguishes masculine and feminine grammatical gender, has a rich inventory of personal pronouns, and contrasts alienable and inalienable possession.
