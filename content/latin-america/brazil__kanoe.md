## Overview

The Kanoê, also called the Canoe, Kapixaná, and Kapixanã, are an Indigenous people of southern Rondônia, Brazil, near the Bolivian border. They live mainly in two areas: along the Guaporé River and in the Rio Omerê Indigenous Territory, located in the municipalities of Corumbiara and Chupinguaia. The Kanoê of the Guaporé River share land with other Indigenous peoples and have largely been assimilated into neighbouring Indigenous and non-Indigenous communities. The Rio Omerê group has had less contact with Brazilian society and was considered an isolated Indigenous people until outside contact in 1995. The sources describe a population of about 319 Kanoê in the language article, while the people article states that four members of the Rio Omerê family remained as of 2025. Their language is a nearly extinct isolate.

## Music & performance

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do describe deaths caused by pertussis, measles, stomach problems, and conflicts with farmers during the history of contact with foreigners.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do document linguistic research, including a vocabulary published by Stanislav Zach in 1943 and republished by Cestmír Loukotka in 1963, as well as later work on phonology, grammar, syntax, morphosyntax, negation, and litotes.

## Language & religion

Kanoê, also called Kapishana, is a nearly extinct language isolate of Rondônia, Brazil. The language names are also spelled Kapixana, Kapixanã, and Canoé. The language article reports only five speakers in a population of about 319 Kanoê people, while the people article states that only three Guaporé River Kanoê still spoke Kanoê at the time described. Most Kapishana people now speak Portuguese or other Indigenous languages as a result of intermarriage. Kanoê has been compared with Kwaza, Aikanã, and Nambikwaran languages, although these proposals do not establish a language family.
