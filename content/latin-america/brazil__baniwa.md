## Overview

The Baniwa, also known through local variants including Baniva, Baniua, Curipaco, Vaniva, Walimanai, and Wakuenai, are Indigenous South Americans who speak Baniwa, a language belonging to the Maipurean (Arawak) language family. They live in the Amazon Region, in the border area of Brazil, Colombia, and Venezuela, along the Rio Negro and its tributaries. The available source estimates 7,145 Baniwa in Brazil, 7,000 in Colombia, and 3,501 in Venezuela’s Amazonas State, while noting that accurate figures are difficult to establish because of the rainforest environment. Their subsistence relies mainly on manioc cultivation and fishing. They are also known for the fine basketry they produce.

## Foodways

The Baniwa rely mainly on manioc cultivation and fishing for subsistence.

## Language & religion

Baniwa is described as a language belonging to the Maipurean (Arawak) language family. The name Baniwa is also applied to several languages of the Amazon, including Avane language (Abane), Baniwa of Içana (Karu), Baniwa of Maroa or Guainía (Warekana), and Baniwa of Yavita (Yavitero).
