## Overview

The Kali'na are an Indigenous people of northern South America who once lived along the coasts from the Amazon to the Orinoco. Today they live mostly in river and coastal villages across Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname, French Guiana, and Brazil. They have no written tradition and passed their history through oral tales. Their material culture—pottery, basketry, and instruments—survives mainly in early twentieth-century museum collections.

## Material culture

Kali'na material culture is known mainly through pottery, basketry, hunting gear, and musical instruments preserved in museum collections from the early 1900s.

### Textile & pattern traditions

No weaving or textile traditions are documented for the Kali'na.

### Clothing & dress

No garments or everyday dress are recorded, only body-painting as part of ceremonies.

- **Body-painting** — Applied during the Epekotono mourning-conclusion celebration among Kali'na of the Guianas.

### Architecture

House forms and building methods are not documented, but Kali'na settlements cluster in specific river and coastal regions.

- **Awala-Yalimapo** — The only Kali'na-majority settlement in French Guiana, between the Maroni and Mana rivers.
- **Galibi** — A main settlement in Suriname along the coast and rivers.
- **São José dos Galibi** — Founded in 1950 on the Oyapock River in Brazil by families from the Mana River region.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pottery is the best-documented Kali'na craft, showing skill in form, decoration, and figurative sculpture.

- **Slip-painted vessels** — Flat-bottomed globular pots with red slip and pale geometric designs, or glazed decorated bowls with waved rims.
- **Polychrome figurines** — Clay figures including frogs, armadillos, dogs, and human forms with raised arms.
- **Red porphyry polishing stone** (*takuwa*) — Pebbles collected from the Essequibo River and prized by Kali'na women for polishing pottery.
- **Stone axes** — Pre-contact tools later replaced by European iron tools.
- **Hunting basket** — Lidded containers woven for carrying hunting gear and supplies.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Little is documented about Kali'na ornaments beyond armlets and body-painting.

- **Armlets** — Ornaments collected from the Barima River region; materials and construction not described.

## Music & performance

Kali'na music centers on percussion drums, rattles, and flutes, performed mainly at the Epekotono ceremony.

- **Sanpula drum** (*sanpula or sambula*) — A large two-headed drum with skin stretched over each end and held by hoops, struck with a mallet.
- **Rattles** (*kalawasi or kalawashi; malaka*) — Two kinds of percussion rattles used in Kali'na music.
- **Kuwama flute** (*kuwama*) — A wooden flute still made but increasingly displaced by European instruments.
- **Terra-cotta horn** (*kuti*) — A clay horn used in traditional Kali'na music.
- **Bone flute** — A three-hole flute with incised ornament, collected from the Barima River in 1927.

## Dance & theatre

Dancing is recorded only as part of the Epekotono ceremony; no named dances or dramatic traditions are documented.

- **Epekotono dancing** (*Epekotono*) — Dancing that accompanies body-painting, music, and the symbolic burning of the deceased's possessions.

## Festivals & rituals

The Epekotono ceremony is the primary documented occasion for Kali'na gathering, marking the end of mourning and use of the Kali'na language.

- **Epekotono** — A public mourning-conclusion celebration lasting a year or more, featuring body-painting, music, dancing, and the burning of the deceased's belongings to release the spirit.
- **Kasiri** — A cassava-derived beer central to traditional Kali'na celebrations.

## Foodways

Only one Kali'na foodstuff is documented: kasiri, a cassava beer.

- **Kasiri** — A beer made from cassava, central to traditional Kali'na celebrations and gatherings.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Kali'na transmitted history orally through myth and legend, with modern documentation beginning in the late twentieth century.

- **Na'na Kali'na — Une histoire des Kali'na en Guyane** — A 2000 history by Gérard Collomb and Félix Tiouka, drawing on Kali'na oral tradition and research.
- **Palanakiłi** — The Kali'na word for Europeans, originally meaning 'spirits of the sea,' still used for white people.
- **Pau:wa** — The Kali'na name for France, meaning 'The Land of the Whites,' destination of Kali'na sent for exhibition in Paris in 1882 and 1892.

## Language & religion

Kali'na is a highly endangered Cariban language with complex grammar; no religious practices are documented.

- **Kali'na language** (*Kali'na (Kari'nja, Carib, Galibi)*) — A Cariban language with 7,400 speakers, mainly elders, spread across five countries in four regional dialects.
- **Aretyry dialect** (*Aretyry*) — The Western and central Surinamese variety of Kali'na.
- **Tyrewuju dialect** (*Tyrewuju*) — The Eastern Surinamese and French Guianese variety, spoken by the majority in Suriname.

## Glossary

- *Epekotono* — A mourning-conclusion celebration marking the end of a mourning period, featuring body-painting, music, dancing, and symbolic burning of the deceased's belongings.
- *kasiri* — A cassava-derived beer central to traditional Kali'na celebrations.
- *takuwa* — Red porphyry pebbles from the Essequibo River, prized for polishing pottery; also denotes jade.
- *malaka* — A second type of rattle used in Kali'na music.
- *kuwama* — A wooden flute still made by the Kali'na but increasingly displaced by European instruments.
- *kuti* — A terra-cotta horn used in traditional Kali'na music.
- *Palanakiłi* — The Kali'na word for Europeans, originally meaning 'spirits of the sea.'
- *Pau:wa* — The Kali'na name for France, meaning 'The Land of the Whites.'
- *Kalina* — Self-designation of the Kali'na people, literally meaning 'person.'
- *Aretyry* — The Western and central Surinamese dialect of Kali'na.
- *Tyrewuju* — The Eastern Surinamese and French Guianese dialect of Kali'na, spoken by the majority in Suriname.
