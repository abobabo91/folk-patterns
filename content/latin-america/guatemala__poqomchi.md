## Overview

The Poqomchiʼ are a Maya people in Guatemala. Their indigenous language, Poqomchiʼ, belongs to the Mayan family and the Quichean–Poqom branch, and is very closely related to Poqomam. The language’s two main dialects, eastern and western, were spoken by about 90,000 people in 2000. Poqomchiʼ is spoken in Purulhá in Baja Verapaz; in Santa Cruz Verapaz, San Cristóbal Verapaz, Tactic, Tamahú and Tucurú in Alta Verapaz; and in Chicamán in El Quiché. The sources emphasize the language’s long but comparatively limited documentation history, beginning with Spanish missionaries in the second half of the 16th century and continuing through the 19th and 20th centuries.

## Language & religion

Poqomchiʼ is a Mayan language spoken by the Poqomchiʼ Maya of Guatemala. It is related to the Quichean–Poqom branch and very closely related to Poqomam. Its two main dialects are eastern and western. The language is spoken in Alta Verapaz, Baja Verapaz and El Quiché, including Panzós in the community of San Vicente II, La Tinta, Tucurú, Tamahú, Tactic, Santa Cruz Verapaz, San Cristóbal Verapaz, Chicamán in the aldea of Belejú, and Purulhá in the community of Ribalcó. The sources describe consonant and vowel features, including the absence of /ɓ/ in Western Poqomchiʼ, the occurrence of glottalized /wʼ/, and the realization of /r/ as a flap when it occurs word-medially.
