## Overview

The Arara are an Indigenous people of Brazil native to the state of Rondônia. They are closely related to the Zoró and Gavião peoples, while remaining distinct from other groups called “Arara” by outsiders, including the Arara of Pará and the Kwaza. They were an uncontacted tribe until the 1950s and were then involved in conflict with neighboring Gavião communities. Through the Gavião, the Brazilian government contacted the Arara and enforced a peace. Their population declined rapidly after contact: only 50 were alive in 1966, but it rose to over 200 by the 1990s after the New Tribes Mission introduced medical care.

## Language & religion

 The Rondônia article states that the New Tribes Mission formally converted most Arara to the Baptist faith, while the community largely retained its traditional beliefs. The article titled “Arara religion” concerns *Arará*, an African diasporic religion that developed in Cuba during the late 19th century, with origins among people descended from the Dahomey kingdom of West Africa; it is therefore not evidence about the religious system of the Indigenous Arara of Rondônia.
