## Overview

The Acolhua are a Mesoamerican people who arrived in the Valley of Mexico in or around 1200 CE. They settled most of the eastern Basin of Mexico, an area known as Acolhuacan. Their first capital was Coatlinchan, after which they moved to Tetzcoco; Huejotla was another important Acolhua city. The Acolhua are included under the term Aztec and were a sister culture of the Mexica, Tepanec, Chalca, Xochimilca and others. Their political importance is connected with the Triple Alliance, founded in 1428 by the rulers of Tenochtitlan, Texcoco and Tlacopan. The alliance eventually integrated the most developed regions of western Mesoamerica and ended with the Spanish conquest of 1521.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources describe Acolhua cities, temples and idols. Coatlinchan was the first capital, Tetzcoco later became the more famous capital, and Huejotla was another important Acolhua city.

## Music & performance

The sources mention that the Acolhua had temples and idols and that their patron deity was called Cocopitl.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that the Acolhua had temples and idols and that Cocopitl was their patron deity at one time, but they provide no further description of rituals.

## Oral tradition & literature

According to Ixtlilxochitl, the Acolhua originated from the northwest beyond Michoacán and were similar to Chichimecs while having temples and idols.

## Language & religion

The ruling family of the Acolhua were likely descended from Otomí speakers and did not speak Nahuatl until their ruler, the *tlatoani* Techotlalatzin, decreed its use. The sources associate the Acolhua with temples and idols and state that their patron deity was called Cocopitl.
