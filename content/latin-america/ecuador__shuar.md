## Overview

The Shuar live in tropical rainforest between the Andes and the Amazon lowlands. Their culture includes hunting, farming, household life, shamanism, clothing, featherwork, and seed adornment. Manioc and chicha are central foods, and women’s farming work is especially important. Shuar communities later formed settlements called centros.

## Material culture

Shuar material culture uses cotton, seeds, feathers, teeth, bones, vegetable fibre, wood, cane, fruit shells, and glass beads.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Cotton textiles, striped cloth, seed decoration, and woven belts are important parts of Shuar dress.

- **Striped cotton cloth** — Rectangular cotton textiles have brown and beige stripes and vertical lines, and appear in skirts, ponchos, belts, and bracelets.
- **Itip** — The itip is a rectangular cloth wrapped around the waist and secured with a belt.
- **Tarach** — The tarach is a long traditional dress that leaves the arms and left shoulder uncovered.
- **Seed-decorated poncho** — A cotton poncho has a V-shaped neck opening and strings of seeds, including a heart-shaped arrangement of lágrimas de San Pedro and Ormosia seeds.
- **Cotton belts** — Cotton belts have brown and beige stripes, braided ends, and sometimes nacre discs.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional clothing includes the male itip, the female tarach, cotton belts, ponchos, and feather headdresses.

- **Male waist cloth** (*itip*) — Men traditionally wear the itip around the waist with the torso usually uncovered.
- **Female dress** (*tarach*) — Women traditionally wear the tarach, a long garment with the arms and left shoulder uncovered.
- **Seed-decorated poncho** — Ponchos are sewn from cotton cloth and may carry strings of seeds.
- **Leader’s toucan-feather headdress** (*tentèmp*) — The tentèmp is a headdress of toucan feathers and skin traditionally worn by male leaders.
- **Feather crowns** — Crowns and headbands may use basketry, toucan feathers, complete birds, bird skins, or beetle wing-case pendants.

### Architecture

The sources give little information about Shuar buildings, but describe dispersed households and later settlements called centros.

- **Settlement center** (*centro*) — From the 1950s, Shuar formed nucleated settlements called centros, commonly holding 5 to 30 families.
- **Dispersed rainforest household** — Shuar formerly lived in separate households spread through the rainforest.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Shuar everyday objects include painted ceramic bowls, shields, arrows, and containers, while the sources do not document metalwork.

- **Painted ceramic bowl** — Ceramic bowls have red slip, black-and-white decoration, glossy varnish, and geometric designs such as stars, diamonds, zigzags, trapezoids, and an anaconda.
- **Wooden circular shield** — A wooden shield has three carved concentric discs.
- **Toothed arrow** — A cane-shafted arrow has a dark-brown wooden point and seven teeth on each side.
- **Fruit-shell container** (*mati*) — The mati is a spherical fruit-bark container filled with cottony ceiba seed fibre and used with a blowgun quiver.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Shuar adornment combines seeds, beads, teeth, bones, feathers, hair, and beetle wings in many kinds of ornaments.

- **Seven-row seed bracelet** — One bracelet has seven rows of small black seeds crossed by five strings of larger white, tear-shaped seeds.
- **Monkey-tooth collar** — One collar contains 66 monkey teeth.
- **Beetle-wing earrings** — Earrings use iridescent green beetle wing-cases, toucan feathers, and black human hair.
- **Beaded pectoral** — A pectoral has six strings of glass beads, fangs, vertebrae, and different seeds.
- **Seed necklaces** — Necklaces use grey and garnet-coloured seeds, bird specimens, and beetle wing-cases.

## Music & performance

The sources do not describe Shuar instruments, song genres, or musical performances, but they mention radio schools using Spanish and Shuar.

- **Radio schools** — Radio schools were established in the late 1960s for communication and education in Spanish and Shuar, then closed in 2001.

## Festivals & rituals

A former warrior ritual sent boys to waterfalls to seek visions and gain spiritual strength.

- **Waterfall vision journey** — Boys of about eight traveled for three to five days with fathers or uncles, drank only tobacco water, and received maikua in hope of seeing arútam.
- **Vision** (*arútam*) — An arútam was a vision believed to come from an ancestral spirit.
- **Ancestral spirit** (*wakaní*) — A wakaní was an ancestral spirit believed to produce visions and give spiritual power.
- **Spirit power** (*arútam wakaní*) — An arútam wakaní was believed to make a person very strong and could later be lost.
- **Warrior title** (*kakáram*) — A warrior who had killed many people was called a kakáram.

## Foodways

Manioc and chicha provide most of the Shuar diet, while agriculture and hunting organize community life.

- **Manioc** — Shuar women cultivate manioc, which provides much of the diet’s calories and carbohydrates.
- **Manioc beer** (*chicha*) — Women make chicha, a beer made from manioc.
- **Hunting weapons** — The material culture includes bows, blowguns, spears, arrows, quivers, and darts used with hunting skills.
- **Blowgun darts** — Darts are carried in a quiver with a mati container.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not record a broad Shuar literary tradition, but they mention the Iwia demon and a full Shuar translation of Ecuador’s Constitution.

- **Jungle Demon** (*Iwia*) — Iwia means “Jungle Demon” and names a feared demon that devours people.
- **Shuar constitutional translation** (*Ecuador Nunka Umíktin Umpuarma*) — The Constitution of Ecuador has been translated in full into Shuar under the name Ecuador Nunka Umíktin Umpuarma.

## Language & religion

Shuar is a Chicham language, and spiritual life centers on ancestral spirits, shamans, invisible darts, and plant medicines.

- **Shuar language** (*Shuar*) — Shuar means “people” and is spoken in the Ecuadorian Amazon, including Morona Santiago and Pastaza.
- **Shaman** (*uwishin*) — An uwishin is a shaman believed to possess and control invisible darts.
- **Invisible darts** (*tsentsak*) — Tsentsak are invisible darts that shamans are believed to control.
- **Ayahuasca** (*natem*) — Shamans ingest natem, identified in the source as Ayahuasca, to control tsentsak.
- **Medicinal plant** (*piripiri*) — Piripiri, a Cyperus species, is used as a medicinal plant.
