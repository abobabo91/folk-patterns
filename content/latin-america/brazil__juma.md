## Overview

The Júma are an Indigenous people of Brazil who live in the Terra Indígena Juma in the Amazonas, along the Mucuim River, a tributary of Rio Açuã. They are also known as Kagwahibm, Kagwahiph, Kagwahiv, Kavahiva, Kawahip, Kawaib, and Yumá people. In the 18th century, the Juma numbered between 12,000–15,000 people; the population was 300 in 1940, four in 1998, and 17 descendants of Aruká's as of 2021. Júma, also called Arara-Kawahib, is one of the eight ethnic varieties of the Kagwahiva language, belonging to Subgroup VI of the Tupi-Guarani languages. The available sources provide limited information about Juma folk culture beyond their location, population history, and language documentation.

## Oral tradition & literature

Juma language documentation has been conducted since 2019 through recordings of vocabulary lists, traditional narratives, and everyday stories. Some texts can be accessed through the website of the Survey of California and Other Indian Languages.

## Language & religion

Júma, or Arara-Kawahib, is one of the eight ethnic varieties of the Kagwahiva language. Kagwahiva belongs to Subgroup VI of the Tupi-Guarani languages. The most elderly native speaker, called Aruka Juma, died in 2021. There are no supersegmentals, including stress.
