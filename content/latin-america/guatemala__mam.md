## Overview

The Mam are an Indigenous Maya people living in the western highlands of Guatemala and south-western Mexico. Most Mam people in Guatemala live in the departments of Huehuetenango, San Marcos, and Quetzaltenango; the Mam population in Mexico lives principally in the Soconusco region of Chiapas. The sources give a Guatemalan population of 617,171 and a Mexican population of 23,632. In pre-Columbian times, the Mam were part of the Maya civilization, and Zaculeu was the pre-Columbian capital of the Mam kingdom. Many Mam live in and around Huehuetenango, while others live in small mountain hamlets. Quetzaltenango, also called Xela, was originally Mam. Mam is a Mayan language, and many Mam people speak both Mam and Spanish.

## Language & religion

Mam is a Mayan language spoken in the Guatemalan departments of Quetzaltenango, Huehuetenango, San Marcos, and Retalhuleu, and in the Mexican states of Campeche and Chiapas. It is closely related to Tektitek; together they form the Mamean sub-branch of the Mayan language family. Along with the Ixilan languages, Awakatek, and Ixil, they form the Greater Mamean sub-branch, one of the two branches of the Eastern Mayan languages.

Mam varieties in Mexico and Guatemala are divided into Northern Mam, Southern Mam, Central Mam, Western Mam, and Soconusco Mam. Contact between Mam communities is limited, and the language varies considerably from village to village, although mutual intelligibility is possible through practice. Mam is spoken in 64 communities in four Guatemalan departments and in numerous communities in Campeche and Chiapas.

Mam has no independent pronouns; pronouns occur as bound morphemes. Its grammar includes Set A ergative markers and Set B absolutive markers. The sources describe Mam as having verbal and non-verbal sentences, predicate-initial structure, and VSO basic word order in verbal sentences with two nominal arguments.
