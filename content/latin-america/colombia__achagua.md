## Overview

The Achagua, also called Achawa and Axagua, are an Indigenous people of Colombia and Venezuela. Their territory at the time of the Spanish colonization of the Americas covered the present-day Venezuelan states of Bolívar, Guárico and Barinas. Achagua people live in large villages, where clans live together in communal houses. In the late twentieth century, several hundred Achaguas remained. Their language belongs to the Maipurean Arawakan group and is spoken in the Meta Department of Colombia. The sources document Achagua farming, communal settlement, polygamy, curare-poisoned arrows, and a language similar to Piapoco.

## Language & religion

Achagua, or Achawa, is an Arawakan language spoken in the Meta Department of Colombia and is similar to Piapoco. It is described as a Maipurean Arawakan language traditionally spoken by the Achagua people of Venezuela and east-central Colombia. An estimated 250 individuals speak it, many of whom also speak Piapoco or Spanish. Achagua has 1–5% literacy.
