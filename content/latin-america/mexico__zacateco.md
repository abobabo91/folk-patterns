## Overview

The Zacatecos, also called Zacatecas, were an Indigenous group identified by the Aztecs among the peoples called Chichimecas. They lived across most of what is now the state of Zacatecas and the northeastern part of Durango, with principal population centers in Malpaís, around Peñón Blanco, and around the Cerro de la Bufa. Their territory also extended toward what is now Los Altos Jalisco and overlapped with the Caxcanes. The sources describe many direct descendants living in Zacatecas, Durango, and other large cities of Mexico, while stating that much of Zacateco culture and tradition has disappeared through assimilation and mestizaje. The Zacatec language is extinct and poorly attested, and its relationship to other languages remains uncertain.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Most Zacatecos were nomadic, although a few groups were essentially sedentary. Both men and women wore their hair down to their waist. Some Chichimeca tribes wore their heads braided. Zacatecos used body paint and tattoos to distinguish themselves from other tribes. They were known to wear skin coverings below the knees and skin headbands on their foreheads, and they occasionally wore leather-soled sandals.

### Architecture

 They state that some tribes had temples dedicated to worship in the southwestern part of the state of Zacatecas. At El Cerro del Sombrero, a hill in El Teul Gonzalez De Ortega, the source describes temples, ball courts, and ancient channels through which the tribe extracted fresh water from the hill.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They mention only leather-soled sandals, skin coverings, skin headbands, temples, ball courts, and ancient water channels.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Zacatecos used body paint and tattoos to distinguish themselves from other tribes. The sources also describe hair worn down to the waist and skin headbands worn on the forehead.

## Music & performance

 A language record from 1940 concerns two travelling musicians from Peña Colorada, Zacatecas, but the source discusses their uncertain language vocabulary rather than their music.

## Dance & theatre

 They mention ancient ball courts at El Cerro del Sombrero.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state that some tribes had temples dedicated to some kind of worship in the southwestern part of the state of Zacatecas, including temples at El Cerro del Sombrero, but provide no detailed account of associated rituals.

## Foodways

 The account of Spanish conquest states that war, slavery, and famine contributed to a drastic decline in the Zacateco population.

## Oral tradition & literature

 The sources state that much of Zacateco culture, language, art, and traditions has become difficult to reconstruct. The only preservation effort described is Pedro Hendrichs’s 1940 recording of a short vocabulary from two travelling musicians who said they were from Peña Colorada, Zacatecas.

## Language & religion

The Zacatec language is described as extinct and poorly attested. It was likely part of the Uto-Aztecan language family, although it has also been suspected to be a Nahuan language or close to Huichol. In 1940, Pedro Hendrichs recorded a short vocabulary from two travelling musicians who called their language Zacatec and said it was distinct from Huichol, Cora, Tepehuan, and Nahuatl. Howard F. Cline proposed that the vocabulary might represent a remnant of the Guachichil language or another regional language rather than the language of the historical Zacatec tribe, while Wick R. Miller proposed that it could have been Huichol. The sources state that some tribes had temples dedicated to worship.
