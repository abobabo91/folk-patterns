## Overview

The Inga are an indigenous ethnic group native to portions of Colombia, Ecuador, and Peru, with a claimed historical relation to the Incas. Many Inga people live traditionally in the Sibundoy Valley, while communities are also found across the borders of Colombia, Ecuador, and Peru. Their language is Inga Kichwa, a dialect of Quechua. Inga Kichwa is spoken in the Colombian Putumayo region, where it has Highland and Jungle dialects. Highland Inga is spoken in the Sibundoy valley, while Jungle Inga is spoken on the Putumayo and Japurá Rivers. Almost all Inga people are bilingual in Inga and Spanish, and concern has been expressed that the Inga language might be endangered.

The sources also state that significant linguistic and anthropological work concerning the Inga peoples has been undertaken in the past 30 years.

## Language & religion

Inga Kichwa is a dialect of Quechua spoken in the Colombian Putumayo region by the Inga people. It has two dialects: Highland Inga, spoken in the Sibundoy valley, and Jungle Inga, spoken on the Putumayo and Japurá Rivers. The sources state that Quechua was used as an administrative language in the Andes Mountains during the Incan Empire and into the Spanish Viceroyalty of Peru. They connect the introduction of Inga Kichwa with relations with the Incas and with a Jesuit mission briefly established in the region. Almost all Inga people are bilingual in Inga and Spanish.
