## Overview

The Suruí de Rondônia, also known as Paiter, Suruí, and Suruí do Jiparaná, are an Indigenous people of Brazil. They live in ten villages near the Mato Grosso–Rondônia border. The Paiter are farmers who cultivate coffee, while historical accounts also describe foraging, fishing, and hunting before prolonged contact with the modern world. Their language, Suruí-Paíter, belongs to the Tupi-Guarani language family; the related description of Suruí de Rondônia places it within the Tupi group and Monde language family. There were 1,375 Suruí-Paíter in 2014. Their recent history includes severe population loss after contact, pressure from wood extraction and mining, territorial organization through Metairelá, and projects focused on forest protection and carbon management. Their use of digital technologies to monitor their territory is also documented.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe major historical changes connected with contact, disease, religious and administrative intervention, resource exploitation, territorial organization, and forest-management projects, but these are not presented as ritual traditions.

## Foodways

The sources identify farming and coffee cultivation among the Paiter. They also state that, before contact, the Surui people lived by foraging, fishing, and hunting.

## Oral tradition & literature

They mention that portions of the Bible were translated into Suruí-Paíter in 1991 and refer to language documentation projects concerning whistled and instrumental speech and traditional culture among the Gavião and Suruí of Rondônia.

## Language & religion

The Suruí speak Suruí-Paíter, also called Suruí of Jiparaná, Paíter, or Suruí-Paíter. The language is described as belonging to the Tupi-Guarani language family and, in the language article, to the Tupi group and Monde language family. The Suruí of Rondônia call themselves Paiter, a term glossed as “the true people, we ourselves.” The sources state that portions of the Bible were translated into Suruí-Paíter in 1991.
