## Overview

The Guató are an Indigenous people living on the upper Paraguay River, along the border of modern-day Brazil and Bolivia. They mainly reside in Guató Indigenous Territory and Baía dos Guató Indigenous Territory. The Guató language is spoken by two Guató people of Brazil and is a language isolate; it has also been provisionally classified as a branch of the Macro-Jê languages, although no evidence for that affiliation was found by Eduardo Ribeiro and Hein van der Voort. The Guató aided the Brazilians in the war with Paraguay from 1865–70. A 2004 movie, *500 Almas* (“500 Souls”), was named after the approximate number of Guatós left.

## Language & religion

Guató is a language isolate spoken by two of the Guató people of Brazil. It is a VSO language with agglutination and ergative alignment, and it has high and low tones. The language has eight oral vowels, five nasal vowels, and 17 consonants. Guató uses a quinary, or base-5, system up to 20, followed by a decimal system for large numbers. It was first documented in wordlists beginning with François-Louis Laporte, comte de Castelnau’s 1851 publication, while the only substantial grammar was written by Adair Pimentel Palácio and published in 1984.
