## Overview

The Tolupan, also called the Jicaque, are an Indigenous ethnic group of Honduras. They primarily inhabit the northwest coast of Honduras and the community Montaña de La Flor in central Honduras. Approximately 500 Tolupan people speak Tol, also known as Eastern Jicaque, Tolupan, Tolpan, and Torupan, in the La Montaña de la Flor reservation in Francisco Morazán Department. The Tolupan are an agrarian people who raise beans, maize, and sweet and bitter manioc; they also fish, hunt, and raise livestock. Their history includes both assimilation into mainstream culture and the maintenance of traditional lifeways in Montaña de la Flor. The sources describe their agricultural practices, language, settlement history, and social organization.

## Language & religion

Tol, also known as Eastern Jicaque, Tolupan, Tolpan, and Torupan, is spoken by approximately 500 Tolupan people in La Montaña de la Flor reservation in Francisco Morazán Department, Honduras. Tol speakers refer to themselves as the Tolpán, while Ladinos call them Jicaques or Turrupanes. Tol was formerly spoken in much of Yoro Department; only a few speakers were reported in the Yoro Valley in 1974. Its former extent ran from the Río Ulúa in the west to modern-day Trujillo in the east and to the Río Sulaco in the inland south, including areas around modern-day El Progreso, La Ceiba, and possibly San Pedro Sula. The language has basic SOV constituent order and consistently head-final constituent order.
