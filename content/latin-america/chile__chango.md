## Overview

The Changos, also known as Camanchacos or Camanchangos, were Indigenous maritime peoples who inhabited the Pacific coast from southern Peru to north-central Chile, including the coast of the Atacama Desert. “Chango” does not designate a single distinct tribe or ethnic group, but a loose grouping of disparate coastal communities that shared a similar way of life. Their culture developed within the Chinchorro tradition and alongside neighbouring cultures such as the Atacameños. Although much of their culture has disappeared and the Chango culture is considered extinct, Chile legally recognized the Changos as an original Indigenous people in 2020; about 4,725 people self-declare membership in the ethnic group. Their original language is basically unknown.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources mention cave paintings showing men hunting and fishing, and sea creatures such as seals, turtles and whales.

### Clothing & dress

The Changos used vicuña wool, feathers, bird skins, shells, and the bones and teeth of sea creatures to make practical and decorative items, including clothing and blankets. Seal skins were especially important in Chango life, although the sources describe them primarily as materials for rafts rather than as garments. The sources do not distinguish everyday and ceremonial dress, men's and women's clothing, head coverings, belts, footwear, or named garment types.

### Architecture

They describe communities as either nomadic or sedentary groups organized around nuclear family units, with each group independent and providing its own food and other resources.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Changos made and painted ceramic utensils. They also used seal meat, fat and bones for food and tools; seal intestines for fishing equipment; and shells, feathers, bird skins, vicuña wool, and the bones and teeth of sea creatures for tools, cutlery, blankets, clothing, jewellery, and other practical or decorative objects. Their fishing technology included nets, hooks and harpoons. Rafts developed from primitive reed constructions to craft made from three wooden planks and later to seal skins fastened to wooden frames.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources state that the Changos made jewellery from shells and from the bones and teeth of sea creatures. One early Spanish source described Chango skin as red, and the sources report that 16th century Changos possibly covered their skin with red iron pigments. No named jewellery forms, tattoo practices, hair practices, or ritual functions are documented.

## Festivals & rituals

The Chinchorro tradition associated with Chango culture is noted for the practice of mummifying the dead.

## Foodways

Chango communities were experts at exploiting the resources of the sea. Groups specialized in particular fish, including tuna, conger eels, mullet, mackerel and octopus. Fish were caught with nets, hooks and harpoons. The capture of seals was crucial: meat, fat and bones were used for food and tools, while skins and intestines were used for rafts and fishing equipment. The Changos also gathered shellfish and traded dried fish, shellfish, animal hide, guano, fat and shells with inland tribes in exchange for wool, fruit, maize and coca.

## Oral tradition & literature

The term “chango” was first documented in the 17th century by Spanish conquistadors, who perceived little cultural difference among local native communities; the sources do not preserve Chango oral texts connected with this history.

## Language & religion

The original language of the Changos is basically unknown and is known only from toponyms and place names. Loukotka classified it with Uru–Chipaya, but the sources state that there is insufficient information to determine this. The language was distinct from Kunza, Quechua and Aymara. By 1870, Changos around Paposo appear to have spoken a dialect of Mapudungun, the language of the Mapuche people of south-central Chile. Coastal toponyms in the Antofagasta Region claimed to be Mapuche include Taltal and Quebrada Mamilla.
