## Overview

The Mura are an Indigenous people living in the central and eastern parts of Amazonas, Brazil, along the Amazon River from the Madeira to the Purus. About 587 Mura occupy the Cunhã-Sapucaia Indigenous Territory along the Igapó-Açu River. The Mura were formerly powerful and played an important part in Brazilian history during colonial times, resisting Portuguese expansion and later suffering enslavement, repression, massacres, and territorial displacement. Their economic life includes fishing, farming, logging, livestock farming, straw farming, and ecotourism. Mura is now represented principally by Pirahã, its sole surviving dialect. The language is notable for agglutinativity, a very small phoneme inventory, whistled speech, tone, and a perception-based numeral system.

## Festivals & rituals

Mura Christianity includes baptism, some feasts, and the veneration of some saints. Mura animism has been influenced by Catholicism and includes characters such as the Holy Ghost, Saint Anthony, Saint Peter, and a Saint John, although the specific Saint John is not stated. Animist practices incorporated into Christianity include flagellation and narcotics usage.

## Language & religion

Mura is a language of Amazonas, Brazil, and is most famous for Pirahã, its sole surviving dialect. The Mura language is typified by agglutinativity, a very small phoneme inventory, whistled speech, tone, and a limited, perception-based numeral system. The historically listed dialects or ethnic names are Bohurá, also called Buxwaray; Pirahã, also called Pirahá or Pirahán; and Yahahí, also spelled Jahahi. Bohurá was spoken on the Autaz River, Pirahã is the surviving dialect, and Yahahí was associated with the Branco River. The Mura/Bohurá endonym is recorded as Buhuraen or Buxivaray / Buxwarahay. Mura animism is syncretic with Catholicism, while shamans are feared and respected and believed to possess magical powers.
