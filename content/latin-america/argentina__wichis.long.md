## Overview

The Wichí are a people of the Chaco, the dry, flat, arid forest that extends across Bolivia, Argentina, Paraguay and Brazil. Museum records for the objects shown here file them under the name *Wichí* and also under the older collecting labels *Mataco* and *Mataco-Vejos*, the latter entered with a question mark by the cataloguer who could not confirm the subgroup. The sources used for this profile give no population figure, no language classification and no settlement names, so the atlas cannot be specific about where within the Chaco these particular objects were made. What the records do document is a material repertoire built almost entirely from plant fibre, wood and animal parts: twined and plaited bags, plaited body garments, carved tobacco pipes, and reed-and-wood hunting and fighting gear. One catalogue entry notes that the Chaco forest is being cleared at speed for commercial plantations and its water poisoned.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Caraguata-fibre bags** — Bags twined or woven from *caraguata* fibre, worked as string rather than as woven cloth. One unfinished example survives with its wooden needle still catalogued alongside it, showing the netting in progress; its pattern is a field of diamonds in beige, red-brown and dark brown.

**Corded carrying bags** — Bags woven of fibre cord and fitted with a carrying strap, patterned with lozenges in black against the undyed ground. The same construction serves as a baby-carrier, one recorded example carrying a faint black lozenge pattern.

**Hexagon-patterned twined bag** — A bag twined of vegetable-fibre string whose pattern is built from hexagons rather than the more usual diamond grid.

**Plaited fibre body garment** — A cylinder of plaited vegetable fibre with two straps to pass over the shoulders, ornamented with horizontal lines of black pattern; a second example of the same form is registered as a war shirt and accented with dark brown stripes rather than black.

**Motif vocabulary.** Diamonds, worked in beige, red-brown and dark brown on bag fronts; lozenges in black on corded bags and baby-carriers; hexagons on twined string bags; horizontal black lines and dark brown stripes banding the plaited body garments; incised and painted zigzags and diamonds on tobacco pipes.

### Clothing & dress

Dress in these records is fibre work rather than cloth. The body garment is a plaited cylinder of vegetable fibre, sleeveless and held up by two straps pressed over the shoulders, its surface ornamented with horizontal lines of black pattern. A second garment of the same cylindrical plaited construction is catalogued as a war shirt and is accented with dark brown stripes. Alongside it the British Museum holds a war head-dress made of feathers, which with the war shirt and the clubs forms the only set of objects in this collection explicitly tied to fighting dress. The sources used say nothing about footwear, belts, head coverings outside the feather war head-dress, or any distinction between men's and women's dress.

### Architecture

The sources used for this profile — museum catalogue records for the objects shown, with no Wikipedia article or UNESCO inscription available — say nothing about Wichí house form, building materials or the built environment.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

No ceramics or metalwork appear in these records; the containers are animal and plant material. A water vessel is made from the shell of an armadillo, collected on the Swedish Hernmarck expedition of 1908–1909 and attributed to the Wichí of the Chaco. Tobacco pipes are carved from wood in several forms: a hollowed-through cylinder incised and painted on the outside with diamonds; a straight stem with a broad mouthpiece, its sides ornamented with rough zigzags; one with a broad mouthpiece and two bands encircling the stem; and one of white wood with a short stem opening into the bowl. Hunting and fighting gear includes bundled arrows of reed and wood, some fletched with feathers and all notched at the bottom, catalogued as used with a bow; a spear of wood, gum and sinew; and war clubs carved from a single piece of wood, thicker at one end. A wooden needle used in bag-making is preserved with its unfinished bag.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The only adornment in these records is the war head-dress made of feathers; the sources used do not cover jewellery, tattooing, body paint or hair practices.

## Language & religion
