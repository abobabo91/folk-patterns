## Overview

The Kaingang are an Indigenous Brazilian ethnic group living in the southern Brazilian states of Paraná, Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul and the southeastern state of São Paulo. Their population was around 51,000 in 2022. The Kaingang language is a member of the Jê family and is spoken by about 60–65% of a nation of about 30,000 people; most speakers also speak Portuguese. Kaingang society is divided into two exogamic groups, *kamé* and *kanhru*, understood in the Kaingang worldview as the two originating halves of the world, nature and humanity. The sources present these divisions, body markings, language, and relations with ancestral land as important aspects of Kaingang culture.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Body marking patterns** — *Kamé* marking is associated with *téi*, meaning lines, and consists of parallel straight lines and zigzags. *Kanhru* marking is associated with *rór*, meaning dots, and consists of dots and concentric circles.

**Motif vocabulary.** *téi* — lines; *rór* — dots; parallel straight lines; zigzags; dots; concentric circles.

### Clothing & dress

 They do document painting or marking the face and body: *kamé* people use parallel straight lines and zigzags, while *kanhru* people use dots and concentric circles.

### Architecture

 They state that Kaingang groups have a crucial relation with the land where they were born and where their ancestors were buried.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 The Wikipedia account mentions two barrels full of fuel in connection with a 2006 confrontation at a small hydro plant, but does not present them as culturally distinctive objects.

### Jewelry & body adornment

 They document face and body marking associated with the two exogamic groups: *kamé* marking uses lines and zigzags, while *kanhru* marking uses dots and concentric circles. The markings are patrilineal; when a child’s father is not Kaingang, the child receives the marking from the maternal grandfather.

## Music & performance

 They mention no musical tradition, dance accompaniment, or ceremonial repertoire.

## Festivals & rituals

 They do state that Kaingang groups have a crucial relation with the land where they were born and their ancestors were buried. The sources also describe *kamé* and *kanhru* as the two originating halves of the world in Kaingang worldview, associated respectively with nature and humanity.

## Foodways

 The Kaingang language source gives *rãgró*, “to plant,” and examples translated as “He planted beans” and “We planted beans,”.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They state that dictionaries and grammars are available for Kaingang and that a Kaingang Bible, a dictionary, and other publications have been published.

## Language & religion

Kaingang is a Southern Jê language spoken by the Kaingang people of southern Brazil. The Kaingang territory occupies São Paulo, Paraná, Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul, and until the beginning of the 20th century also included Misiones, Argentina. The language has about 30,000 people in the Kaingang nation, with about 60–65% speaking it; most also speak Portuguese. In the 1960s, Ursula Wiesemann studied the language through missionary interest conducted by the Summer Institute of Linguistics. A school was established in 1969 to teach reading and writing. Wiesemann proposed an alphabet based on the Latin script, consisting of fourteen consonants and fourteen vowels.
