## Overview

The Tremembé, also called Teremembé, are an Indigenous people of Brazil, centered today in the Almofala district of Itarema, Ceará, with additional communities in Acaraú and Itapipoca on the Atlantic coast. In 2020, their population was recorded as 3,837. Their historical territory extended across coastal areas of Pará, Maranhão, Piauí and Ceará, including regions near the Acaraú River and Fortaleza. The Tremembé were identified as a Tapuia people, meaning a non-Tupi population, and lived among Tupi peoples including the Tupinambá, Potiguara and Tabajara. Their original language is extinct and entirely unknown; current members speak Portuguese as their mother tongue. Their documented material culture includes crescent-shaped axes, also called anchor axes.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that crescent-shaped axes, also called “anchor axes,” were produced on the first night of a crescent moon, and that the Tremembé believed these axes made them invincible in battle.

## Language & religion

Tremembé, also called Teremembé or Taramembe, is an extinct language of Brazil that was once spoken along the northern Brazilian coasts from Pará to Ceará. Only a few personal names were recorded before the language became extinct in the early 19th century. It was described as a Tapuia language rather than one of the dominant Tupi–Guarani languages; its classification is uncertain, and it was considered “very likely” to belong to the Macro-Jê languages by John Alden Mason. Current Tremembé members speak Portuguese as their mother tongue.
