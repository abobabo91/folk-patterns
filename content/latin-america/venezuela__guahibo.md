## Overview

The Guahibo are an Indigenous people native to the Llanos, or savanna plains, in eastern Colombia and southern Venezuela near the Colombian border. In Colombia, their territory includes Arauca, Meta, Guainia, and Vichada departments. Their population was estimated at 23,772 people in 1998. The Guahibo language belongs to the Guahiboan language family of South America. The sources describe Guahibo history, language, and several historical depictions.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources identify José Eustasio Rivera’s 1924 novel *La Vorágine* as portraying hunting parties organized against Indigenous people. An 1856 watercolor by Manuel María Paz is described as an early depiction of the Guahibo people in Casanare Province.

## Language & religion

Guahibo is a Guahiban language spoken by about 23,006 people in Colombia and an additional 8,428 in Venezuela. The Guahiboan language family includes the Guahibo language, also called Sikuani, and the existing dialects listed are Guahibo, Amorua, and Tigrero. Many languages have been lost and replaced by Spanish. The sources describe a written form of Guahibo, a Guahibo newspaper, a dictionary, and a grammar book.
