## Overview

The Raizal are a Black Colombian ethnic group from the Archipelago of San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina, off Colombia’s Caribbean coast. Colombian authorities classify them among the Afro-Colombian ethnic groups under the multicultural policy pursued since 1991. In 2005, they represented 57% of the 60,000 inhabitants of the San Andrés y Providencia Department; according to the 2015 census, they represented 39.4% of the archipelago’s population. Raizals are mostly multi-racial, with a majority of West African and Northern European descent. Their cultural identity is closely associated with San Andrés–Providencia Creole, an English-based Caribbean creole spoken by approximately 20,000–30,000 people.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that interest in preserving San Andrés–Providencia Creole has become important for locals and Colombians, and that Creole plays a symbolic role in Raizal cohesion, identity, and cultural heritage.

## Language & religion

San Andrés–Providencia Creole is an English-based creole language spoken in the San Andrés and Providencia Department of Colombia by the native Raizals. It is also known as “San Andrés Creole,” “Bende,” and “Islander Creole English,” and its two main strands are San Andres Creole English, or Saintandrewan, and Providence Creole English. Its vocabulary originates in English, while its phonetics and expressions also reflect Spanish and African languages, particularly Kwa languages, including Twi and Ewe, and Igbo languages. The archipelago uses Creole, English, and Spanish. San Andrés–Providencia Creole became an official language in its territory of influence under the Colombian Constitution of 1991. The sources mention Baptist churches and liturgical use of English.
