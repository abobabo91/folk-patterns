## Overview

The Okaina, also called Ocaina, are an Amazonian Indigenous people of Peru and Colombia who are today in danger of extinction. In 2012, there were 176 Okaina. Their language, Ocaina, belongs to the Witotoan language family and forms its own group within the Huitoto-Ocaina sub-family. Ocaina is spoken in northeastern Peru and in the Amazonas region of Colombia, although few children speak it. In Amazonia, only 50 people were reported as still speaking Ocaina, while another account gives 54 speakers in northeastern Peru and 12 in Colombia. The Okaina share history and cultural characteristics with the Huitotos, Resígaros, and Andoques.

### Clothing & dress

They state that ceremonial body painting was practiced among Indigenous peoples in the Putumayo region and that the Okaina were particularly well-known for their elaborate patterns.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ceremonial body painting, usually made with red dye, was a common practice among many Indigenous peoples in the Putumayo region. The Okaina were particularly well-known for elaborate body-painting patterns.

## Language & religion

Ocaina is an Indigenous American language spoken in western South America. It belongs to the Witotoan language family and is its own group within the Huitoto-Ocaina sub-family. The language has two dialects, Dukaiya and Ibo'tsa. Ocaina syllables may carry high or low tone, and its writing system uses a Latin alphabet. High tone is indicated with an acute accent, while nasalization is indicated by inserting n after a vowel.
