## Overview

The Chachi, also known as Cayapas, are an ethnic group living in the rainforest of northwestern Esmeraldas, on the northern coast of Ecuador. They inhabit the river valleys of the Cayapas, Canandé, and Muisne Rivers, including the Centro El Encanto section of the Cotacachi Cayapas Ecological Reserve, where they have co-existed with Afro Ecuadorian people for about 400 years. There were approximately 5,000 Chachi in 2003. Their language, Chaʼpalaa, is also known as Chachi or Cayapa and belongs to the Barbacoan language family. Chachi material culture includes stilt houses, bark paper or fabric, canoes, subsistence agriculture, fishing, and notably basketwork sold in several Ecuadorian towns.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Women’s clothing includes a loose skirt with a sash at the waist. Men wear a long shirt with pants. The sources state that both men and women have bare chests.

### Architecture

Chachi homes are built on stilts, with sides and a roof made of thatch.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They identify the canoe as a means of transportation and state that bark is used to form a kind of paper or fabric. Chachi economy is based on traditional subsistence agriculture and fishing, and the people are well noted for basketwork, which they sell in stalls in Borbón, Limones, Esmeraldas, and Quito.

## Music & performance

They identify Chaʼpalaa as the language of the Chachi people and mention documentation by the missionary P. Alberto Vittadello, who had lived for seven years among the tribe by the time his description was published in Guayaquil, Ecuador, in 1988. This linguistic documentation does not provide information about music or performance.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources mention Chachi death rituals only in the title of a further-reading article, “People into Ghosts. Chachi Death Rituals as Shape-Shifting,”. They also state that, since colonization, Chachi have converted to Catholicism.

## Foodways

They state only that the Chachi economy is based on traditional subsistence agriculture and fishing.

## Oral tradition & literature

According to Chachi oral tradition, the people originated in Imbabura Province, in the mountains near Ibarra. The tradition states that they fled after the Castilian invasion, spent time in Chimborazo, and were forced to settle in their present area after the Spanish conquest of Ibarra led them to move to Esmeraldas. The sources also mention folktale collections and a publication titled *Cha'palaachi: Textos*.

## Language & religion

Chaʼpalaa, also known as Chachi or Cayapa, is a Barbacoan language spoken in northern Ecuador by around 5,870 Chachi people. The name “Chaʼpalaa” means “language of the Chachi people.” The language has four vowels and 23 consonant phonemes. It is written with the Latin alphabet and has agglutinative morphology with Subject-Object-Verb word order. The sources state that the Chachi have converted to Catholicism since colonization. Their group leader is known as “Uni,” while the “Chaitalas” hold a lesser role.
