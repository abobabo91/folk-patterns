## Overview

The Wiwa, also known as the Damana, are an Indigenous people of Colombia who live in the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta mountain region, particularly between the basins of the northern Guachaca River and the southwestern Frío River. Their territories are within the Cesar, La Guajira, and Magdalena Departments, and some also live in the Sierra Nevada de Gonawindua territory in Santa Marta and the Campo Alegre Reservation in Cesar. The 2018 Colombian National Census recorded 18,202 Damana people. They speak Dʉmʉna, also called Damana, Malayo, Wiwa, or Guamaka, a Chibchan language spoken by more than one thousand people. The Damana are direct descendants of the Tairona people. Their villages function as ceremonial and ritual centers, while mamos and sagas advise the community and direct ceremonies.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Shamarra* — A white tea shirt made by Damana women. The source identifies the garment and its makers.

*Yina* — A sash worn by Damana women as part of their clothing.

*Duadu* — A backpack made of home-spun cotton and carried by men.

*Suzu* — A backpack made from fique fiber.

### Clothing & dress

Damana men dress in white pants called *ganzurra*, white tea shirts called *shamarra*, and hats. Damana women wear blanket ponchos made by hand with textiles and a sash called *yina*. Male children and teenagers wear a white coat or tunic reaching the knees, together with a small backpack carried on the shoulder. Female children and adolescents also wear a tunic, typically longer and reaching the ankles.

### Architecture

Damana settlements consist of dispersed rectangular single-family houses located in the foothills and valleys of the Sierra mountains. The villages are ceremonial and ritual centers, and meetings are held in homes where stories and advice from the mamos are heard.

## Music & performance

They state that Damana villages are ceremonial and ritual centers, that meetings are held in homes, and that mamos and sagas direct ceremonies and rituals. The sources also mention *angag+k+n* talks, in which the spiritual and political authorities advise community members.

## Festivals & rituals

They describe villages as ceremonial and ritual centers, with meetings held in homes where stories and advice from the mamos are heard. The traditional male spiritual and political authorities are called *mamos*, while the female equivalent are called *sagas*. Both receive special education to learn about the Creator, understand nature, society, and people, heal, interpret dreams, and direct ceremonies and rituals. Mamos advise people individually, in meetings, and in *angag+k+n* talks. Mamo training begins at an early age and requires years of dedication.

## Foodways

The Damana are farmers who grow yuca, ñame, malanga, bananas, corn, beans, coca, and sugarcane or panela for family consumption. They grow coffee, identified in the source as *coffea arabica*, for commerce. Men and women also produce fique, whose fiber is made into hammocks and *suzu* backpacks. Damana people hunt iguanas, rabbits, rodents, and birds. They collect shells in order to extract lime required for the traditional consumption of coca.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that Damana villages are places where stories and advice from the mamos are heard.

## Language & religion

The native language of the Damana is Dʉmʉna, also known as Damana, Malayo, Wiwa, or Guamaka. It belongs to the Chibchan language family and is spoken by more than one thousand people. The language has seven vowel phonemes and nineteen consonant phonemes. A 2008 survey by the Colombian Ministry of Culture reported that 60% of Damana people spoke Damana well, 3% understood but did not speak it, 7.7% spoke and understood it little, and 28.2% neither spoke nor understood it. A large part of the population also speaks Spanish, and some Damana and Kogui people speak each other’s languages. The sources describe belief and ritual authority through the mamos and sagas, who learn about the Creator, nature, society, and people.
