## Overview

The Wai-wai, also written Waiwai or Wai Wai, are a Carib-speaking Indigenous people of Guyana and northern Brazil. In Guyana they live in the far south, near the headwaters of the Essequibo River, particularly in the Kanashen region and the Konashen Amerindian Community. There are approximately 200 Wai-wai in Guyana and about 2,000 in Brazil. Their society consists of different lowland forest peoples who have maintained much of their cultural identity, although Christianity was introduced in the late 1950s. They are associated with hunting, farming, hammock weaving, pottery, musical instruments, and a cultural and spiritual relationship with the rainforest. Waiwai is a Cariban language, with speakers in northern Brazil and across the border in southern Guyana and Suriname.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Hammock weaving** — The Wai-wai twist cotton into yarn for weaving. Along with Lokono and Kalina people, they are known for hammock weaving, and hammocks are woven on square hammock frames.

No named Wai-wai motifs or pattern vocabulary are given in the sources.

### Architecture

 The Umana Yana in Georgetown, Guyana, takes its name from the Wai-Wai for “meeting place,”.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Wai-wai artistic skills include pottery, woven combs, bone flutes, and other crafts. Primate body parts have non-game uses, including saki tail dusters and bone arrow points.

## Music & performance

The Wai Wai have an affinity for guitars, flutes, and hand drums. They often craft flutes and drums from natural materials found in the surrounding Amazon rainforest. Bone flutes are also listed among their artistic skills.

A Waiwai Collection of Niels Fock contains audio recordings of ceremonial chants and photographs made in the 1950s.

## Festivals & rituals

 They do describe several spiritual practices associated with the *yaskomo*, also called a medicine man or shaman in literature. The *yaskomo* is believed to be able to perform a soul flight for healing, to fly to the sky to consult cosmological beings for a name for a newborn baby, to fly to the cave of peccaries’ mountains to ask the father of peccaries for abundance of game, and to fly deep down in a river to obtain the help of other beings.

Marriage is called *Mansiya*. For women, marriage cannot take place until after the first menses, around age thirteen, and most women are married by age seventeen.

## Foodways

The Wai Wai are hunters and farmers. Their traditional farming method was “slash and burn.” The light, thin soil and annual rainfall of 4 meters make it challenging to produce enough food.

Consuming primate meat is described as a source of pride for Waiwai, distinguishing them as “‘true’ forest people” and separating them from most other Indigenous groups in Guyana. Primate hunting can demonstrate masculinity and improve chances of betrothal. Various primate body parts also have non-game uses, including saki tail dusters and bone arrow points.

The rainforest is described as providing nuts, fruit, and fresh fish, as well as natural medicines.

## Language & religion

Waiwai, also called Uaiuai, Uaieue, or Ouayeone, is a Cariban language of northern Brazil, with a couple hundred speakers across the border in southern Guyana and Suriname. The Waiwai people have been described as an amalgamation of distinct peoples, including the Mawayana, Hixkaryana, Katwena, Sherew, the uncontacted Karafawyana, Sikiana, Tunayana, and Parukoto. The Waiwai-Parukoto language subsequently became the lingua franca of these people, while some other languages continued to be used in the homes of their respective groups. Since 2023, Waiwai has been one of 17 official languages of Amazonas state in Brazil. As of 1998, 85% of Waiwai people, including “many” young people, were literate in their language.

The *yaskomo* is believed to reach sky, earth, and water through soul flight. Western influence severely changed Wai-wai traditional culture and religion, and many Wai-wai converted to Christianity. In 1949, many migrated to Brazil following the persecution, violent imprisonment, and forceful extradition of Christian missionaries by the authoritarian government run by Forbes Burnham. When the missions left Guyana because their residence permits were not renewed, 700 Wai-Wai people followed them to Brazil. Some 150 Wai-Wai people who remained in Guyana came under the influence of Christian Brethren Bible Outreach.
