## Overview

The Lupaca, also called Lupaka or Lupaqa, were one of the divisions of the ancestral Aymaras. They lived for many centuries near Lake Titicaca in Peru, and their lands possibly extended into Bolivia. Their territory included seven urban centers in the present-day Puno region: Chucuito, Acora, Ilave, Juli, Pomata, Yunguyo and Zepita. The Lupaca formed a powerful kingdom after the collapse of the Tiwanaku Empire in the 11th century, were incorporated into the Inca Empire in the mid 15th century, and came under Spanish control in the 1530s. Their society is documented through its political organization, high-altitude pastoralism, agricultural adaptations and system of colonies at lower elevations.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The Lupaca occupied seven urban centers: Chucuito, Acora, Ilave, Juli, Pomata, Yunguyo and Zepita. The residence of the pre-Inca kings was probably at what is now the archaeological site of Cutimbo. Chucuito was the capital of the Inca province and is associated with the archaeological site of Inca Uyu.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

A *Quipu* recorded a 16th-century census of Chucuito province and was interpreted for the Spanish.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do describe a political division into two moieties, each with its own leader: the upper moiety, called “Alassa,” was headed by Qari, while the lower moiety, called “Massaa,” was headed by Kusi.

## Foodways

Lupaca subsistence combined high-altitude herding with agriculture in suitable locations. Potatoes, oca, olluco and quinoa could be grown on the Altiplano using terracing, raised beds and *Waru Waru* to moderate cold temperatures. Their herds of llamas and alpacas grazed the barren uplands up to the snowline. Lower western colonies in irrigated desert valleys of the Moquegua Region supplied maize, cotton and chili peppers, while eastern colonies supplied coca and tropical forest products. Communication with these colonies was maintained through llama caravans.

## Oral tradition & literature

A 16th-century census of Chucuito province was recorded on a *Quipu* and interpreted for the Spanish.

## Language & religion

The Lupaca were one of the divisions of the ancestral Aymaras, and the population recorded in the 16th-century Chucuito census included 15,778 Aymara, 4.129 Uru and 173 mitma households. Spanish control increasingly weakened the authority of traditional leaders, forced Aymaras to work in the silver mines of Potosí and resettle in “reductions,” and appropriated much former Lupaca land.
