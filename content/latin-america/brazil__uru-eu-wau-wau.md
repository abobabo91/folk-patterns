## Overview

The Uru-Eu-Wau-Wau are an Indigenous people of Brazil who live in the state of Rondônia, in six villages on the borders of the Uru-Eu-Uaw-Uaw Indigenous Territory. The territory is shared with the Amondawa, Uru Pa In, Jurureí, Parakua, and two uncontacted tribes whose names are not known. The people call themselves *Jupaú* and are also known by several other names, including Uru-Eu-Wau-Wau. Their population was initially estimated at fewer than 1,000 and was approximately 180 in a 2022 account. Uru-Eu-Wau-Wau is a Tupi–Guarani language and is described as a variety of Kagwahiva. Their documented cultural practices include hunting, the use of poisoned arrows, and distinctive genipapo tattoos around the mouth.

## Material culture

### Jewelry & body adornment

The Uru-Eu-Wau-Wau are known for distinctive tattoos around their mouths. These tattoos are made with genipapo, described in the source as a black vegetal dye.

## Foodways

The Uru-Eu-Wau-Wau are described as hunter-gatherers who hunt tapir and other game. The sources state that they use a poison made from tree bark on their arrows when hunting.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention the documentaries *Ten Thousand Years Older* and *The Territory*.

## Language & religion

The Uru-Eu-Wau-Wau call their language Jupaú. The language is identified as one of the varieties of Kagwahiva, a Tupi–Guarani language of Subgroup VI; the two Wikipedia articles describe it as one of eight and one of nine varieties, respectively. The language is also spelled Uru-Eu-Uau-Uau, Eru-Eu-Wau-Wau, and Ureuwawau, and is sometimes known simply as Kagwahiva. Missionaries are active among the Uru-Eu-Wau-Wau.
