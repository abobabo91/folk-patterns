## Overview

The Bokota, also called Bogotá or Bugleres, are an Indigenous people of Panama. They live in Bocas del Toro and north of Veraguas, in the west of the country. According to the 2010 Census, 26,871 Bogota lived in Panama. The Bokota are described as the smallest tribe in Panama. Their traditional language is Bokota, a dialect of Buglere, also known as Bugle, Murire, and Muoy. Buglere is a Chibchan language closely related to Guaymí. Bokota folk culture includes livestock, fishing, hunting, textile and vegetable-fiber production, distinctive clothing and adornment, round houses on stilts, and traditional ceremonies including the ceremony of lightning.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

* **Cobo** — Cobo are daily dresses made by the Bokota. The sources identify the garment.

* **Vegetable-fiber hats** — The Bokota make hats from vegetable fibers.

* **Backpacks** — Bokota material culture includes backpacks made by the community.

* **Baskets** — The Bokota make baskets.

### Clothing & dress

Men wear shirts made of manta-sucia. Women dress similarly to the Ngobes. The sources also mention daily dresses called cobo. Bokota clothing is accompanied by necklaces, facial paint in black and red, and shiny hair combs.

### Architecture

The Bokota live in round houses on stilts. They do record a traditional ceremony of lightning, which is intended to prevent lightning from striking the houses.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They do mention bows and arrows, spears, fishnets, vegetable-fiber hats, backpacks, baskets, and daily dresses called cobo, without providing further catalogue-style descriptions.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Bokota people wear necklaces, use facial paint of black and red, and wear shiny hair combs.

## Music & performance

 They do state that many traditional ceremonies are maintained, including the ceremony of lightning, which prevents lightning from striking Bokota houses, but provide no description of music or performance within that ceremony.

## Festivals & rituals

Many traditional Bokota ceremonies are maintained. The sources specifically name the ceremony of lightning, whose purpose is to prevent lightning from striking the community’s houses. No date, season, annual calendar, ritual sequence, officiant, or associated objects is provided.

## Foodways

The Bokota dedicate themselves to livestock, fishing, and hunting.

## Language & religion

Bokota is a traditional language of the Bokota people and is described as a dialect of Buglere. Buglere is also known as Bugle, Murire, and Muoy. It is a Chibchan language closely related to Guaymí. The language has two dialects, Sabanero and Bokotá (Bogota), spoken by the Bokota people. The sources describe phonological features including possible fricative realizations of voiced sounds in intervocalic position, an alternative realization of /dʒ/ in intervocalic position, and possible palatal realization of word-initial /ŋ/ before a vowel. They also state that /e/ and /o/ can have short allophones and that vowels can be nasalized beside nasal consonants.
