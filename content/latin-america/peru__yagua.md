## Overview

The Yagua are an Amazonian people living in small villages of 2 to 30 families along several rivers in Peru and Colombia. They are known above all for their palm-fibre crafts and their sophisticated hunting technology using blowguns and poisoned darts. Manioc is their food staple, complemented by blowgun-hunted game. Native arts and crafts remain an important part of household income.

## Material culture

Yagua material culture is dominated by palm fibre, with clay and wood used for everyday objects, and no metalwork documented in the record.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Palm fibre, especially chambira, is the foundation of Yagua textile work.

- **Chambira hammock** (*chambira*) — Spun palm fibre worked into hammocks for rest and sleep.
- **Carrying net** (*bärnät*) — Netted bags of palm or cotton fibre used by women to transport produce and household goods.
- **Dart quiver** (*koger*) — A 30-cm quiver of palm leaf holding blowgun darts wound tightly like a Venetian blind.
- **Men's fibre costume** — Complete costumes made from fibre of the Mauritia palm, documented in 1941 photographs.
- **Women's neck ornament** — Neck adornment woven from Astrocaryum palm fibre and photographed in manufacture in 1941.

### Clothing & dress

Palm fibre, not woven cloth, is the material of Yagua dress.

- **Men's fibre costume** — Complete garments made from Mauritia flexuosa palm fibre, worn by Yagua men.
- **Women's neck ornament** — Fibre neck adornment worn by Yagua women.
- **Arm ornament** — Paired arm ornaments collected from the Ucayali region, the earliest dated dress item in the museum record (1930).
- **Face paint** (*achiote*) — Blood-red pigment from annatto seeds used to paint faces, a practice conspicuous enough to shape the group's name.
- **Walking staff** — Special staffs carried by Yagua people, documented in 1941 photographs.

### Architecture

Yagua house form is not described in the available sources.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Yagua everyday goods are made of clay, wood, and palm fibre, with no metalwork.

- **Cooking pot** (*apati*) — Fired clay vessel used for cooking, photographed in use in 1941 and collected.
- **Grinding trough** — Wooden vessel of quinilla wood for pounding manioc, collected in 1941 with its original labels.
- **Blowgun** — Hunting weapon with separate cleaner, used to deliver curare-tipped darts at game.
- **Blowgun dart** (*leaf-midrib dart*) — Darts made of leaf midrib and carried in the koger quiver, some poisoned with curare.
- **Hammock** (*chambira hammock*) — Palm-fibre sleeping net, a daily necessity in Yagua life.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Yagua adornment is made of fibre and pigment rather than metal or beads.

- **Neck ornament** — Fibre necklace worn by women, made from Astrocaryum palm.
- **Arm ornament** — Paired bracelets from the Ucayali region, the earliest dated ornament in the collection (1930).
- **Face paint** (*achiote*) — Blood-red annatto seed pigment used for face painting, a signature Yagua practice.

## Music & performance

Musical practice in the record is documented through a single instrument.

- **Panpipe** — A panpipe photographed in use among the Wuisana subgroup on the Río Ampiyacu in 1941, now at the Museum of the American Indian in New York.

## Dance & theatre

Yagua dance and dramatic traditions are not described in the available sources.

## Festivals & rituals

Shamanism and ancestor veneration are the documented ritual practices.

- **Ancestor figures** — Balsa-wood figures made in memory of ancestors—three male and three female—photographed in 1941 among the Wuisana.
- **Shaman** (*rimyurá*) — Ritual specialist whose role is named in Yagua language and attested in early colonial records.
- **Clan** (*(j)ápiiryá*) — Social-ritual unit still used as family names, such as the red macaw clan, documented from early colonial times.

## Foodways

Manioc is the staple food, supplemented by blowgun-hunted game.

- **Manioc** (*yucca*) — Root crop ground in wooden troughs and a staple of Yagua diet.
- **Blowgun game** — Protein from animals hunted with blowgun and curare-tipped darts.
- **Cooking vessel** (*apati*) — Fired clay pot for cooking manioc and other foods.
- **Carrying net** (*bärnät*) — Chambira-fibre net for transporting food and household goods between garden, forest and village.

## Oral tradition & literature

Yagua stories are told at night and frame their truth through attribution to dead relatives.

- **Narrative formula** — Stories open with 'My deceased father (mother, grandfather, grandmother etc.) used to tell me,' lending authority to the telling.
- **Yagua Mythology** — Paul Powlison's book-length study arguing that Yagua narratives tend toward epic form, published in 1985.
- **Twins Stories** — Thomas E. Payne's linguistic study of character tracking in Yagua narrative cycles, published by University of California Press.

## Language & religion

Yagua is a small-family language with distinctive grammar; shamanism and Christianity coexist.

- **Yagua language** (*Peba–Yaguan family*) — Six vowels (oral and nasal), eleven consonants, highly agglutinative, verb-initial (VSO), with over forty noun classifiers and no adjectives.
- **Quinary counting** — Number system based on five, with separate words for animate and inanimate objects.
- **Self-referent** (*nijyąąmíy*) — Yagua word meaning 'people', used in contrast with 'white people' (mááy) and 'non-Yaguas' (munuñúmiy).
- **Shaman** (*rimyurá*) — Ritual specialist in traditional Yagua religion.
- **Quechua loanwords** — Quechua words outnumber Spanish ones in Yagua, evidence of either pre-conquest Inca contact or Spanish missionary influence.

## Glossary

- *chambira* — palm fibre used for hammocks, nets, and other goods
- *bärnät* — netted carrying bag for produce and household loads
- *koger* — palm-leaf dart quiver holding blowgun darts
- *achiote* — blood-red pigment from annatto seeds used for face paint
- *apati* — fired clay cooking vessel
- *rimyurá* — shaman or ritual specialist
- *(j)ápiiryá* — red macaw clan
- *Mauritia flexuosa* — palm species used for men's fibre garments
- *Astrocaryum* — palm species used for women's ornaments
- *curare* — poison applied to blowgun darts
- *Wuisana* — subgroup of the Yagua, also called 'Ants'
- *nijyąąmíy* — Yagua word for 'people'
- *mááy* — Yagua word for 'white people'
- *munuñúmiy* — Yagua word for 'savages, enemies, non-Yaguas'
- *Peba–Yaguan* — language family to which Yagua belongs
- *VSO* — verb-initial clause structure (verb-subject-object)
- *agglutinative* — language type building words from many morphemes
- *noun classifier* — grammatical element indicating object class
- *quinary* — counting system based on five
