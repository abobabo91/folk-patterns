## Overview

The Uspantek, also called Uspantecos or Uspantekos, are a Maya people in Guatemala, principally located in the municipality of Uspantán. Their language, Uspantek, is a K’ichean-Mamean language closely related to Kʼicheʼ. Uspantek is spoken in the Uspantán and Playa Grande Ixcán municipios, in the Department El Quiché. The available sources document the group’s geographic distribution and language, including its consonant, vowel, tone, and syllable structures.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources identify *Gramática uspanteka* [*Jkemiik yoloj li uspanteko*], *Vocabulario uspanteko* [*Cholyool Tzʼunun Kaabʼ], and *Diccionario bilingüe uspanteko-español* [*Cholaj tzijbʼal li Uspanteko*] as works concerning the language. They also mention a New Testament in Uspanteco.

## Language & religion

Uspantek, also called Uspanteco, Uspanteko, or Uspantec, is a Mayan language of Guatemala and is closely related to Kʼicheʼ. It is a K’ichean-Mamean language spoken in the Uspantán and Playa Grande Ixcán municipios, in the Department El Quiché. Uspantek is one of only four Mayan languages to have developed contrastive tone; the others named in the source are Yukatek, Mochoʼ, and one dialect of Tzotzil. It distinguishes vowels with high tone from vowels with low tone and has two phonemic tones: high and falling. The language has 24 consonants, including the glottal stop, 5 vowels contrasting in vowel length, and the principal syllable structures CVC, CV, and CCVC.
