## Overview

The Apurinã, also called the Ipurinã, Ipurinãn, Kangite, and Popukare, are an Indigenous people living near the Purus River in western Brazil. Their language, Apurinã, is a Southern Maipurean language spoken in the Amazon basin. Apurinã Indigenous communities are predominantly found along the Purus River in the Northwestern Amazon region, in the Amazonas state. Their population is spread over twenty-seven Indigenous lands along the Purus River, with an estimated total population of 9,500 people. The sources describe distinctive houses, small bark canoes, poisoned arrows, and the native god Guintiniri. They also document an endangered language with active–stative syntax and an extensive grammatical system.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Apurinã houses are long, low, and narrow. Their side walls and roof form a single structure: poles are fixed in the ground and then bent together until they meet, producing a pointed arch in cross-section.

## Language & religion

Apurinã, also called Ipurina, is a Southern Maipurean language spoken by the Apurinã people of the Amazon basin. It has active–stative syntax. The language is predominantly found along the Purus River in the Northwestern Amazon region of Brazil, especially in the Amazonas state. Its estimated total population is 9,500 people, but fewer than 30% of the Apurinã population are estimated to speak the language fluently. The language is described as endangered at level 3: adults still speak it, but children are no longer being exposed to it. Apurinã has future and non-future tense; the future is marked by the morpheme –ko, while the non-future has no specific tense marker. The sources identify Guintiniri as a native god.
