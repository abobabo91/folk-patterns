## Overview

The Yaneshaʼ, also known as the Amuesha, are an ethnic group of the Peruvian Amazon rainforest. Their population is over 7,000, distributed among 48 communities in Puerto Inca Province in Huánuco, Chanchamayo Province in Junín, and Oxapampa Province in Pasco. Their communities occupy altitudes ranging from 200 to 1600 meters above sea level and are situated along rivers including the Pichis, Palcazu, Pachitea, Huancabamba, Cacazú, Chorobamba, and Yurinaqui. The Yaneshaʼ speak Yaneshaʼ, a Maipurean language related to Asháninka and Yine. The sources connect their folk-culture profile with subsistence practices, agricultural diversification, ceramic crafts, language preservation, and the organization of Yaneshaʼ communities.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The marketing of ceramic crafts is identified as a source of income for Yaneshaʼ communities.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that Father Francisco de San José founded missions around Cerro de la Sal in the Villa Rica District and Quimiri in La Merced, Junin, to convert Indigenous populations to Christianity. In 1742, Indigenous people commanded by Juan Santos Atahualpa rebelled against the Spaniards and destroyed a number of missions, cutting off outside contact for several decades.

## Foodways

The Yaneshaʼ historically lived through hunting, fishing, and subsistence agriculture. In modern times, agricultural diversification and the cultivation of cash crops such as coffee and annatto have become more important. Hunting became much rarer after Yaneshaʼ communities began raising animals for consumption, and groups have also begun exploiting Cat's claw.

## Oral tradition & literature

A Yaneshaʼ Talking Dictionary was produced by Living Tongues Institute for Endangered Languages.

## Language & religion

Yaneshaʼ, also called Amuesha or Amoesha, is spoken by the Amuesha people of Peru in central and eastern Pasco Region. The language belongs to the Maipurean language family, which also includes Asháninka, Yine, and other languages. Yaneshaʼ is written in the Latin script, and a dictionary and grammar have been published in the language. The language has 26 consonants and 9 vowel phonemes, with three basic vowel qualities, /a/, /e/, and /o/. The sources describe Christian missionary activity from the 16th, 18th, and 19th centuries.
