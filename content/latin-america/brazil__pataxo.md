## Overview

The Pataxó are an Indigenous people in Bahia, Brazil, with a population of about 11,800 individuals. Their traditional region was largely converted into private farms by settlers who persecuted them. In 1961, they were expelled from the largest remaining forest, integrated into the dominant society, and many settled in cities. Others moved to coastal areas and formed new Indigenous villages, including Coroa Vermelha, founded in 1972 and now home to approximately 6,000 people. The Pataxó formerly spoke Pataxó, an extinct Maxakalían language, but now speak Portuguese and a reconstructed form called Patxohã. Their cultural work includes language revival, community-based identity projects, and ecotourism.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do describe the revival of the Pataxó language in the form called Patxohã.

## Language & religion

Pataxó was an extinct Maxakalían language formerly spoken by the Pataxó people of Bahia and Minas Gerais, including Pôsto Paraguassu in Itabuna municipality. The Pataxó now speak Portuguese, while retaining a few Pataxó words and words from neighbouring peoples. The language has been revived as Patxohã, whose grammar required extensive reconstruction because of poor documentation. Patxohã was declared an official language in Porto Seguro, Bahia, in 2023.
