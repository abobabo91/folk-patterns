## Overview

The Guarayos are an indigenous group living in their ancestral land in eastern Bolivia, north of the department of Santa Cruz. Their current population in Bolivia is 12,000. They primarily speak Guarayu, a language of the Tupían language family. The Guarayos are predominantly agricultural, and much of their culture and lifestyle relies on their land. They reside in the Bolivian lowlands and are described as predominantly hunter-gatherers and small farmers. Their communities grow corn, bananas and rice. Guarayo society has also been shaped by land organization, forest management, religious beliefs, marriage practices, and political struggles over territorial rights.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Before missionaries came into their villages, Guarayos wore feather ornaments and paint without any other clothing. Today, Guarayo women wear dresses made from long cloth, while men wear long bark-cloth tunics.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Guarayos formerly wore feather ornaments and paint before the arrival of missionaries.

## Festivals & rituals

The Guarayos believe that three persons formed the world: Alaangui, Mbirachucha, and Candir. They respect Zaguaguayu as their first ancestor. Their traditional religious beliefs attribute marks on the moon to the misbehavior of Abeulo, their Great Father.

Marriage practices historically included polygyny, which was difficult for the Guarayo people to abandon because they resisted accepting the marriage traditions of the church. Young women require the consent of both their father and brother, and marriage between a man and his sister’s daughter is preferred. Following the birth of a child, couvade is practiced: fathers remain in their hammocks for the first three days after the child’s birth so that the child’s soul follows its father and is not injured.

When a Guarayo dies, the soul travels to the land of Tamoi, the Great Ancestor. During the journey, the soul passes through various temptations and great danger. At the end, Tamoi washes the soul, after which it becomes young and attractive again.

## Foodways

Guarayo communities grow corn, bananas and rice. The sources describe the Guarayos as predominantly hunter-gatherers and small farmers.

## Language & religion

The Guarayos primarily speak Guarayu, which belongs to the Tupían language family. Most likely, Guarayos migrated to Bolivia from Paraguay centuries before, when the Guaraní attacked the frontiers of the Inca Empire. Their ancestors are believed to have been the Guaraní.

Seventy percent of the Guarayo population is Roman Catholic, while the remaining thirty percent practices ethnic religions. Traditional beliefs state that Alaangui, Mbirachucha, and Candir formed the world. Zaguaguayu is respected as the first ancestor, and Abeulo is regarded as the Great Father. Guarayo beliefs also include the journey of the soul to Tamoi, the Great Ancestor, after death.
