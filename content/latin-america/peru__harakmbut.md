## Overview

The Harakmbut, also called Arakmbut or Harakmbet, are Indigenous people of Peru who live in the Madre de Dios Region near the Brazilian border, along the Madre de Dios and Colorado Rivers and in communities in the Peruvian rainforest. An estimated 2,000 Harakmbut people live in the region. Their native language, Harákmbut or Harakmbet, has two vital dialects, Amarakaeri and Watipaeri, which are reported to be mutually intelligible, although the relationship between their speakers is hostile. The language has been accepted as a language isolate since the 1960s, while proposed relationships with the Katukinan family and other language families remain matters of linguistic discussion. The sources document Harakmbut history, communities, dialects, language structure, and demographic change.

## Music & performance

The language sources do record phonological features of Amarakaeri and other dialects, including vowel and consonant contrasts, stress on the penultimate syllable, nasalization, and variation among speakers, but these descriptions concern language rather than music.

## Festivals & rituals

The historical sources do describe major events affecting Harakmbut peoples, including the rubber boom, slave raids beginning with the development of the Isthmus of Fitzcarrald in 1894, the massacre of around two thousand Toyeri natives around 1895 at El Mirador Grande, contact with members of the Dominican Order in 1940, and a smallpox epidemic in 1948 associated with the development of a road from Cusco into the Madre de Dios.

## Foodways

They state that some Amarakaeri people pan for gold as a means of subsistence.

## Oral tradition & literature

Andrew Gray’s *The Arakmbut--mythology, Spirituality, and History* is listed among the sources for Harakmbut history and cultural interpretation, but the supplied material does not summarize particular myths, folktales, epics, proverbs, riddles, genres, or storytelling contexts. The sources do record the meanings of several linguistic forms, including *wa-mba-arak-a-eri*, glossed as “murderers,” the preferred endonym Arakmbut, and dialectal forms referring to people associated with rivers, such as *arãsã-eri*, “people of the Marcatapa River,” and *pukiri-eri*, “people of the Pukiri River.”

## Language & religion

Harákmbut or Harakmbet is the native language of the Harakmbut people of Peru. Its two vital dialects are Amarakaeri, also called Arakmbut, and Watipaeri, also called Huachipaeri or Wachipaeri; six documented dialects are listed: Amarakaeri, Watipaeri, Arasaeri, Pukirieri, Sapiteri, Kisambaeri, and Toyoeri, the last of which is extinct. Amarakaeri is spoken in Puerto Luz, Shintuya, San José Del Karene, Barranco Chico, Boca Inambari, Boca Ishiriwe, Puerto Azul, Masenawa, and Kotsimba, while Watipaeri speakers are concentrated in Queros and Santa Rosa de Huacaria. Harakmbut contains two independent demonstrative pronouns, noun cases, nominalizers, mood distinctions, aspect and tense marking, and a basic Subject-Object-Verb word order. The sources mention spirituality in the title of Gray’s book.
