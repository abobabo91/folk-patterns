## Overview

The Guambiano, also called Misak, are an Indigenous people of Colombia, concentrated in the department of Cauca in the western Colombian Andes range, or Cordillera; some are also found in Huila Department. Their language is known as Guambiano and is also called Misak, Namuy Wam, Nam Trik, or Moguex. Guambiano belongs to the Barbacoan language family and forms, with Totoró and the long-extinct Coconuco language, a distinct subgroup often treated as dialects of one Coconucan language. The Guambiano population is estimated at 20 782 people. Agriculture is central to their economy, while distinctive clothing, kinship organization, hereditary offices, and male-line descent are documented features of their social and material life.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The Misak people are identified with distinctive clothing. Men wear a blue scarf as a sarong, rectangular ponchos, and a black bowler hat. Women wear a black skirt, a solid-color top, a blue scarf, and a dark bowler hat.

## Festivals & rituals

 The sources describe society as having a patriarchal kinship system with hereditary offices, descent lines, and property passing through the male line.

## Foodways

Agriculture is the base of the Guambiano economy. The main products cultivated include wheat, corn, coffee, onions, garlic, cassava, potatoes, beans, and cabbage.

## Oral tradition & literature

 A sample of Guambiano appears as a translation of the Lord’s Prayer, beginning “Ñimpe Tiuspa waminchip pɵntrappe,”.

## Language & religion

Guambiano, also called Misak, Namuy Wam, Nam Trik, or Moguex, is spoken in southwestern Colombia, in Cauca Department, by the Guambianos. It belongs to the Barbacoan language family. Guambiano, Totoró, and the long-extinct Coconuco language form a distinct subgroup of the Barbacoan languages; these languages are often considered dialects of one Coconucan language. The Coconucan languages and Awa Pit constitute the northern branch of the Barbacoan family. Bárbara Muelas is identified as a language activist and academic.
