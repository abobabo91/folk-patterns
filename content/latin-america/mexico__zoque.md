## Overview

The Zoque are an Indigenous people of Mexico who mainly live in northwestern Chiapas and are also found in the Isthmus region of Oaxaca, including the Selva Zoque. In 2020, there were 86,589 Zoque. They speak various Zoque languages, which form a primary branch of the Mixe–Zoquean language family and are diverse and sometimes mutually unintelligible. Zoque territory includes municipalities and towns such as Amatán, Copainalá, Chapultenango, Francisco León, Ixhuatán, Ixtacomitán, Jitotol, Ocotepec, Ostuacán, Pantepec, Rayón, Totolapa, Tapilula, Tecpatán, Acala, Blanca Rosa, and Ocozocoautla. Their documented folk-culture features include white-rimmed black pottery, distinctive women’s clothing, adobe houses, and agricultural practices centered on maize, beans, chiles, and squash.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Zoque traditional dress is worn almost exclusively by women and on special occasions. Women traditionally wear short-sleeved white blouses with colorfully embroidered open necklines and long poplin skirts in various colors. More recently, knee-length dresses in bright colors with white lacy trims have become common. Some elderly men in remote communities wear white cotton shirts. Historically, married women uncovered the upper half of their bodies while working in the heat, although recent generations do not follow this custom.

### Architecture

Zoque houses are mainly rectangular and have one or two rooms. Traditionally, walls were built with adobe, or mud bricks, and were whitewashed inside and out. Houses had earthen floors and roofs with four sloping sides made of tile or thatch. More recently, houses have been built with concrete blocks, cement floors, and corrugated iron roofs. The kitchen is usually a separate structure from the main house.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

White-rimmed black pottery is characteristic of the Zoque people.

## Music & performance

 The Zoque language article mentions Central Zoque-language programming carried by the CDI’s radio station XECOPA, broadcasting from Copainalá, Chiapas, and describes a Sierra Popoluca Collection of Lynda Boudreault containing audio recordings and transcriptions of Zoque and Soteapan in a wide range of genres.

## Foodways

Agriculture is the Zoque’s primary economic activity, and crops vary according to the topography of the terrain. For the most part, they raise maize, beans, chiles, and squash. Commercial crops include coffee, cocoa, peppers, bananas, mamey, sweetsop, and guava. The soil is of poor quality, and output is low. Pigs and domesticated fowl are raised in small quantities to augment the diet.

## Language & religion

Zoque languages form a primary branch of the Mixe–Zoquean language family indigenous to southern Mexico. There are over 100,000 speakers of Zoque languages; 74,000 people reported their language as “Zoque” in a 2020 census, and an additional 36,000 reported their language as Sierra Popoluca. Most of the remaining 8,400 “Popoluca” speakers are presumably also Zoque. The sources list Gulf Zoquean, Sierra Popoluca, Texistepec Popoluca, Ayapa Zoque, Oaxacan Zoque, and Chiapas Zoque, including Copainalá Zoque, Francisco León Zoque, and Rayón Zoque.
