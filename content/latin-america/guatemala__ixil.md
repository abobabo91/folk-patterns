## Overview

The Ixil are a Maya people living in Guatemala and Mexico. In Guatemala, they are concentrated in Santa María Nebaj, San Gaspar Chajul, and San Juan Cotzal, municipalities in the northern part of the Cuchumatanes mountains of the department of Quiché. These municipalities form the Ixil Triangle, described as the place of origin of Ixil culture and the area where most of the population lives. Ixil communities also live in Campeche and Quintana Roo, Mexico. The Ixil language belongs to the Mamean branch of Mayan languages and is closely related to Awakatek. Ixil culture has been shaped by agricultural practice, ritual relationships with the Sun and Mother Earth, displacement during the Guatemalan Civil War, and the survival of Ixil-speaking communities in Guatemala and Mexico.

## Festivals & rituals

In Campeche, the Ixil celebrate the Day of the Nature with a ceremony thanking Mother Earth for food granted to them. The ceremony begins near a body of water, where people gather and share cultivated products including corn, beans, fruits, and vegetables. The sources also describe a daily ritual directed toward the Sun, called *K'ii* in Ixil. In this ritual, the head of the family goes toward the sunset and ceremonially thanks the Sun for its light and for guiding the path to work, while asking that no accident occur along the way and that he not become lost.

## Foodways

The sources name corn, beans, fruits, and vegetables as cultivated products shared during the Day of the Nature ceremony in Campeche. The ceremony’s purpose is described as giving thanks to Mother Earth for the food granted to the people.

## Language & religion

Ixil, also called Ixhil, is a Mayan language spoken in Mexico and Guatemala. It belongs to the Mamean branch of Mayan languages and has two dialects, Ixil Nebajeño and Ixil Chajuleño; the sources also state that Ixil spoken in the three principal Guatemalan towns has slight vocabulary differences while remaining mutually intelligible. Ixil is closely related to Awakatek. The sources state that Ixil emerged as a separate language around the year 500 AD. In Campeche, Ixil religious practice includes praise of the Sun, *K'ii*, in a daily ritual, as well as the Day of the Nature ceremony thanking Mother Earth.
