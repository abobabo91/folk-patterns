## Overview

The Amahuaca, also known as Amhuaca and by several other names, are Indigenous peoples of the southeastern Amazon Basin in Peru and Brazil. Their largest community is in Puerto Varadero, a jungle community on the Peruvian–Brazilian border. They were isolated until the 18th century and established contact with Westerners during that century. In 1998, they numbered about 520; the Amahuaca language sources also report around 220 speakers in Brazil and around 328 in Peru. Amahuaca is a Panoan language. The group has faced ecological devastation, disease, violence, illegal logging, oil extraction, and loss of territory. Their documented cultural practices include hunting, fishing, farming, Brazil-nut processing, and the ritual use of ayahuasca by shamans.

## Music & performance

They state that Amahuaca shamans are known to use ayahuasca in ritual ceremonies, typically to enter the realm of spirits and deceased relatives or ancestors; this use is described as spiritual rather than recreational.

## Festivals & rituals

They document the ritual use of ayahuasca by shamans, commonly for entering the realm of spirits and deceased relatives or ancestors. The sources describe this use as spiritual and not recreational.

## Foodways

Amahuaca people hunt, fish, and farm, and they harvest and process Brazil nuts.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that portions of the Bible were translated into Amahuaca from 1963 to 1997.

## Language & religion

Amahuaca is an Indigenous language of the Amazon Basin in Peru and Brazil and belongs to the Panoan language family. It is also known as Amawaka, Amaguaco, Ameuhaque, Ipitineri, and Sayaco. The language is written in a Latin-based script; a grammar, dictionary, grammatical description, and Bible portions have been developed. As of 2000, approximately 220 Amahuaca spoke the language according to one source, while the language source reports around 220 speakers in Brazil and around 328 in Peru. The language is endangered and poised to become extinct in the near future. The sources document shamanic ayahuasca use in ritual ceremonies.
