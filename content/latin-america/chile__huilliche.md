## Overview

The Huilliche, also called Huiliche or Huilliche-Mapuche, are the southern part of the Mapuche macroethnic group in Chile and Argentina. They inhabit Futahuillimapu, meaning “great land of the south,” and, as the Cunco or Veliche subgroup, the northern half of Chiloé Island. Their principal modern settlement areas are San Juan de la Costa west of Osorno and Compu-Chadmo in the southeast of Chiloé Island. Huilliche is a moribund branch of the Araucanian language family, historically spoken in Los Lagos and Los Ríos and in mountain valleys between Valdivia and the Chiloé Archipelago. Huilliche culture is documented here through its regional history, language, ritual tree, settlement patterns, and relations with Mapuche communities.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources mention Huilliche ponchos. José de Moraleda wrote in the late 18th century that the ponchos of the Huilliches of Osorno were less pleasing, or “vistosos,” than those of Chiloé.

No other documented Huilliche textile or pattern traditions are covered by the sources.

### Clothing & dress

The sources mention ponchos as Huilliche clothing, particularly in an account by the Basque navigator José de Moraleda concerning the Huilliches of Osorno in the late 18th century. That account compares their ponchos with those of Chiloé and describes them as less pleasing, or “vistosos.”

## Festivals & rituals

The sources identify *Laurelia sempervirens*, called *triwe* in Huilliche and laurel in Spanish, as the ritual tree of the Huilliche of Futahuillimapu. They also state that Huilliche mythological cosmologies have many parallels with Indigenous religions of the Central Andes, including the Inca religion.

The sources describe several historical political and military events rather than an annual ritual calendar. A peace parliament and treaty was signed in 1793 after the Huilliches were ravaged by a Spanish army led by Tomás de Figueroa in 1792; the treaty recognized Huilliche property.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state only that Huilliche mythological cosmologies have many parallels in Indigenous religions of the Central Andes, including the Inca religion.

The sources mention a 2015 study of Huilliche mutual intelligibility and preservation concerns.

## Language & religion

Huilliche is a moribund branch of the Araucanian language family. It can also be spelled Williche, Huiliche, or Veliche. In 1982 it was spoken by about 2,000 ethnic Huilliche people in Chile, while in 2012 it was spoken by only a few elderly speakers. Most ethnic Huilliche speak Spanish as their first language, and both Huilliche varieties are highly endangered.

The language has at least two varieties, Huillichesungun and Tsesungún. Huillichesungun is spoken in Wequetrumao on Chiloé Island, while Tsesungun is spoken in Choroy Traiguen on the coast of Osorno province. Huilliche is closely related to Mapudungun, the language of the Mapuche. They identify the ritual tree *triwe* and note parallels between Huilliche cosmologies and Indigenous religions of the Central Andes, including the Inca religion.
