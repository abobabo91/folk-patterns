## Overview

The Machinere are an Indigenous people of Brazil, Bolivia, and Peru. They live along the Acre River in Bolivia. In Brazil, they mostly live in the Mamoadate Indigenous Territory, although some live in the Chico Mendes Extractivist Reserve, both in Acre. They are also called Machineri, Manchinere, Manchineri, Manitenére, Manitenerí, and Maxinéri. Their language is an Arawakan language, specifically a Piro language within the Southern Maipuran language family. It is spoken by over 1,000 Machinere people and is highly similar to the Yine language, although mutual intelligibility between the two groups is low. Machinere subsistence includes hunting, fishing, and swidden farming.

## Foodways

Machinere people hunt, fish, and farm using the swidden method. They grow maize, manioc, rice, papaya, peanut, pumpkin, sugarcane, and sweet potato.

## Language & religion

Machinere is an Arawakan language, a Piro language, and part of the Southern Maipuran language family. It is highly similar to the Yine language, but mutual intelligibility is low between the two groups. Machinere is one of the official languages of Bolivia. It is written in the Latin script, and the Bible was translated into Machinere in 1960.
