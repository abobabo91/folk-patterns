## Overview

The Matipu are an Indigenous people of Brazil who live in the southern part of the Xingu Indigenous Park, in Mato Grosso. Their population was estimated at 149 individuals in 2011, compared with 40 in the 1995 census. They are also known as the *Mariape-Nahuqua* and *Matipuhy*. The Matipu traditionally spoke Matipu, a Carib language, although only 10 living speakers are known; they currently speak Kuikúro-Kalapálo. Their cultural life includes hunting, fishing, farming, and animist religious practice, while sharing many cultural traits with other Xingu peoples.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that the Matipu are mainly of animist faith.

## Foodways

The Matipu hunt, fish, and farm for subsistence. Manioc and maize are their primary crops.

## Oral tradition & literature

In collaboration with linguist Bruna Franchetto, the Kuikuro have created a library of recordings featuring Kuikuro stories in the language. The collection is archived at the Archive of the Indigenous Languages of Latin America.

## Language & religion

Matipu is described as a Carib language, while Matipuhy is closely related and is sometimes considered a distinct language. Matipuhy is moribund, with only 10 speakers remaining. The Matipu currently speak the Kuikúro-Kalapálo language. Kuikúro, also known as Amonap, Upper Xingu Cariban, and Apalakiri, is a Cariban language spoken by the Kuikuro and Kalapalo peoples of Brazil. It is spoken in seven villages along the Culuene River in the Xingu Indigenous Park of Mato Grosso. As of 2006, Kuikúro was estimated to have 1,100 native speakers, including 600 Kuikúro and 500 Kalapálo speakers. Brazilian Portuguese bilingualism is prevalent among the men of the community. Kuikúro is an agglutinative, head-final language with dependent marking and an ergative case system. The Matipu are mainly animist.
