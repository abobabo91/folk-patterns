## Overview

The Qʼanjobʼal, also known as Kanjobal, are a Maya people living in Huehuetenango Department, Guatemala. Their Indigenous language is also called Qʼanjobʼal and belongs to the Qʼanjobʼalan branch of the Mayan language family. The language is spoken primarily in Guatemala and in part of Mexico. In Guatemala, its documented municipalities include San Juan Ixcoy, San Pedro Soloma, Santa Eulalia, Santa Cruz Barillas, San Rafael La Independencia, and San Miguel Acatán; in Mexico, it is spoken in municipalities including Catazajá, Amatenango de la Frontera, La Trinitaria, Bella Vista, Frontera Comalapa, Las Margaritas, and Maravilla Tenejapa. According to 1998 estimates compiled by SIL International in Ethnologue, there were approximately 77,700 native speakers. The sources emphasize the distinctiveness and resilience of Qʼanjobʼal culture and identify San Pedro Soloma as a prominent Qʼanjobʼal municipality.

## Music & performance

The Qʼanjobʼal are associated in the source material with the marimba. The Qʼanjobʼal marimba is described as differing from those of other groups because it has an intense feeling. No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription concerns the Qʼanjobʼal in the supplied material.

## Language & religion

Qʼanjobʼal, also called Kanjobal, is a Mayan language in the Qʼanjobʼalan branch. That branch includes Qʼanjobʼal, Chuj, Akatek, and Jakaltek, which are spoken in Mexico and Guatemala. The Mayan language family includes 31 languages, two of which are now extinct. Qʼanjobʼal has 26 consonant sounds and 5 vowel sounds. It is described as an ergative–absolutive language with a fixed verb–subject–object word order, although pragmatic and syntactic factors can produce other orders. Its grammar uses aspect distinctions including incompletive, completive, and future or irrealis, and the language has classifiers such as *noʼ* for animals, *te* for trees or wood, *ix* for female, *naq* for male, *chʼen* for stone or metal, *xim* for corn, and *an* for plants.
