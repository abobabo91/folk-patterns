## Overview

The Ese Ejja are an Indigenous people of Bolivia and Peru in the southwestern Amazon basin. In Peru, they live along the Tambopata and Heath Rivers, near Puerto Maldonado, and in the Madre de Dios and Puno departments. The Ese Ejja language is spoken along the Madre de Dios and Tambopata rivers and at their sources in Sonene, Palma Real, and Infierno. Approximately 1,500 Ese Ejja are distributed among communities in Peru and Bolivia; the Peruvian ethnic group and its language are described as seriously threatened, with 840 speakers. Their folk-culture profile in the supplied sources is defined mainly through subsistence practices, language, community distribution, and linguistic documentation.

## Foodways

The supplied sources state that Ese Ejja people are traditionally hunter-gatherers, farmers, rangers, and fishermen.

 They state that a dictionary has been produced for the Ese Ejja language and identify documentation projects connected with the language.

## Language & religion

Ese Ejja is a Tacanan language of Bolivia and Peru, spoken by Ese Ejja people of all ages and written in the Latin script. Names used for the language include *Ese'eha*, *Chama*, and *Warayo*; the sources state that *Chama* is a pejorative regional name and that *Guarayo* is also the name of a Tupí-Guaraní language group. The language has the dialects Guacanawa, also called Guarayo or Huarayo, Baguaja, and Echoja. Chama, Chuncho, Huanayo, Kinaki, and Mohino are described as possibly extinct dialects, while Chunene is described as “similar” to Ese Ejja, although its status as a dialect or separate language is unclear.

Ese Ejja has 17 consonant phonemes and four vowels. Its practical orthography uses the Latin script, and its syllables have the structure (C)V.
