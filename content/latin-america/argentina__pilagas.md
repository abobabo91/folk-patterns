## Overview

The Pilagá (*pit'laxá*) are an Indigenous people of the Guaycuru group who inhabit the center of the province of Formosa, in Argentina. Some migrant groups also live in the provinces of Chaco and Santa Fe. Their language belongs to the Mataco-Guaicurú linguistic family, and they are closely related to the Toba people. About 2000 people speak Pilagá alongside Spanish. The 2004-2005 Complementary Survey of Indigenous Peoples recorded 4465 people in Argentina recognized and/or descended in first generation from the Pilagá people, while the 2010 National Population Census recorded 5,137 people who recognized themselves as Pilagá throughout the country. The Pilagá have preserved much of their native culture.

## Festivals & rituals

The Pilagá historical record includes events connected with military occupation, missions, and massacres. The mission of San Francisco Solano de Tacaaglé was founded on March 21, 1901, with 150 Toba and Pilagá, and the massacre of Fortín Yunká took place on March 19, 1919. The Pilagá Federation filed a civil lawsuit on April 1, 2005, concerning the Rincón Bomba massacre, which occurred in October 1947 and was declared a crime against humanity.

## Foodways

In ancient times, the Pilagá were hunters and gatherers. Among the fruits they gathered were those of the carob tree, chañar, mistol, prickly pear, and molle.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that the Guarani of Paraguay called the peoples living in the Eastern area of the provinces of Chaco and Formosa “frentones,” referring to their custom of shaving the front of their heads.

## Language & religion

Pilagá belongs to the Mataco-Guaicurú linguistic family, and the Pilagá are closely related to the Toba people linguistically and culturally. About 2000 people speak their own language along with Spanish. Since 1996, Pilagá has been written in a Latin alphabet of 4 vowels and 19 consonants.
