## Overview

The Cinta Larga, also called Cinturão Largo, are an Indigenous people of the western Amazon rainforest of Brazil, numbering almost 2,000. Their name means “broad belt” in Portuguese and refers to the large bark sashes they once wore. The Cinta Larga language is a Mondé language within the Tupi language family and is written in the Latin script. The group became known for shadowing Theodore Roosevelt’s Roosevelt–Rondon Scientific Expedition without making contact. Their modern history includes violent conflict with prospectors seeking rubber, timber, gold, and diamonds, including the Massacre at 11th Parallel in the 1960s and a later controversy over diamond mining.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Large bark sashes** — The sources identify large bark sashes as an item once worn by the Cinta Larga and as the basis for the meaning of their name, “broad belt.” They provide no further information about their production, decoration, or relationship to neighboring textile traditions.

### Clothing & dress

The sources mention only the large bark sashes formerly worn by the Cinta Larga.

## Dance & theatre

The sources state only that the Cinta Larga shadowed the Roosevelt–Rondon Scientific Expedition without making contact; this is presented as a historical event rather than a dance or theatre tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe the Massacre at 11th Parallel, in which rubber prospectors attacked a Cinta Larga village by dropping dynamite from a plane and then killing survivors, including women and children; the sources do not present this event as a ritual.

## Foodways

The account of the Massacre at 11th Parallel says that sugar was dropped during an initial pass over the village to lure a crowd toward the central plaza, but it provides no broader information about Cinta Larga foodways.

## Language & religion

Cinta Larga is described as a Tupian dialect cluster of Brazil and as the largest language of the Monde branch. The Cinta Larga language belongs to the Mondé language group within the Tupi language family and is written in the Latin script. Arara do Rio Guariba, or Guariba River Arara, is spoken in the northern part of Aripuanã Indigenous Park and is closely related to the Cinta Larga dialect cluster; it also shares some features with Suruí. Hargreaves collected 26 words in 2001, and the language remains unclassified because of the lack of data.
