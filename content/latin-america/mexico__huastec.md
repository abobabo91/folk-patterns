## Overview

The Huastec are an indigenous people of northeastern Mexico living in a region of tropical forests stretching from the Sierra Madre Oriental to the Gulf of Mexico. Their language, Téenek, is a Mayan language remarkable for having tone and was the first branch to split from Mayan proper, an estimated 2200 to 1200 BCE. Other Mesoamerican peoples admired them for their musical abilities. Their material culture shows distinctive skills in weaving agave fiber and plaiting palm, creating decorated textiles, clothing, and practical objects.

## Material culture

The Huastec crafted practical and decorated objects in agave fiber and palm, using dyed stripes and stamped dyes rather than embroidery or brocade.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Huastec wove agave fiber in a range of bright dyes, decorating cloth with dyed stripes and stamped patterns.

- **Zapupe weaving** (*zapupe*) — Agave fiber woven in undyed form and dyed in fuchsia, green, yellow, turquoise and purple, with occasional colored weft fibers crossing the cloth.
- **Shoulder bag** (*morral*) — A single piece of agave-fiber cloth folded and sewn, decorated with stamped four-petalled flowers in turquoise and fuchsia dyes, or woven with colored stripes.
- **Carrying-strap and tumpline** (*tumpline*) — Agave-fiber weaving for carrying loads, with the tumpline having a leather strip sewn to its center to rest on the head.
- **Palm plaiting** (*petate*) — Palm fiber worked by plaiting and coiling to make baskets with stiff sides and looped handles, fans with diagonal stitching, and mats.

### Clothing & dress

The Huastec made tall palm-fiber hats in two constructions, plaited or coiled-and-sewn, finished with hatbands and sometimes decorated with cut-leather work.

- **Plaited palm hat** — A tall flat crown with horizontal stripes and wide brim, finished with a black velvet hatband and stamped interior leather band.
- **Coiled-and-sewn palm hat** — A tall crown with a concave top crossed by a raised bar and a wide brim, edged with a hanging tassel, sometimes decorated with cut-leather squares.
- **Machete sheath** — Folded and stitched leather carried on a leather strap with a metal buckle and decorated with rivets.

### Architecture

The Huastec built temples on step-pyramids in the pre-Columbian era.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Huastec pottery is lightly fired and burnished, often marked with maize gruel, while metalwork includes machetes with leather-wrapped handles.

- **Circular tapering pot** — The most common pottery form, tapering at the neck and flaring for the lip, often splashed or streaked with brown maize gruel.
- **Bottle-gourd pot** (*calabazo*) — A pot shaped like a gourd with a small circle above a large flat-bottomed base and thin neck, painted with white flowers.
- **Rimmed bowl** — A flattened-bottomed semicircle painted orange outside with darker orange rim triangles and a four-petalled flower on the inside bottom.
- **Machete** — A blade straight on one side and curved on the sharp side, with a metal handle wrapped in leather.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Pre-Columbian Huastec made engraved shell gorgets and fan headdresses, and tooth mutilation was associated with ritual practices.

- **Engraved shell gorget** — Worked from shell and part of pre-Columbian Huastec production.
- **Fan headdress** — A pre-Columbian ornament made from shell.

## Music & performance

Other Mesoamerican peoples admired the Huastecs for their musical abilities, though specific instruments and genres are not named in the sources.

- **Huastec-language radio broadcasting** (*XEANT-AM*) — The CDI's radio station based in Tancanhuitz de Santos, San Luis Potosí broadcasts in the Huastec language.

## Dance & theatre

No information on Huastec dance or dramatic traditions is available in the sources.

## Festivals & rituals

Pre-Columbian ritual practices are known only through carved iconography and are not documented for present-day practice.

- **Yoke-palm-axe complex** (*yoke-palm-axe*) — A pre-Columbian ritual complex found from coastal Campeche to the Huasteca, associated with the pelota ballgame, decapitation and tooth mutilation.
- **Pelota ballgame** (*pelota*) — A Mesoamerican ballgame associated with pre-Columbian Huastec ritual practices.

## Foodways

The Huasteca was known to the Aztecs for its fertile abundance, though specific Huastec dishes are not documented.

- **Maize gruel** — Brown streaks and splashes on burnished pots suggest maize gruel was a common food preparation.
- **Squash** (*ciw*) — Proto-Huastecs borrowed the word for squash from proto-Mixe–Zoque while passing through the southern Gulf lowlands.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Huastec have a documented tradition of narratives and rituals preserved in recordings and archives, with a written language tradition dating back at least to the Spanish colonial period.

- **Huasteco Collection** — An archive of recordings of narratives, words and rituals held by Barbara Edmonson in the Archive of the Indigenous Languages of Latin America.
- **Andrés de Olmos grammar** — The first grammatical and lexical description of Huastec accessible to Europeans, created by a friar who also wrote grammars of Nahuatl and Totonac.

## Language & religion

Téenek is a Mayan language with tone, the first branch to split from Mayan proper, spoken in three dialects across the Huasteca.

- **Western dialect (Potosino)** — About 48,000 speakers across nine San Luis Potosí towns.
- **Central dialect (Veracruz)** — About 22,000 speakers in Tempoal and Tantoyuca.
- **Eastern dialect (Otontepec)** — About 1,700 to 12,000 speakers across northern Veracruz towns depending on source.
- **Chicomuceltec** — The only sister language to Huastec, once spoken in Chiapas near Comitán and now extinct.

## Glossary

- *Téenek* — The Huastec name for themselves; means 'people from here'
- *zapupe* — Agave fiber used for weaving cloth
- *morral* — A shoulder bag or carrying pouch
- *tumpline* — A carrying strap that rests on the head for load-bearing
- *petate* — A mat woven from palm fiber
- *calabazo* — A bottle-gourd shaped pot
- *yoke-palm-axe* — A pre-Columbian ritual complex associated with the ballgame and decapitation
- *pelota* — A Mesoamerican ballgame
- *ciw* — Squash; a word borrowed from proto-Mixe–Zoque
- *XEANT-AM* — A CDI radio station broadcasting in Huastec language
- *Huasteca* — The geographic region in northeastern Mexico where the Huastec live
- *Pánuco River* — A river along which the Huastec are concentrated
