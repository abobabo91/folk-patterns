## Overview

The Aché are an Indigenous people of Paraguay who traditionally lived as nomadic hunter-gatherers in small bands, depending on wild forest resources for subsistence. In the 20th century, four ethnolinguistic populations were contacted and pacified: the Northern Aché, the Yvytyruzu Aché, the Ypety Aché, and the Ñacunday Aché. Legally recognized Aché communities include Cerro Moroti, Ypetimi, Puerto Barra, Chupa Pou, Kuetuvy, and Arroyo Bandera. Chupa Pou is the largest community and the principal center of the Northern Aché. Aché language analysis identifies a Tupí-Guaraní lexicon combined with a distinctive grammatical structure. Their history includes persecution, forced removal, reservation settlement, and efforts to regain ancestral lands and continue customary subsistence activities.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources describe houses and school buildings among the internal uses proposed for forestry products in the Kuetuvy Indigenous Reserve.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Aché used stone flaked tools and Celt-type stone axes; comparable axes from the Paraná River region have been dated to about 9,000 Before Present. The sources also mention bows and arrows, shotguns, GPS receivers, detailed maps, and aerial overflights in connection with hunting, resistance, conservation work, and territorial management.

## Music & performance

The historical descriptions mention that some Aché fastened little stones to their lips, which made them look ferocious, and state that they worshipped only thunder; these accounts do not provide a documented musical tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

They do record spiritual beliefs in which “Berendy,” associated with booming meteors, occupied a central position, and they report an early description stating that Aché people worshipped only thunder.

## Foodways

Aché subsistence was based on wild forest resources. The sources name palm pith, fruits, venison, meat, roots, and edible insects, and describe an economy based on palm pith and meat. In the proposed management plan for Finca 470, customary activities included sustainable hunting and the collection of edible fruits and insects, as well as medicinal plants.

## Oral tradition & literature

Early written information about the Aché includes Jesuit accounts summarized by Lozano and descriptions by Techo, while Federico Maynthusen published information about Aché language and culture after contacting a group in 1908.

## Language & religion

Aché, also known as Guayaki, is a Guarani language of Paraguay with three living dialects: Ache gatu, Ache wa, and Ñacunday River Ache. The Ñacunday River dialect has low mutual intelligibility with the other two. Current analysis identifies a Tupí-Guaraní lexicon overlaid on a unique grammatical structure not found in sister Guaraní languages. The sources describe the Aché as culturally and phenotypically distinct from neighboring Guarani. Historical accounts associate Aché spiritual beliefs with “Berendy,” linked to booming meteors, and report worship of thunder.
