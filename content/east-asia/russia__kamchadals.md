## Overview

The Kamchadals (Russian: *камчадалы*) are an ethnic group inhabiting Kamchatka, Russia. The name originally referred to descendants of local Siberians and aboriginal peoples—the Itelmens, Ainu, Koryaks, and Chuvans—who assimilated with Russians. Today, Kamchadals are descendants of Russian settlers who mixed with indigenous peoples during the 18th and 19th centuries. They speak Russian with a touch of local dialects of the aboriginal languages of Kamchatka. Their economic activities include fur trading, fishing, market gardening, and dairy farming, and the majority are of the Russian Orthodox faith.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe historical events involving Kamchadals. In 1767 and 1768, a Russian ship brought smallpox to the region, and the disease is believed to have killed three-quarters of the native population. The sources also describe the movement and settlement of North Kuril Ainu in Kamchatka: 83 arrived in Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky on 18 September 1877, later settled in Yavin, and founded Golygino; under Soviet rule, both villages were forced to disband and their residents were moved to the Russian-dominated Zaporozhye rural settlement in Ust-Bolsheretsky Raion.

## Language & religion

Kamchadals speak Russian with a touch of local dialects of the aboriginal languages of Kamchatka. The name “Kamchadal” can also refer to people who speak, or whose ancestors spoke, one of the Kamchadal languages; the article distinguishes this usage from its account of Kamchadals as descendants of Russian settlers and indigenous peoples.
