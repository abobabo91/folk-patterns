## Overview

The Atayal are an Indigenous people of Taiwan numbering around 90,000. They are known above all for women's ramie weaving, facial tattoos, and gaga—the unwritten customary order that governs hunting, marriage and ritual. Their earliest settlements were near the Zhuoshui River; in the late 17th century groups crossed the Central Mountain Ranges eastward to the Liwu River valley, where seventy-nine Atayal villages now stand. Religious life centers on ancestral spirits and the rainbow bridge.

## Material culture

Atayal material culture is built on woven ramie cloth decorated with geometric figures, bamboo and rattan containers, and weapons reserved for men.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Weaving ramie thread is a skill expected of every Atayal woman, and the finest garments are decorated with thousands of tiny shell beads.

- **Ramie weaving** (*Kgi (ramie plant), Nuka (thread)*) — Thread is made from ramie plant by shredding, pestling and working into smooth threads, then cleaned by broiling and dyed red ochre with tapioca roots or Shoulang yams.
- **Lukkus-kaxa** — The most valuable Atayal garment, reserved for leaders and warriors, decorated with thousands to millions of tiny beads cut from giant clam shell and serving as currency, heirloom, and gift.
- **Rattan and bamboo plaiting** (*Kiri', qbun, tokan, bluku', gitu'*) — Large-capacity vessels plaited by women from fresh bamboo, used to harvest crops and carry goods, with sizes varying by function.
- **Motif vocabulary** — Diamonds represent the eye of ancestors for protection; horizontal lines represent the rainbow bridge to the afterlife; red signifies blood and power.

### Clothing & dress

Atayal dress is woven in bright colors with geometric figures and decorated with shell beads, bells, buttons and other ornaments.

- **Women's skirts and leggings** — Worn in ramie and wool with red leggings patterned with diamonds and stripes.
- **Belts** — Woven of cotton in stripes or blue cotton hung with white buttons, woollen tassels and bells.
- **Rattan hat** (*Qbubu'*) — The man's head covering, most often rimless and bowl-shaped, sometimes with a brim; hunters lined theirs with animal skins or teeth.
- **Shell plate ornament** (*Mayon*) — A white circular shell plate sewn at the center of the forehead of a successful headhunter's rattan hat.

### Architecture

Traditional Atayal houses are rectangular and half-underground, built with layered bamboo, bark, stone, wood and thatch for insulation.

- **Half-underground house** — Found especially in Nantou, Yilan and Taichung counties, with half the structure below ground for protection and insulation.
- **Nan'ao-style house** — Built of bamboo, rock, rattan and wood with length of three adult arm spans and width of two, roofed with bamboo and cypress bark paved with stone.
- **Pavilion** (*Pslyan*) — A community building larger than a house with a central fire pit, used for discussion, governance and dispute resolution.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Atayal have no ceramic or metalworking tradition; instead they work in wood and bamboo, with daggers and swords reserved strictly for men.

- **Long knife** (*Lalaw Behuw*) — An Atayal weapon and hunting tool unique to its owner, buried with him and never inherited.
- **Three-stone stove** (*Hka'*) — The hearth center, with three stones buried slightly at one end and standing 20 to 30 cm high, leaving channels for fire's draught.
- **Storage shelves** (*Gaga*) — Shelves built above the stove where smoke keeps hunting tools, rattan hats and weapons dry.
- **Smoking-pipe bowl** — Carved from bamboo root and rattan.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The great Atayal body art is the facial tattoo, applied with needles and ash, which served as a rite of passage and mark of eligibility for marriage.

- **Facial tattoo** (*Ptasan*) — Applied with a cluster of needles lashed to a stick and tapped in with a hammer, then rubbed with black ash; men received bands on forehead and chin, women from ears across cheeks to lips in a V.
- **Ear ornaments** — Carved from bamboo, sometimes combined with grass skin and wool, and worn in pairs.
- **Tattoo tools** (*Atok (needle cluster), totsin (hammer)*) — Used to apply facial tattoos, with healing taking about a month.

## Music & performance

The Atayal play the bamboo Jew's harp, an instrument used for self-entertainment, communication and courtship.

- **Bamboo Jew's harp** (*Lubuw*) — About 10 cm long with a slot holding a thin tongue-shaped reed of bamboo or copper, played by holding between the lips and plucking hemp ropes, prohibited during hunting and funerals.

## Dance & theatre

The Atayal have culturally specific dances and musical instruments, but sources provide no detailed description of named dances or dramatic traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

Facial tattooing and teeth filing form the coming-of-age initiation, requiring proof of adult accomplishment before eligibility for marriage.

- **Facial tattooing initiation** (*Ptasan*) — Before tattooing, a candidate must prove adult accomplishment—for men, taking an enemy head; for women, mastering weaving.
- **Rainbow bridge** (*Hongu utux*) — Only the tattooed could cross this spirit bridge to the hereafter; absence of a tattoo barred marriage and pushed a person to community margins.
- **Head-taking ritual** — Taken heads were highly honored, given food and drink, and expected to bring good harvests.
- **Hunting rites** — Hunters are taught not to overhunt, not to deforest, not to take young animals; women are forbidden to hunt or touch hunting tools.
- **Ancestral communication** — Ancestors buried beneath house beds receive messages through smoke from the hearth, so vows and promises are kept.

## Foodways

The Atayal were traditionally self-sufficient through fishing, hunting, gathering and slash-and-burn farming, cooking on a three-stone hearth.

- **Game animals** — Common game included wild boar, Reeves's muntjac and goats.
- **Three-stone stove** (*Hka'*) — The cooking hearth at the center of living space, serving for meals, warmth, smoke for ancestral messages and preservation of the house.

## Oral tradition & literature

Atayal creation myth tells of ancestors emerging from a cracked stone, and gaga—an unwritten customary code—carries transmitted knowledge across generations.

- **Creation stone** (*Pinspkan*) — In Atayal origin myth, a stone that cracked open to release three ancestors; facial tattooing is traced to the woman who blackened her face.
- **Customary law** (*Gaga*) — An unwritten code governing marriage, community conduct, ritual and hunting, interpreted and enforced by leaders and elders who mediate disputes.
- **Naming custom** — A child takes the father's first name as a second element, inscribing seniority in names.

## Language & religion

Atayal is an Austronesian language with two dialect clusters and seven recognized dialects; religious life centers on ancestral spirits and the rainbow bridge.

- **Squliq dialect** (*Squliq*) — The prestige and most widespread form of Atayal.
- **Yilan Creole Japanese** — The island's only Japanese creole, spoken in four Atayal communities and arising in the 1930s–40s from contact with colonists.
- **Ancestral spirits** (*Utux, rutux*) — Central to Atayal religious life, believed to receive messages through hearth smoke and to guide the living.

## Glossary

- *Tayal* — Atayal language term meaning 'human' or 'man'
- *Gaga* — Unwritten customary law governing hunting, marriage, ritual and community conduct
- *Ptasan* — Facial tattoo applied as coming-of-age rite
- *Lubuw* — Bamboo Jew's harp played for entertainment and communication
- *Kgi* — Ramie plant used for weaving thread
- *Nuka* — Ramie thread after processing
- *Lukkus-kaxa* — Most valuable garment decorated with shell beads, reserved for leaders and warriors
- *Kiri'* — Plaited rattan or bamboo container for crops and goods
- *Qbubu'* — Rattan hat worn by men
- *Mayon* — White circular shell plate ornament worn on headhunter's hat
- *Hka'* — Three-stone hearth stove for cooking and warmth
- *Pslyan* — Community pavilion with central fire pit for meetings and dispute resolution
- *Lalaw Behuw* — Long knife weapon unique to individual hunter
- *Atok* — Cluster of needles lashed to stick for applying tattoos
- *Totsin* — Hammer used to tap tattoo needles
- *Hongu utux* — Spirit bridge or rainbow crossed by tattooed ancestors to the hereafter
- *Utux* — Ancestral spirits
- *Rutux* — Ancestral spirits (variant)
- *Pinspkan* — The creation stone from which first ancestors emerged
- *Squliq* — Prestige and most widespread Atayal dialect
