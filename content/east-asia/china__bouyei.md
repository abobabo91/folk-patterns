## Overview

The Bouyei are the tenth largest of China's officially recognised ethnic groups, living mainly in southern Guizhou. They speak a Northern Tai language and are known regionally for their handicrafts and batik, particularly in women's clothing. Their culture blends ancestral worship with divination practices and Christianity, and their literature includes ancient Nuo books and the epic poem Wang Yulian. They farm rice, silk, and other crops for consumption and sale, and have long acted as intermediate merchants in their region.

## Material culture

Bouyei material culture centers on textiles, particularly batik, embroidery and appliqué on cotton and silk, with documented objects being almost entirely women's and girls' dress.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Batik, embroidery and appliqué on cotton and silk are the main techniques, with embroidered ribbons and patterned cloth also documented.

- **Batik** — Wax-resist patterning on cotton, renowned throughout the region.
- **Embroidery and appliqué** — Embroidery and appliqué on cotton and silk, the dominant technique in catalogued garments.
- **Embroidered ribbons** — Narrow cotton-and-silk bands sewn onto clothing, produced as stock and applied to garments.
- **Striped and patterned cloth** — Woven cotton with stripes, patterns and embroidery, used for head covers.

### Clothing & dress

Women's dress combines a cotton jacket decorated with batik, embroidery and appliqué, trousers, embroidered aprons with ties, belt-pairs, striped head covers and straw sandals.

- **Woman's jacket** — Cotton jacket decorated with batik, embroidery and appliqué.
- **Aprons** — Embroidered cotton and silk aprons worn with matching appliquéd apron-ties.
- **Tie-belts** — Cotton-and-silk belts worn in pairs.
- **Head cover** — Length of striped, patterned and embroidered cotton worn instead of a shaped hat.
- **Straw sandals** — Footwear made of straw.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Bouyei are primarily documented as farmers and merchants; the only catalogued everyday objects are straw sandals and an embroidered baby-carrier.

- **Baby-carrier** — Embroidered cotton and probable silk garment extending the decorative repertoire to infant care.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Silver is the documented material for Bouyei personal ornament.

- **Silver bracelet** — Woman's silver bracelet catalogued by form and metal.
- **Silver finger-ring** — Woman's silver finger-ring catalogued by form and metal.

## Dance & theatre

Nuo books and scripts in the ancient Bouyei script have circulated for more than a thousand years, praising goodness and promoting moral values.

- **Nuo books and Nuo scripts** (*Nuo*) — Ancient texts in the ancient Bouyei script that praise goodness, condemn evil, advocate filiality and promote truth, kindness and beauty, serving as a local code of conduct.

## Festivals & rituals

Two annual observances are documented: Ox King's Day honours agricultural work, and June 6 is an important holiday for ancestral worship connected to Bouyei origin mythology.

- **Ox King's Day** (*牛王节*) — Annual observance on April 8 that honours oxen and their contribution to agricultural work.
- **June 6** (*sixth day of the sixth lunar month*) — Important traditional holiday for ancestral worship, commemorating Pangu's death and the reconciliation of Xinheng and his stepmother.
- *Shamans' books* — Ancient-script manuscripts used to select auspicious days, lucky numbers and directions and for divination.

## Foodways

The Bouyei cultivate rice, millet, wheat, potatoes, maize, cocoa and tea for consumption or sale, with rice prominent in origin mythology.

- **Rice** — Primary crop, central to origin mythology and the June 6 observance.

## Oral tradition & literature

Traditional literature includes the epic poem Wang Yulian, Nuo books, shamans' divination manuscripts, and the Pangu origin myth accounting for Bouyei origins.

- **Wang Yulian** — Epic poem understood as a retelling of a Chinese story in the Bouyei language, with hand copies remaining popular in Wangmo County.
- **Pangu and Dragon King's daughter** — Mythology accounting for Bouyei origins and the meaning of the June 6 observance.

## Language & religion

Bouyei is a Northern Tai language with over 2.5 million native speakers, and religious life combines ancestral worship and divination with Christianity.

- **Ancient Bouyei script** — Script built from borrowed or imitated Chinese characters, used for shamans' and Nuo manuscripts.
- **Latin alphabet script** — Latin-based script approved in 1956 and used from 1957 to 1960, based on the Longli County dialect.
- **Current script** — Script redesigned between 1981 and 1985 on Wangmo County speech and official since March 1985.
- **Diocese of Nanlong** — Established in 1879 at Nanlong (now Anlong County), with missionaries devising a Latin-based Bouyei script for religious education around 1797–1800.

## Glossary

- *Batik* — Wax-resist patterning on cotton
- *Nuo* — Sacred books and scripts in ancient Bouyei script
- *牛王节* — Ox King's Day, April 8 observance
- *Pangu* — Creator figure in Bouyei mythology who mastered rice farming
- *Xinheng* — Son of Pangu in origin mythology
- *Wang Yulian* — Epic poem, a retelling of a Chinese story in Bouyei
- *Northern Tai* — Language family of Bouyei, part of Tai–Kadai
- *Qianxinan* — Prefecture in Guizhou where Bouyei are concentrated
- *Qiannan* — Prefecture in Guizhou where Bouyei are concentrated
- *Wangmo County* — County in Qianxinan where current Bouyei script is based
- *Appliqué* — Technique of applying decorative fabric pieces to garments
- *Diocese of Nanlong* — Catholic administrative region established 1879 for Bouyei religious education
