## Overview

The Lhotshampa, also called Lhotsampa, are a heterogeneous Bhutanese people of Nepali descent. They live principally in southern Bhutan, where people of Nepali origin began settling in uninhabited areas in the 19th century. The group was estimated to comprise around 35% of Bhutan’s population in 2008, although more than 100,000 Lhotshampa had been forcibly displaced by the 1990s. Since 2007, many Bhutanese refugees have been resettled in the United States, Canada, Australia, the United Kingdom, and other European countries, while others remain in Nepal. The Lhotshampa are predominantly Hindu and Buddhist and speak Nepali.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

In 1989, the Bhutanese government made the national dress code known as *Driglam namzha* mandatory in public during business hours. Lhotshampa objected to being required to wear the clothing of the Ngalong majority.

## Festivals & rituals

The Lhotshampa’s main festivals include **Dashain** and **Tihar**. The sources identify these festivals.

## Foodways

The Lhotshampa are predominantly Hindu and Buddhist. Most abstain from beef, particularly people belonging to orthodox classes, who are vegetarians.

## Oral tradition & literature

They identify Dilliram Sharma Acharya as a Bhutanese poet in the Nepali language.

## Language & religion

Lhotshampas speak Nepali as their first language. Nepali is the first language for most southern Bhutanese and is commonly used in the home; it is also commonly used in schools outside the classes. In southern Bhutan, Nepali was formerly taught in schools and used in both speech and writing, but after racial conflict during the 1980s it became primarily a spoken home language, and some Nepali speakers cannot read or write it. Nepali in Bhutan differs between rural areas and Thimphu, and some Nepali words are used differently in Bhutan than in Nepal.

The Lhotshampa are generally classified as Hindus, although groups including Tamang and Gurung are largely Buddhist, while Kiranti groups including Rai and Limbu are largely animist followers of Mundhum. The sources state that these latter groups are mainly found in eastern Bhutan.
