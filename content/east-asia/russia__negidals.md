## Overview

The Negidals are an Indigenous ethnic group of Khabarovsk Krai in Russia, living along the Amgun River and the Amur River. Their principal present-day distribution is in Ulchskiy District and, to a lesser extent, Imeni Poliny Osipenko District, especially in Vladimirovka village; Negidal families also live in Nikolayevsky, Nanaisky and other districts. According to the 2002 census, Russia had 567 Negidals, including 147 speakers of the Negidal language. The 2010 census recorded 513 Negidals, of whom 75 spoke the language. Negidal belongs to the Tungusic language family and is closely related to Evenki. Their folk culture is associated with fishing, hunting, reindeer transport, animal-hide clothing, bark housing, animistic beliefs and shamanism.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Fish- and seal-skin garments** — Negidal traditional garments included clothing and footwear made from fish skin, seal skin and dog hides. The sources describe materials and garment forms.

**Animal-hide and fur clothing** — Negidals traditionally crafted clothing and footwear from animal hides and fur. They also produced fur blankets and other handmade household items.

**Birch bark utensils and mats** — Birch bark was used for household utensils and for flooring mats inside log cabins.

**Dog-hide garments** — Dog hides formed part of the materials used for traditional Negidal attire. The sources do not distinguish this clothing by gender, rank or ceremonial use.

**Reindeer-hide tent covering** — Upper Negidals covered their movable *chum* tents with reindeer hides in winter. The sources identify the material and seasonal use.

No named Negidal motifs or pattern terms are given in the sources.

### Clothing & dress

Negidal traditional attire consisted of robes, leggings, footwear and headwear. The robes were called *tetchennge* and *uykeli*; leggings were called *heykii*; footwear was called *onta*; and headwear was called *avun*. These garments were made from fish skin, seal skin, dog hides, animal hides and fur. The sources do not distinguish men’s and women’s dress, everyday clothing from ceremonial clothing, or separate forms of dress for particular social occasions. Fur blankets were among the handmade household products associated with Negidal material life.

### Architecture

Negidal housing varied according to the distinction between Upper and Lower Negidals and according to the season. Upper Negidals traditionally lived in large movable *chum* tents, covered with reindeer hides during winter. Lower Negidals were more settled: their winter lodgings were large carcass houses with a *kang* along one wall, while their summer lodgings were small gable-roofed bark huts. Hunters built forest huts for temporary shelter.

Log cabins began to be used by Negidals in the late 18th century. Their floors were made from birch bark mats. A hut contained a hearth, later a stove borrowed from Russian settlers, and beds covered with conifer branches and animal hides. These beds served as both tables and resting places. Tools and utensils were hung on walls or placed in corners.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do document birch bark utensils, fur blankets, boats, skis, dog sleds, reindeer sleds and travois. The travois was called *kelchi* and was generally used for dragging large prey. Both Upper and Lower Negidals used dog sleds; Upper Negidals also used reindeer sleds and sometimes rode on the backs of reindeer. Boats and skis were additional traditional means of transportation.

## Music & performance

No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription is supplied for the Negidals.

## Festivals & rituals

Religious information is limited to the statement that Negidals are officially considered Orthodox Christians while preserving animistic beliefs and shamanism.

The sources do record historical and seasonal practices connected with subsistence. Negidals fished in winter and summer and hunted marine mammals, primarily seals, with harpoons. Upper Negidals used reindeer herding exclusively as a means of transport. Their housing, clothing and travel arrangements varied between seasons and between Upper and Lower Negidals.

## Foodways

They state that Negidals traditionally relied primarily on fishing and hunting, and that marine mammals, especially seals, were hunted with harpoons. The sources also record the later adoption of farming from Russian settlers in the late 19th century. Farming was actively introduced in Soviet kolkhozes and, after the fall of the Soviet Union, remained mostly in the form of gardens.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that the Amgun River served as a link connecting isolated Negidal settlements and that ancient Negidals preserved economic and cultural traditions while living in the region. No contemporary literary revival or preservation project is described.

## Language & religion

The Negidal language belongs to the Tungusic language family and is closely related to Evenki. It has two dialects: Upper Negidal, also called Verkhovskoj, and Lower Negidal, also called Nizovskoj. This dialect division corresponds to the division between Upper and Lower Negidals, whose local cultural, traditional and linguistic differences are described in the source. The Negidal alphabet was introduced in 2009 but is scarcely, if ever, used. Most Negidals living in Russia speak Russian.

Negidal is considered an endangered language. A 2017 study found no active speakers of the Lower dialect and only a few elderly active speakers of Upper Negidal in Vladimirovka. Negidals are officially considered Orthodox Christians, while preserving their own animistic beliefs and shamanism.
