## Overview

The Utsuls are a Chamic-speaking ethnic group living on the southernmost tip of Hainan in the People’s Republic of China. They are concentrated in the two villages of Huihui (回辉) and Huixin (回新), in Tianya, Sanya. The group numbers approximately 4,500 speakers of Tsat, also known as Utsat, Utset, Hainan Cham, or Huihui. Utsul is an Austronesian language of the Malayo-Polynesian group and belongs to the Chamic languages originating on the coast of present-day Vietnam. The Utsuls are considered one of China’s unrecognized ethnic groups, although the Chinese government classifies them as Hui because of their Islamic faith. Their folk-cultural significance in the supplied sources lies chiefly in their Cham historical origins, Islamic identity, and distinctive language shaped by contact with Hlai and Sinitic languages.

## Oral tradition & literature

The origins of the Utsuls are described through several traditional accounts recorded by Thurgood, Thurgood, and Li. These accounts mention Tang-dynasty Xinjiang, Song Guangdong, and post-Vijaya Champa as distinct legendary origins. The sources state that all have some basis in historical fact and that they emphasize Muslim religious identity more strongly than ethnolinguistic heritage. ## Language & religion

Tsat, also called Utsat, Utset, Hainan Cham, or Huihui, is an Austronesian language in the Malayo-Polynesian group and a member of the Chamic languages. It is closely related to Acehnese, Cham, and Jarai. Spoken in Huihui and Huixin near Sanya, it shows extensive influence from neighboring Hlai and Sinitic languages. Its phonology, word structure, and grammar have been strongly reshaped through language contact: unlike Old Cham, it is primarily monosyllabic and heavily tonal. Tsat grammar is analytic and uses word order, adpositions, and independent modifiers instead of bound affixes. The sources distinguish an older “Colloquial Cham” from a more recent “Mandarinised” form. The Utsuls practice Islam, and this religious identity contributes to their classification as Hui by the Chinese government.
