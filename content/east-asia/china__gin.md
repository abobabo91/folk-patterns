## Overview

The Gin or Jing people are a community of descendants of ethnic Viet people living in China. They mainly live on the Jing Islands, off the coast of Dongxing, Fangchenggang, in the Chinese autonomous region of Guangxi. The islands are Wanwei (Vạn Vĩ), Wutou (Vu Đầu), and Shanxin (Sơn Tâm), with some Gin also living in nearby villages of Zhushan and Tanji. The 2020 census recorded 33,112 Gin in China. The Gin language is a dialect of Vietnamese, and many people also speak Standard Cantonese or Mandarin Chinese. Their folk culture includes antiphonal songs, distinctive musical instruments, dances featuring lanterns and dragons, folk stories and legends, traditional clothing, and the Ha Festival, which has been recognized as a national intangible cultural heritage of China.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Gin costume is described as simple and practical. Traditionally, women wear tight-fitting, collarless short blouses buttoned in front, similar to clothing worn in the south of Vietnam, together with broad black or brown trousers. When going out, women put on a light-colored gown with narrow sleeves. Men wear long jackets reaching down to the knees and girdles. Most people now dress like their Han neighbors, although a few elderly women retain the older style.

## Music & performance

Gin people like antiphonal songs, which are described as melodious and lyrical. Their traditional instruments include the two-stringed fiddle, flute, drum, gong, and the single-stringed fiddle, which is identified as a unique musical instrument of the ethnic group.

Folk stories and legends are said to abound. The single-stringed fiddle is the only instrument specifically distinguished as unique to the Gin ethnic group in the supplied material.

## Dance & theatre

The favorite dances of the Gin feature lanterns, fancy colored sticks, embroidery, and dragons.

## Festivals & rituals

The biggest festival of the Gin people is the Ha Festival. It has been recognized as a national intangible cultural heritage of China.

## Foodways

The Gin live in a subtropical area with plenty of rainfall and rich mineral resources. The Gulf of Tonkin is described as an ideal fishing ground. More than 700 species of fish are found there, of which over 200 are of great economic value and high yields. Pearls, sea horses, and sea otters are abundant and are prized for their medicinal value. Seawater from the Gulf of Tonkin is suitable for salt making.

The main crops are rice, sweet potato, peanut, taro, and millet. Subtropical fruits including papaya, banana, and longan are also plentiful.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that folk stories and legends abound among the Gin.

Gin people historically used chữ Hán and chữ Nôm to write Vietnamese. Chữ Nôm was created on the basis of the script of the Han people toward the end of the 13th century and is found in old song books and religious scriptures. The supplied material does not identify individual song books or scriptures.

## Language & religion

The Gin language is a dialect of Vietnamese. For writing, the Gin still use chữ Hán and chữ Nôm to write Vietnamese. The sources state that the Gin were not affected by the transition to the Vietnamese Latin alphabet by the French colonial government during the French colonial period. Standard Cantonese is spoken by many people in the community, as is Mandarin Chinese.

A survey in 1980 indicated that one third of Gin people had lost their native language and could speak only Cantonese or Mandarin. Another third were bilingual in Gin Vietnamese and Chinese. The survey suggested a decline in use of the Gin language, although in the 2000s there appeared to be a revival.

Many Gin are believers in Buddhism or Taoism, with a few followers of Catholicism.
