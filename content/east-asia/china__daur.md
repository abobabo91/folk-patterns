## Overview

The Daurs, also called Dagur, Daghur, or Dahur, are a Mongolic people originally native to Dauriya. They are now predominantly located in Northeast China, with some communities near Tacheng in Xinjiang; the source also records a historical presence in Siberia, Russia. Most live in Morin Dawa Daur Autonomous Banner in Hulun Buir, northeastern Inner Mongolia, and Meilisi Daur District in Qiqihar, Heilongjiang. The 2020 census recorded 132,299 Daurs. Their language, Dagur, is Mongolic and has three dialects identified in the general Daur account: Batgan, Hailar, and Qiqihar. Daur folk culture includes distinctive clan organization, seasonal clothing, shamanic practice, Tibetan Buddhism, and the long-played sport Beikou.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Daur clothing is described in relation to season and gender. During winter, Daur women wear long dresses, generally blue in color, together with boots of skin. In summer, they change to long trousers. Men wear orejeros caps made from fox or red deer skin in winter. In summer, they cover the animal’s head with white-colored fabrics or use straw hats.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe clan-based ceremonies rather than an annual festival calendar. Each Daur clan has its own shaman, who is responsible for important ceremonies in the lives of the Daur. Many Daurs practice shamanism, while a significant number have adopted Tibetan Buddhism.

Daurian shamanism gives an important place to a cult of the sky called *tenger*. Ancient people worshiped the image of *tenger* as the sun and the moon. In mythical representation, the sun appeared as mother; the sun and moon were considered a man and a woman, respectively, and these celestial bodies represented father and mother in Daur mythology.

## Foodways

 They mention Daur farmers in accounts of the seventeenth-century Amur region, but provide no information about their cuisine or food preparation.

## Oral tradition & literature

During the Qing, the Daur knew a version of the Tale of the Nisan Shaman. In this version, the female shaman Ny Dan competed against rivals at the Qing court: Tibetan monks who persuaded the Qing emperor to execute her. The Qing emperor is presented as a fool tricked by the lamas.

The sources also record a historical interpretation by Konan Naito, who identified Takri Kingdom, where King Dongmyeong, a founder of Buyeo, was born, as a country of Daur people living by the Songhua River. This is presented as an observation about Daur history rather than as a Daur oral text.

## Language & religion

Dagur, Daghur, Dahur, or Daur is a Mongolic language and a distinct branch of the Mongolic language family. It is primarily spoken by members of the Daur ethnic group. The language has no written standard in current use, although a Pinyin-based orthography has been devised. Dagur speakers instead use Mongolian or Chinese, while during the Qing dynasty Dagur was written with the Manchu alphabet.

The language is described as having five dialects: Amur Dagur, Nonni Dagur, Hailar Dagur, and Sinkiang Dagur, with Nonni Dagur divided into Morin Daba Dagur, Butha or Buteha, Tsitsikar or Qiqihar, and Mergen or Nenjiang Dagur. The general Daur account identifies Batgan, Hailar, and Qiqihar as three dialects. Dagur retains some Khitan substratal features and has vocabulary borrowed from Chinese, Manchu, Evenki, and Russian.

Many Daurs practice shamanism, with a shaman attached to each clan. Tibetan Buddhism is also practiced by a significant number of Daurs.
