## Overview

The Achang, also known by their own name *Ngac'ang*, are an ethnic group officially recognized as one of the 56 ethnic groups of the People’s Republic of China. They number approximately 43,775, with most living in Yunnan province, primarily in Lianghe County of Dehong Autonomous Prefecture. Achang communities also live in Longchuan County, Luxi County, and Longling County, while related communities are found in Myanmar, where they are known as Maingtha in Shan State and Ngochang in Kachin State. The Achang speak Achang, a Burmish language within the Tibeto-Burman language family. Their folk culture, as described by the sources, is particularly associated with music, songs, dances, Buddhist funeral practice, and regional forms of language and religious life.

## Music & performance

Music and songs are central to the Achang cultural account given by the sources. Much of Achang history and tradition has been passed down through music and songs, and music is described as an integral part of their culture. Celebrations typically end with songs and dances, placing musical performance within communal festive contexts.

 The Achang language source records that Achang is spoken by communities also known as Maingtha and Ngochang in Yunnan, China, and northern Myanmar.

## Dance & theatre

The sources state that Achang celebrations typically conclude with songs and dances. The sources used therefore cover communal song-and-dance performance only in general terms and do not distinguish ceremonial dance from entertainment dance.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state that celebrations typically end with songs and dances.

The sources do document Buddhist funerals. During the ceremony, the Achang tie a long fabric tape, about 20 metres (66 ft), to the coffin. A monk walks ahead while holding the tape, which guides the soul of the deceased toward its final destination. The deceased is buried without metallic objects such as jewelry, because these are believed to contaminate the soul for future reincarnation.

The Husa Achang, who live in Longchuan County, are described as practicing a mixture of Theravada Buddhism and Taoism when following their traditional religious practice. Confucian-style ancestral memorial tablets are commonly found in Husa homes, and the Husa are described as more Sinicized than other Achang.

## Language & religion

Achang is a Tibeto-Burman language spoken by the Achang in Yunnan, China, and northern Myanmar. It is also known as Ngachang in Achang, 阿昌 in Chinese, and အာချန် in Burmese. The Achang language is described as a Burmish language related to Burmese. There is no indigenous writing system for Achang, and Chinese characters are often used instead. Many Achang also speak Tai Lü, mainly for commercial transactions with the Dai people.

In China, Achang is spoken in Longchuan County, Lianghe County, Luxi City, and Longling County. The three main Chinese dialects are Longchuan, Lianghe, and Mangshi, the last formerly known as Luxi. A Xiandao dialect, with 100 speakers, is spoken in Xiandaozhai and Meng’ezhai in Yingjiang County. The sources describe Husa Achang religious practice as a mixture of Theravada Buddhism and Taoism, while also noting Confucian-style ancestral memorial tablets in Husa homes.
