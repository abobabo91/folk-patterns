## Overview

The Darkhad (*Дархад*) are a subgroup of the Mongols living mainly in northern Mongolia, particularly in the Bayanzürkh, Ulaan-Uul, Renchinlkhümbe, and Tsagaannuur Districts of Khövsgöl Province. Speakers of Darkhad live mainly west of Lake Khövsgöl, in Bayanzürkh, Ulaan-Uul, and Rinchinlkhümbe. The Darkhad dialect is a regional variety of Mongolian, described as intermediate between Central Mongolian and Oirat and also as a dialect of Khalkha Mongolian with Oirat features. The 2000 census recorded 16,268 people identifying themselves as Darkhad; Ethnologue reports a population of 24,000 without providing a date. Darkhad history includes Oirat, Khotgoid, Khalkha, Samoyedic, Turkic, and other historical connections. Many Darkhad practise Mongolian shamanism.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The historical account describes forest tribes living in yurts. It also states that the Darkhad led a nomadic life, moving 200–300 km annually between summer pastures near the Shishged River and wintering areas in Bayan-Zurkh, Agar, Beltes, and Altraga.

## Music & performance

They do mention shamanic chants of the Khori Buryats, in which an ancestral spirit named Manjilkha is frequently mentioned, but this is presented in connection with the Maanjrag Darkhad clan rather than as a description of a Darkhad musical tradition. Many Darkhad practise Mongolian shamanism. The Khorlomai account also describes the shaman Noyon Böö, who defeated or overcame a group of marauders near the Tengis River. No instrument or musical performance is named.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe ancestral and spiritual practices: the Sharanuud Darkhad venerate ancestral spirits represented by the banners of Tsagaadai and Tsankhilkhan, and the Bööguud (“Shamans”) claim descent from a shaman ancestor. Many Darkhad practise Mongolian shamanism.

## Foodways

The historical material states that some forest tribes hunted in the taiga and herded reindeer, while more powerful tribes raised livestock, including the Oirat Mongols.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources describe a Khorlomai origin story connected with the Tengis River and the shaman Noyon Böö. According to the account, the Khorlomai were marauders who attempted to kill Noyon Böö with weapons and fire. He instructed each warrior to build a raft and float downstream, while he remained behind with a pregnant woman; the others perished, and the Khorlomai clan is said to have descended from her son. The sources also state that the Sharanuud Darkhad trace their lineage to tribes in the Darkhad Valley and venerate ancestral spirits represented by the banners of Tsagaadai and Tsankhilkhan.

## Language & religion

Darkhad is a dialect of Mongolian, described as intermediate between Central Mongolian and Oirat and variously classified as closer to Oirat or as Khalkha Mongolian with Oirat features. It has substantially assimilated to the Khalkha dialect since it was first described by Sanžeev. The case system is the same as in Khalkha, while Oirat plurals like –mud are absent. Many Darkhad practise Mongolian shamanism. The sources also describe ancestral-spirit veneration among the Sharanuud Darkhad and shamanic ancestry claimed by the Bööguud.
