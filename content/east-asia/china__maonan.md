## Overview

The Maonan are one of the 56 ethnic groups officially recognized by the People’s Republic of China. In 2010, their total population was 124,092, with most living in northern Guangxi and southern Guizhou in southern China. Huanjiang Maonan Autonomous County in Hechi, northern Guangxi, contains a concentrated number of Maonan speakers. The Maonan language is a Kam–Sui language, although approximately half of Maonan people can speak it and many also speak Chinese or a Zhuang language. Maonan culture documented in the sources includes clan-organized settlements, distinctive pickled foods, household shrines, animistic and polytheistic religious practices, and rituals associated with marriage, birth, and death.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Maonan towns generally contain no more than 100 dwellings and are organized by clan. Their houses are similar to those of the Zhuang and usually have two floors, constructed from planks and clay. The upper floor serves as living quarters, while the lower floor is used as a granary and stable.

## Festivals & rituals

The Maonan practice a redemptive ritual called *vɛ4 da:u4* to express gratitude to *ni4 fa:n1 su:i5*, the Goddess of Birth. During weddings, couples ask the birth goddess for fertility. After they have children, they thank her by offering 26 animals: 1 ox, 7 small pigs, and 19 chickens and ducks.

Traditionally, marriages were arranged by parents while the future newlyweds were still children. When a Maonan woman became a widow, the brother of her late husband traditionally took her as his wife. During funerals, the children of the deceased had to “buy” water from a river to wash the corpse. Before burial, chicken blood was poured on the land to purify it and bless the spirit of the dead person.

## Foodways

Maonan cuisine includes many pickled dishes. The best-known group is called *sa:m1 səm3 ta5 ma:u6na:n6*, meaning “The Three Maonan Sour Dishes.” The three dishes are *na:n4 səm3*, pickled meat; *zo5 fa:t7*, fermented soup; and *ʔɔŋ5 ʔwu:i5*, a dish preserved with lime in an urn.

Rice wine is also popular among the Maonan.

## Language & religion

The Maonan language is a Kam–Sui language spoken mainly in China, especially in northern Guangxi and southern Guizhou. It is spoken in Huanjiang Maonan Autonomous County, Nandan County, Du’an Yao Autonomous County, and Yizhou in Guangxi, and in Libo County and Pingtang County in Guizhou. Approximately half of all Maonan people can speak Maonan. Many also speak Chinese or a Zhuang language. The Maonan do not have a writing system, although a writing system was established in 2010 using 26 Latin letters and tonal markers.

Traditional Maonan religion is animistic and polytheistic, with strong Taoist influences. Many deities and rituals are borrowed from Chinese folk religion. Household shrines are called *ji:ŋ1 wa3* and consist of a wooden plate on a wall facing the front entrance, bearing the names of deities and family ancestors. Other named figures include the Kitchen God, the God of Earth, General Li Guang, Shentu and Yulei, the Thunder God, the Thunder Soldiers, the Flower God, Guanyin, *muŋ2 ku:n1*, *lu2 pa:n1*, *lɔk8 gi:u2*, *ʔbi:u2*, *sa:m3 ȵɔn2*, *za4 wa:ŋ2*, and *ni4 fa:n1 su:i5*.
