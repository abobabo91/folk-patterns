## Overview

The Mulao are an ethnic group officially recognized as one of the 56 ethnic groups of the People’s Republic of China. They call themselves *Mulam*. A large portion of the population in Guangxi lives in Luocheng Mulao Autonomous County of Hechi, Guangxi, China. The 2020 Chinese Census recorded 277,233 Mulao people in China, while the 2010 Chinese Census recorded 216,257. The Mulao speak the Mulam language, a Tai–Kadai language that uses Hanzi, or Chinese characters, when written. Their documented folk culture includes arranged marriage, distinctive three-room clay houses with brick roofs, men’s clothing with large buttons and wide pants, women’s hair arrangements, animist traditions, the Yifan festival, and ceremonies associated with the dragon boat festival.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The traditional clothing of Mulao men consists of a jacket with large buttons, wide pants, and sandals. For women, the sources document a distinctive hair practice: single women arranged their hair into two tresses, which became a tuft when they were married. No further details of women’s clothing are given.

### Architecture

Mulao homes are made of clay and have brick roofs. They are composed of three rooms, while animals are maintained far away from the family dwellings.

### Jewelry & body adornment

They document only the arrangement of women’s hair: two tresses for single women and a tuft after marriage.

## Music & performance

They mention shamans only in connection with ceremonies held during the dragon boat festival, when ceremonies were carried out to assure good crop harvests and expel harmful insects. No instrument, song, dance, or musical style is named.

## Dance & theatre

The only documented ceremonial performance context is the dragon boat festival, during which shamans carried out ceremonies connected with harvests and harmful insects.

## Festivals & rituals

Traditionally, the Mulao were mostly animists, although religion no longer plays a significant role in their daily life. Each month they celebrated diverse festivals. The most important was the festival *Yifan*, during which diverse sacrifices of animals were carried out.

The dragon boat festival was celebrated on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month. During this celebration, shamans carried out ceremonies intended to assure good crop harvests and to expel harmful insects.

Marriage was traditionally arranged by parents. New wives traditionally did not live together with their new husbands until the birth of their first son.

## Foodways

They mention animal sacrifices at the Yifan festival.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention only the Mulam language and its use of Hanzi, or Chinese characters, when written.

## Language & religion

The Mulao speak the Mulam language, which is a Tai–Kadai language and uses Hanzi, or Chinese characters, when written. The related term “Mulao language” can refer to the Mulam language, described as a Kam-Sui language spoken in Guangxi, or to an extinct Kra language that was spoken in Guizhou.

Traditionally, the Mulao were mostly animists. Religion no longer plays a significant role in their daily life. Their documented religious and ritual practices include diverse animal sacrifices at the Yifan festival and ceremonies conducted by shamans during the dragon boat festival. These ceremonies were intended to assure good crop harvests and expel harmful insects.
