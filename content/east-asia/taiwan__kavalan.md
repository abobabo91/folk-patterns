## Overview

The Kavalan, also known as Kuvalan, are an indigenous people of Taiwan. Their endonym is *kbalan*, meaning “people living in the plain,” and their language is also known as Kavalan. Most Kavalan moved to the coastal area of Hualien County and Taitung County in the 19th century because of encroachment by Han settlers. The largest Kavalan settlement is currently Xinshe, or *pateRongan*, Village in Fengbin Township, Hualien County. Their earlier homeland was associated with the area now called Yilan City and with the Lanyang River. Kavalan belongs to the East Formosan branch of the Austronesian language family. The sources present Kavalan culture through its relationship to the plain, migration, clothing, banana-fiber weaving, ritual practice, and the preservation of a critically endangered language.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Banana-fiber weaving** — Banana fiber is woven into hemp fabric used for traditional Kavalan attire, daily necessities, and accessories. The sources specifically mention belts, straw capes, and tote bags as objects made from or complementing this textile tradition.

**Kavalan traditional attire** — Kavalan clothing is characterized by simplicity, usually using only one or two colors, predominantly black or white. The sources distinguish it from clothing with elaborate dyeing and colorful geometric patterns.

**Hemp fabric** — Hemp fabric made from woven banana fiber is used for clothing and household accessories.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no Kavalan textile motifs.

### Clothing & dress

Kavalan people wear traditional clothing during important traditional performances and ritual practices. The attire is unisex and is generally simple, with black or white as the predominant colors. The upper garment is practically identical for men and women: a white shirt with a round neck, long sleeves, and a row of Chinese-style buttons down the middle, with black cloth on the front. The lower garment usually consists of a black or white skirt. Chiefs, elders, and shamans are exceptions, wearing black pants and a black shirt. In the past, Kavalan men sometimes wore shorter garments or exposed their chests and backs. Han Chinese influence introduced cotton clothing in the Han style. Banana fiber is an important clothing material and is also used for belts, straw capes, and tote bags.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention tote bags as accessories made from banana-fiber textiles, but provide no further catalogue or technical information.

## Dance & theatre

They refer generally to important traditional performances and ritual practices, in which traditional clothing is worn, without describing their forms or contexts.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that traditional clothes are worn during important traditional performances and ritual practices. Chiefs, elders, and shamans are identified as people whose clothing differs from the usual black-or-white attire.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources preserve a historical legend concerning the Kavalan’s arrival. According to the account, they arrived by sea from the east, saw the beauty of the location, and decided to settle the land. The newly arrived Kavalan fought many battles against the local Atayal people and eventually drove them into the mountains. The name “Kavalan” is explained as meaning “flatland people”; it subsequently changed into “Hamalan,” ultimately yielding the modern-day Yilan City.

## Language & religion

Kavalan is also known as Kvalan, Kebalan, or Kbalan. It is an East Formosan language of the Austronesian family. It was formerly spoken on the northeast coast of Taiwan and is no longer spoken in its original area. In 1930 it was used only as a home language; in 1987 it was still spoken in Atayal territories; and in 2000 it was reported to have 24 speakers and was considered moribund. A 2017 study found Kavalan to be critically endangered and among the most lexically distinct Austronesian languages. Speech communities include Kariawan, Patʀungan, Kulis, and Kralut. Many Kavalan also speak Amis, Taiwanese, Mandarin, and Japanese.
