## Overview

The Jino, also spelled Jinuo, are a Tibeto-Burman ethnic group and one of the 56 ethnic groups officially recognized by the People’s Republic of China. They live in the Jino Mountains, or Jinuoshan, in eastern Jinghong, Xishuangbanna, Yunnan province. Most Jino concentrate in Jinoshan, a series of mild hills with a wet climate near Mengyang Township in Jinghong Municipality, Xishuangbanna Dai Autonomous Prefecture. Their area is about 70x50 km and lies in subtropical rainforest. The Jino had a population of 26,025 according to the census of 2020. They were recognized as a national minority in 1979, making them the last group included in that category in China. Their cultural practices include tooth painting using soot made from pear trees.

## Material culture

### Jewelry & body adornment

Tooth painting is identified as a Jino cultural practice; it uses soot made from pear trees.

## Language & religion

Jino, also called Jinuo, constitutes a pair of Loloish language varieties spoken by the Jino people of Yunnan, China. The two varieties are Youle Jino and Buyuan Jinuo, and they are not mutually intelligible. The sources state that 70–80% of Jinuo people can speak either variety fluently, while Buyuan Jino is spoken by 21,000 people. Jino has no official writing system, although the Jino developed systems of signs and used engraved wooden or bamboo boards to record debts between villages.
