## Overview

The Truku, also known as the Taroko people, are an Indigenous Taiwanese people living primarily in Wanrong Township, Xiulin Township, and Zhuoxi Township in Hualien County, Eastern Taiwan. Taroko is also the name of the area of Taiwan where they reside. The Executive Yuan, Republic of China officially recognized the Taroko on 15 January 2004, making them the 12th Indigenous group in Taiwan to receive this recognition. Previously, the Taroko and the related Seediq people were classified in the Atayal group. The Taroko pursued separate status through a “name rectification” campaign. Their material culture is especially documented through clothing, weaving, geometric patterns, and shell ornamentation.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Flax weaving** — The Truku people’s clothing, accessories, and bedding are predominantly made from linen, with wool and cotton also described as viable materials. Garment production involves spinning, bleaching, and warping, after which women weave flax in colors including green, yellow, red, black, and white.

**Ramie-cloth garment construction** — Men’s square garments incorporate long strips sewn into white ramie cloth. The upper edge of the breast pocket has fastening straps, with additional straps on each side for securing the garment behind the neck and back.

**Geometric line and rhombus patterning** — Truku textiles incorporate geometric lines and rhombus patterns as shared symbols aligned with belief. The techniques, styles, and textures vary across regions.

**Motif vocabulary.** Horizontal lines — the path leading to the spiritual bridge; diamond pattern — the ancestral spirit’s vigilant eye and protection.

### Clothing & dress

The traditional attire of the Truku people is predominantly fashioned from linen, although wool and cotton are also viable materials. Men’s attire typically consists of a sleeveless square jacket with a breast pocket. Long strips are sewn into white ramie cloth, and straps attached to the pocket and garment help fasten it behind the neck and back. Chiefs and warriors distinguish themselves through shell clothes decorated with cylindrical shell ornaments.

Women’s common attire consists of a black headdress with colorful diamond shapes, a vivid blue top, and a black skirt secured by two strips around the breast and waist. Women also wear two black leggings on the calves and ankles.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources document shell ornamentation in clothing. Chiefs and warriors wear shell clothes adorned with cylindrical shell ornaments.

## Music & performance

The sources identify Bokeh Kosang and Tseng Shu-chin as singers among notable people.

## Festivals & rituals

The clothing article states that horizontal lines symbolize the path leading to the spiritual bridge and that the diamond pattern represents the ancestral spirit’s vigilant eye, signifying protection.

## Foodways

The Seediq language article records *saws* as an interjection uttered when offering food to ancestors.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention the “name rectification” campaign through which the Taroko people demanded separate status from the Atayal group.

## Language & religion

Seediq, also known as Sediq and Truku, and previously transcribed as Taroko, is an Atayalic language spoken in the mountains of Northern Taiwan by the Seediq and Taroko people. Seediq has three main dialects: Truku, Toda, and Tgdaya. Members of each dialect group refer to themselves by the name of their dialect, while the Amis people call them “Taroko.”

The Truku dialect is described as having stress on the final syllable. The language has 19 consonant phonemes and 4 vowel phonemes, and its word order is verb–object–subject. Seediq uses reduplication, prefixes, and suffixes in grammatical and derivational processes. The sources describe spiritual symbolism in textile patterns, including the spiritual bridge and the ancestral spirit’s vigilant eye.
