## Overview

Oroks, also called Uilta, are a people of Sakhalin Oblast, mainly in the eastern part of Sakhalin Island in Russia. Their self-designations include *Ulta*, *Ulcha*, and *Nani*. They live principally in Poronaysk, Nogliki, and the village of Val in Nogliksky District, as well as in other villages and districts of Sakhalin Oblast. In the two thousand two Russian census, three hundred forty-six Oroks were recorded; the two thousand ten census recorded two hundred ninety-five Oroks in Russia. A community also lived near Abashiri on Hokkaido in one thousand nine hundred eighty-nine. The Orok language belongs to the Southern group of the Tungusic language family. Orok culture has been shaped especially by reindeer herding, fishing, hunting, vegetable farming, and cattle ranching.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources state that reindeer provided the Oroks, particularly in northern Sakhalin, with clothing.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources describe a rite in which a successful hunter of the *kaluga sturgeon* extracted one of the predator’s teeth and carved a mark on his forehead or arm.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe a rite of passage for boys: when the time came, a boy would usually participate in a sturgeon hunt, specifically a hunt for the *kaluga sturgeon*. Individual hunters went out with a small supply of food, usually enough to last a week, and a special type of spear. After a successful kill, the hunter extracted one of the predator’s teeth and carved a mark on his forehead or arm to indicate that the hunt had succeeded. The sources note that failure to kill the sturgeon usually resulted in the hunter’s death, while also recording a citation questioning the statement that sturgeon have teeth. No Orok birth, wedding, funeral, or other life-cycle rites are described.

## Foodways

The sources state that reindeer provided the Oroks with food, clothing, and transportation. They also practiced fishing and hunting. Some northern Oroks combine semi-nomadic reindeer herding with vegetable farming and cattle ranching, while fishing and industrial labor are leading occupations in the south.

## Oral tradition & literature

Orok oral tradition indicates that the Oroks share history with the Ulch people and migrated to Sakhalin from the area of the Amgun River in mainland Russia. Research cited in the source places this migration at the latest in the seventeenth century.

## Language & religion

The Orok language belongs to the Southern group of the Tungusic language family. At present, sixty-four people of the Sakhalin Oroks speak Orok, and all Oroks speak Russian. An alphabetic script based on Cyrillic was introduced in two thousand seven; a primer has been published, and Orok is taught in one school on Sakhalin.
