## Overview

Siberian Cossacks were Cossacks who settled in the Siberian region of Russia from the end of the 16th century, following Yermak Timofeyevich’s conquest of Siberia. In the early period, practically the whole Russian population in Siberia, especially the serving-men, were called Cossacks in the loose sense of being neither land-owners nor peasants. Most came from northwest Russia and had little connection to the Don Cossacks or Zaporozhian Cossacks. Their documented significance in the supplied sources is primarily military and uniform-related: the Siberian Host served the Tsars, garrisoned settlements and frontier posts, and developed distinctive dress shaped by service and the northern climate.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

In 1802 the Siberian Host was authorized to wear uniforms, replacing traditional dress. These uniforms were initially based on those of the Don Cossacks, but after 1812 a more conventional lancer style was adopted. In practice, Siberian Cossacks continued to provide their own clothing and equipment, so both were varied.

By the 1880s, the distinguishing colour of the three regiments of the Siberian Cossack Host was red. It appeared on cap bands, epaulettes and wide trouser stripes, combined with a green uniform of the loose-fitting cut common to the Steppe Cossacks. High fleece hats with red cloth tops were worn on occasion. Early-1900s officers’ modifications included black collars and pointed cuffs edged with red piping; epaulettes and shoulder-strap braiding were silver. In 1909, khaki government-issue tunics and caps were provided for other ranks, while red facings and green breeches were retained.

The northern climate produced heavier green tunics and khaki or grey overcoats than those of southern hosts. The sheep’s-wool fleece headdress was taller and wider, producing a matted and tangled appearance.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do record military service on behalf of the Tsars from the 18th century until the revolution of 1917, and they state that the Siberian Host was disbanded in 1919 following the Russian Revolution.

In recognition of service during the French Invasion of Russia in 1812, the regiments of the Siberian Host received the privilege of attaching coloured pennants to their lances. These pennants were connected with military service rather than with a documented festival or ritual calendar.
