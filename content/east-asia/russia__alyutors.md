## Overview

The Alyutors are an ethnic group of the Russian Far East, formerly classified as a subgroup of Koryaks. They live on the Kamchatka Peninsula and Chukchi Peninsula, and most currently live in Koryak Okrug of Kamchatka Krai. The name also appears as Olyutors and Olutors; Olyutorka was a settlement where many Alyutors formerly lived. According to the 2021 Russian census, 96 people identified as Alyutors, although estimates place the number living in Russia at approximately 2,000 to 3,000. Their language belongs to the Chukotko-Kamchatkan language family, but less than 10% still speak it. Their folk-cultural significance in the available source lies in reindeer breeding, fishing, trapping, hunting, settlement architecture, shamanism, and the preservation of a traditional way of life under present ecological and demographic pressure.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They state that the Alyutors traditionally practiced reindeer breeding, fishing, trapping, and hunting.

### Architecture

The Alyutors traditionally positioned their settlements along rivers on elevated spots with good visibility around them. Up until the 19th century, their only type of housing was an octagonal earth house with vertical walls, intended for 3 to 5 families. The elevated settlement locations and the communal scale of the houses are the only documented architectural characteristics.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention reindeer breeding, fishing, trapping, and hunting as traditional occupations.

## Music & performance

They state that many Alyutors are practitioners of shamanism and Orthodox Christianity.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that most Alyutors are practitioners of shamanism and Orthodox Christianity.

The historical source describes several events affecting the Alyutors. In 1697, Russian Cossacks imposed taxes on them, and the Alyutors showed armed resistance in the following years. The 1751 uprising was suppressed, after which their number significantly decreased. They were also repeatedly attacked by the Chukchis, who often confiscated their reindeer herds. In the late 18th century, their isolated and secluded circumstances helped them avoid smallpox epidemics almost unharmed and preserve their traditional way of life.

## Foodways

Fishing, hunting, trapping, and reindeer breeding are identified as traditional occupations.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that the Alyutors are mentioned in the earliest chronicles concerning the Russian colonisation of Kamchatka.

The sources do describe a major interruption in cultural transmission. From the 1950s through the 1970s, Alyutor children were sent to boarding schools. This increased the loss of the Alyutor language and decreased their training in Alyutor traditions. During the Soviet period, many Alyutors became teachers, doctors, geologists, and zoo technicians.

## Language & religion

The Alyutors spoke the Alyutor language, also known as the Nymylan language. It belongs to the Chukotko-Kamchatkan language family. The language was previously considered a dialect of Koryak, while many Russian experts now consider it a separate language. Less than 10% of Alyutors still speak it; most present Alyutors instead speak Koryak and Russian.

The sources give the self-designation *Alutal'u* and identify the Alyutor term *nəməlʔu*. Most Alyutors are described as practitioners of shamanism and Orthodox Christianity.
