## Overview

The Evenki are a Tungusic people spread across two and a half million square kilometers of Siberian taiga. They live as a mixture of pastoralists and hunter-gatherers, keeping small herds of domesticated reindeer for transport and milk while hunting wild elk, reindeer and game. Their material culture shows distinctive clothing cut for cold and movement, with soft hide garments decorated with applied cloth strips and glass beads. Shamanic belief and practice remain central to their worldview.

## Material culture

Evenki material culture centers on portable, practical objects built for a mobile life in cold taiga: soft reindeer and elk hide clothing, birch-bark and hide tents, and specialized hunting and herding gear.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Evenki textiles combine soft hide with applied cloth strips and glass-bead embroidery in geometric and figurative patterns.

- **Appliqué on reindeer and elk hide** — Applied strips of trade cloth in blue, red and striped silk create geometric patterns on soft hide garments.
- **Glass-bead embroidery** — Beads are stitched onto applied cloth strips in cream, turquoise, black, light brown, red, green and transparent colors, often covering rectangular hide panels.
- **Hide dyed with alder bark and soot** — Reindeer hide is dyed reddish with alder bark and blackish with soot, arranged in concentric bands.
- **Pigment painting on leather** — Summer trousers carry chevron bands in brown and tan pigment with painted human figures in black.
- **Impressed birch bark** — Fine birch bark covered with impressed lines sheathes the face, edges and mid-section of composite bows.

### Clothing & dress

Evenki clothing is built from soft reindeer or elk skin in brief garments designed for cold, dry air and constant movement.

- **Deerskin coat** — A tan or waisted coat, knee-length or shorter, left open at the front and fastened with leather strips.
- **Men's summer trousers** — Short pointed trousers drawn with a leather string, decorated with chevron bands and human figures.
- **Women's beaded underpants** — The most heavily worked garment in the collection, covered with dense glass-bead couching and metal discs.
- **Summer boots** — Long supple boots with brown leather soles, legs stained pink and blue, with applied wool, silk and cotton in geometric shapes.
- **Hide loincloth and leggings** — Constant elements of the costume worn under outer garments for protection and mobility.

### Architecture

The Evenki lived in conical tents built of birch bark or reindeer skin tied to birch poles, a form with roots reaching back to Neolithic times.

- **Conical birch-bark tent** — A portable dwelling with a birch pole framework, the covering carried when camps moved and frameworks left standing for reuse.
- **Conical reindeer-skin tent** — A portable dwelling similar to the birch-bark tent, constructed with hide stretched over birch poles.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Evenki metalwork and crafted objects center on hunting, herding and travel equipment, made from iron, bone, wood and ivory.

- **Reindeer saddle** — An Evenki-specific saddle set on the animal's shoulders to spare its back, ridden with a balancing stick in place of stirrups.
- **Composite bow** — Wood faced with whalebone strips, rimmed with ivory, sheathed in impressed birch bark, with knobs under each bend to catch the string.
- **Lance-head** — A large iron head for bear hunting, inlaid with copper and brass in figures of men in dog-drawn sledges and fish.
- **Harpoon head** — A barbed iron head perforated for a looped cord, used in marine hunting.
- **Pike** — A large knife on a long handle used as an axe in thick taiga or as a bear spear.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Evenki body adornment centers on facial tattooing and clothing embellished with imported glass beads and metal discs.

- **Facial tattooing** — Very common among the Evenki, marking identity and status.
- **Glass beads** — Imported beads in transparent, light brown, black, cream, turquoise, red, green and blue stitched onto cloth and hide.
- **Silver-coloured metal discs** — Round discs used in rows at hemlines and in pairs on beaded motifs on clothing.

## Music & performance

The sources document little secular Evenki music beyond a herder's signalling tool, but shamanic performance is well attested.

- **Wooden reindeer-call horn** — A herder's signalling tool strengthened with birch bark at its wide end, the only named Evenki instrument in the sources.
- **Evenki folk songs** — A collection transcribed in IPA and Mongolian script with Chinese-style numbered musical notation, published with the Evenki–Mongolian–Chinese dictionary of 1983.
- **Shamanic performance** — The shamaness Dula'r kept hidden an Abagaldi bear spirit shaman mask of a type also documented among the Mongols and Daur.

## Festivals & rituals

Evenki ritual life centers on animism, belief in upper, middle and lower worlds, the soul, and totemistic concepts, with shamanism taking over magical hunting and herding rituals.

- **Soul** (*omi*) — A central concept in Evenki belief, tied to all living things and the practice of shamanism.
- **Hunting prohibitions** — Animals must not be tormented, wounded animals must be finished at once, animal blood must not be spilt or defiled.
- **Pastoral ritual year** — Winter hunting camps of one or two tents, spring break-up toward calving grounds, joint summer pasturing of herds.
- **Shamanic vocation** — Described through initiatory illness and multi-year apprenticeship; the shamaness Dula'r was permitted to heal at 25 or 26.

## Foodways

The Evenki were a mixture of pastoralists and hunter-gatherers, with domesticated reindeer supplying milk and transport while hunted game provided meat.

- **Wild reindeer hunting** — Wild reindeer were hunted and eaten, while domesticated ones were kept for transport and milk, not slaughter.
- **Elk hunting** — Key game for taiga dwellers, along with wild reindeer and fowl.
- **Fishing** — Completed the diet; bone fish-lures are among the oldest elements of Evenki culture.
- **Small reindeer herds** — Most households kept about 25 head of reindeer, bred for transport rather than slaughter.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Evenki built a written literature fast and deliberately from the 1920s onward, despite predictions of its quick extinction.

- **First Book for Reading in the Tungus Language** (*əwənkil dukuwuntin*) — Prepared by G. M. Vasilevich, published a year after his Memo to Tungus-vacationers in 1928.
- **Evenki literary works** — From the 1930s onward, folklore, novels, poetry, numerous translations from Russian and other languages, textbooks and dictionaries were published.
- **Nikita Sakharov** — A poet and prose writer (1915–1945) who contributed to early Evenki literature.
- **Alitet Nemtushkin** — A poet (1939–2006) who wrote in Evenki.

## Language & religion

Evenki is the largest northern Tungusic language, highly agglutinating with 13 cases, but endangered with only 5,831 speakers among 39,226 Evenki in 2021.

- **Evenki language** — Closely related to Even, Negidal and Oroqen, with 14 dialects and 50 sub-dialects in Russia grouped into northern, southern and eastern.
- **Northern dialects** — One of three major phonological groupings of Evenki spoken in Russia.
- **Southern dialects** — One of three major phonological groupings of Evenki spoken in Russia.
- **Eastern dialects** — One of three major phonological groupings of Evenki spoken in Russia.
- **Cyrillic script** — Replaced the officially approved Latin alphabet from 1937 onward for writing Evenki.

## Glossary

- *omi* — soul, central concept in Evenki belief
- *Tungus* — former external name for the Evenki people
- *taiga* — boreal forest region of Siberia where Evenki live
- *reindeer saddle* — Evenki-specific saddle set on animal's shoulders
- *pike* — large knife on a long handle, used as axe or spear
- *Abagaldi* — bear spirit in shamanic tradition
- *animistic belief* — belief in animation and personification of natural phenomena
- *upper, middle and lower world* — three-level cosmology in Evenki worldview
- *totemistic* — belief systems involving animal and natural spirits
- *shamanism* — religious practice centered on shamans and spirit communication
