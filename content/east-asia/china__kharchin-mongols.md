## Overview

The Kharchin Mongols, also called Kharachin, are a subgroup of the Mongols residing mainly in northwestern Liaoning and Chifeng, Inner Mongolia. Their name is written in Mongolian as ᠬᠠᠷᠠᠴᠢᠨ and Харчин, and in Chinese as 喀喇沁部. They descended from the Three Döyin (Uriyangkhan) Guards of the Ming dynasty and the Yüngshiyebü Tümen established by Dayan Khan. These groups merged around 1600 and were organized into three banners of the Josotu League by the Manchus in 1626. The Kharchin have an agricultural lifestyle and live interspersed among the Han Chinese. They are almost completely Chinese-speaking.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe historical political and administrative changes. The Three Döyin Guards were established by the Ming in 1389, were resettled much closer to the Ming border after 1448, and the Kharchins submitted to the Qing dynasty in 1626. These are historical events rather than documented ritual practices.

## Foodways

 The historical account explains that the Kipchaks and Qanqlis were famous for distilling *khara-airag*, described as black kumis, and that they were called Kharachin by the Mongols. The source presents this as an explanation of the name’s history rather than as a documented Kharchin food tradition.
