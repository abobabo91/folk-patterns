## Overview

The Xibe, also called Sibe, are a Tungusic-speaking ethnic group living mainly in Xinjiang, Jilin, and Shenyang in Liaoning. China officially recognizes them as one of its 56 ethnic groups. The 2020 Chinese Census recorded 191,911 Sibe people. In Xinjiang, approximately 43,000 Sibe people live in the autonomous region, including approximately 34,000 in the Ili Kazakh Autonomous Prefecture, where the Qapqal Xibe Autonomous County is located. The Sibe are associated with a Southern Tungusic language, a distinctive written tradition derived from the Manchu alphabet, historical movement through Northeast China and Dzungaria, and cultural practices shaped by shamanism, Buddhism, Chinese, Russian, Uyghur, and Kazakh influences.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Customary Sibe clothing included short buttoned jackets and trousers for men, and close-fitting, long, lace-trimmed gowns for women. The sources state that almost all Sibe people now wear modern clothing, while traditional clothing is worn by elders during festivals.

### Architecture

 They state only that, until modern times, Sibe dwellings housed up to three generations of the same family, because a son was believed not to be able to leave the family clan while his father was alive.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They mention that the Sibe cultivated vegetables, tobacco, and poppies in Ili.

## Music & performance

 They state that the different ethnicities of Northern Xinjiang have shared musical culture and adopted elements from one another’s music. The sources also record that Sibe-language radio broadcasting in Qapqal was allocated 15 minutes per day, while television programmes lasted 15 to 30 minutes and were broadcast once or twice per month. These records concern broadcasting and shared musical culture.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that traditional clothing is worn by elders during festivals. Arranged marriage was historically common, and women in some Northeast China Sibe communities were not allowed to live with men before formal marriage. During this period, the man had to formally propose and express his intention to marry through specific acts and ceremonies. The woman’s acceptance and finalization of the marriage preceded her move into the man’s home.

## Foodways

 They state that Sibe communities in Ili cultivated vegetables, tobacco, and poppies. During the crisis surrounding the Dungan Revolt and the Russian occupation of Ili, the sources report that provisions were exhausted and that horses, dogs, and cats became the only available food. This historical account does not provide a description of ordinary Xibe cuisine.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They state that the People’s Publishing House in Ürümqi published over 285 significant works in Sibe between 1954 and 1959, including government documents, belles-lettres, and schoolbooks. Since 1946, the Sibe-language *Qapqal News* has been published in Yining.

## Language & religion

Sibe is a Southern Tungusic language spoken by members of the Sibe minority in Xinjiang, in Northwest China, with over 30 thousand speakers. It is conventionally treated as a separate language within the southern group of Tungusic languages, although some researchers regard it as a dialect of Manchu and others as the successor to Manchu. Sibe is mutually intelligible with Manchu and has seven case morphemes. Its written form is a derivative of the Manchu alphabet. The language contains influences from Mandarin Chinese, Russian, Uyghur, and Kazakh. In Northeast China, Sibe people speak Mandarin Chinese as their first language. Historical Sibe religions included shamanism and Buddhism.
