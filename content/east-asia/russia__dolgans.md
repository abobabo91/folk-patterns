## Overview

The Dolgans (Russian: Долганы; Dolgan: долган, дулҕан, Һака; Yakut: тыа-киһи) are a Turkic ethnic group who mostly inhabit Krasnoyarsk Krai, Russia. The 2010 Census counted 7,885 Dolgans, including 5,517 in Taymyrsky Dolgano-Nenetsky District. In the 17th century they lived in the basins of the Olenyok River and Lena River, and in the 18th century they moved to their current location, Taymyr. Their identity emerged during the 19th and early 20th centuries under the influence of Evenks, Yakuts, Enets, and ethnic Russian tundra peasants. Their documented folk-culture history is especially connected with hunting, reindeer herding, fishing, and collective-farm life.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state that most Dolgans practice old shamanistic beliefs, while most are influenced by Eastern Orthodox Christianity.

## Foodways

During the Soviet era, Dolgans were required to form kolkhozy, or state “collective farms,” which engaged in reindeer breeding, fishing, dairy farming, and market gardening in addition to their traditional activities.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources identify Ogdo Aksyonova as a poet and the founder of Dolgan literature.

## Language & religion

Dolgans speak the Dolgan language, which is closely related to the Yakut language. The sources give Dolgan forms including долган, дулҕан, and Һака, and Yakut тыа-киһи. Most Dolgans practice old shamanistic beliefs, although most are influenced by Eastern Orthodox Christianity.
