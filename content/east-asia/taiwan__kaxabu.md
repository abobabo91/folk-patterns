## Overview

The Kaxabu are a variant of the Pazeh/Kaxabu ethno-linguistic group among the Taiwanese Aborigines. They are associated with Puli Township in Nantou County, where twelve speakers of Kaxabu remain. Kaxabu is described as a variety of Southern Min with unique features for Austronesian languages, although it was once listed as a separate language and is now considered a dialect. Kaxabu and Pazeh are also described as dialects of a Formosan language belonging to the Austronesian language family. The sources provide linguistic information and details about language-revival efforts.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do document language-preservation and revival efforts. Before her death, the last remaining native speaker of the Pazeh dialect, Pan Jin-yu, offered Pazeh classes to about 200 regular students in Puli and a small number of students in Miaoli and Taichung. The sources state that efforts to revive the language continued after her death.

## Language & religion

Kaxabu is associated with Pazeh as part of the Pazeh/Kaxabu ethno-linguistic group. Pazeh and Kaxabu are described as dialects of a Formosan language in the Austronesian language family. The Kaxabu people article describes Kaxabu as a variety of Southern Min with distinctive features for Austronesian languages; it was listed as a separate language but is now considered a dialect.

The Kaxabu language source states that twelve Kaxabu speakers remain in Puli Township, Nantou County. It also records that the last remaining native speaker of the Pazeh dialect, Pan Jin-yu, died in 2010 at the age of 96. The sources describe prejudice faced by the Pazeh and other indigenous groups of Taiwan, as well as the displacement of Pazeh by Hoklo Taiwanese.
