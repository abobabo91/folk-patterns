## Overview

The Nanai are a Tungusic people living along the Amur and Ussuri rivers in Russia and China, descended from the Wild Jurchens of northern Manchuria. They are renowned for clothing made from tanned fish skin, earning them the name "Fish-skin Tartars". Their culture centers on shamanic practice, an intricate funerary cycle involving wooden soul figures, and a river-based economy centered on fishing.

## Material culture

Nanai material culture is built from the river: fish skin for garments, birch bark for boxes, wood and leather for tools and ritual objects, with appliqué and embroidery as the main decorative arts.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Nanai made clothing and decorated objects from fish skin, dried and softened by mallet-striking, with cut-and-stitched appliqué and embroidery in scrolls and geometric patterns.

- **Fish-skin garment work** — Skins were dried, struck repeatedly with a mallet until smooth, then sewn together, with fish weighing more than 50 kilograms selected for the purpose.
- **Fish-skin appliqué** — White fish skin was cut and stitched onto dyed fish-skin ground in large circular scroll and smaller motifs, as on leggings with blue dye and darker lower thirds.
- **Cloth appliqué on leather and fur** — Square cloth appliqués with scroll patterning in cream linen against indigo ground were stitched onto leather and fur quivers, outlined in green, yellow and blue yarns.
- **Embroidered collars** — Dark blue cloth collars carried scroll patterning in buttonhole and chain stitch, with threaded glass beads and suspended pendants along the hem.

### Clothing & dress

Everyday dress was fish skin and fur, layered and tapered, with decorated collars, leggings and cloth boots; shamanic dress was separate and more elaborate.

- **Woman's full fish-skin costume** — A complete outfit made from fish skin, with examples recorded from the early 20th century showing the full range of garment construction and appliqué decoration.
- **Splayed arc-shaped collar** — A decorative men's collar with beading and suspended stone and pearl-like pendants, marking status and formal wear.
- **Cloth boots** — Boots with cream tabby cotton lower leg and sole, quilted in self-colour thread, with pale blue band at mid-boot and yellow cloth carrying dark blue and cream curved appliqué.
- **Shaman's costume** — A skirt and jacket with leather belt hung with conical metal pendants, mittens figured with serpents and lizards, and a hat with branching horns or fur.

### Architecture

The Nanai lived in villages along river banks, with three ethnic villages remaining in Russia, but sources provide no details about house form or construction.

- **Nanai villages** — Three ethnic Nanai villages remain in Russia: Dzhuen, Ulika and Dada, settled on river banks.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Household and hunting equipment was made from natural materials—bark, skin, wood and leather—rather than clay or cast metal.

- **Birch-bark boxes** — Lidded containers made from birch bark, with separate covers, used for storage.
- **Fish-skin pouches** — Sewn containers made from fish skin, serving both practical and symbolic functions.
- **Leather and fur quiver** — Long rectangular hunting quiver with top flap and side ties, made in six horizontally stitched sections, decorated with cloth and fur appliqué.
- **Conical metal pendants** — Worked metal ornaments hung from shamanic leather belts, among the few metal objects documented in collections.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Adornment appeared primarily as part of garment construction rather than separate ornament, using glass beads, semi-precious stones and shamanic metal pieces.

- **Beaded collar hems** — Cream, light blue and dark blue glass beads threaded in diamond patterns along garment hems, often with suspended pendants of semi-precious stones and pearl-like beads.
- **Coal body marks for funerary rites** — Coal markings drawn on an infant's body during funerary preparation, such as bracelets around the wrist, to identify the soul if it returned in a later child.
- **Shamanic mirror insets** — Fragments of Chinese mirrors worked into shamanic costume as spiritual protection.

## Music & performance

Nanai performance included shamanic ritual voice for expelling spirits and guiding souls, and 20th-century professional singers.

- **Kasa tavori incantations** (*kasa tavori*) — Shamanic ritual voices recorded during the three-day funerary rite, with the shaman's voice carrying souls of the dead toward the underworld.
- **Kola Beldy** — The best-known Nanai singer (1929–1993), popular across the Soviet Union and identified with his rendition of "I will take you to the tundra".
- **Yimakan oral literature** (*Yimakan*) — A body of oral literature of the Chinese Hezhen people, belonging as much to performance as to written tradition.

## Dance & theatre

No Nanai dance or dramatic tradition is documented in sources; the only staged material mentioned is the dog-sledge procession of soul figures in funerary ritual.

## Festivals & rituals

The Nanai funerary cycle is elaborate and detailed: the soul and spirit part at death, the soul moves through temporary cloth shelter, wooden doll, and finally large wooden figures for the journey to the underworld.

- **Lachako** — A temporary cloth shelter where the soul rests for seven days after death before moving into a wooden figure.
- **Panyo** — A wooden doll into which the soul is placed after leaving the lachako, treated as a living person with pillow, blanket and miniature pipe.
- **Mugdeh** — Large human-shaped wooden figures made to the height of the deceased, loaded onto a dog sledge for the journey to the underworld Buni.
- **Kasa tavori** — The three-day final funerary rite with much feasting, in which souls pass from the panyo into mugdeh figures for the underworld journey.
- **Wind burial** — Infants under one year are placed in birch-bark paper wrapping high in a forest tree, their souls freed to enter another woman as a bird.

## Foodways

The Nanai subsistence was based on river fishing, with sturgeon especially prized, supplemented by dried fish for winter and small amounts of tobacco agriculture.

- **Sturgeon** — The most prized fish species in the Nanai diet, eaten fresh in summer and dried for winter.
- **Fadzya Mama** — The fire personified as an elderly woman, respected as a person—children were not allowed to startle her and men were always courteous in her presence.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Nanai possess cosmological narratives, and written literature is a Soviet and post-Soviet project, while the Chinese Hezhen maintain a rich oral tradition called Yimakan.

- **Nanai cosmology** — The Nanai creation narrative holds that great serpents gouged out the river valleys in a once-flat land, and every thing in the universe holds an independent spirit.
- **Dersu Uzala** — A Nanai guide and friend of Russian explorer Vladimir Arsenyev, the subject of two books later adapted by Akira Kurosawa in his 1975 film.
- **Yimakan** — The rich oral literature of the Chinese Hezhen people, with narrative and performance traditions.

## Language & religion

Nanai is Manchu-Tungusic with about 1,400 speakers; the Nanai are mainly Shamanist, revering the bear and tiger and worshipping spirits of nature.

- **Nanai language** (*Nanai*) — Also called Gold, Goldi or Hezhen; Manchu-Tungusic with dialects including Naykhin, Dzhuen, Bolon and others, taught in 13 schools in Khabarovsk.
- **Doonta** — The bear, a revered animal spirit in Nanai Shamanism.
- **Amba** — The tiger, a revered animal spirit in Nanai Shamanism.
- **Buni** — The underworld to which souls travel in the mugdeh wooden figures, reached via dog sledge.

- *kasa tavori* — three-day final funerary rite with feasting and soul passage
- *Buni* — the underworld destination of Nanai souls

- *fish-skin garments* — clothing made from dried, mallet-softened fish skins
- *appliqué* — cut and stitched decoration sewn onto garment ground
- *Hezhe* — Chinese name for the Nanai ethnic group
- *Manchu-Tungusic* — language family to which Nanai belongs
- *Wild Jurchens* — ancestral people of the Nanai from northern Manchuria
- *wind burial* — placing an infant's body high in a forest tree for sky burial
