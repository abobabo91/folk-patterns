## Overview

The Bonan are an ethno-linguistic group distinct from other Mongolic peoples who live in Gansu and Qinghai provinces in Northwestern China. They are one of the “titular nationalities” of Gansu’s Jishishan Bonan, Dongxiang and Salar Autonomous County, south of the Yellow River and near the border between Gansu and Qinghai. They are the 10th-smallest, or 47th out of 56, of the ethnic groups officially recognized by the People’s Republic of China. 95% of Bonan live in Jishishan County, numbering 21,400 of the county’s population. Bonan culture combines Mongolic language traditions with Tibetan, Hui, Dongxiang, Han, Monguor and Islamic influences. The group is especially associated with knife production, agricultural and pastoral life, distinctive dress, oral and musical practices, and the coexistence of Muslim Bonans in Gansu with Buddhist Bonan-related communities in Qinghai.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Bonan traditional dress includes elements of Tibetan, Hui and Dongxiang clothing. Married Bonan women wear black veils, while unmarried women wear green veils. Women’s clothing is more colorful and includes trousers with colored cuffs. Bonan men typically wear black or white skullcaps and white or dark blue jackets. Fur-lined jackets are used during the winter.

### Architecture

Bonan traditional villages have houses whose roofs are connected, allowing villagers to walk between houses over the roofs. These traditional houses are rare nowadays.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Bonan are especially known for knife production, and the surrounding areas are rich in copper deposits. Over 620 Bonan knife crafters produce 400,000 knives annually. During the Cultural Revolution, knife-making tools were confiscated, but Bonan continued producing knives in secret, maintaining their skills. In 2006, Bonan knife making was added to the Intangible Cultural Heritage of China list.

## Music & performance

Popular Bonan pastimes include horse-riding, wrestling and archery. Bonan also enjoy poetry, singing, dancing and playing traditional Chinese instruments. They likewise do not describe wedding, funeral, court, religious, or tea-house performance contexts. The sources therefore provide only a general description of singing, dancing and instrumental activity.

## Dance & theatre

The sources state that Bonan enjoy dancing.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe the Bonan as predominantly Muslim and state that a small minority are Tibetan Buddhists residing in Qinghai.

## Foodways

The Bonan in Jishishan County follow a halal diet consisting mainly of beef and mutton, combined with carrots, potatoes and glass noodles. They also enjoy drinking tea. The dietary description identifies halal practice and several principal foods.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that Bonan enjoy poetry and singing.

## Language & religion

Bonan is a Mongolic language spoken by the Bonan people of China. The language is also known as *Baonang* and has the endonym *Manikacha*; it is called *Dorké* in Amdo Tibetan. As of 1985, it was spoken by about 8,000 people, including about 75% of the total Bonan ethnic population and many ethnic Monguor in Gansu and Qinghai provinces. Several dialects exist and are strongly influenced by Chinese and Tibetan. The Tongren dialect is the most commonly studied. Bonan is not typically written by speakers, although there is a folk practice of writing it with the Tibetan syllabary following Amdo pronunciation.

The Bonan language has 11 vowels and 26 consonants, five of which are unstable. The Bonan language of Gansu has undergone Chinese influence, while the Bonan language of Qinghai has been influenced by Tibetan. The Buddhist Monguor of Qinghai speak a slightly different dialect from the Muslim Bonan of Gansu. Bonan has no script in the general description of the people, while the language account notes the folk use of the Tibetan syllabary.

The ancestors of the Bonan were Tibetan Buddhists and lived in Tongren County in the Amdo Region, presently in Qinghai Province, around 1585. Later, some Bonan-speaking community members converted to Islam and moved north to Xunhua County. The Muslim Bonans subsequently moved farther east after the Dungan Rebellion of 1862–1874, into what is now Jishishan Bonan, Dongxiang and Salar Autonomous County of Gansu Province. The Buddhist Bonans who remained in Tongren are officially classified as part of the Monguor, or Tu, ethnic group.
