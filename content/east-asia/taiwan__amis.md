## Overview

The Amis, also known as the Pangcah, are an indigenous Austronesian ethnic group native to Taiwan. Their traditional territory includes the long, narrow Huadong Valley between the Central Mountains and the Coastal Mountains, the Pacific coastal plain east of the Coastal Mountains, and the Hengchun Peninsula. In 2014, they numbered 200,604, approximately 37.1% of Taiwan’s total indigenous population. They are Taiwan’s largest indigenous group and are primarily fishermen because of their coastal location. Their traditional society had a matrilineal kinship system, with inheritance and property passing through the maternal line. The Amis have historically spoken the Amis language, also called *Sowal no ʼAmis* or Pangcah, an Austronesian Formosan language.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources describe traditional Amis clothing.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Amis clothes were made with cloth knitted from bark and banana threads using bamboo needles, together with wear-resistant linens and pelts. During the 20th century, cotton acquired through barter progressively replaced these materials. The sources identify two clothing styles, Hualien and Taitung; both use a head scarf, chest covering, and skirt. Chiefs and priests wear robes and rattan headgear to distinguish themselves from others. Traditional clothing was black, but during the 20th century it increasingly used red because of its perceived festiveness and compatibility with ethno-tourism. The coloring of necklaces distinguishes different Amis communities. Floral headdresses are often used on special occasions, including the harvest. An *alufo*, or “lover’s bag,” is made with betel nut by a woman’s mother or lover and is used in everyday life and rituals to carry betel nut, limestone, betel, and smoking pipes.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention necklaces whose coloring distinguishes different Amis communities, as well as floral headdresses used during special occasions such as the harvest.

## Music & performance

The Amis use the *Kokang*, a traditional xylophone and percussion instrument. It was initially made to drive animals away from residential areas. Kokang is made of softwood aged for about three months so that its sound becomes clear; some versions are made of bamboo and are called *Kakeng*. Kokang and Kakeng are called “Tatuk, Dodang” in neighboring tribes such as “Truku,” “Chimei,” and “Atayal.” The Amis Kakeng Musical Group mainly uses bamboo Kakeng together with several types of Amis Kokang in performances. Ensembles are presented as group performances and sometimes as solo percussion performances. Kokang performances have also been held as cultural events for Taiwanese people.

Amis singing is known for complex contrapuntal polyphony. An Amis chant was used in Enigma’s song “Return to Innocence,” on the album *The Cross of Changes* (1993), and the song became the theme song of the 1996 Atlanta Olympics. The chorus was sung by Difang and Igay Duana, who belonged to a Taiwanese aboriginal cultural performance group. The recording was previously made by Maison des Cultures du Monde while the group was on tour and released on a CD. The recording studio and the Taiwanese group later filed a copyright-infringement suit, which was settled out of court.

## Festivals & rituals

The most important traditional ceremony identified in the sources is the Amis Harvest Festival. It is held every July to September to express thanks and appreciation to the gods and to pray for the harvest in the following year. Floral headdresses are often worn during special occasions such as the harvest. Family affairs, including family finances, are decided by the female householder in the Amis tradition.

## Foodways

The sources identify the Amis as primarily fishermen because of their coastal location. Betel nut, limestone, betel, and smoking pipes are mentioned as items carried in the *alufo*, rather than as part of a documented food system.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They record that the Amis language has a translated version of Article 1 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and mention contemporary musical representation through the chant used in “Return to Innocence.”

## Language & religion

Amis, also called *Sowal no ʼAmis* or Pangcah, is an Austronesian Formosan language spoken along Taiwan’s east coast, between Hualien in the north and Taitung in the south, with another population of speakers in the Hengchun Peninsula. It is the largest of the Formosan languages. The language has five dialects: Southern Amis, Tavalong-Vataan, Central Amis, Chengkung-Kwangshan, and Northern Amis, which includes Nanshi Amis and Nataoran. Sakizaya is described as a moribund language spoken among the northernmost ethnic Amis and is not mutually intelligible with Northern Amis. The sources mention gods in connection with the Harvest Festival.
