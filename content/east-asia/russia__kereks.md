## Overview

The Kereks are an ethnic group of people in Russia. Their autonym, *aӈӄaлҕaкку* (*angqalghakku*), means “seaside people”; the Russian name is Кереки, romanized as Kereky. In the 2021 census, 23 people registered as ethnic Kereks in Russia; the comparable figures were 4 in 2010, 8 in 2002, and 102 in 1897. During the twentieth century, Kereks were almost completely assimilated into the Chukchi people. Historically, they were settled people who fished and hunted wild deer and mountain sheep, while southern Kereks also practiced small-scale reindeer herding.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that shamanism and animism were strong among the Kereks and that the Kereks never converted to Christianity.

## Foodways

Historically, Kereks engaged in fishing and hunting wild deer and mountain sheep. Southern Kereks also practiced small-scale reindeer herding, kept sled dogs, and collected fur from marine mammals.

## Language & religion

The traditional Kerek language is no longer spoken. Kerek descendants speak Chukchi and Russian. The Kerek language belongs to the Chukchi–Kamchatka family and is included in Paleoasiatic languages; it is close to the Koryak language and is often considered a dialect of it.
