## Overview

The Chelkans, whose native names are *Chalkandu* and *Shalkandu*, are a small group of Turkic Indigenous people of Siberia. They live in the Altai Republic and Kemerovo Oblast, where they have sometimes been grouped administratively or ethnographically with the Altai ethnic group and the Shors. They are recognized as a separate ethnic group within the list of Indigenous small-numbered peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East by ethnographers, the Resolution of the Government of the Russian Federation No. 255 dated March 24, 2000, and the Russian Census of 2002. According to the 2010 census, there were 1,181 Chelkans in Russia.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Chelkan dress included short breeches, linen shirts, and single-breasted robes. The sources do not distinguish between men’s and women’s clothing, everyday and ceremonial dress, or provide information about head coverings, belts, footwear, or other forms of adornment.

### Architecture

Chelkan traditional dwellings included polygonal yurts made of bark or logs and topped with a conic bark roof. Other dwellings included conic yurts made of bark or perches.

## Foodways

The Chelkans originally mainly hunted local animals, which were vital to their subsistence lifestyle. Around the 19th century, they took up cedar nut picking as an additional economic activity.

## Language & religion

The Chelkans speak the Northern Altai Chelkan language. It is sometimes classified as a dialect of Northern Altai. The language was counted as separate in the 2002 Russian Census but not in the 2010 census, which counted 2,000 speakers. A writing system was developed in 2014.

Most modern Chelkans are Orthodox Christian. Burkhanism and shamanism are also found among the Chelkans.
