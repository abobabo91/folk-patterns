## Overview

The Enets (Russian: энцы, *entsy*; singular: энец, *enets*) are a Samoyedic ethnic group living on the east bank, near the mouth, of the Yenisei River. Historically, they were nomadic people. As of 2002, most Enets lived in Potapovo, in Krasnoyarsk Krai in western Siberia near the Arctic Circle. According to the 2010 Census, there were 227 Enets in Russia. The Enets language is a Samoyedic language of Northern Siberia, spoken on the Lower Yenisei within the Taimyr Municipality District. The sources describe the group primarily through its population, location, historical nomadism, and language, and do not provide further ethnographic information about material or intangible culture.

## Oral tradition & literature

 The Enets language article does state that a written form of the language was created during the 1980s, that it was used to produce a number of books, and that a local newspaper with an insert in local languages appeared during the 1990s.

## Language & religion

Enets is a Samoyedic language belonging to the Northern branch of the Samoyedic languages, which is itself a branch of the Uralic language family. It is spoken on the Lower Yenisei within the Taimyr Municipality District, a subdivision of Krasnoyarsk Krai, Russian Federation. There are two distinct dialects: Forest Enets (*Bai*) and Tundra Enets (*Madu* or *Somatu*). In 2010, about 40 people claimed to be native Enets speakers; in 2020, 69 people claimed to speak Enets natively and 97 people claimed to know Enets in total. The older generation still speaks the language, while education is in Russian and very little Enets is taught. Enets is written in the Cyrillic alphabet with additional letters not used in the Russian alphabet. The written form was created during the 1980s; in 2019 the alphabet was reformed, and in April 2020 an Enets primer was published in a new version of the alphabet.
