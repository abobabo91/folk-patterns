## Overview

The Saisiyat are one of Taiwan's smallest indigenous groups, making up about 1.3% of the total indigenous population. They live in two branches: the Northern Branch in the Wufeng area of Hsinchu and the Southern Branch in the Nanzhuang and Shitan highlands of Miaoli. They are known above all for the Pasta'ay festival and their weaving, basketry and bead and shell ornament. Their language, Saisiyat, is endangered despite having roughly 4,750 speakers.

## Material culture

Saisiyat material culture is best known through textiles, rattan work and extensive bead, shell and seed ornament held in museum collections.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Saisiyat wove ramie combined with wool into upper garments in two forms: a long jacket and a sleeveless jacket.

- **Woven ramie-and-wool garments** — Upper garments worked in white, red and black, combining a bast fibre with wool in a single woven cloth.
- **Coiled rattan basketry** — Rattan is coiled into closed, deep forms including jar-shaped baskets and basket-worked rattan vessels.

### Clothing & dress

The documented wardrobe consists of woven ramie-and-wool upper garments in two cuts and a brimless rattan cap, with body adornment carrying much of the visual weight of dress.

- **Long jacket** — An upper long garment woven from ramie and wool.
- **Sleeveless jacket** — A sleeveless jacket worked in white, red and black ramie-and-wool weave.
- **Brimless rattan cap** — Headgear made from coiled rattan.

### Architecture

No information is available about Saisiyat house forms, building materials, roofing or settlement layout.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The everyday objects documented are made of rattan: coiled baskets and basket-worked vessels, with metal appearing only as a component of ornament.

- **Jar-shaped basket** — A coiled rattan container.
- **Basket-worked rattan vessels** — Rattan vessels made by basket-working techniques.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ornament is the best-documented part of Saisiyat material culture, built from glass beads, shell and plant seeds, with bone and brass as occasional additions.

- **Ear ornaments and ear pendants** — Made of shell alone, or of shell combined with glass beads, catalogued in pairs.
- **Necklaces and necklets** — Strung from plant seeds and string, or made of black glass beads and brass, or plain glass beads.
- **Bracelets** — Worn in pairs, combining glass beads, shell beads and bone in a single piece.

- **Ritual chanting** — The god Otspoehobong chanted an incantation over pounded flesh in the flood account.

## Festivals & rituals

The Pasta'ay festival, held every two years, is the single named element of Saisiyat ceremonial life in documented sources.

- **Pasta'ay** (*pas-ta'ai*) — A biennial festival with rituals that the Saisiyat have deliberately worked to keep alive.
- **Museum of Saisiyat Folklore** — The institutional point of reference for the group's ceremonial and material culture.

## Foodways

No information is available on Saisiyat staples, cooking, dishes, ceremonial food or drink.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Saisiyat flood account is the one narrative transmitted in full, deriving the Saisiyat and their neighbours from one body.

- **Saisiyat flood account** — A creation narrative where the god Otspoehobong creates the Saisiyat from pounded human flesh, the Taiwanese from intestines, and the Atayal from bones.
- **Airubia Mountain** — The place where a man seated on a weaving loom floated to after the flood.
- **Siasiat** — The ancestor name given to the first humans created by the god from pounded flesh.

## Language & religion

Saisiyat is a Formosan language of the Austronesian family with roughly 4,750 speakers, endangered despite its comparatively large speaker count.

- **Ta'ai dialect** (*Ta'ai*) — The northern Saisiyat dialect spoken in the Wufeng area.
- **Tungho dialect** (*Tungho*) — The southern Saisiyat dialect spoken in Nanzhuang and Shitan.
- **Otspoehobong** — The creator god of the flood account, also given as Oopenhaboon.

## Glossary

- *Pasta'ay* — biennial Saisiyat festival with ritual practices
- *pas-ta'ai* — alternative name for Pasta'ay festival and its rituals
- *Ta'ai* — northern Saisiyat dialect spoken in Wufeng
- *Tungho* — southern Saisiyat dialect spoken in Nanzhuang and Shitan
- *Otspoehobong* — creator god in Saisiyat flood narrative
- *Oopenhaboon* — alternative name for Otspoehobong, the creator god
- *Siasiat* — ancestor name given to the first Saisiyat humans
- *Airubia Mountain* — place where survivor of the flood landed on a weaving loom
- *ramie* — bast fibre used in Saisiyat weaving
- *Formosan language* — language family of Austronesian origin spoken in Taiwan
- *Wufeng* — mountainous area in Hsinchu where Northern Branch Saisiyat live
- *Nanzhuang* — highland area in Miaoli where Southern Branch Saisiyat live
- *Shitan* — highland area in Miaoli where Southern Branch Saisiyat live
- *Northern Branch* — Saisiyat group living in Wufeng, Hsinchu
- *Southern Branch* — Saisiyat group living in Nanzhuang and Shitan, Miaoli
