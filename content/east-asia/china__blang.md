## Overview

The Blang people, also known in China as Bulang and in Thailand as Plang, are an ethnic group officially recognized as one of the 56 ethnic groups of the People’s Republic of China. They live mainly in southern China, especially in Yunnan province, and also in parts of Myanmar and Thailand. Their settlements include the Lancang river valley, Xishuangbanna, Jinghong City, and numerous townships and villages in counties including Menghai, Shuangjiang, Yongde, Yun, Gengma, Lincang, Zhenkang, Fengqing, Shidian, Changning, Lancang, Mojiang, Jinggu, Jingdong, and Simao. There are estimated 1,200 Blang in Thailand, mostly in Chiang Rai province. The Blang speak Palaungic languages, including Blang and U, and their culture combines tea cultivation, bamboo architecture, clan organization, animism, ancestor worship, and Theravada Buddhism.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Blang women usually wear jackets with black skirts. Men wear wide black trousers and jackets buttoned at the front. Men are also described as having tattoos on the torso and stomach. Turbans made from white or black fabric are often worn.

### Architecture

Blang houses are made of bamboo and usually have two floors. The first floor serves as a warehouse for food and as a stable for livestock such as chickens. The second floor houses the family. A chimney is located in the center of the house.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do state that the first floor of the Blang house is used to store food and keep livestock such as chickens.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources describe blackened teeth produced by chewing betel nuts as a traditional beauty characteristic. They also document tattoos on men’s torsos and stomachs.

## Music & performance

The Blang language has been represented in recordings: recordings of “Pang” are available in the Xuan Guan Collection of the Kaipuleohone archive, and Samtao is represented in the RWAAI Digital Archive. These references concern language documentation rather than a described musical tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe Blang religious life as traditionally associated with animism, ancestor worship, and Theravada Buddhism. Local beliefs and customs relate to traditional ecology, with special attention to rice, water, bees, beeswax, and local spirits associated with them. Blang clans traditionally own their own land, and every Blang town has cemeteries divided by clans. Deceased people are buried, except those who died from unnatural causes, who are cremated.

## Foodways

The sources identify rice as an important element of Blang religious and ecological traditions and describe the storage of food on the first floor of the house. They also state that the Blang were among the earliest known cultivators of tea. Their natural tea forest canopy is described as supporting unique species and ecosystems, in contrast to monoculture tea plantations that consume fertilizer and pesticides. Betel nuts are chewed to blacken the teeth, traditionally regarded as a beauty characteristic.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do identify two writing systems based on the Latin alphabet: “Totham” in Xishuangbanna and “Tolek” from Dehong and Lincang.

## Language & religion

Blang is a Waic language cluster spoken by the Blang people of China and Myanmar. The Blang language belongs to the Palaungic branch of the Austroasiatic language family and is placed in the Waic subgroup, alongside the languages of the Wa and Lawa peoples. People classified as Bulang in China speak various Palaungic languages, including Blang and U. Samtao of Myanmar is described as a dialect of Blang. In Yunnan, named dialects include Bulang, represented by Xinman’e in Bulangshan District of Menghai County, and A’erwa, also called Awa, represented by Guanshuang in Mengman Township of Menghai County. Blang has two tones, high and low. Some Blang also speak Chinese and Southwestern Tai languages. Writing systems based on the Latin alphabet include Totham and Tolek.

Blang religious life is associated with animism, ancestor worship, and Theravada Buddhism. Local beliefs and customs are connected with rice, water, bees, beeswax, and local spirits. A Christian missionary source describes 80% of the Bulang as professing Buddhists and 35% as practicing Buddhists. A Christian Plang community in Thailand founded a church building in Nakhon Pathom province in 2008.
