## Overview

The Yi are the sixth largest ethnic minority in China, with nearly 10 million people living mainly in mountainous regions of Yunnan, Sichuan, Guizhou and Guangxi. They speak Loloish languages and have used their own script for centuries, preserved by bimo priests. Their culture centers on patrilineal descent, a four-caste system, and rich oral traditions including the hero Zhyge Alu. Today many Yi practice Bimoism alongside Buddhism and Christianity.

## Material culture

Yi material culture is known through museum collections of textiles and ritual objects, with limited documentation of craft traditions.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Yi textile objects in museum collections come from the Stone Forest in Yunnan, acquired in 1949.

- **Shoulder bag** (*reistas*) — A travel bag collected from the Stone Forest, also called a shoulder bag.
- **Baby carrier** — A cloth baby sling from the Stone Forest collection.

### Clothing & dress

Black is a revered colour in Yi culture, connected to the autonym Ni.

### Architecture

Yi villages sit on mountain slopes with separate settlements for aristocratic and commoner castes.

- **Old Yi stone tablets and steles** — Stone monuments in Yi areas carrying texts in the pre-standardized script.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Yi ritual and everyday objects are documented through museum pieces rather than craft descriptions.

- **Family tablet** — A ritual object in the British Museum made of straw, wood, cotton cloth, bamboo and paper.

### Jewelry & body adornment

No documentation exists in the sources for Yi jewelry, silverwork, tattooing or hair practice.

## Music & performance

The Yi play wind, string and small plucked instruments, with ritual texts transmitted by bimo priests.

- **Bawu** (*巴乌*) — A wind instrument played by the Yi.
- **Mabu** (*马布*) — A wind instrument played by the Yi.
- **Hulu sheng** — A gourd pipe played by the Yi, but unlike other Yunnan minorities, not for courtship songs.
- **Kouxian** — A small four-pronged instrument comparable to a Jew's harp, most common among Liangshan Yi, with improvised pieces reflecting the player's mood or environment.

## Dance & theatre

Dance is the most widely recognized form of Yi musical performance.

## Festivals & rituals

The Torch Festival on the 24th day of the sixth lunar month is a main Yi holiday celebrating a hero who saved crops from locusts.

- **Torch Festival** — A major holiday held on the 24th day of the sixth lunar month, celebrating Atilaba who used pine torches to burn locusts and save the crops.
- **Kiemobbur** — Sacred sticks erected by the bimo at ritual sites to fight the ghost Jiegujienuo.

## Foodways

Yi women drank and smoked noticeably more than Han Chinese women, and farming was done by the White Yi and slaves.

## Oral tradition & literature

Yi origin myths tell of life emerging from water and snowmelt, with the hero Zhyge Alu central to their mythology.

- **Ni** — A creature formed from dripping snowmelt that gave birth to all life and another name for the Yi themselves.
- **Apu Dumu** (*ꀉꁌꅋꃅ / ꀉꁌꐧꃅ*) — The common ancestor whose three wives bore six sons who founded the Wu, Zha, Nuo, Heng, Bu and Mo clans.
- **Zhyge Alu** — A great hero, son of a dragon and an eagle, with supernatural strength against magic and ghosts, who rode a nine-winged flying horse and shot down suns to save the people.
- **Shuotnyie Voplie** — A magical peacock that helped Zhyge Alu, whose cry deafened those who heard it but consumed evil and expelled leprosy.
- **Bbahxa Ayuosse** — A python defeated by Zhyge Alu that could detect leprosy, cure tuberculosis and eradicate epidemics.

## Language & religion

The Yi speak six mutually unintelligible Loloish languages, with Northern Yi as the literary standard, and practice Bimoism, Buddhism or Christianity.

- **Bimoism** — The indigenous Yi religion served by the bimo priests.
- **Yi script** — A logosyllabic script dating to at least the 13th century, standardized into a syllabary in the 1970s and 1980s for use in books, newspapers and schooling.
- **Bimo** — Priests who preserved and transmitted the Yi script and ritual texts across generations.
- **Northern Yi** (*Nuosu*) — The literary standard Yi language with some two million speakers.

## Glossary

- *Nuosu* — The Yi's name for themselves
- *bimo* — Priests who preserve and transmit Yi ritual texts and knowledge
- *Bimoism* — The indigenous Yi religion
- *Jiegujienuo* — A ghost blamed for dizziness, slowness, dementia and anxiety
- *kiemobbur* — Sacred sticks erected by bimo at ritual sites
- *bawu* — A Yi wind instrument
- *mabu* — A Yi wind instrument
- *hulu sheng* — A gourd pipe played by Yi but not for courtship
- *kouxian* — A small four-pronged instrument like a Jew's harp
- *Zhyge Alu* — A great hero in Yi mythology with supernatural powers
- *Apu Dumu* — The common ancestor of the Yi
- *Loloish* — The Tibeto-Burman language family spoken by the Yi
- *Ni* — A creature from snowmelt that gave birth to all life; another name for the Yi
- *Torch Festival* — A main Yi holiday on the 24th day of the sixth lunar month
