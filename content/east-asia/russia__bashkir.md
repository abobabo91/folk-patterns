## Overview

The Bashkirs are a Turkic people of the Ural steppe whose ancestors were half-nomadic herders and beekeepers. Their culture preserves ancient Turkic epic poetry and material traditions tied to mounted life and dairy production. Most are now settled Sunni Muslims living in Bashkortostan, though older Tengrist beliefs survive in their epics. Their museums hold archery gear, felt and linen dress, and carved wooden vessels.

## Material culture

Bashkir material culture centers on objects for herding life, archery, dairy work, and beekeeping, made chiefly from wood, bone, leather, horn and felt.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Bashkir textiles use cream and red linen or felt with simple leaf motifs and red cotton trim.

- **Embroidered linen shirt** — A man's shirt of cream tabby linen with an A-line body, red linen gussets, and a neckline trimmed with applied red cotton and glass beads.
- **Felted men's headwear** — A domed cream-grey felt hat with side points, hand-stitched and bound with dark red cotton.

### Clothing & dress

Men's dress reflects mounted herding life, with linen shirts, felt hats, leather and horse-skin boots, and a riding whip.

- **Leather and horse-skin boots** — Footwear made from leather and horse skin, indicating equipment for mounted herding.
- **Riding whip** — Part of the equestrian kit of the half-nomadic Bashkirs.

### Architecture

Little is recorded.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Bashkir everyday objects are chiefly wood, bone, leather and horn, shaped for dairy work, honey collection, and archery.

- **Chelak** — A wooden honey barrel used for storage.
- **Bow and arrow set** — Archery equipment made of wood and horn, with a leather bow case, leather quiver, arrows, and a bone-bladed knife.
- **Bee-collecting ladle** — A specialized wooden tool for collecting wild honey in the Burzyansky District.
- **Holiday scoop** — A wooden scoop fitted with a chain, held in several museum examples.
- **Wooden basins for kumis** (*kumis*) — Turned and carved wooden vessels for dairy work.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The only documented body adornment is beadwork on men's shirts.

- **Glass beads on shirt neckline** — White and green glass beads grouped in threes along the red trim of the neckline, with one group of yellow beads.

## Music & performance

The quray, an end-blown reed flute from the Ural Mountains, is the central Bashkir instrument and most important in the ensemble.

- **Quray** — An end-blown flute made from a reed found only in the Ural Mountains, played in traditional and modern music.
- **National Symphonic Orchestra of the Bashkortostan Republic** — Founded in 1992 under Tahir Kamalov, one of the major symphonies of late twentieth-century Russia.

## Dance & theatre

Bashkir dance was professionalized through a folk dance ensemble founded in 1939 by G. Gaskarav.

- **Professional folk dance ensemble** — Founded in 1939 by G. Gaskarav as the first artistic leader of Bashkir folk theatrical choreography, it has toured widely.

## Festivals & rituals

Bashkir ritual life centers on work gatherings and weddings with strict rules for food, hospitality, and handwork.

- **Kaz-oemahe** — A work-and-feast gathering where the hostess invites neighbors to help process geese, then treats everyone to fresh goose.
- **Chak-chak** — A wedding dish prepared by the bride, who places a piece in the mouth of every guest as an obligatory part of the wedding rite.
- **Keyeu-bilmene** — Small dumplings made for the wedding in honor of the groom.

## Foodways

Bashkir cooking is built on herded meat and dairy, with spare spicing and foods that keep through the semi-nomadic year.

- **Qazi** (*Qazı*) — A horse sausage, eaten in thick slices with fat and washed down with qurut.
- **Beshbarmaq** — The signature dish of boiled meat with salma or halma, coarsely chopped noodles, heaped with herbs and onions and seasoned with qurut or sour cream.
- **Qimiz** (*Qımıð*) — Fermented mare's milk, the national healing drink, made from the milk of the Bashkir horse and said to have been made on the move in a vessel hung from the saddle.
- **Qurut** — A young dry cheese or sour fermented-milk used to cut the richness of horse meat.
- **Bashkir honey** — From the Burzyan bee, a point of family honor and served at every tea party.

## Oral tradition & literature

Bashkir epics preserve ancient Turkic mythology in tales of heroes and a three-tiered cosmos of sky, earth, and underworld.

- **Ural Batyr** — The central epic, telling of the hero Ural who destroys the deva and obtains the living water, and gives the Ural Mountains their name.
- **Aqbuzat** — An epic central to Bashkir identity.
- **Shezhere** — Bashkir family trees that preserve genealogical tradition and material about the sixteenth century.
- **Umay** — The Tengrist fertility goddess who granted land to the Bashkirs, appearing in epics as a swan or beautiful girl.

## Language & religion

Bashkir is a Kipchak Turkic language with roughly 1.6 million native speakers, co-official with Russian in Bashkortostan.

- **Bashkir language** (*Bashkir*) — A Kipchak Turkic language with three dialect groups (Southern, Eastern, North-Western), agglutinative and SOV word order, distinguished by dental fricatives and labial vowel harmony.
- **Sunni Islam of the Hanafi madhhab** — The predominant religion of most Bashkirs, following the Jadid doctrine.
- **Tengrist beliefs** (*Tengrist*) — The older pre-Islamic layer of Bashkir belief in a sky god and twelve powers of nature, surviving in epic tradition.

## Glossary

- *Bashkir* — the Turkic people of the Urals
- *Kipchak* — a branch of Turkic languages
- *Tengrist* — pre-Islamic Bashkir belief in sky god and nature powers
- *Umay* — the Tengrist fertility goddess
- *quray* — an end-blown reed flute
- *Qazı* — horse sausage
- *Qımıð* — fermented mare's milk, the national healing drink
- *qurut* — sour fermented-milk or young dry cheese
- *Kaz-oemahe* — a work-and-feast gathering for processing geese
- *chak-chak* — a wedding dish made by the bride
- *keyeu-bilmene* — small dumplings made for a wedding
- *chelak* — a wooden honey barrel
- *salma* — coarsely chopped noodles for beshbarmaq
- *shezhere* — Bashkir family trees recording genealogy
- *Hanafi madhhab* — a school of Islamic law
- *Jadid doctrine* — Islamic modernist teaching followed by Bashkirs
