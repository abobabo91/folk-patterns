## Overview

The Zakhchin are a subgroup of the Oirats residing in Khovd Province, Mongolia. Their name means “Frontiersmen,” referring to their origins in the border garrison of the Dzungar Khanate. The garrison was formed mainly from Torghut, Dorbet Oirat, and Dzungar groups. The Zakhchin originally spoke the Zakhchin dialect of the Oirat language. Pure Oirat is used by elder generations, while younger generations use a dialect under strong Khalkha influence. In 2000, the Zakhchin numbered 29,800. Their documented history includes incorporation into the Qing dynasty in 1754, revolt with Amursana against the Qing, and later administration within the Bogd Khanate of Mongolia. Their named clans and banner divisions provide the available evidence for their social organization in these sources.

## Language & religion

The Zakhchin originally spoke the Zakhchin dialect of the Oirat language. The sources state that pure Oirat is used by elder generations, while younger generations use a dialect strongly influenced by Khalkha.

Historically, the Zakhchin were associated with the Dzungar Khanate, the Qing dynasty, Zasagt Khan aimag, and the Bogd Khanate of Mongolia. After the Qing conquest in 1754, they were controlled by Zasagt Khan aimag’s Tsevdenjav gün and moved to Zereg and Shar Khulsan. During the Bogd Khanate, they were subject to Dörbet Ünen Zorigt Khan aimag.

The sources identify 16 tamga, or seals, and 30 clans among the Zakhchin. Named clans include Donjooniikhon, Damjaaniikhan, Shurdaankhan, Baykhiinkhan, Emchiinkhen, Khereid, Tsagaan Yas, Aatiinkhan, Dumiyenkhen, Burd Tariachin, Adsagiinkhan, Tavagzaaniikhan, Nokhoikhon, Khotonguud, Khurmshtiinkhan, and Mukhlainkhan. These names are the only documented internal cultural vocabulary supplied by the sources.

The Zakhchin banner organization included Bichgiin meeren’s sum, Güüj zan’s sum, Baljinnyam zahiragch sum, Jantsandorj’s sum or Hoit sum, and Guniikhen. Bichgiin meeren’s sum was located in modern Mankhan, Khovd Province; Güüj zan’s sum in modern Altai and part of Must, Khovd Province; Baljinnyam zahiragch sum in modern Zereg and parts of Mankhan; Jantsandorj’s or Hoit sum in modern Mankhan; and Guniikhen in modern Uyench. The south banner was called Goviinkhon, “people of the Gobi,” while the north was called Shiliinkhen, “people of the mountain range.”
