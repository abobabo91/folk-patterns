## Overview

The Derung, also spelt Drung or Dulong, are one of the 56 ethnic groups officially recognized by China. Their population of 7,310 is concentrated in the Nujiang Lisu Autonomous Prefecture of Yunnan, especially in the Derung Valley of Gongshan Derung and Nu Autonomous County. Another 600 live east of the Derung Valley, in the mountains above the Nu River, also called the Salween River, near Binzhongluo. The Derung language belongs to the Sino-Tibetan language family and is closely related to Rawang in Myanmar. Derung folk culture, as described by the sources, includes clan organization, distinctive women’s dress and facial tattoos, wooden houses, animist ritual, animal sacrifice, and an unwritten language formerly recorded through notches on wooden logs.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The typical dress of Derung women consists of a dress made from fabric lined with black and white colors. They state that women formerly tattooed their faces when they reached the age of twelve or thirteen; the tattoos of some women resembled masculine mustaches.

### Architecture

Derung houses are usually constructed from wood and are two stories high. The second floor serves as the family’s living quarters, while the first level functions as a barn and stable. When a male member of the family marries, a new section is added to the family house for him and his new wife.

Before the formation of the People’s Republic of China, Derung society was organized through patriarchal clans. Fifteen clans, called *nile*, existed, and each consisted of diverse familiar communities. Each clan was divided into *ke’eng*, towns in which the Derung lived in common houses. Marriages between clans were prohibited.

### Jewelry & body adornment

 They do describe former facial tattooing among women, beginning when they reached the age of twelve or thirteen. Some women’s tattoos resembled masculine mustaches.

## Music & performance

 They do state that Derung religious life gives an important role to the shaman, who is responsible for rituals. During the **Derung New Year**, diverse animal sacrifices are made as offerings to the sky.

## Festivals & rituals

The **Derung New Year** is celebrated in December according to the lunar calendar. During the celebration, diverse animal sacrifices are made as offerings to the sky.

Derung religious practice is described as animist. Although some Derung have converted to Christianity, the vast majority continue to believe in their animist native religion. There is a belief that all creatures have their own souls, and diverse sacrifices are usually made to calm malignant spirits. The shaman has an important role because shamans are responsible for the rituals.

## Foodways

 The historical account states that the Derung Valley was known by Tibetans as Changyul or Kiongyul, meaning the “valley of beer,” because Derung people enjoy drinking. No further information about beer, its preparation, or its ritual use is supplied.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Derung language is unwritten. In the past, Derung people transmitted messages and made records by making notches on wooden logs.

The sources describe few documents concerning the origins of the Derung. They state that during the period of the Tang dynasty the Derung were under the jurisdiction of Nanzhao and the Dali Kingdom. From the Yuan dynasty to the Qing dynasty, they were governed by local Tibetan or Nakhi rulers and paid yearly tribute to China. The local Lama, called *Changputong*, was responsible for sending the tribute to Weixi.

## Language & religion

Dulong, also called Drung, Derung, Rawang, or Trung, is a Sino-Tibetan language. It belongs to the Nungish language family of the Central Tibeto-Burman branch of the Tibeto-Burman branch of the Sino-Tibetan language family. The other two languages in the same family are Anong and Rawang. Dulong is closely related to the Rawang language of Myanmar.

Dulong has two dialects: Dulong River, also called Central Dulongjiang, Derung River, Northern Dulongjiang, or Southern Dulongjiang; and Nu River, also called Nujiang Dulong. The sources state that there are 14,000 speakers in the two dialect areas: 8,500 in the Nu River dialect and 5,500 in the Dulong River dialect. Locations include Yunnan province, Gongshan Dulong-Nu autonomous county, Xizang Autonomous Region, Chayu county, Chawalong Town, and Bingzhongluo.

The language is unwritten among the Derung. An alphabet based on the Latin script was developed in 1983, but it is not widely used and is not officially recognized. Dulong is also spoken alongside Burmese, Lisu, and Mandarin Chinese by most Derung speakers, except for a few very elderly people.

Although some Derung have converted to Christianity, the vast majority continue to follow their animist native religion. The sources describe belief in individual souls belonging to all creatures, sacrifices intended to calm malignant spirits, and the ritual role of the shaman.
