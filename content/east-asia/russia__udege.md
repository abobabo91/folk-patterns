## Overview

The Udege are a native people of the Primorsky Krai and Khabarovsk Krai regions of Russia. They live along tributaries of the Ussuri, Amur, Khungari, Samarga, and Anyuy Rivers, and the Udege language is spoken on the Khor and Bikin rivers. According to the 2010 census, there were 1,496 Udege in Russia; 620 were registered in Khabarovsky krai and 793 in Primorsky krai. The Udege language belongs to the Tungusic, or Manchu-Tungusic, language family and is critically endangered. Udege religious beliefs include animism, animal worship, and shamanism. Hunting, fishing, and ginseng harvesting are central occupations named by the source.

## Language & religion

Udege, also known as Udihe, Udekhe, and Udeghe, is a critically endangered language of the Tungusic family. It is spoken in the southern part of the Russian Far East, including Khabarovsk Krai, Primorsky krai, the Jewish Autonomous Oblast, and the Khor and Bikin river areas. The sources list territorial dialect groups associated with Kur-Urmi, Samarga, Anyuy, Khungari, Khor, Bikin, Iman, and the sea shore, or Namunka. Northern dialects include Khor, Anyuy, Samarga, and Khungari; southern dialects include Bikin, Iman, and Primorskiy. A Latin-based alphabet was created in 1931. In 1938, the written language was banned by Soviet authorities, and books in Udihe were collected and burned. Udege is currently written in two Cyrillic versions, known as the “Petersburg” and “Khabarovsk” versions. The sources identify animism, animal worship, and shamanism as Udege religious beliefs.
