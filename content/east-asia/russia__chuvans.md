## Overview

The Chuvans are one of the forty or so Indigenous small-numbered peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East recognized by the Russian government. Most currently live in Chukotka Autonomous Okrug, in the far northeast of Russia. Historical accounts describe them as a Yukaghir group that roamed along the upper tributaries of the Anadyr River and Anyuy River in the 17th century. They practiced hunting, fishing and reindeer-breeding. According to the 2002 Russian Census, 1,087 Chuvans lived in Russia. Today, people who self-identify as Chuvans are associated especially with small villages and tundra areas primarily connected with reindeer herding.

## Language & religion

The Chuvan language was a Yukaghir language and became extinct by the early 1900s. Many Chuvans speak Chukchi in addition to Russian, while some Chuvans, including people living in Markovo on the Anadyr River, are unable to speak Chukchi.
