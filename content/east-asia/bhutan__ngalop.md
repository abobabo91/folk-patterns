## Overview

The Ngalop are people of ethnic Tibetan origin who migrated from Tibet to Bhutan as early as the ninth century. They are concentrated in the western and central valleys of Bhutan and are dominant in western and northern Bhutan, including Thimphu and the Dzongkha-speaking region. Their language, Dzongkha, is descended from Old Tibetan and is the national language of Bhutan. The Ngalop introduced Tibetan culture and Buddhism to Bhutan and remain the dominant political and cultural element in modern Bhutan. The term can include several related linguistic and cultural groups, such as the Kheng people and speakers of Bumthang language. The Ngalop population is not separately established in the sources; the 1981 census claimed that Ngalops represented about 17% of the population.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They state that all citizens of Bhutan are required to follow the national dress code, the *driglam namzha*, which is Ngalop in origin.

### Architecture

Ngalop houses are built from timber, stone, clay, and brick. Ngalop people are also known for building large fortress-monasteries known as *dzongs*. These buildings now serve as government offices.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do state that Ngalops largely follow Tibetan Buddhism, particularly the Drukpa Lineage of the Kagyu school of Vajrayana, which is the state religion of Bhutan. A significant number also follow the Nyingma school, which was dominant in early Bhutanese history. Bon practitioners are a minority, while Ngalop practices, like those of most Bhutanese, incorporate elements of an older ethnic religion also called Bon.

## Foodways

The primary agricultural crops of the Ngalop are Bhutanese red rice, potatoes, barley, and other temperate climate crops.

## Oral tradition & literature

They record a folk etymology for the name Ngalop, explaining it as “earliest risen people” or “first converted people.”

## Language & religion

Ngalops speak Dzongkha, which is descended from Old Tibetan and is the national language of Bhutan. Because Ngalops are politically and culturally dominant in Bhutan, Dzongkha is the language of government and education throughout the kingdom. Other groups that identify as culturally Ngalop speak the Kheng and Bumthang languages. The Sharchops of eastern Bhutan speak Tshangla, but many have adopted Ngalop culture and may identify as Ngalop. Ngalops largely follow Tibetan Buddhism, especially the Drukpa Lineage of the Kagyu school of Vajrayana. A significant number follow the Nyingma school, and a minority practice Bon or incorporate elements of Bon into their religious practices.
