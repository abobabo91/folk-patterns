## Overview

The Puyuma, also known as the Pinuyumayan, Peinan, or Beinan, are one group of Taiwanese Indigenous peoples. They are generally divided into the Chihpen and Nanwang groups, both resident in Taitung County on the east coast of Taiwan. In 2000, the Puyuma numbered 9,606, approximately 2.4% of Taiwan’s total indigenous population, making them the sixth-largest indigenous group. The name “Puyuma” means “unity” or “concord” and was originally the autonym of speakers of the Nanwang dialect. The Puyuma language belongs to the Austronesian family. Puyuma culture, as covered by the sources, includes distinctive clothing, textile patterns, village names, age-based dress categories, and a language with several documented dialect groupings.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Puyuma clothing patterns** — Traditional clothing combines red, yellow, green, black, and white. It features a multi-layered diamond pattern resembling snake scales, together with cross-stitch designs.

**Men’s cross-stitch ensembles** — Men over 22 years wear dark blue or black ensembles with cross-stitch patterns. These are combined with a green belt, bells belt, sword, betel nut bag, and, in some cases, glass beads or silver breast ornaments.

**Women’s embroidered aprons** — Women wear a *bagelup*, a small apron usually black and sometimes white, with a front embroidered pocket. In old age, the *bagelup* is replaced by the *sugun*, an apron tied at the waist with a pink belt.

**Embroidered lower garments** — Women’s clothing includes a black underskirt with subtle embroidery and an open skirt crossing on the left side, revealing inner-skirt embroidery. The lower legs are covered by two rectangles of embroidered black cloth secured by four straps around the calves.

**Motif vocabulary.** Multi-layered diamond pattern resembling snake scales; cross-stitch designs; subtle embroidery.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional clothing remains predominant during significant holidays and festivals, although most Puyuma nowadays wear western-style clothes. Clothing varies by age and includes an important transition for men. Boys and men aged 13–21 wear a plain blue blouse and a short skirt tied with a red belt during strict training. After reaching marriageable age, over 22 years, men wear dark blue or black ensembles with cross-stitch patterns, a green belt, a bells belt attached on the back, a sword at the waist, and a betel nut bag. Glass beads or silver breast ornaments may be added. A white headscarf and the flower crown *sahaputan* complete this attire. At 60 years, men shift to a sleeveless waistcoat called *lumbaw* and a square hat called *kabun*, marking their transition to the Elder category.

Women wear the same long-sleeved cropped bodice as men, in bright blue or white. Their clothing includes the *bagelup*, the *sugun*, embroidered skirts, and embroidered calf coverings. Women also wear silver bells, Japanese coins, bead headbands, silver hairpins, chest ornaments, silver and bead bracelets, and the *sahaputan* flower crown.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The clothing account mentions swords, betel nut bags, Japanese coins, silver ornaments, silver hairpins, silver bells, and silver bracelets.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Puyuma clothing is accessorized with glass beads, silver breast ornaments, silver bells, Japanese coins, bead headbands, silver hairpins, chest ornaments, and silver and bead bracelets. Men wear a white headscarf and a crown of flowers called *sahaputan*. The same flower crown is also part of women’s attire.

## Music & performance

The sources name several Puyuma singers, including A-mei, Saya Chang, Wan Sha Lang, Erica Chiang, Jane Huang, Samingad, Jia Jia, Purdur, Panai, Tank, and Sangpuy Katatepan Mavaliyw.

## Festivals & rituals

Traditional clothing still predominates during significant holidays and festivals. The sources describe age-based transitions in men’s clothing: boys and men aged 13–21 wear simple attire during strict training; men over 22 wear clothing associated with marriageable age; and men aged 60 shift to *lumbaw* and *kabun*, symbolizing their transition to the Elder category.

## Foodways

The only food-related object named is the betel nut bag worn as part of men’s marriageable-age attire.

## Language & religion

Puyuma, also called Pinuyumayan, is the language of the Puyuma, an Indigenous people of Taiwan. It belongs to the Austronesian family, is one of the more divergent Austronesian languages, and falls outside reconstructions of Proto-Austronesian. Most speakers are older adults. The sources describe the Nanwang dialect as relatively phonologically conservative but grammatically innovative. Other named dialect groupings include Pinaski–Ulivelivek and Kasavakan–Katipul. The language has 18 consonants and 4 vowels, verb-initial word order, four types of focus, three verbal aspects, two modes, and the articles *i*, *a*, and *na*.
