## Overview

The Tai Nua are a Tai people living in the river valleys of southwest China and Myanmar, with significant populations in Yunnan's Dehong Dai and Jingpo Autonomous Prefecture. They have their own writing system, the Tai Le script, and a distinct theatrical tradition called Daiju that blends Dai performance with narratives from literature and Buddhist tales. Their material culture centers on woven textiles with geometric patterning and festival observances centered on the lunar New Year.

## Material culture

Tai Nua material culture is documented mainly through woven textiles, festival objects, and theatrical performance, with limited records of everyday crafts.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Tai Nua weavers produce cloths with supplementary weft techniques that create banded geometric ornament.

- **Shoulder cloth** — A narrow woven cloth patterned with red and blue supplementary weft yarns carrying typical Tai geometric patterning.
- **Offering cloth** — Cotton cloths made for presentation at ceremonies and gift-giving occasions.

### Clothing & dress

Tai Nua dress is documented only fragmentarily, with the shoulder cloth serving as a shawl-and-wrap worn over the upper body.

- **Shoulder cloth** — A shawl-and-wrap garment worn over the upper body, patterned with geometric designs.
- **Straw hat** — A hat made of straw, one of the few documented head coverings.

### Architecture

Tai Nua architecture is barely documented in available sources.

- **Buddhist temple** — Serves as the site of the final and largest day of the New Year festival, where monks give their blessing.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Tai Nua metalwork combines worked metal with plaited plant fiber in the Southeast Asian tradition.

- **Sword** — Made of metal and rattan, combining a blade with rattan used in its mounting or binding.
- **Ki Tong Ja** — A swing used during the New Year festival week.
- **Spinning tops** — Toys played with alongside the swing during New Year celebrations.
- **Bull effigy** — A straw effigy of a bull built for the last day of the festival and burned to mark the departure of the old year.

### Jewelry & body adornment

No Tai Nua jewelry or body adornment practices are recorded in available sources.

## Music & performance

Tai Nua music is primarily documented through Daiju theatre, which blends Dai song and dance with Han Chinese stage traditions.

- **Daiju** (*Daiju (傣剧)*) — The Tai Nua dramatic form combining Dai music and dance with narrative material, built in the late Qing period from translated scripts of Beijing opera, Sichuan opera, Yunnan opera and shadow puppet play.
- **Toh Mark Khang** — A competitive game played during the New Year festival week.
- **Peak Gon** — A bean-bag tossing game played as part of New Year celebrations.

## Dance & theatre

Daiju is the named theatrical tradition, a deliberately constructed theatre for Dai audiences that emerged in the late Qing Dynasty.

- **Daiju** — A minority drama classified in Chinese scholarship as shaoshan minzu xiqu, drawing plots from long narrative poems and Buddhist folk tales.
- **Xiang Meng** (*Xiang Meng (相勐)*) — The first Daiju play, composed during the reign of the Tongzhi Emperor by Shang He, a resident of Yingjiang County.

## Festivals & rituals

The New Year festival Jin Leun Sam, falling on the first day of the third lunar month, is the documented centrepiece of the Tai Nua year.

- **Jin Leun Sam** — The New Year festival normally falling on the first day of the third month of the lunar calendar, running for a week and drawing people back to their birthplaces.
- **Ki Tong Ja** — A swing set up during the New Year week for celebrations.
- **Su Ma** — The occasion when children ask their elders' forgiveness and present an offering of flowers.
- **Meu Ho Leun Sam** — New Year's Day itself, when the week's special meals culminate in a major feast.

## Foodways

Tai Nua festival food centers on rice cakes made for New Year, which anchor a pattern of hospitality and invited meals.

- **Khao Pook Nga** — Sesame rice cakes baked for the New Year festival.
- **Kao Ke Mah** — Rice ball cakes made for New Year celebrations as material for hospitality.

## Oral tradition & literature

Tai Nua have their own literature in long narrative poems and Buddhist-influenced folk tales that supply material for theatre.

- **Narrative poems** — Long narrative poems that served as source material for Daiju playwrights.
- **Folk tales** — Stories frequently drawn from Buddhist tradition, used as plot sources for Daiju theatre.
- **Tai Nua proverbs** — A collection of 108 Tai Nua proverbs has been published in Thailand with translations into Thai and English.

## Language & religion

Tai Nuea belongs to the Tai language family and has its own script, the Tai Le writing system, while Buddhism provides the religious framework and narrative tradition.

- **Tai Nuea language** (*Tai Nuea*) — A Tai language closely related to Shan, with speakers dividing into Dehong dialect (332,000 speakers) and Menggeng dialect (209,000).
- **Tai Le script** (*Tai Le*) — A Mon-Burmese writing system thought to date to the 14th century, with modern reforms introducing tone diacritics in 1963 and tone letters in 1988, creating an alphabet of 35 letters.

## Glossary

- *Daiju (傣剧)* — Tai Nua theatrical form blending Dai music, dance and narrative
- *Jin Leun Sam* — Tai Nua New Year festival on the first day of the third lunar month
- *Ki Tong Ja* — A swing used in New Year celebrations
- *Su Ma* — New Year occasion when children ask elders' forgiveness with flower offerings
- *Meu Ho Leun Sam* — New Year's Day, culminating day for festival meals
- *Khao Pook Nga* — Sesame rice cakes made for New Year
- *Kao Ke Mah* — Rice ball cakes made for New Year
- *Toh Mark Khang* — Competitive game played during New Year festival
- *Peak Gon* — Bean-bag tossing game played at New Year
- *Tai Nuea* — The Tai Nua language, closely related to Shan
- *Tai Le* — Tai Nua writing system of the Mon-Burmese family; also an autonym meaning Upper Tai
- *Dehong* — Autonomous Prefecture in western Yunnan where most Tai Nua in China live; means Tai of the lower Hong River
- *Xiang Meng (相勐)* — The first Daiju play, composed during the Tongzhi Emperor's reign
