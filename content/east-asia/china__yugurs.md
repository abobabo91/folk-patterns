## Overview

The Yugurs, also known as Yughurs or Yugu, are a Turko-Mongol ethnic group and one of China’s 56 officially recognized ethnic groups. They number 14,706 persons according to the 2020 census and live primarily in Sunan Yugur Autonomous County in Gansu. Their historical settlement is associated with the valley of the Ejin River, the Qilian Mountains, and the area near present Zhangye, where the Ganzhou Uyghur Kingdom established its capital. The Yugurs are mostly Tibetan Buddhists. About 4,600 speak Western Yugur, a Siberian Turkic language, and about 2,800 speak Eastern Yugur, a Mongolic language. Mongolic and Chinese are also used in eastern provinces. Their folk-cultural significance in the available sources lies chiefly in their history, languages, and religious continuity.

## Language & religion

The Yugurs use two principal languages. About 4,600 speak Western Yugur, a Siberian Turkic language, while about 2,800 speak Eastern Yugur, a Mongolic language. Western Yugur has preserved many archaisms of Old Uyghur. Both Yugur languages are now unwritten, although the Old Uyghur alphabet was used in some Yugur communities until the end of the 17th century. Mongolic and Chinese are also used in eastern provinces. The Yugurs are mostly Tibetan Buddhists and have remained Vajrayana Buddhists to the present day. They adopted the Gelug order of Tibetan Buddhism in the late 16th century under the influence of Sonam Gyatso, the third Dalai Lama.
