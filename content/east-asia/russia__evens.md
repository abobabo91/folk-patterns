## Overview

The Evens are a nomadic people of Siberia and the Russian Far East, living in the Magadan Oblast, Kamchatka Krai, and northern parts of Sakha east of the Lena River. According to the 2002 census, there were 19,071 Evens in Russia; according to the 2010 census, there were 22,383. They speak Even, one of the Tungusic languages, which is closely related to Evenk and strongly shaped by nomadic life and reindeer herding. Their traditional economy centers on domesticated reindeer, supplemented by hunting, fishing, and animal-trapping. Evens are closely related to the Evenks by origins and culture, and their history includes migration from central China around 10,000 years ago.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Even clothing consists of a coat with an apron, pants, and boots, with the same general type worn by both genders. Garments are made from reindeer hides and skins and from moose hair. The sources distinguish Even clothing from that of neighboring indigenous groups through its open coats and aprons. Evens also wear a pouch on the coat for a pipe and tobacco. No separate ceremonial dress, head covering, belt, or special life-cycle clothing is described in the sources used.

### Architecture

Traditional Even lodgings are conical tents covered with animal skins. In southern coastal areas, fish skins were used as well as hides. These tents are called *churns* in the source and are described as smaller than the larger circular tents called *chorama-diu*, associated there with the Chukchi and the Koryak. Settled Evens used earth and log dugouts. Sheds were built near dwellings to store frozen fish and meat. During the Soviet reign, permanent housing was constructed as part of the effort to settle nomadic Evens.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They do describe wooden skis, saddles used for riding reindeer, storage sheds, pipes, and pouches for carrying tobacco.

## Music & performance

Even culture includes folklore consisting of stories and songs. These usually feature ground animals, birds, and cliché ideas or stereotypes of people.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state that Evens have officially been considered Orthodox since the 19th century, while retaining some pre-Christian practices, including shamanism.

## Foodways

The Even economy and subsistence system include reindeer herding, hunting, fishing, and animal-trapping. Evens generally kept smaller reindeer herds than the Chukchi, Koryak, and Yakut; unlike those groups, they mainly used reindeer as transportation rather than as food sources and trade goods. Evens also hunted small game, including undomesticated reindeer and other tundra animals. Hunting for fur, especially squirrel, became an important source of income after contact with Russians. Inland Evens focused more on herding and the fur trade, while those living on the Okhotsk Sea relied heavily on fishing.

## Oral tradition & literature

Even folklore consists of stories and songs, commonly involving ground animals, birds, and cliché ideas or stereotypes of people.

## Language & religion

Even is a Tungusic language closely related to Evenk. The Even language contains extensive vocabulary connected with nomadic movement and reindeer herding, including *oralchid'ak*, *nulgän*, *nulgänmäj*, and *nulgädäj*, words associated with a nomadic lifestyle and movement from one place to another. *Nulgä* can describe a measure of distance—the distance one can travel in a single night—or a group of reindeer herders. Before the Soviet period, Evens were referred to by other groups as Lamut, a name associated with the Okhotsk Sea in languages spoken in eastern Siberia. The Soviets created a written language in the 1930s. Evens have officially been considered Orthodox since the 19th century, while retaining pre-Christian practices such as shamanism.
