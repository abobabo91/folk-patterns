## Overview

The Dongxiang, also known by the autonyms *Sarta* and *Santa*, are a Mongolic people and one of the 56 ethnic groups officially recognized by the People’s Republic of China. Half of their population lives in Dongxiang Autonomous County in Linxia Prefecture, Gansu Province. Other Dongxiang communities are located in Hezheng County, Linxia City, Lanzhou, Dingxi and Ningxia. The China Statistical Yearbook 2021 gives a population of 774,947, although research reported in the source notes that this figure may be inflated by Hui identifying themselves as Dongxiang for census purposes. Dongxiang culture combines agriculture, carpet production, Islamic history, distinctive clothing, oral literature and the Dongxiang language, a member of the Mongolic family.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Traditional carpets** — Dongxiang are recognized as craftsmen specializing in the elaboration of traditional carpets.

### Clothing & dress

Men’s traditional clothing includes buttoned robes and a broad waistband. The waistband can be used to hang knives, snuff bottles or small bags. A vest worn over a white shirt, together with trousers and a beret-like cap, forms the remainder of the male outfit. Sheepskin coats are also worn during winter.

Women wear embroidered outfits consisting of wide-sleeved shirts and trousers. Older women wear kerchiefs, while younger women tend to wear brightly decorated cotton caps and silk veils. On special occasions, women wear embroidered shoes with a medium heel.

## Foodways

Dongxiang cuisine commonly uses a potato mash for noodles, snacks, alcoholic drinks and other foods. Agriculture forms the base of the economy, with potatoes, corn, barley, millet and wheat identified as the main products. The sources therefore document potatoes and other grains as important food resources.

The supplied material does not describe cooking vessels, preparation techniques beyond the use of potato mash, ceremonial foods, tea or coffee traditions, dietary rules, or the role of food in festivals and life-cycle events.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Dongxiang have a rich tradition of oral literature.

An early ethnography was documented in 1940 by the American Asiatic Association. Its author interviewed Ma Chuanyuan, described as a Muslim Mongol and the magistrate of five districts, about the origins of his people. The account characterized the community as one hundred thousand people, Mongol by race, Muslim by religion and Chinese by culture.

The Dongxiang language article also records a number of examples of vocabulary and grammar, including forms written in the Latin alphabet. They state that the Ford Foundation provided US$30,000 in 2004 for a pilot project promoting bilingual education in Mandarin and Dongxiang. The project was credited with publishing a Dongxiang–Chinese bilingual dictionary and with recent rises in test scores.

## Language & religion

Dongxiang, also called Santa, is a Mongolic language spoken by the Dongxiang people in Northwest China. It has three local varieties: *Suonanba*, spoken by approximately 50% of Dongxiang speakers; *Wangjiaji*, spoken by approximately 30%; and *Sijiaji*, spoken by approximately 20%. The language has distinct features resembling Middle Mongolian and contains up to 35% loan words from Mandarin Chinese, alongside negligible Persian, Turkic and Arabic elements.

Dongxiang is an agglutinative, strictly suffixing language and is traditionally an SOV language. In the Linxia dialect area, SVO sentences have also developed under the influence of neighboring Hui varieties of Mandarin Chinese. Dongxiang uses six primary cases and has borrowed Chinese numeral classifiers. Numbers above ten have been heavily replaced by Mandarin loans, including *sanshi* for 30 and *sishi* for 40.

Knowledge of Arabic is widespread among the Sarta, and Arabic script is often used informally to write Dongxiang. An official Latin alphabet developed on the basis of the Monguor alphabet remained experimental as of 2003. Around 20,000 people in northeastern Dongxiang County speak the Tangwang language, a Dongxiang-influenced form of Mandarin Chinese. The sources describe the Dongxiang as Muslim and state that their conversion to Islam took place in the 1340s.
