## Overview

The Kamenschiks, also called Bukhtarman, are a group of Russians descended from the earliest settlers of South Siberia. They are Old Believers whose ancestors originally lived along the Kerzhenets River in Nizhny Novgorod Governorate before moving to the Bukhtarma River in the Altai Mountains. Their historical settlements developed in the Bukhtarma region and later along the Argut and Katun rivers, including the Old Believer Uimon Village and other settlements in the Uimon Valley. Their descendants now identify with the broader Russian ethnic group and live in Kazakhstan, Russia, China, the United States, and other countries. The largest concentration remains in East Kazakhstan Region, the historical core of Kamenschik settlement.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Kamenschik villages were typically built near rivers, and mills and blacksmith shops served as important communal structures. Early settlements consisted of scattered homesteads and small villages of five to six households; after official recognition, thirty small settlements consolidated into nine larger villages, including Osochikha (Bogatyrevo), Bykovo, Sennoye, Korobikha, Pechi, Yazovoye, Beloye, Fykalka, and Malonarymskoye (Ognevo).

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention mills and blacksmith shops as communal structures and describe hunting, agriculture, fishing, beekeeping, cattle breeding, farming, ore mining, and trade, but they do not catalogue specific ceramics, metalwork, wooden objects, tools, or household goods.

## Festivals & rituals

 They record the persecution of Old Believers, their migration to the Bukhtarma region, their incorporation into the Russian Empire on September 15, 1791, and their exemption from conscription, mining and factory work, and some other state duties.

## Foodways

 They state that the Kamenschiks engaged in hunting, agriculture, fishing, beekeeping, cattle breeding, farming, ore mining, and trade.

## Oral tradition & literature

 The historical account records that the Bukhtarma valley came to be known as Belovodye and names the families of the Berdyugins, Lykovs, Korobeinikovs, and Lysovs.

## Language & religion

The Kamenschiks are Russians and Old Believers. They originally lived along the Kerzhenets River in Nizhny Novgorod Governorate and later settled along the Bukhtarma River and in the Altai Mountains.
