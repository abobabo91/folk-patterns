## Overview

The Golok peoples live in Golog Tibetan Autonomous Prefecture, Qinghai, China, around the upper reaches of the Yellow River (*dmar chu*) and the sacred mountain Amne Machin (*rma rgyal spom ra*). The Golok are not a homogeneous group: they comprise peoples of different geographic origins across the Khams and Amdo region, and the area was a haven for refugees and immigrants from across Amdo and Kham. They were renowned in Tibet and China as ferocious fighters who remained free from Tibetan and Chinese control. The historical territory known as Golog included parts of northern Sichuan, Maqu County in Ganlho Prefecture in Gansu, and other places in the traditional Tibetan regions of Amdo and Khams.

## Music & performance

The sources used mention a folk song recorded in 1951. Its quoted words assert resistance to the orders of the Dharma King of Tibet and to China, and state that the Golok make their own laws. No instrument, musical genre, performance setting, singer, or other musical practice is identified.

## Festivals & rituals

 They report legends that the Golok were ruled by a Queen, described as a reincarnated goddess whose power was handed down from mother to daughter. No further ritual details are provided.

## Foodways

The sources mention yaks, sheep, and horses in historical accounts of livestock, dispatches, warfare, indemnities, and confiscation.

## Oral tradition & literature

Golok oral tradition is represented in the sources by legends of a Queen who was a reincarnated goddess and by a folk song recorded in 1951. The song presents the Golok as rejecting the authority of the Dharma King of Tibet and China and making their own laws. The sources also record that, in 1828, the mystic and poet Shabkar Tsokdruk Rangdrol was attacked and pillaged by Golok tribesmen while returning to Amdo from Central Tibet.

## Language & religion

The sources give Tibetan and Chinese forms of the name Golok: Tibetan མགོ་ལོག and Chinese 果洛. They state that the territory is referred to in Tibetan as *smar kog*, and give *mgo log* and *’go log* as Wylie forms of the name. The name is sometimes interpreted as meaning “rebellious,” while a Chinese government document translated it as “turned head.” The sources mention the Dharma King of Tibet, the Dalai and Panchen Lamas, a reincarnated goddess, and monasteries.
