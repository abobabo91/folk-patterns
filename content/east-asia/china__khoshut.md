## Overview

The Khoshut are one of the four major tribes of the Oirat people. Their name is given in Mongolian as Хошууд and ᠬᠤᠱᠤᠳ, in qoşūd, and in Chinese as 和碩特; the source translates it as “bannermen,” deriving it from Middle Mongolian qosighu, “flag, banner.” They established the Khoshut Khanate in the area of Qinghai in 1642–1717. The sources describe Khoshut communities in Alashan, around Bosten Lake, in Kalmykia, and in parts of Mongolia and China. The Khoshuts in Alashan numbered 36,900 in 1990, while those around Bosten Lake numbered more than 12,000 in 1999.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe the Khoshuts’ conversion to Buddhism and state that Güshi Khan led many Khoshuds to occupy Kokenuur (Qinghai) in 1636.

## Language & religion

The Khoshut are identified as one of the four major tribes of the Oirat people. The source records Khoshut forms in Mongolian, traditional Mongolian script, qoşūd, and Chinese, and explains the name through Middle Mongolian qosighu. It states that the Khoshuts led others in converting to Buddhism.
