## Overview

The Barga, also called the Barghut, are a subgroup of the Buryats. They originated east of Lake Baikal in Siberia and migrated first into Outer Mongolia, then into Inner Mongolia and Manchuria. In the early 18th century, the Qing dynasty resettled them in the Hulun Buir region. Most Bargas now live in the three Barga banners of Hulun Buir, Inner Mongolia, China, where they are classified as ethnic Mongols. In 1864, the ten Barga banners in Hulun Buir had 3,055 households. Their documented history connects them with the Khori-Buryats, Mongol and Oirat political worlds, Qing military organization, Tibetan Buddhism, and continuing shamanic influence.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state that the Barga converted to Tibetan Buddhism around the 17th and 18th centuries because of Mongol influence, while shamanism continues to have significant influence in Barga beliefs and culture.

## Foodways

 The only economic food-related information recorded is that Barga households raised domestic animals, including horses, cattle, camels, sheep, and goats.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention that local people have many tales about Bayan Barga Rich families. They also record that Dimin Ogorda had 10,000 horses during the Qing dynasty period, Jingdi Jangi had 12,000 horses during the Japanese occupation period, Yorong Jangi had 20,000 sheep, and Bosang Hafan had 1,000 white cows.

## Language & religion

The sources identify the Barga as a subgroup of the Buryats and give Mongolian, Chinese, and pinyin forms of the name Barga. The Barga are Tibetan Buddhists. They converted around the 17th and 18th centuries because of Mongol influence, while shamanism retains a significant influence in Barga beliefs and culture.
