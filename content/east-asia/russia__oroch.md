## Overview

The Oroch, also called Orochs, Orochons, or Orochis, are a people of Russia whose self-designation is Nani. They traditionally settled in the southern part of the Khabarovsk Krai and on the Amur and Kopp rivers; some migrated to Sakhalin in the 19th century. According to the 2002 census, there were 686 Orochs in Russia, and according to the 2010 census, 596. The Oroch language belongs to the Tungusic languages and is closely related to Nanai and Udege. Oroch society underwent major family and marriage changes between 1963 and 1993, while the language became endangered. The Orochi National District was created in the early 1930s but was cancelled shortly thereafter.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that the Orochs follow Shamanism and the Russian Orthodox Church.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Oroch people never had a written language and were educated in Russian.

## Language & religion

Oroch is a Tungusic language spoken by the Oroch people. The language is associated with the southern group of the Tungusic languages in the Oroch language article and with the northern subgroup in its classification section. It is closely related to the Nanai and Udege languages and was spoken in the Khabarovsk Krai districts of Komsomolsky, Sovetskaya Gavan, and Ulchsky. The language is divided into the Tumninsky, Khadinsky, and Hungarisky dialects; another list names Koppi, Oroch Xadi, and Tumnin. A written form was created at the beginning of the 21st century, although the people previously had no written language. The Russian government and scientific field disagree on whether Oroch is living or extinct. The last active speaker died in 2008, and researchers reported that elderly Orochi at a 2010 meeting remembered about 20 Oroch words and could not count to ten. The Orochs follow Shamanism and the Russian Orthodox Church.
