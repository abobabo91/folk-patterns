## Overview

The Teochew, also called Teo-Swa or Chaoshanese, are a Han Chinese sub-ethnic group native to the Chaoshan region in south China. The region encompasses Chaozhou, Shantou, and Jieyang in eastern Guangdong Province. Teochew people also live in Hong Kong and throughout diasporas in Southeast Asia, including Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Cambodia, Vietnam, and the Philippines, as well as the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and France. The Teochew language is a Southern Min language, spoken by roughly 10 million people in Chaoshan and more than five million outside the Chinese mainland. Teochew culture is documented through language, opera, music, cuisine, tea practice, embroidery, woodcarving, architecture, and dance.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Chaoshan embroidery** — Chaoshan embroidery is called “three-dimensional painting on silk.” A piece can take months of work.

### Clothing & dress

They describe dress expectations connected with *jiat dot* banquets: guests were expected to be neatly dressed, with clean hats and footwear. In earlier times, men were expected to wear long gowns and felt hats, while women carefully combed their hair, applied light makeup, and wore *ruyi hairpins*.

### Architecture

Chaoshan traditional houses are usually composed of rectangular courtyards and rooms forming a “mansion-style” folk house. Their overall layout is generally “front courtyard and back house.” The front part is called the “outer courtyard” or “front courtyard”; its surface is rammed with shell lime sand or paved with stone and is surrounded by walls on three sides. The “back house” consists of a main building on the central axis and symmetrical side rooms on the left and right. The sources also state that Teochew people used substantial wood carving in buildings, especially temples and ancestral halls. Named landmarks include the Chaozhou Ancient City, Paifang Street, Swatow Old Town, and Nan'ao Island.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Teochew woodcarving is described as one of the oldest carving arts in China, with precise and lifelike presentations. Teochew people used wood carving in buildings, especially temples and ancestral halls, and Teochew gold-leaf gilded carving is specifically named. The sources also describe Chaoshan porcelain as famous for delicate colors and classic designs.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources used mention women’s careful hair arrangement, light makeup, and *ruyi hairpins* in the context of earlier *jiat dot* banquets.

## Music & performance

Teochew music is popular in Chaoshan’s teahouse scene. Traditional forms include Teochew string music, gong and drum music, and flute music. Teochew string music consists mostly of plucked and bowed string instruments, with wind instruments used on some occasions. Named instruments include the *erxian* or *touxian*, *tihu*, *yehu*, *sanxian*, *pipa*, *ruan*, *guzheng*, and *yangqin*. The *erxian*, *tihu*, and *yehu* are described as two-stringed bowed lutes. The current Chaozhou drum music is said to resemble the Drum and Wind Music form of the Han and Tang dynasties.

Gongfu tea is an important part of social etiquette in Chaoshan. It is described as an “espresso” served in tiny cups of strong and fragrant tea and is said to have been first consumed in the Song dynasty. Teochew cuisine sources describe very strong oolong tea called *Tieguanyin*, served in very tiny cups before and after meals. When presented as gongfu tea, it has a thickly bittersweet taste known colloquially as *gam gam*.

## Dance & theatre

Teochew opera is a traditional art form based on local folk dances and ballads and influenced by Nanxi Opera. Nanxi is described as one of the oldest Chinese operas and as originating in the Song dynasty. Teochew opera has its own style, preserves an old form of choral accompaniment, and features clowns and female characters as distinctive figures. Fan play and acrobatic skills are prominent. The sources state that Teochew opera has a history of more than 400 years in one account and more than 500 years in another, and that it has been performed in over 20 countries and regions.

*Yingge dance* is a Chinese folk dance that began in the Qing dynasty. With a history of more than 300 years, it combines martial arts and street theater and is regarded as one of the most representative folk arts in Teochew culture. The sources do not distinguish ceremonial and entertainment versions of the dance.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe etiquette associated with the *jiat dot* feast, also called the “food table.” Guests were expected to dress neatly and behave with decorum. At the table, they were expected to be refined and composed, handle tableware gently, and avoid spilling liquids, making noises while eating, or loudly gnawing bones.

## Foodways

Teochew cuisine originated in the Chaoshan region of eastern Guangdong Province, including Chaozhou, Shantou, and Jieyang. It is known for seafood and vegetarian dishes, relatively light flavoring, and emphasis on the freshness and quality of ingredients. Poaching, steaming, braising, and stir-frying are common, while oil is not often used in large quantities. Teochew meals may include congee called *mue*, steamed rice, or noodles.

Named dishes and foodways include Chaoshan brined meat, marinated raw seafood, Braised Lion-head Goose, Chaoshan Hotpot, Teochew oyster omelet, and the traditional preparation of thinly sliced raw fish. Teochew chefs also use sauces and stocks extensively. Examples include crab vinegar, goose vinegar, shacha sauce, fish sauce, and *siang teng*, a special stock that is continuously replenished. Vegetable carving is used to garnish cold dishes and banquet tables.

The *jiat dot* feast may include shark fin soup, bird’s nest soup, lobster, steamed fish, roasted suckling pig, and braised goose, although the sources state that shark fin soup and bird’s nest soup are no longer served there because of legal and environmental considerations. A late-night meal is known as *meh siao* or *daa laang*. Teochew restaurants often include a dessert section. Gongfu tea and *Tieguanyin* are important tea practices.

## Oral tradition & literature

They mention Teochew oral traditions in Singapore, including accounts published by Phua Chye Long in *Teo-chews in Malaya* in 1950. The sources also identify local folk dances and ballads as a basis for Teochew opera. No contemporary literary revival or preservation project is described.

## Language & religion

Teochew is also known as Swatow or Teo-Swa and is a Southern Min language. It is closely related to Hokkien and preserves many Old Chinese pronunciations and vocabulary. The language has dialect divisions including Northern Teochew, or the Chaozhou division, and Southern Teochew, or the Chaoyang-Puning division. Named dialects include Chaozhou, Jieyang, Shantou, Raoping, Chaoyang, Puning, and Huilai. Teochew is not mutually intelligible with Mandarin, Cantonese, or Shanghainese and has only limited intelligibility with Hokkien.

Written Southern Min is known since at least the 16th century. The earliest known work named in the sources is a 1566 edition of *The Tale of the Lychee Mirror*, written in a mixture of Teochew and Chinchew Hokkien. Teochew writing is not standardized or widely used. They do mention the Yueh Hai Ching Temple and Teochew religious charitable groups in Thailand and Singapore.
