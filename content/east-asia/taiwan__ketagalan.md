## Overview

The Ketagalan, also called Ketangalan, are a Taiwanese aboriginal people originating in what is now the Taipei Basin. Their language has become extinct. Beitou District in Taipei City houses the Ketagalan Culture Center, a cultural center about the Ketagalan people.

## Festivals & rituals

The Wikipedia account does record a traditional narrative in which villagers left an island after a monster terrorized them, sailed to Taiwan near the Shuang River in the north, and later divided between plain-dwelling and mountain-dwelling peoples by drawing straws.

## Oral tradition & literature

A folklore account says that the Ketagalan forebears originally lived on another island called Sanasay. A monster repeatedly terrorized the village, killed a child, and drove the villagers to leave after they could find no other way to deal with it. After an arduous sea voyage, they reached Taiwan near the Shuang River in the north. Later, as the community grew, the villagers drew straws: those who drew long straws remained on the fertile plain, while those who drew short straws moved into the mountains.

## Language & religion

Luilang, also ambiguously called Ketagalan, Ketangalan, or Tangalan, was a Formosan language spoken south of modern-day Taipei in northern Taiwan by one of several peoples called Ketagalan. It probably became extinct in the mid-20th century and is little researched. According to oral tradition, the Luilang people originally inhabited four villages near Taipei: Luili, Siulang, Bulisiat, and Liau-a. These merged under the name Luilang and later migrated to Outer Oat-a in the 18th century.
