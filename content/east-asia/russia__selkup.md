## Overview

The Selkup are an Indigenous people of Siberia who traditionally settled in the northern parts of Tomsk Oblast, Krasnoyarsk Krai and Tyumen Oblast, including Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug. They originated in the middle basin of the Ob River through interactions between the aboriginal Yeniseian population and Samoyedic peoples who came from the Sayan Mountains during the early part of the first millennium CE. The 2002 Census recorded 4,249 Selkups in Russia. Their language belongs to the Samoyedic branch of the Uralic language family, although only about 600 people still use the native language. Selkup subsistence traditionally centered on hunting, fishing and reindeer herding, with dugout canoes used on rivers.

## Language & religion

Selkup belongs to the Samoyedic group of the Uralic language family and is spoken in the region between the Ob and Yenisei Rivers in Siberia. The language name comes from the Russian селькуп and is based on the native name used in the Taz dialect, *шӧльӄумыт әты* (*šöľqumyt əty*), meaning “forest-man language.” The main varieties are the Taz, or Northern, dialect; the Tym, or Central, dialect; and the Ket dialect. The Taz dialect became the basis of the Selkup written language in the 1930s. The dialects at the ends of the continuum are no longer mutually intelligible, and Selkup is not related to the Ket language. Only a portion of the general Selkup population speaks the native language today, and Selkup-language teaching is not included in basic education. The sources state that many Selkups retained some ancient religious beliefs and customs after attempts at Christianization.
