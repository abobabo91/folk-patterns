## Overview

The Khoton or Qotung people are a Mongolian-speaking ethnic group in (Outer) Mongolia and Inner Mongolia. Most Mongolian Khotons live in Uvs Province, especially in Tarialan, Naranbulag and Ulaangom. In China, Khotons, often called Qotungs, live in Inner Mongolia, concentrated in Alxa League and classified as ethnic Mongols. There were officially about 6,100 Khotons in 1989 and around 12,057 in Mongolia in 2020. A 2014 estimate gave approximately 20,000 Khotons in Uvs Province, while a 2004 study reported an estimated total of about 1,200 for Khotons in Inner Mongolia. Their cultural distinctiveness includes Turkic origins, adoption of Oirat speech, and a form of Islam with Buddhist and traditional elements.

## Language & religion

Khotons are a Mongolian-speaking ethnic group. They formerly spoke the Khoton language, described as a dialect of the Uyghur language; this language became extinct around the 19th century. After settlement in Mongolia, they adopted the Dörbet or northern dialect of Oirat. In Inner Mongolia, they speak the Alasha dialect of Oirat. Khoton religious practice is described as a form of Islam containing Buddhist and traditional elements such as Tengrism. The sources state that Khotons traditionally avoid intermarriage with other ethnic groups and tend to avoid mainstream Mongolian written culture.
