## Overview

The Khanty are an indigenous Siberian people of the Ob river basin who call themselves As Khoyat, the Obian people. They have lived as fishers, hunters and reindeer herders whose ceremonial life, especially the Bear Celebration, survived Christianization and Soviet rule. Their material culture is rich in decorated fur coats, birch-bark vessels and beaded ornaments. Today about 13,900 speakers of Khanty remain, with the language existing as several mutually unintelligible dialect groups.

## Material culture

Khanty material culture centers on wood, birch bark and fur, decorated with patchwork, applied cloth strips and beadwork.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Khanty decoration builds from fur patchwork, applied cloth strips, beadwork and woven sashes.

- **Fur patchwork** — Cream and mid-brown fur pieces assembled into coats and boots with the joins emphasized by added strips of red, dark blue and yellow tabby wool.
- **Beadwork** — Applied threaded glass beads worked in diamond patterning on head ornaments and cloth borders in white, red and green.
- **Embroidered cloth** — Alternating bands of blue and brown wool embroidery with stylized floral motifs, zigzag patterning in salmon pink, lilac and blue, and bead bands on all four edges.
- **Woven sashes** — Hand-spun, hand-woven tabby woollen cloth girdles with plied warp ends, dyed in the piece.

### Clothing & dress

Men wear knee-length flared fur coats with mittens stitched to sleeves and decorated fur boots; women's dress centers on beaded head ornaments and necklaces.

- **Man's fur coat** — Knee-length flared coat of fur, lined with fur and finished with a fur collar, with mittens stitched directly to the sleeve ends and thin leather thongs as front fastenings.
- **Fur boots** — Knee-length boots with top front curved to cover the knee, on a brown sole, with short leather thongs inside the upper edge.
- **Man's sealskin cap** — Shaped like a coif with red napped tabby wool framing the face, bordered by cream seal skin, crown of pale fur segments, with an oval sealskin panel at the back.
- **Snow-spectacles** — Goggles made of metal, cloth, brass, beads, brass buttons and reindeer skin with the fur left on.

### Architecture

The Khanty followed a two-season settlement pattern, with winter villages of dirt and branch huts and spring hunting grounds with temporary birch-bark shelters.

- **Winter hut** — Stationary huts built of dirt and branches, part of permanent winter villages.
- **Temporary hunting shelter** — Rectangular structures of birch-bark and poles put up in spring at hunting and fishing grounds.
- **Model tent** — A model dwelling of fur, wood and skin, the only tent form represented as an object in the collection.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Everyday material is overwhelmingly wood and birch bark, with hunting and fishing gear dominating the recorded objects.

- **Birch-bark vessels** — Include a fish basket with basketry-and-bark lid, a dish, a tumbler, and a bowl for eating fish, all made of birch bark.
- **Ermine trap** (*cherpak*) — A bow trap of wood used for hunting ermine.
- **Fish trap** (*morda*) — A wooden trap used for fishing.
- **Man's knife** — Combines ivory, white metal and wood.
- **Smoking pipe** — Has a hard-wood stem with a cylindrical mouthpiece and bowl of white metal, attached with inlaid metal pieces.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Women's ornament dominates the record, featuring beaded necklaces with pendants, head ornaments with metal plaques and beadwork, and crosses showing Orthodox influence.

- **Holiday necklace with pendants** — Made of beads and cloth with pendant ornaments.
- **Beaded necklace with crucifix** — A necklace of beads, cloth, copper and string hung with a crucifix, a direct trace of Orthodox conversion in personal dress.
- **Head ornament with metal plaques** — Built on two joined strips of cream tabby linen faced with dark brown wool, with square silver-coloured metal plaques stitched in a vertical line, edged with couched black and white beads.
- **Hair ornament with beaten metal disc** — Red tabby wool with white, red and green bead diamonds, cotton-ribbon-wrapped hangings and a large beaten metal disc suspended on cream beads.

## Music & performance

Two instrument types appear: the swan harp and the shaman's tambourine; song is best documented as part of the Bear Celebration.

- **Swan harp** — A stringed instrument of wood and string.
- **Shaman's tambourine** — Represented in three forms: wood and hide, wood with skin, brass and red cotton cloth fitted with bells, and a separate wood-and-hide beater.
- **Nukh Kiltatty Ar** — The Awakening Song, a central song of the Bear Celebration.
- **Ily Vukhalty Ar** — The Coming Down From The Sky Song, which tells how the son of Torum, the sky god, was sent to rule the Earth, forgot his father's advice, lost his immortality, turned into a beast and was killed by hunters.
- **Il Veltatty Ar** — The Lullaby, a central song of the Bear Celebration.

## Dance & theatre

Named Khanty dances or dramatic traditions are not described, though performances form part of the Bear Celebration alongside its songs.

## Festivals & rituals

Two calendar observances are named: the Crow spring celebration in April and the Bear Celebration held after a successful bear hunt.

- **Crow spring celebration** — Falls in April, now on 7 April, the same day as the Orthodox Annunciation.
- **Bear Celebration** — Held occasionally after a bear has been successfully hunted, running five or six days with more than 300 songs and performances, the duration depending on the sex of the animal.
- **Divination figure** — A cylindrical body with a roughly carved face, dressed in a full-length red cloak of napped tabby wool trimmed with yellow gold-coloured metal ribbon at hood, cuffs and hem.

## Foodways

Khanty subsistence was based on fishery, taiga hunting, reindeer herding and gathering, with eating equipment of birch bark and wood.

- **Birch-bark bowl** — A bowl specifically for eating fish.
- **Fishing traps** (*morda and cherpak*) — Wooden traps by which food was taken.

## Oral tradition & literature

Khanty folklore comprises bear songs, fairy tales and epic poetry; written literature began in the first half of the 20th century.

- **Grigori Lazarev** — The first notable Khanty writer (1917–1979), best known for the novel Sorneng tow.
- **Yeremey Aypin** — A writer and politician born in 1948.
- **Maria Vagatova** — The first Khanty poetess (1936–2026), also a storyteller, publicist, teacher, editor and journalist.
- **Khanty yasang** — A Khanty-language newspaper founded in 1957 that carries the written language today.
- **Lukh avt** — A Khanty-language newspaper founded in 2001 that carries the written language today.

## Language & religion

Khanty is a Ugric language of the Uralic family with three main dialect groups—Northern, Southern and Eastern—and most Khanty today are Orthodox Christians mixed with traditional shamanic belief.

- **Northern Khanty dialect** — One of the three main mutually unintelligible dialect groups.
- **Southern Khanty dialect** — One of the three main dialect groups, extinct since the early 20th century.
- **Eastern Khanty dialect** — One of the three main dialect groups including Surgut and Vakh-Vasyugan dialects.
- **Kazym dialect** — Used in newspaper reporting and broadcasting today, one of the dialects in which literary work appears.

## Glossary

- *As Khoyat* — Obian people, people from Ob; what the Khanty call themselves
- *Yugra* — historical name for the Khanty–Mansi region in western Siberia
- *por* — one of two exogamic phratries in historical Khanty social organization
- *cherpak* — a bow trap for ermine made of wood
- *morda* — a fish trap made of wood
- *Torum* — the sky god in Khanty tradition
- *Nukh Kiltatty Ar* — the Awakening Song of the Bear Celebration
- *Ily Vukhalty Ar* — the Coming Down From The Sky Song of the Bear Celebration
- *Il Veltatty Ar* — the Lullaby song of the Bear Celebration
- *Sorneng tow* — novel by Grigori Lazarev, the first notable Khanty writer
- *Khanty yasang* — Khanty-language newspaper founded in 1957
- *Lukh avt* — Khanty-language newspaper founded in 2001
- *Ob-Ugric* — branch of the Uralic language family to which Khanty belongs
- *Kazym dialect* — Khanty dialect used in newspaper reporting and literary work
