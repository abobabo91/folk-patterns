## Overview

The Teleuts are a Turkic Indigenous people of Siberia living mainly in Kemerovo Oblast, Russia. Most live along the Great and Little Bachat Rivers, while a few live in the Altai Republic. According to the 2010 census, there were 2,643 Teleuts in Russia. They speak the Teleut language or dialect of the Southern Altai language. The Teleuts consider themselves a distinct people, and many do not accept being labeled as Altaian. Their folk culture, as covered by the source, includes nomadic and semi-nomadic livestock herding, hunting in the taiga, conic dwellings, distinctive clothing, Orthodox Christianity, shamanism, and the Muslim Teleut community known as Kalmaks.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Teleut dress was composed of linen shirts, short breeches, and single-breasted robes. The sources do not distinguish between men’s and women’s clothing, nor do they describe ceremonial dress, head coverings, belts, footwear, ornament, or differences between everyday and special-occasion clothing.

### Architecture

Traditional Teleut dwellings included conic yurts made out of bark or perches.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that most Teleuts are Orthodox Christians, while a minority practices shamanism. Burkhanism was once widely practiced by the Teleuts but was effectively eliminated during the Soviet era. Contemporary revivals of the religion among other Altaian groups have not affected the Teleuts.

## Foodways

They state that some Teleuts were hunters and relied on animals living in the taiga for subsistence, while most Teleuts formerly practiced nomadic or semi-nomadic livestock herding.

## Language & religion

Teleut is described as a language or dialect of the Southern Altai language, and the Teleuts are identified as a Turkic Indigenous people. Most Teleuts are Orthodox Christians, while a minority practices shamanism. Burkhanism was formerly widely practiced but was effectively eliminated during the Soviet era. A group known as Kalmaks are Muslims: around the 17th century, they moved north of Kemerovo Oblast, interacted with local Siberian Tatars, and became Sunni Muslims. Today they number around 500 and have mostly assimilated into Tatar culture while retaining their Teleut roots. They speak a local dialect of the Siberian Tatar language heavily influenced by Teleut.
