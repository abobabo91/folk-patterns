## Overview

Telengits, also called Telengut, are a Turkic ethnic group native to Central Asia and recognized as one of the minor indigenous peoples of Russia. They are an ethnic subgroup of the Altaians and mainly live in the Kosh-Agachsky District of the federal Altai Republic, along the Chuya River in the western Altai. They call themselves Chui-kizhi, meaning Chuya people. In 2002, 2,398 Telengits were recorded as their own census category, although the source states that this figure may be inaccurate because 8,000 or 9,000 people might identify as Altaians rather than Telengits. Their importance in folk culture is documented through pastoral life, felt yurts, distinctive dress, shamanism, Christianity, and a strong relationship with the Altai homeland.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Telengit dress was similar for men and women. It consisted of long-sleeved shirts, breeches, and robes. Double-breasted sheepskin coats, fur hats, and high boots were commonly worn. Married women additionally wore a sleeveless jacket over their coats. The sources do not distinguish everyday clothing from ceremonial dress, nor do they provide vernacular names for garments, belts, or head coverings.

### Architecture

Traditional Telengit dwellings included felt yurts. Modern Telengits live in wooden homes but commonly inhabit yurts during the summer months.

## Music & performance

The source does state that shamanism continues to exert a strong influence in Telengit culture and refers to a Telengit community leader and shaman from the Russian Altai’s high-altitude Kosh Agach Raion in a United Nations University digital video concerning Altai’s human-nature relationships.

## Festivals & rituals

They do document a religious landscape in which most Telengits practice shamanism and Eastern Orthodox Christianity, while smaller numbers practice Burkhanism. The source describes the Telengits’ relationship with their homeland as sacred: Altaians and Telengits are said to worship their special homeland, and Telengits say that a person who leaves the Altai will become ill and die because of physical separation from the land.

## Foodways

They state only that most Telengits were historically nomadic or semi-nomadic cattle herders and commonly raised sheep, cattle, goats, and horses.

## Oral tradition & literature

The history described in the source includes Chinese chroniclers, Russian documents, and modern historians’ use of the term “Telengit Ulus,”.

## Language & religion

The source identifies the Telengits as a Turkic ethnic group. It states that most Telengits practice shamanism and Eastern Orthodox Christianity, while smaller numbers practice Burkhanism. Shamanism continues to exert a strong influence in Telengit culture, while Orthodoxy has seen a recent revival among the Telengits. The source also emphasizes the sacred status of the homeland and the connection between people and the land.
