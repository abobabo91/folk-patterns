## Overview

Fārsīwān is a contemporary designation for Persian-speaking communities in Afghanistan and its diaspora. Historically, the term referred to settled farmers and urban dwellers who spoke Persian and were largely identified with the Tajik population. Farsiwan communities are concentrated in western and northwestern Afghanistan, particularly in Herat, Farah, Ghor, and Mazar-i-Sharif; they form the majority of the population in the city of Herāt. Smaller communities exist in Kabul, Kandahar, and Ghazni, while diaspora communities are recorded in Mashhad and Tehran. The term primarily describes Persian-speaking, sedentary communities rather than a separate ethnic group. Historically associated with agriculture, urban life, craftsmanship, and trade, Farsiwan identity combines language, settlement, and adherence to Shia Islam.

## Language & religion

Farsiwan is primarily a linguistic and social designation for Persian-speaking, sedentary communities. The name combines *Fārsī* with the Persian suffix *wān*; *wān* is described as the local colloquial pronunciation of the more commonly known Persian suffix *bān*. The sources explain that the exchange of the “b” sound for the softer “w” sound is common in Persian spoken in Afghanistan, as in *āb*, meaning water, pronounced *aw*. Farsiwan communities speak Persian, including Afghan Persian or Dari. Some rural Farsiwan speak dialects more akin to Dari dialects, including the dialect of Kabul, while most Fārsīwān speak the Khorasani dialect associated with the Afghanistan–Iran border region, including Harāt and Farāh, and the Iranian provinces of Khorasan. Farsiwan are often distinguished from other Tajiks by adherence to Shia Islam rather than the Sunni Islam favored by the majority of Tajiks. The sources state that the Farsiwan do not show, or show only very limited, traces of Turkic and Mongol ancestry. They distinguish Farsiwan from the Hazara and from the Qizilbash, although these groups are also described as Persian-speaking and Shia.
