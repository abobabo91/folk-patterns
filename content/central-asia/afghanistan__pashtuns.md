## Overview

Pashtuns are the largest ethnic group in Afghanistan and second-largest in Pakistan, with about 60–70 million people spread across 350–400 tribes. They speak Pashto, an Eastern Iranian language with Sanskrit influence. The collection represents them through a heavily ornamented woman's ceremonial dress decorated with glass beads, cowrie shells, coins and metal stamped disks, and through patterned leather shoes worn by women, men and children. Their material culture reflects a rich tradition of textile work, embroidery and decorated footwear.

## Material culture

Pashtun material culture is known for dense, multi-material decoration concentrated on dress and footwear, combining silk embroidery, glass beads, cowrie shells, coins and metal ornaments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Pashtun textiles feature silk embroidery and floral patterning worked into both cloth and leather.

- **Bead-, shell- and coin-worked woman's dress** — A rust-coloured velvet skirt with green cotton sleeves embroidered in multiple colours and set with glass beads, metal disks, cowrie shells, pearly buttons, beaded tassels, bells and Afghan coins dated 1968–1981 on the display bodice.
- **Patterned leather footwear** — Pale blue to turquoise leather uppers worked with floral ornament and sewn with yellow cotton and metal thread, over red insoles and green or turquoise turned-up pointed soles.
- **Gul-i peron discs** (*gul-i peron*) — Metal stamped discs sewn onto the woman's dress bodice as adornment.

### Clothing & dress

Pashtun dress divides into ceremonial garments for women and everyday wear, with shoes shared in construction between men, women and children but differing in heel height and sole colour.

- **Woman's ceremonial dress** — Heavy ornamented garment representing ceremonial dress rather than daily clothing, with the bodice functioning as a mounted surface for beads, buttons, shells and coins.
- **Men's shoes** — Turquoise leather uppers with floral ornament, red insoles and green turned-up pointed soles, with leather extended upwards at the heel, showing heavy wear to the sides.
- **Women's shoes** — Turquoise leather uppers with floral ornament, red insoles and green turned-up pointed soles, sharing men's construction but without extended heel.
- **Child's shoes** — Turquoise leather uppers and red insole with turquoise turned-up pointed sole and leather strip rising from the inside of the heel, unworn.
- **Burka** — Full-body covering worn by women in everyday village settings, as shown in a nineteenth- or early twentieth-century photograph.

### Architecture

The only documented Pashtun structures are small domed grass huts associated with nomadic groups.

- **Domed grass huts** — Small structures photographed with a village group, closely resembling nomad groups still known today.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pashtun metalwork appears only as applied ornament on clothing.

- **Metal stamped disks** — Metal ornaments set along the cuffs of the dress.
- **Afghan coins** — Four coins dated 1968–1981 fixed to the dress bodice, dating the garment's assembly.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Pashtun adornment materials were sewn directly onto garments rather than worn separately, concentrated on the bodice and sleeve cuffs.

- **Glass beads** — Multi-coloured beads sewn onto the woman's dress bodice and along shoe cuffs.
- **Cowrie shells** — Marine shells sewn onto the dress bodice as adornment.
- **Pearly buttons** — Buttons sewn onto the dress bodice among other ornaments.
- **Beaded tassels and bells** — Decorative elements sewn onto the dress bodice.

- **Sericulture** — Silk production, listed by UNESCO as a country-level inscription for Afghanistan.

## Oral tradition & literature

Pashtun oral tradition is best documented through genealogical claims, particularly tribal descent narratives and legendary progenitors.

- **Yusufzai tribal tradition** (*Yusufzai*) — Tribal tradition holding that when the twelve tribes of Israel were dispersed, the tribe of Joseph and other Hebrew tribes settled in the Afghanistan region, giving rise to the tribal name meaning 'son of Joseph' in Pashto.
- **Qais Abdur Rashid tradition** — Legendary progenitor said to have borne the title batan, a possible origin of 'Pathan' from the Arabic word for 'rudder.'
- **Nimat Allah al-Harawi's history** — Seventeenth-century written compilation of descent claims for Khan-e-Jehan Lodhi under Mughal emperor Jahangir.

## Language & religion

Pashto is an Eastern Iranian language with Sanskrit influence and affinities to Saka dialects, though not all Pashtuns speak it.

- **Pashto** — Eastern Iranian language spoken by most Pashtuns, carrying strong Sanskrit influence and sharing vocabulary cognates with Ossetian.
- **Hindko** — Language spoken by Pashtuns of the Hazara region.
- **Wanetsi** — Language spoken mainly within the Tareen tribe.
- **Dari and Urdu** — Dari serves as a second language in Afghanistan and Urdu in Pakistan among Pashtun speakers.
