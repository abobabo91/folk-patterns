## Overview

The Uyghurs are a Turkic people of Central Asia who live in oases around the Taklamakan Desert in Xinjiang. Their culture blends Persian, Central Asian, Chinese, and Indian influences through centuries of trade and settlement. They are known for their textiles, music traditions, distinctive clothing, and Islamic practices shaped by Sufi traditions. Today they maintain strong folk customs in weaving, music, dance, food, and family rituals.

## Material culture

Uyghur material culture centers on textiles and decorative arts shaped by oasis trade and Islamic aesthetics.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Uyghurs are master weavers of silk and wool, creating some of Central Asia's finest ikat and carpets.

- **Atlas silk** (*atlas*) — A warp-ikat silk woven mainly in Hotan and Yarkand with soft, liquid-edged patterns in black, red, yellow, blue, and green.
- **Khan-atlas** — The finest grade of atlas silk, historically for wealthy families and brides, with more colors and elaborate patterns.
- **Hotan carpet** (*Khotan gilam*) — Hand-knotted wool carpets with a central medallion, pomegranate borders, and a palette of madder red, indigo, and camel-wool ivory.
- **Doppa embroidery** (*doppa*) — A four-paneled square skullcap embroidered in silk or metal thread, with the Kashgar badam doppa showing white paisley motifs on black.
- **Felt rugs** (*kigiz*) — Pressed-wool felts used for floor covering, sleeping surfaces, and horse gear.

### Clothing & dress

Daily dress centers on long robes and trousers, with the doppa skullcap worn by men, women, and children to mark their oasis home.

- **Woman's everyday dress** (*köynek*) — A long-sleeved dress worn over trousers, made from atlas silk for celebrations and cotton prints for daily wear.
- **Coat** (*chapan*) — A long belted coat of velvet or wool worn over the dress by older women and by men, sometimes heavily embroidered.
- **Skullcap** (*doppa*) — A four-paneled cap worn year-round by men, boys, and many women; its style and color identify the wearer's oasis.
- **Boots** (*ötük*) — Leather boots worn with men's long trousers and tucked at the ankle.
- **Belt** (*bäl-baghliq*) — A leather or embroidered belt worn with the chapan coat.

### Architecture

Uyghur homes are mud-brick structures built around interior courtyards, with carved wooden details and tiled mosques marking the landscape.

- **Oasis house** (*aywan*) — A single-story mud-brick home with a central courtyard and a covered verandah supported by carved wooden posts.
- **Interior platform** (*supa*) — A raised platform in living rooms covered with felts and carpets where the household eats, sits, and sleeps.
- **Id Kah Mosque** — Kashgar's largest mosque with a yellow-tiled portal and timber hypostyle hall.
- **Emin Minaret** — A tapered brick tower at Turpan patterned with brickwork lozenges and rhomboids.
- **Shrine** (*mazar*) — A tomb complex for Sufi saints and local heroes, decorated with glazed tilework in turquoise and green.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Artisans in Kashgar and Yarkand craft glazed pottery, hammered copperware, and carved wood, with the Yengisar knife a signature blade.

- **Glazed earthenware** (*aptawa*) — Bowls, plates, and ewers from Kashgar and Yarkand potters glazed in green, yellow, and turquoise with floral motifs.
- **Yengisar knife** (*pichaq*) — A slender single-edged blade with a horn, brass, or bone handle inlaid with colored mastic, worn as a male accessory.
- **Clay oven** (*tonur*) — A wood-fired clay oven for baking naan bread, a household staple.
- **Cooking pot** (*choghan*) — A cast-iron pot used for daily cooking.
- **Qur'an stand** (*lauh*) — A wooden stand for the Qur'an, carved by Yarkand and Kucha woodworkers.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Silver jewelry and henna mark a woman's status, with elaborate bridal sets including turquoise and coral ornaments.

- **Earrings** (*halqa*) — Hoop or pendant silver earrings worn by married women.
- **Bangles** (*bilähzük*) — Multiple silver bracelets worn on the wrists.
- **Amulet case** (*tumar*) — A triangular or cylindrical case hung on a cord or belt, holding folded prayers or Qur'anic verses.
- **Henna** (*hena*) — A reddish dye applied to a bride's palms and fingertips the night before a wedding.
- **Eyebrow mark** (*osma*) — Historically, a single line joining the brows over the nose, drawn with plant juice as a mark of adult female beauty.

## Music & performance

The core classical tradition is the Twelve Muqams, extended suites of sung poetry and dance that can last for hours.

- **Twelve Muqams** (*On Ikki Muqam*) — Twelve extended suites of sung poetry, instrumental interludes, and dance, each named for its musical mode, inscribed by UNESCO in 2008.
- **Long-necked lute** (*dutar*) — A two-stringed lute with a soft, domestic sound, used to accompany narrative songs.
- **Wire-strung lute** (*tämbür*) — A long, wire-strung lute with a brilliant sound, a lead instrument in muqam performance.
- **Spike fiddle** (*ghijäk*) — A bowed string instrument that carries melody in ensemble music.
- **Narrative song** (*dastan*) — Long sung tales such as Gherip-Sänäm and Tahir-Zöhrä, performed by bards or in meshrep gatherings.

## Dance & theatre

Uyghur dances are soloistic and virtuosic, centered on head, wrist, and shoulder movement, often performed within the Meshrep social gathering.

- **Sanam** — The everyday social dance where couples and soloists enter and leave a circle.
- **Dolan dance** — A more ecstatic paired dance from Dolan communities where hunters mime the stalking of a deer.
- **Plate dance** (*chinä ussuli*) — A set-piece dance balancing porcelain bowls on the head and hands.
- **Lamp dance** — A dance performed with oil lamps balanced on the palms and crown.
- **Meshrep** — A men's social gathering combining food, music, sung poetry, dance, and moral discussion, inscribed on UNESCO's Urgent Safeguarding List in 2010.

## Festivals & rituals

The festival year follows the Islamic lunar calendar, with the two Eids and life-cycle rites marking time and family transitions.

- **Eid al-Fitr** (*Roza Héyt*) — The spring festival ending the Ramadan fast, celebrated with family gatherings and feasting.
- **Eid al-Adha** (*Qurban Héyt*) — The autumn sacrifice feast and the largest gathering of the year, marked by mass prayer and household mutton feasts.
- **Spring new year** (*Noruz*) — Celebrated on 21 March with spring foods, outdoor meshrep, and shrine visits in some districts.
- **Circumcision ceremony** (*khätnä toy*) — A large feast held when boys between five and seven years are circumcised.
- **Wedding** (*toy*) — A multi-day celebration including the religious nikah ceremony, the bride's procession in atlas silk, and a closing meshrep.

## Foodways

Wheat and mutton anchor the diet, with flatbread baked fresh daily and rice pilaf at every celebration.

- **Flatbread** (*nan*) — Bread baked daily against tonur oven walls, ranging from thin toqach to thick, seed-crusted girdä.
- **Hand-pulled noodles** (*laghman*) — Noodles served with a stir-fried sauce of mutton, tomato, pepper, and celery.
- **Pilaf** (*polu*) — Rice layered with mutton, carrot batons, onion, and cumin, served at weddings and Eids.
- **Skewered kebab** (*kawap*) — Cumin- and chilli-dusted mutton kebab, the common street food.
- **Tea** (*dora chay*) — Green or spiced tea with cardamom, saffron, rose, and pepper, served with naan and dried fruit, drunk constantly throughout the day.

## Oral tradition & literature

Narrative songs and classical Chagatai poetry form the backbone of oral literature, blending folk performance with high-culture texts.

- **Gherip-Sänäm** — A great narrative song performed by professional bards and in meshrep gatherings.
- **Wisdom of Royal Glory** (*Kutadgu Bilig*) — An eleventh-century classical text by Yusuf Khass Hajib of Balasaghun, part of the shared Chagatai literary canon.
- **Dictionary of Turkic languages** (*Divan-i Lughat-it-Türk*) — A roughly eleventh-century comparative dictionary by Mahmud al-Kashgari, foundational to Uyghur literary tradition.
- **Proverb** (*maqal*) — Traditional sayings and wisdom circulated domestically.
- **Riddle** (*tepishmaq*) — A folk game of wit, transmitted orally in domestic and meshrep settings.

## Language & religion

Uyghur is a Turkic language written in Arabic script; the people are Sunni Muslims whose practice is shaped by centuries of Sufi influence.

- **Uyghur language** (*Uyghur*) — A Karluk Turkic language closely related to Uzbek, with dialect zones in the central Tarim, east, and north.
- **Arabic script** (*Uyghur Ereb Yëziqi*) — The standard orthography for Uyghur in Xinjiang today, derived from Perso-Arabic writing.
- **Sunni Islam** (*Hanafi*) — The overwhelmingly dominant form of Islam practiced, following the Hanafi school of law.
- **Sufi lodge** (*Naqshbandi*) — A mystical Islamic order whose lodges shaped shrine pilgrimage, healing practice, and ecstatic zikr traditions.
- **Shrine pilgrimage** (*zikr*) — An ecstatic devotional practice, embedded in some meshrep gatherings and muqam performance, connected to Sufi orders.
