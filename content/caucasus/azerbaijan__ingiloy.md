## Overview

Ingiloys, also known natively as Heretians, are an ethnographic subgroup of Georgians who speak the Ingiloy dialect of Georgian. They are indigenous to Saingilo, formerly known as Hereti, a cultural and historical region in the northwest of present-day Azerbaijan. The Saingilo area traditionally includes the Balakan, Zaqatala and Qakh districts and has a territory of 4,780 km2. In the census of 2009, the number of Georgians fell to 9,900 people; most lived in the Qax district or in the city of Baku. Ingiloy culture is documented through clothing, vernacular architecture, wood carving, stone carving, Georgian schools, churches and theatre.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Woolen fabrics** — Woolen fabrics of local production were widely marketed in the 19th and early 20th centuries. The villages of Muxax, İlisu and Sarıbaş were especially known for producing woolen fabrics, including shawls.

**Silk fabrics** — For clothing, the population also bought factory fabrics, mainly cotton and silk. A significant place was occupied by silk fabrics of local Azerbaijani production.

### Clothing & dress

The women’s traditional costume consisted of a long shirt, trousers, an apron, an archaluk and a headdress. Local and factory-made fabrics were used to make clothing in the 19th and early 20th centuries. The traditional costumes were fully preserved in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, even up until the 1930s. The sources state that Ingiloy costumes were very similar to the costumes of neighbouring Azerbaijanis, Tsakhurs and Avars inhabiting the Balakan, Zaqatala and Qakh districts.

### Architecture

The most ancient type of dwelling used by Georgians was the *darbazi* type house, and analogous dwellings were common in Saingilo. The *erdoiani-akariani* dwellings represent related types with changes and resemble the design of Kartlian Darbazi houses. They no longer give the same importance to the “mother pillar,” called *dedabodzi* in Georgian; instead, their pillars act as supports. Wooden houses were common in Saingilo and had a design similar to Erdoiani Akarani houses. Most commonly, Ingilo houses were made of bricks and stone.

Gates, called *Darvaza*, were important features of the Ingilo farmstead. Their wooden parts were often decorated with carvings and fretwork. Farm buildings included cattle sheds, *kasrbegeli* barrel barns, haylofts, wine cellars and mills. The cattle shed, or “cow-house,” was separated from the dwelling *karami*. Grain storage was retained by the *akari*.

Wood carving was used in house interiors, including the *dedabodzi*, small shelves called *Korte* and shrines. Wooden fretwork was used especially for banisters and farmstead gates. Stone carving was also present, and churches were often adorned with stone carvings.

## Dance & theatre

In the village Alibeyli, the Qakh State Georgian Drama Theatre is located.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state that most Ingiloys are Sunni Muslims and that some converted to Christianity through missionary activity in 1860, while most of those converts later reverted to Islam in 1863.

## Foodways

 They mention the *khula*, a place where food products are stored, and state that Saingilo farmsteads could lack a maize granary, barn or *khula* because the *akari* retained its original grain-storage function.

## Oral tradition & literature

According to traditional accounts, the name of the Heretians, and therefore of the Ingiloys, originates from the legendary patriarch “Heros,” son of Thargamos. Heros was said to have founded the city of Hereti, later known as Khoranta, at the Alazani River.

## Language & religion

Ingiloys speak the Ingiloy dialect of Georgian and are described as an ethnographic subgroup of Georgians. Georgian schools operated in Saingilo: the sources state that there are seven Georgian schools, while four Azerbaijani schools have Georgian sectors. Georgian language teachers are hired by education authorities of the Zaqatala and Qax districts.

Ingiloys are mostly Sunni Muslims, with a minority practicing Orthodox Christianity. They converted to Islam in the 17th century through Lezghin and Ottoman influence. There are three active Georgian Orthodox Churches in Qakh district: Saint George’s Church in Qakh Ingiloy village, Patara Alaverdi in Qax and Saint Nino’s Church in Alibeyli village. Georgian Churches in Azerbaijan are under the jurisdiction of the Eparchy of Khornabuji and Hereti.
