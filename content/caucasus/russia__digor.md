## Overview

The Digor or West Ossetians are a subgroup of the Ossetians, also identified with the Alans. They mainly live in Digorsky, Irafsky, and Mozdoksky districts and Vladikavkaz in North Ossetia–Alania, as well as in Kabardino-Balkaria and Turkey, including Poyrazlı and Boğazlıyan. In the 2002 Russian Census, 607 Digors were registered; by the 2010 Russian Census, the number had dropped to 223. It was estimated that there were 100,000 speakers of the Digor dialect, most of whom identify as Ossetians. The sources describe the Digor language and its history.

## Festivals & rituals

Most Digors are Muslim. They were converted to Sunni Islam around the 17–18th centuries under the influence of the neighboring Kabarday people, who introduced Islam to them.

## Oral tradition & literature

The early medieval Ashkharatsuyts mentions the “nation of the Ash-Tigor Alans,” or simply the “Dikor nation,” which is generally regarded as an early reference to the Digor.

## Language & religion

Digor Ossetian is a dialect of the Ossetian language spoken by the Digor subgroup. Ossetian belongs to the Eastern Iranian language group. Digor is also called Digor Ossetic or Digor-Ossetic, and its name in the source is *digoron ævzag*. The dialect was considered a separate language in the USSR until 1937; since 1932, it has been considered a dialect of Ossetian. Digor and Iron are not mutually comprehensible, and about 2,500 words in the Digor dialect do not exist in the Iron dialect. Digor is spoken in the west of the Republic of North Ossetia and in neighboring Kabardino-Balkaria. In 2011, North Ossetia launched a Digor-language version of the REGNUM News Agency and began efforts to promote study of Digor among young students; the government also began offering textbooks in Digor. Most Digors are Muslim and were converted to Sunni Islam around the 17–18th centuries.
