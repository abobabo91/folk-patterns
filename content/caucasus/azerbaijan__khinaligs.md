## Overview

The Khinalugs (Azerbaijani: Xınalıqlılar; Khinalugh: кеттитурдур, кетш халх) are an indigenous people of Azerbaijan who speak Khinalug, a Northeast Caucasian language. They are indigenous to the Quba District and are named after their main village, Khinalug. The Khinalugs are one of the peoples traditionally called Shahdagh, together with the Budukh, Jek, and Kryts people.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They mention a mosque called Jomard, also given as Gomard, or Abu Muslim, located in the settlement.

## Festivals & rituals

The Khinalugs are overwhelmingly Sunni Muslims. According to a legend, they converted to Islam in the mosque Jomard, also called Gomard, or Abu Muslim, located in their settlement. They celebrate Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, and Nowruz.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that the first written information about the Khinalug people dates from the 18th century and that there is insufficient information about their history to study their ethnogenesis. The sources mention attempts to identify an ethnogenetical relation with the tribes of Caucasian Albania, including proposals involving the endonym ketid, the Caucasian Albanian tribe ket/gat, and the Khenuks or Khenuts. These proposals are presented as disputed: Anatoly Novoseltsev associated the Khenuks with the Khinalugs, while N. G. Volkova regarded this approach as hardly acceptable, and R. M. Magomedov considered the Khenoks to be Rutuls.

## Language & religion

Khinalug is a Northeast Caucasian language. It is described either as forming its own sub-branch or as belonging to the Lezgic languages. Because it is spoken only in one settlement, it has no dialects, although phonetic differences have been noticed in the speech of the upper, middle, and lower parts of the settlement. The language is unwritten, but *ХӀикмаьти чаьлаьнг* was published in Khinalug in Cyrillic script in Baku in 1991. It was taught in primary school from 1993 to 1999, after which this was discontinued. A new alphabet based on Latin script was adopted in 2007. Khinalugs are overwhelmingly Sunni Muslims and also celebrate Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, and Nowruz.
