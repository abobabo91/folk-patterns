## Overview

The Bagvalal, also called Bagulal, Kwantl Hekwa, Bagolal, Kwanadi, Bagulaltsy, Kvanadin, and Kvanadintsy, are an Avar–Andi–Dido people of Dagestan who speak the Bagvalal language. They live in mountain villages in the Tsumadinsky District of Dagestan, near the right bank of the river Andi-Koisu and the surrounding hills. Their villages include Kvanada, Gimerso, Tlisi, Tlibisho, Khushtada, and Tlondada. There were 3,054 Bagvalals in 1926, while the 2020 Russian census recorded 2,297 Bagvalal speakers. Since the 1930s, the Bagvalal have largely been classed as and assimilated by the Avars. Their tradition and culture are described as very similar to those of the Avars because of their shared history within the Avar Khanate.

## Festivals & rituals

The Bagvalals are Sunni Muslims and adopted Islam by the 16th century through the influence of Sufi missionaries.

## Language & religion

Bagvalal, also called Bagulal, is an Avar–Andic language spoken in southwestern Dagestan, Russia, along the right bank of the river Andi-Koisu and the surrounding hills near the Georgian border. It is fairly similar to Tindi, described as its closest relative. The language has three dialects named after the villages in which they are spoken: Kwanada, Khushtada, and Tlisi. Only the Tlisi dialect has been studied to a significant extent because of its similarities to Tindi. Bagvalal has loanwords from Arabic, Russian, Turkish, and Avar. It is used only as an oral language, while Avar or Russian are used as written languages. Bagvalal pupils are taught in Avar in primary school and in Russian in secondary school. The language is in serious danger of extinction, although it remains used for everyday communication and in family settings.
