## Overview

The Khwarshi are a North Caucasian people living in Dagestan, in several small settlements. Their traditional homeland is the southeastern part of Tsumadinsky District, where seven settlements are located: Upper and Lower Inkhokwari, Kwantlada, Santlada, Khwarshi, Khonokh and Khwayni. They do not have one ethnonym for themselves as a united people, instead identifying with their settlement of origin. During August 1944, they were deported to Vedeno and Rityalb; by 1957, 30% had returned to the traditional settlements. Today, some 7,000 live outside those settlements and 1,500 remain there. They speak Khwarshi, a Tsezic language, and traditionally follow Sunni Islam.

## Language & religion

Khwarshi, also spelled Xvarshi or Khvarshi, is a Northeast Caucasian language spoken in the Tsumadinsky, Kizilyurtovsky and Khasavyurtovsky districts of Dagestan by the Khwarshi people. The linguist Zaira Khalilova, whose fieldwork took place from 2005 to 2009, gives a figure of 8,500 speakers; other sources give 1,870 and 3,296. The lower figures are attributed to many Khwarshi registering themselves as Avar speakers, since Avar is their literary language.

Khwarshi has six dialects: Upper and Lower Inkhokwari, Kwantlada, Santlada, Khwayni and Khwarshi Proper. These originate in their respective villages in Tsumadinsky District, while emigration has also produced related-speaking communities in Oktyabrskoe, Pervomayskoe and Mutsalaul. The language has about 87 distinct phonemes, including 21 vowel phonemes and 66 consonants. It has 51 cases: 8 grammatical cases and 43 locative or spatial cases. Khwarshi nouns belong to five genders, or noun classes, in the singular. Traditionally, the Khwarshi are Sunni Muslims.
