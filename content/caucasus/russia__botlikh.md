## Overview

The Botlikh, also known as Bótligh, Botlig, Botlog or Buikhatli, are an Andi–Dido people of Dagestan. They live in and around the village of Botlikh, just north of the Andi Koysu River, and the Botlikh language is also spoken in Miarso, Ashino, Chontaul, Ankho and Batlakhatli. The group numbered 3,354 people in 1926; according to the 2021 Russian census, 3,788 people in Russia declared themselves Botlikhs, while 5,073 declared speaking the Botlikh language. Botlikh is an Andic language of the Northeast Caucasian language family. Until the 1930s the Botlikh were considered a distinct people, after which they were classified as Caucasian Avars and faced a campaign of assimilation. The available sources document their ethnonym, settlement geography, language and religious history.

## Language & religion

Botlikh, also spelled Botlix, is an Andic language of the Northeast Caucasian language family. It is spoken by the Botlikhs in Botlikh, also called Buikhe, Miarso and Ashino, as well as in Chontaul, Ankho and Batlakhatli, in southwestern Dagestan, Russia. According to the 2020 census, approximately 5,000 people spoke it. Botlikh has two dialects, Botlikh proper and Miarso; their differences in phonology and morphology are small, and they are mutually intelligible. The language has five basic vowels, which can also be long or nasalized. Botlikh is unwritten, and Botlikhs have mostly used Avar for written communication. When they write Botlikh, they use the Avar alphabet. The Botlikh are primarily Sunni Muslims and adopted Islam by the 16th century through Sufi missionaries.
