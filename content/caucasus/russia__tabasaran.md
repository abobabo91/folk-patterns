## Overview

Tabasarans are a Lezgin sub-ethnic group native predominantly to Tabasaransky district of Dagestan, with communities partly on the plain and in cities including Dagestanskiye Ogni, Derbent, and Makhachkala. Their population in Russia is about 150,000. They speak Tabasaran, a language of the Lezgic branch of the Northeast Caucasian family; Lezgin and Russian are also common. Tabasaran has two main dialects, North Tabasaran, also called Khanag, and South Tabasaran, with the literary language based on the Southern dialect. The group’s documented folk-culture profile includes agriculture, animal husbandry, orchards, viticulture, carpet weaving, leatherworking, woolen clothes, woodworking, and beekeeping. Most Tabasarans are Sunni Muslims and belong to the Shafi'i school.

## Festivals & rituals

The vast majority of Tabasarans profess Sunni Islam and belong to the Shafi'i school. Elements originating from pre-Islamic Tabasaran beliefs have been preserved in contemporary society, including deity names and an annual spring celebration known as *Elbetsan*. The source compares Elbetsan with Iranian *Nowruz* and Lezgian *Yaran-Suvar*.

 They state that Tabasarans were converted to Islam at a fairly early date and that Arab historians referred to their land as “Tabarstan,”.

## Foodways

The sources do state that the traditional economy was based on agriculture and animal husbandry in regions with a mild and warm climate and abundant water sources. Grain farming, orchards, viticulture, and beekeeping are documented, but no specific foods or recipes are named.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They do identify Zumrud Khanmagomedova as the first Tabasaran woman to receive a higher education and the first Tabasaran poetess.

 They state that Tabasaran has a literary language based on the Southern dialect and provide several sample sentences, including “You are falling,” “I love you,” “Who are you?,” “How are you?,” “I'll come tomorrow,” and “I'll be back.”

## Language & religion

Tabasaran is a Northeast Caucasian language of the Lezgic branch, spoken by the Tabasaran people in the southern part of the Russian Republic of Dagestan. Its two main dialects are North Tabasaran, or Khanag, and South Tabasaran. The literary language is based on the Southern dialect, and Tabasaran is one of the official languages of Dagestan. Under Soviet control, it became one of Dagestan’s official languages.

Tabasaran is an ergative, agglutinating, and primarily suffixing language with ergative-absolutive alignment. North Tabasaran has two noun classes, while Southern Tabasaran lacks noun classes. Tabasaran has been written using Cyrillic since 1938; a Latin alphabet was used from 1931 to 1938, and the first book in that alphabet was published in 1932. Peter von Uslar devised Cyrillic-based orthographies for Caucasian languages, including Tabasaran.

According to the supplied sources, most Tabasarans are Sunni Muslims and belong to the Shafi'i school. Pre-Islamic elements include deity names and the annual spring celebration Elbetsan.
