## Overview

Dargins, also called Dargwa (*дарганти*, romanized *darganti*), are a Northeast Caucasian native ethnic group originating in the North Caucasus. They are the second largest ethnic group in the North Caucasian republic of Dagestan, where the 2021 Census recorded 521,381 Dargins, or 16.6% of the population. They are concentrated in the Kaytagsky, Dakhadayevsky, Levashinsky, Akushinsky and Sergokalinsky Districts. They speak the Dargin languages, a Northeast Caucasian dialect continuum. Their folk-culture significance is documented in architecture, decorative and applied arts, Kaitag textiles, Kubachi silversmithing, weaving, leatherwork, fur work, folk dance and vocal music.

### Clothing & dress

The sources mention Dargin weaving, leatherwork and fur work.

### Architecture

Dargin infrastructure and architecture were described as highly developed in comparison with neighboring peoples. Folk masters built and ornamented towers and fortresses, ensembles of buildings, mosques and bridges, and constructed irrigation works at springs and wells. The sources also identify Kubachi silversmiths, stonecutters, toolmakers, woodworkers, and ceramic and tile workers.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Dargin decorative and applied arts include the work of Kubachi silversmiths, stonecutters, toolmakers, woodworkers, and ceramic and tile workers.

## Language & religion

Dargwa (*дарган мез*, *dargan mez*) is a Northeast Caucasian language spoken by the Dargin people in the Russian republic of Dagestan. The literary dialect discussed in the source is based on the Aqusha and Urakhi dialects of Northern Dargin. Kajtak, Kubachi, Itsari and Chirag are described as languages in the same dialect continuum, although they are also sometimes treated as separate languages. Dargwa has five vowel sounds and a consonant inventory that includes over 40 phonemes, with the exact number varying by dialect. Its current alphabet is based on Cyrillic; the first Dargin alphabet was created by Peter von Uslar in the late 19th century, and a Latin alphabet existed in the 1920s. Dargins are mostly Sunni Muslims of the Shafi'i school.
