## Overview

The Tushetians, also called Tush, are a subgroup of Georgians who mainly live in Tusheti. They divide themselves into the Chagma-Tush, who speak a local Georgian dialect, and the Tsova-Tush, also known as Bats or Batsbi, who speak the Bats language, a Nakh language. Most Bats also speak Georgian, and linguistic assimilation is continuing. Traditionally, Tushetians are shepherds, and sheep and cattle breeding remains the leading branch of the economy of highland Tusheti. Their customs and traditions are similar to those of other eastern Georgian mountaineers, including the Khevsurs and Pshavs.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Tushetian Guda cheese** — This product is associated with Tusheti and was famous for its quality. It was exported to Europe and Russia, and its making technique differs from that of European Gouda cheese.

**High-quality wool** — Wool from Tusheti was famous and was exported to Europe and Russia.

## Language & religion

The Chagma-Tush speak a local Georgian dialect, while the Tsova-Tush, also known as Bats or Batsbi, speak the Bats language, a Nakh language. Most Bats also speak Georgian, and there is a continuing trend of linguistic assimilation. Like most Georgian groups, Tushetians are mainly Georgian Orthodox Christian in religion. The sources describe the origins of the Tush as mysterious and debated, with different theories concerning the Tsova-Tush and Chagma-Tush. The region of Tusheti was first mentioned by its name in the 4th century BCE.
