## Overview

The Andis, called *къӀваннал* in Andi and *ГӀандал* in Avar, are an indigenous Dagestan people of North Caucasia. Their territory lies in the Botlikhsky District of Dagestan, in western Dagestan. Their principal settlement area is Andia, a broad valley bordered by the Andi ridge and its spurs. The Andi Koysu River flows south of Andi and joins the Avar Koysu to form the Sulak River. The Andis are Sunni Muslims. Their language belongs to the Andic subgroup of the Avar–Andic branch of the Northeast Caucasian language family. The Andi language was spoken by about 5,800 ethnic Andi in 2010, while the population was recorded as 21,808 in 2002.

The Andi language article states that Andi is usually unwritten, although there have been some nineteenth-century texts written in Andi. Speakers generally use Avar or Russian as their literary language. A 2015 translation of the Gospel of Luke uses an Andi alphabet.

## Language & religion

Andi is a Northeast Caucasian language belonging to the Avar–Andic branch and is spoken in the Botlikh region of Dagestan. It is spoken in the villages Andi, Gunkha, Gagatl, Ashali, Rikvani, Chanko, Zilo, and Kvanxidatl. The language has four main dialects: Munin, Rikvani, Kvanxidatl, and Gagatl. The upper group contains Andi, Gagatl, Rikvani, and Zilo, while the lower group contains Munin and Kvanxidatl. Andi has 43 consonants and five vowels. It is usually unwritten, though Russian Cyrillic has been used in attempts to write it. Speakers generally use Avar or Russian as literary languages. In the village of Andi, men’s and women’s speech differs in certain phonetic, lexical, and stylistic features. The Andis are Sunni Muslims.
