## Overview

The Chamalals are an indigenous people of Dagestan in North Caucasia. They live in a few villages in the Tsumadinsky District, on the left bank of the Andi-Koysu river. The Chamalal language is spoken in southwestern Dagestan, Russia, and by approximately 5,100 ethnic Chamalals; another source gives approximately 5,000 ethnic Chamalals in 1999. Chamalal belongs to the Andic branch of the Northeast Caucasian language family. The Chamalals are culturally similar to the Avars and neighbor the Godoberi, Avars, Bagvalals, and Tindis. The sources identify their language, settlement area, religious affiliation, and relations with neighboring peoples.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that Islam reached the Chamalal people around the 8th or 9th century and that they primarily follow Sunni Islam.

## Language & religion

Chamalal, also called Camalal or Chamalin, is an Andic language of the Northeast Caucasian language family. It is spoken in southwestern Dagestan, Russia, by approximately 5,100 ethnic Chamalals. The language has three quite distinct dialects: Gadyri, Gakvari, and Gigatl. Gadyri is also identified as Gachitl-Kvankhi; Gakvari as Agvali-Richaganik-Tsumada-Urukh; and Gigatl as Hihatl. Kwenkhi and Tsumada are also listed as dialects. Gigatl and Chamalal proper, with the Gadyri, Gakvari, Tsumada, and Kwenkhi dialects, are considered sublanguages. Chamalal is an unwritten language. Avar and Russian are used in school, while Avar is also used for literary purposes. There are no countries with Chamalal as an official language. The speakers are mostly Muslim and primarily follow Sunni Islam. The language is described as having a 6b, or threatened, status.
