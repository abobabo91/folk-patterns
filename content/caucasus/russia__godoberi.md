## Overview

The Godoberi (Godoberi: *гъибдилъи*, Ghibdilhi; Russian: *годоберинцы*) are an Andic ethnic group indigenous to the mountainous region of southwestern Dagestan, Russia. Their traditional homeland consists mainly of the villages of Godoberi and Zibirkhali in the modern Botlikhsky District, in the upper Andi Koisu river basin. The villages lie in steep mountain valleys and isolated settlements connected by traditional mountain routes. The Godoberi language belongs to the Andic branch of the Northeast Caucasian language family. Modern ethnographic estimates generally place the number of ethnic Godoberi at approximately 3,000–4,000 people, while the language article states that Godoberi is spoken by approximately 3,000 people. The Godoberi are significant for the study of small Andic-speaking communities whose identity has persisted through oral language, village institutions, Islamic traditions, and local forms of social organization.

## Music & performance

Godoberi cultural traditions include songs, music, oral poetry, and traditional forms of storytelling. Because the language historically lacked a standardized written form, oral transmission played an especially important role in preserving cultural memory. The sources identify heroic legends, village history, family traditions, moral teachings, and relations between neighboring communities as themes in folklore. Music and oral poetry are described as important elements of traditional cultural expression, and many elements of Godoberi culture are shared with other Andic and Avar-speaking peoples because of centuries of regional interaction.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state that most Godoberi are Sunni Muslims and that religious life historically combined Islamic practices with local customs and social institutions. Islamic law (*Sharia*) existed alongside customary law (*adat*), while local village structures remained influential in everyday social organization. Under the Caucasian Imamate, Islamic institutions gained increased political importance under Imam Shamil. Under Soviet rule, religious activity was restricted, many mosques were closed, and religious education was suppressed. After the collapse of the Soviet Union, Islamic practice experienced a revival throughout Dagestan, including among the Godoberi.

## Foodways

 Traditional economic activities included cultivation of grain crops and vegetables, sheep and cattle breeding, horticulture, handicrafts, and trade with neighboring mountain communities. Livestock provided food, materials, and household wealth.

## Oral tradition & literature

Godoberi cultural traditions include oral literature, historical narratives, songs, proverbs, and traditional storytelling. Oral transmission was especially important because Godoberi historically lacked a standardized written form. Folklore includes heroic legends, village history, family traditions, moral teachings, and relations between neighboring communities. Early history is poorly documented because small mountain communities traditionally preserved historical knowledge through oral traditions rather than written chronicles. The Godoberi language article states that the history of the Godoberis is unknown and that the language is the main feature distinguishing the cultural group from other groups in the area. Modern documentation has produced grammatical descriptions, dictionaries, and recordings of Godoberi, contributing to the preservation and study of the language and its oral traditions.

## Language & religion

Godoberi, also rendered Ghodoberi, is an Andic language of the Northeast Caucasian language family. Its self-designation is *Ghibdilhi mittsi*. The language has two main varieties, the Godoberi village dialect and the Zibirkhali dialect; the language article names the dialects as Godoberi and Zibirhali. Native speakers live in Godoberi, called Ghidu in Godoberi, and Zibirkhali, called Shalu in Godoberi. The varieties differ mainly in pronunciation, while the broader descriptions also note phonetic and lexical differences. Godoberi is not a written language and has traditionally been used primarily in the home. Schools teach Avar and Russian, and native speakers use Avar or Russian as written languages. Godoberi vocabulary contains influences from Avar, Arabic, Turkish, and Russian. Most Godoberi are Sunni Muslims. Religious life combines Islamic practices with local customs, and Islamic law exists alongside *adat*.
