## Overview

The Mingrelians (Mingrelian: მარგალეფი, romanized: *margalepi*; Georgian: მეგრელები, romanized: *megrelebi*) are an indigenous Kartvelian-speaking ethnic subgroup of Georgians. They mostly live in the Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti region of Georgia, called სამარგალო (*samargalo*) in Mingrelian and სამეგრელო (*samegrelo*) in Georgian. They also live in considerable numbers in Abkhazia and Tbilisi. Mingrelians speak Mingrelian and are typically bilingual in Georgian; both languages belong to the Kartvelian language family. They have preserved characteristic cultural features, including the Mingrelian language, dating back to the pre-Christian Colchian era.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources identify Tedo Sakhokia as a Georgian ethnographer, lexicologist, folklore scientist, translator, opinion journalist, and educator.

## Language & religion

Mingrelian is a Kartvelian language, and Mingrelians are typically bilingual in Georgian. Both Mingrelian and Georgian belong to the Kartvelian language family. The sources state that the nobility and clergy of Lazica changed from the Hellenic ecclesiastic tradition to the Georgian one, and that Georgian became the language of culture and education. They also state that, in the fifth century, Gubazes I declared Christianity the state religion of Lazica.
