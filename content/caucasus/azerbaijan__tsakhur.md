## Overview

The Tsakhur or Saxur are a people of northern Azerbaijan and southern Dagestan in Russia. They number around 30,000 people. They live in Azerbaijan’s Zaqatala region, where they make up 14% of the population, and in Gakh, where they constitute less than 2%; in Dagestan, they live in the mountainous parts of the Rutulsky district. Tsakhur belongs to the Lezgic group of the Northeast Caucasian language family. The group’s documented folk-culture profile includes sheep herding, carpet-weaving, knitting, and other handicrafts, alongside skills as stonemasons, tailors, and carpenters. Most Tsakhurs speak Tsakhur as their native language, while bilingualism in Tsakhur and Azeri is high.

## Language & religion

Tsakhur is a Northeast Caucasian language of the Lezgic group. It is spoken mostly in rural areas of Azerbaijan’s Zaqatala and Qakh rayons and in the mountainous parts of Dagestan’s Rutul region. The language has two major dialects, Tsakh and Gelmets. The first written documentation dates to 1895, and the first description of Tsakhur grammar was published in 1913. A literary form was developed in the 1930s. From 1934, Tsakhur was taught in primary schools in Azerbaijan and Dagestan; its use in Azerbaijan was discontinued in 1938 and regained status in 1989. The alphabet is based on Latin script in Azerbaijan and Cyrillic in Dagestan; attempts to write Tsakhur in Arabic script existed as early as the 11th century. Tsakhur is taught in grades 1 to 4 in Tsakhur-populated regions, and newspapers and radio broadcasts are available. It is one of twelve official languages of Dagestan, but has no official status in Azerbaijan. The sources state that Tsakhurs converted from Christianity to Islam by the 11th century.
