## Overview

The Bats, also known as the Batsbi or Ts’ova-Tush, are a Nakh-speaking people of Georgia associated historically with Tusheti and the Ts’ova Gorge. Most currently live in Zemo Alvani in Kakheti, near Akhmeta, while some families live in Tbilisi and other larger Georgian towns. Around half of Zemo Alvani’s approximately 7,000 inhabitants are of Bats origin. The Bats language belongs to the Nakh branch of Northeast Caucasian languages and is closely related to Chechen and Ingush, although it is not mutually intelligible with either. The group is significant in folk-culture research because its language, migration history, and customs preserve connections between Tushetian mountain communities and Georgian lowland life.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The Bats villages in the Ts’ova Gorge were Ts’aro, Shavts’qala, Nazarta, Nadirta, Mozarta, Indurta, Sagirta, and Etelta. The sources describe these settlements as inhabited by extended families. The Batsbi later migrated from the gorge to lowland settlements around Alvani in Kakheti.

## Music & performance

The sources used mention a song in Batsbur through an external link.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that Bats customs and traditions now resemble those of other Eastern Georgian mountaineers, particularly the Tush, and that deeper pagan-religious links exist between the Tush and neighbouring Khevsur.

## Oral tradition & literature

Bats folk legends preserve a tradition that the Bats came from the area of Vabua, also called Fappi, in mountainous Ingushetia. These legends are cited as evidence concerning the group’s origins. The first Tsova-Tushino writer named in the sources is I. Tsiskarov, who acknowledged the Tsova-Tush origins from the Ingush, or Galgai.

## Language & religion

Batsbi, also called Batsbur Mott or Batsba Moṭṭ, is an endangered Nakh language spoken in Georgia. It belongs to the Nakh branch of the Northeast Caucasian language family and is not mutually intelligible with Chechen or Ingush. The language had 2,500 to 3,000 speakers in 1975, but only several hundred Tsova-Tush people are described as speaking it today. Batsbur is principally a spoken language; Georgian has been used for writing and trade for centuries, and all Batsbi now speak Georgian, usually with a Tushetian or Kakhetian accent. Batsbi has one dialect, a vigesimal numeral system, two grammatical numbers, and nine noun cases. The first grammar of Batsbi, *Über die Thusch-Sprache*, was compiled by Anton Schiefner.

The sources describe the Bats as considering themselves Georgians and as not traditionally following Vainakh customs or law. They mention deeper pagan-religious links between the Tush and the neighbouring Khevsur.
