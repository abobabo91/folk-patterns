## Overview

The Abazins, also known as the Abaza or Abazinians, are an ethnic group of the Northwest Caucasus closely related to the Abkhaz and Circassian peoples. They live mainly in Turkey, Syria, Jordan, Egypt, Karachay-Cherkessia and Stavropol Krai in the North Caucasus region of Russia. The Tapanta branch historically lived between the Besleney and Kabardian princedoms on the upper Kuban. According to the 2010 Russian census, there were 43,341 Abazins in Russia. Their culture and traditions are similar to those of the Circassians, while their historical language belongs to the Northwest Caucasian language family.

## Foodways

Historically, the Abazin engaged in animal herding and some farming.

## Language & religion

The Abaza language is a Northwest Caucasian language most closely related to Abkhaz and more distantly related to the Ubykh and Circassian languages. Two Abaza dialects are spoken in Karachay-Cherkessia: Ashkharua and Tapanta. The Abazins are dominantly Sunni Muslims. They first encountered Islam during migrations to the Abazinia region through contact with the Nogais and other Muslim people, and adopted Islam through the influence of Muslim merchants and missionaries from the 16th to 19th centuries.
