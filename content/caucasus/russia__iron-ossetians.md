## Overview

The Irons are a subgroup of the Ossetians who speak the Iron dialect of the Eastern Iranian Ossetian language. They live chiefly in the Caucasus, including the lowland villages of North Ossetia. The majority profess Russian Orthodoxy; a small minority, mainly in the lowland villages of North Ossetia, profess Sunni Islam, while the Uatsdin faith has also been preserved by a minority. The ethnonym Iron has the same root, *arya*, as Iran and many Iranian-speaking peoples. The Alans are described as precursors of the Iron. Following Mongol invasions in the 1200s and incursions by Tamerlane in the late 1300s, Alans retreated into remote Caucasian valleys. The Irons became a distinct ethnic group from the Digor.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that, in the late Soviet period and later in the 1990s, Ossetian intellectuals began to reclaim their Alanian heritage in an attempt to unite Ossetian subgroups divided by dialect and religion.

## Language & religion

The Irons speak the Iron dialect of the Eastern Iranian Ossetian language. The majority profess Russian Orthodoxy, a small minority profess Sunni Islam mainly in the lowland villages of North Ossetia, and the Uatsdin faith has also been preserved by a minority. By the Russian conquest in the late 1700s, Orthodox Christianity had become the dominant religion among the Irons after a revival. This differed from the Digors, who became majority Islamic.
