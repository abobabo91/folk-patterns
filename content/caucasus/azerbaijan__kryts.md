## Overview

The Kryts, who self-designate as *хърыцӏаь* and are also called Gryz or *qrızlar*, are a people of Azerbaijan. They reside in several villages in the Quba, Khachmaz, Ismayilli and Gabala regions, as well as in Baku and Sumgait. They have traditionally been grouped among the Shahdagh peoples, together with the Budukh people and Khinalug people. In 2005, their number was reported as between 10,000 and 15,000; a figure extracted from family lists for 1886 records 7,767 people. Their language belongs to the Lezgic branch of the Nakh-Dagestan language family. The sources identify livestock raising, carpet and rug manufacture, and woolen patterned socks as elements of Kryt material culture.

## Language & religion

Kryts, also called Kryc, is a Lezgic language of the Northeast Caucasian language family. It was spoken in parts of the Quba Rayon of Azerbaijan by 6,000 people in 1975. Its dialects are Kryts, Jek, Khaput, Yergyudzh and Alyk; the sources state that these are sufficiently distinct to have only partial mutual intelligibility and could also be considered separate languages in a dialect continuum. Kryts is classified as “severely endangered” by UNESCO’s Atlas of the World’s Languages in Danger. In 2020, a dictionary was published in the Khaput variety using the Latin script. The Kryts are predominantly Sunni Muslims.
