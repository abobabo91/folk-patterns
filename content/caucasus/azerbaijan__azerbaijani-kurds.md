## Overview

The Kurds in Azerbaijan form part of the historically significant Kurdish population in the post-Soviet space. Kurdish presence in the Caucasus is associated with the Shaddadid dynasty in the 10th and 11th centuries, while some Kurdish tribes were recorded in Karabakh by the end of the sixteenth century. The contemporary Kurdish population largely descends from migrants from 19th-century Qajar Iran. Historically, Kurdish communities lived in areas including Karabakh, Zangezur, Mughan, Nakhchivan, Sharur-Daralagoz, Aresh, Lachin, Qubadli and Zangilan. Estimates from the 1920s placed the population at around 40 thousand; estimates in the 1990s ranged from 10 thousand to 200 thousand. Assimilation into Azerbaijani society was extensive.

## Dance & theatre

A traditional Kurdish puppet theatre called *kilim arasi* functioned in Aghjakand during the 1930s.

## Oral tradition & literature

Shamil Asgarov is identified as a poet and folklorist.

## Language & religion

Kurds in Azerbaijan were predominantly Shi'a Muslim, like most Azeris, unlike the majority of Kurds. Shared religion and cultural elements contributed to rapid assimilation. Statistical data from 1886 reported that Kurds in Jabrayil, Arash and partly Javanshir spoke Azeri as a first language. According to the first Soviet census in 1926, only 3,100 people, or 8.3% of Azerbaijan’s Kurdish population, spoke Kurdish.
