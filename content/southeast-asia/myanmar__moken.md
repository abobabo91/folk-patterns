## Overview

The Moken, also called Mawken or Morgan, are an Austronesian people associated with the Mergui Archipelago, the Surin Islands, and the coast and islands of the Andaman Sea in Myanmar and Thailand. Their traditional way of life is semi-nomadic and strongly dependent on the sea. Moken knowledge of marine and forest environments supports fishing, foraging, boatbuilding, and the use of plants for food, medicine, construction, and other purposes. The Moken language is a distinct Austronesian language, classified within the Malayo-Polynesian group. Their culture is significant in folk-cultural terms because of its maritime subsistence, kin-based mobility, boat traditions, oral knowledge, and distinctive relationship with the sea.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Moken temporary settlements include camps established on the mainland during the monsoon season, which falls between May and October. The sources describe huts among the objects for which Moken use plant species. Moken mobility historically involved living on boats during the dry north-east monsoon season, when the sea is relatively calm. These boats, called *kabang*, served as transportation as well as kitchen, bedroom, and living area. The last *kabang* of the Surin Islands was built in 2006, and an initiative to revive the tradition began in 2018.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do describe nets, spears, boats, and other tools used in subsistence activities. Moken plant knowledge includes 53 species used for the construction of huts, boats, and tools, while 54 species are used for other purposes. The earlier *kabang koman* was a dug-out boat equipped with a salacca gunwale. Its parts were fitted together using natural resources found on land.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The Moken language article gives examples involving an earring, *cʰɔbaŋ*, and the verb *ŋɔbaŋ*, “to put on earring,”.

## Music & performance

They do identify the Moken language as an oral Malayo-Polynesian language spoken in southern Myanmar and Southern Thailand. The language is spoken by inhabitants who refer to themselves as Moken and Mawken. Its main speakers are described as belonging to the elder generations, while limited literacy makes transmission difficult and has also helped conserve the language. The sources further describe traditional boating skills and knowledge of the sea, marine life, and forests.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe ancestral worship in which supernatural beings are regularly presented with food offerings. Moken mobility is also connected to seasonal conditions: during the dry north-east monsoon season, when the sea is relatively calm, people historically lived on their *kabang* boats; during the monsoon season between May and October, they established temporary mainland camps, built additional boats, and foraged in the forest.

The Moken in the Surin Islands were able to recognize the approach of the tsunami that struck on 26 December 2004. Their ancestors’ legends included the “seven rollers” and the *laboon*, described in the sources as a giant wave.

## Foodways

Moken subsistence is based heavily on the sea. Their knowledge of marine environments enables them to live from fauna and flora using simple tools such as nets and spears. Moken children are described as freediving to collect clams and sea cucumbers. More recently, Moken communities have traded sea cucumbers, edible bird’s nests, and marine products such as pearls at local markets in exchange for other necessities.

The sources state that the Moken use 83 plant species for food and 33 for medicinal purposes. Food offerings are made to supernatural beings in the context of ancestral worship. During epidemics, infected members remain at a location with a small amount of provisions while healthy members depart.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Moken language is oral, and the sources state that its lack of literacy makes transmission difficult while also helping conserve the language. The Moken language article records ancestral legends of the “seven rollers” and the *laboon*, a giant wave, in connection with the tsunami experienced in the Surin Islands in December 2004.

## Language & religion

Moken is a Malayo-Polynesian language within the Austronesian language family. It is spoken in Myanmar and Thailand and is closely related to the Moklen language, while being distantly related to Urak Lawoi’. The sources list the varieties Dung, Jait, Lebi or L’be, Niawi, and Jadiak. Dung, Jait, Lebi, and Niawi are spoken in Myanmar, while Jadiak is spoken in Thailand. The language has an estimated 1,500 native speakers as of 2009, within a population of 4,000 Moken, and is described as threatened with extinction.

Moken society includes ancestral worship, with supernatural beings receiving food offerings.
