## Overview

The Intha, also called Insar and Innsar, are a Tibeto-Burman ethnic group living around Inle Lake in Myanmar. There are around 215,800 Insar. They live primarily in four cities bordering the lake, in numerous small villages along its shores, and on the lake itself; the entire lake area is in Nyaungshwe Township. Their origins are disputed: the Insar believe their ancestors arrived from the southern tip of modern-day Myanmar, while a commonly held theory places their migration from southern Myanmar during the 14th century. Their language, a divergent dialect of Burmese, is part of the southern Burmish languages of Shan State. Intha folk culture includes floating gardens, stilt houses, distinctive leg-rowing, specialized clothing, bitter soups, dances, and poem recitation.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Hat yar* is an indigenous type of longyi associated with the Insar village of Ywama. It is sewn with silk and cotton fabric in varying brown and yellow stripes.

*Chin in* is a tailored cotton jacket worn by Intha women during the pre-colonial era. It was worn over a penny cloth white upper garment and a satin and silk htamein decorated in alternating shades of light and dark pink stripes and waves.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name stripes and waves as textile decoration, including alternating shades of light and dark pink stripes and waves.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Insar attire includes a pink-colored short sleeved jacket and loose trousers for men. During the pre-colonial era, women wore tailored cotton jackets called *chin in* over a penny cloth white upper garment, together with a satin and silk *htamein* decorated in alternating shades of light and dark pink stripes and waves. The village of Ywama is associated with the indigenous longyi called *hat yar*, made from silk and cotton fabric in varying brown and yellow stripes.

### Architecture

Insar live in simple houses of wood and woven bamboo built on stilts.

## Music & performance

 They do describe poem recitation traditions called *taiktay*, in which Intha bachelors and maidens recite poetry. The sources also mention traditional dances called the *lansi*, *lunsi*, and *ozigyi* dances, but provide no further information about their music, instruments, occasions, or performance structure.

## Dance & theatre

Traditional Insar dances include the *lansi*, *lunsi*, and *ozigyi* dances.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state that Insar are overwhelmingly Buddhists, although there is a small Christian sect.

## Foodways

Insar cuisine is known for bitter soups called *saykha hin*. Their traditional beverage of choice is brewed green tea served with salt. Traditional meals consist of cooked rice and an array of curries served in a *daunglan*. The sources also state that the Insar are largely self-sufficient farmers and support themselves through vegetable farms on floating gardens.

## Oral tradition & literature

Intha bachelors and maidens participate in poem recitation traditions called *taiktay*.

## Language & religion

Intha is a southern Burmish language of Shan State, Burma, spoken by the Intha. It is a divergent dialect of Burmese, and colonial observers described it as resembling Burmese with a Shan accent. Unlike other dialects of Burmese, the Intha language does not exhibit voicing sandhi, similar to the Danu and Taungyo languages. Intha and standard Burmese differ in the pronunciation of certain phonemes and in a few hundred local vocabulary terms; Intha has 95% lexical similarity with standard Burmese. Intha and Danu are written using the Burmese alphabet. Insar are overwhelmingly Buddhists, with a small Christian sect also present.
