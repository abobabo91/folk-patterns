## Overview

The Bunong, also known as Phnong, Punong, or Pnong, are an indigenous ethnic group in Cambodia, found primarily in Mondulkiri and Ratanakiri. They are Cambodia’s largest indigenous highland ethnic group. Bunong communities also occur in neighbouring Vietnam, where Bunong-speaking peoples are recurrently referred to as Mnong. Their language, Bunong, belongs to the Bahnaric branch of Austroasiatic languages and is distantly related to Khmer. Bunong culture is closely connected with forest environments, highland rice cultivation, traditional medicine, spirit practices, ritual life, and knowledge of plants. The Bunong language and cultural traditions have been affected by war, relocation, changing agricultural practices, Christian missionary activity, and proximity to Khmer Buddhism.

## Festivals & rituals

Bunong spirit practices centre on spirit-gods called *brah-yaang*, who are said to live in surrounding forests, mountains, and rivers. Bunong people interact with these spirits through rituals, offering jar wine and sacrificed animals. The Spirit Forests are described as possessing especially powerful spirits. Ancestors also have an important place in ritual life and, like the spirit-gods, are called to join every ceremony. During the Vietnam war, in the early 1970s, a number of Bunong converted to Christianity during exile in South Vietnamese refugee camps. Bunong spirit practices have also been blended with Khmer Buddhism through proximity.

## Foodways

Highland rice varieties traditionally formed a staple of Bunong agriculture. They were grown in slash-and-burn or swidden plots called *mir*, typically located on valley slopes. These plots could be used for up to 5 years before being left to regrow into forest, allowing soil fertility to recover. Rice was supplemented with vegetables, plants, roots, and honey gathered from the forest. Over the last two decades, Bunong communities have increasingly shifted towards cash-crop cultivation, especially cassava and cashew. In ritual contexts, jar wine and sacrificed animals are offered to spirit-gods.

## Language & religion

Bunong is the native language of the Bunong people and belongs to the Bahnaric branch of Austroasiatic languages. It is distantly related to Khmer and to other languages spoken in the Cambodian highlands, while Jarai and Rade are identified as Austronesian languages closely related to Cham. Bunong has several dialects, some recognised by linguists as distinct languages. Most Bunong dialects are spoken in neighbouring Vietnam, except for Kraol, which is spoken within Cambodia. The majority of Bunong people are animists, while minorities follow Christianity, including Evangelical Protestantism and Roman Catholicism, or Theravada Buddhism. Traditional religion centres on spirit-gods, ancestors, forests, mountains, rivers, rituals, offerings, and sacrificed animals. Bunong spirit practices have also blended with Khmer Buddhism.
