## Overview

The Lamaholot, also known as the Lamahalot or Solorese, are an Indigenous ethnic group of Flores Island in Indonesia and the nearby islands of Solor, Adonara, and Lomblen. Their language belongs to the Malayo-Polynesian languages of the Austronesian language family, and the number of speakers is between 150,000 and 200,000. Lamaholot is divided into many dialects and is used as a lingua franca for communication among ethnic groups around Larantuka and Solor. On Flores, Lamaholot people also speak Larantuka Malay, or Bahasa Nagi. Their folk culture includes a war dance known as **Hedung**, rich verbal and musical folklore, distinctive mountain settlements, agricultural practices, fishing, and specialized coastal trade.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources state that Lamaholot people adopt Malay-style clothing.

### Architecture

Traditional mountain settlements are surrounded by a dense hedge of corals. Their houses are rectangular and skeleton-pillared, with a high, steep roof. Before the beginning of the 20th century, long houses existed. Modern settlements are coastal farms, and dwellings are designed for a small family.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They state only that residents of some coastal villages specialize in intermediary trade in textiles, clothing, and agricultural implements.

## Music & performance

The Lamaholot people preserve rich verbal and musical folklore.

## Dance & theatre

**Hedung** is identified as the famous traditional dance of the Lamaholot people. It is a war dance.

## Foodways

The main occupation of the Lamaholot is manual tropical farming. Important crops include corn, dried rice, millet, yam, cassava, sweet potato, legumes, pumpkin, and various fruits and vegetables. Fishing is developed on the coast, while livestock plays an auxiliary role. The main dietary food includes boiled corn and rice with vegetable and fish seasonings.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Lamaholot people preserve rich verbal folklore. A children's book in the Muhang dialect, *Walde Nenang Uran Wair*, was published in 2022; Muhang is spoken by the Ata Tana 'Ai people living in Sikka Regency.

## Language & religion

Lamaholot, also known as Solor or Solorese, is a Central Malayo-Polynesian dialect cluster of Flores, Indonesia. The varieties are not all mutually intelligible, and Keraf reported 18 languages under the name. Lamaholot dialects are commonly divided into western, central, and eastern groupings. The language shows evidence of a Papuan, or non-Austronesian, substratum, with about 50 percent of its lexicon being non-Austronesian. Most Lamaholot people are Roman Catholics, following conversion from the 16th century onwards under Portuguese influence and further conversions during the 20th century through missionary efforts. A minority are Muslims. A few Lamaholot follow a traditional monotheistic religion centered on Lera Wulan Tanah Ekan, described as the combined power of the Sun, Moon, and Earth; death cults and shamanism are also mentioned.
