## Overview

The Togutil, also known as O'Hongana Manyawa or Inner Tobelo, are an indigenous group with a semi-nomadic lifestyle in the jungles of Totodoku, Tukur-Tukur, Lolobata, Kobekulo, and Buli, within Aketajawe-Lolobata National Park in North Halmahera Regency, North Maluku, Indonesia. Their communities are often categorized as living in isolation in the inner jungle, in contrast to the coastal Tobelo. Estimates place the total population between 1,500 and 3,000 people, with approximately 300–500 living in isolation in the forest. The Togutil language is identified as ISO 639-3:Tuj; it is also described as a dialect of Tobelo by some classifications. Their folk-culture significance in the sources lies especially in their semi-nomadic forest life, hunting and gathering, river settlements, and knowledge of the jungle environment.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Some Togutil continue to wear traditional loincloths, although most have adopted modern clothing.

### Architecture

Togutil settlements are usually arranged in groups of communities along river banks. Houses are made of wood and bamboo, with Pelem leaves, a type of Livistona, used as roofing. Generally, the houses do not have walls or wooden flooring. Some Togutil have also settled in houses built by Indonesian authorities as part of social integration programs, including in Dodaga, Maba.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention houses made from wood and bamboo, Pelem-leaf roofing, hunting, fishing, horticulture, and the gathering of resins, megapode eggs, and antlers for exchange or sale.

## Music & performance

They state only that the Togutil have been the subject of programs on Indonesian national TV presenting their habits and customs.

## Festivals & rituals

The historical source describes an indigenous cosmology that included ancestor worship, but states that these beliefs and practices were discarded by many people who converted to Christianity. It also reports that converted community members discourage attempts to reinitiate these practices. No further ritual details are given.

## Foodways

Sago is a staple food for the Togutil. Traditionally, they are described as nomadic hunter-gatherers who hunt wild boar, deer, and other animals, fish, and rely on sago palms as their main source of carbohydrates. They also harvest megapode eggs, resins, and antlers to sell to people from coastal areas.

Their gardens may contain bananas, cassava, sweet potatoes, papayas, and sugar cane, although the gardens are not cultivated intensively because of their semi-nomadic lifestyle.

## Oral tradition & literature

A local legend says that the Togutil descend from coastal dwellers who moved into the jungles to avoid taxes. The sources explicitly state that there is no evidence supporting this account of their origins.

## Language & religion

The Togutil use the Togutil language, identified as ISO 639-3:Tuj. Some classifications describe it as a dialect of the Tobelo language spoken by coastal Tobelo communities. The related language material recognizes varieties called Tugutil and states that these may include additional dialectal variants, although these have not been satisfactorily documented.

Ethnos360, formerly New Tribes Mission, built a church and established a village at Tanjung Lili in eastern North Maluku in 1982. The sources state that proselytisation was largely successful and that almost all people who formerly lived along the Waisango, Lili, and Afu Rivers had converted to Christianity. Previous indigenous cosmological beliefs, including ancestor worship, are described as having been discarded by converts as incompatible with Christianity.
