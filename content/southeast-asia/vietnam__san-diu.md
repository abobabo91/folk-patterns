## Overview

The Sán Dìu, also known as San Deo, Trai, Trai Dat and Man Quan Coc, are an ethnic group in northern Vietnam. They predominantly speak the Sán Dìu language, a Yue Chinese variety, and are believed to have migrated from the Liangguang region of modern-day China around the 15th century. Their estimated population was 117,500 in 2000, while the 2019 census recorded 183,004. The group is mainly concentrated around the Tam Đảo range in Thái Nguyên Province. Their documented religious landscape includes Mahayana Buddhism and Taoism, alongside elements of animism and veneration of the dead; about 400 are adherents of the Catholic Church and a few are evangelical Protestants.

## Language & religion

The Sán Dìu predominantly speak the Sán Dìu language, a Yue Chinese variety. It is suggested that some still speak Iu Mien. The sources also record the Chinese name 山由族, the pinyin form Shān yóu zú, the Jyutping form saan1 jau4 zuk6, the Cantonese Yale form Sanyau Juk, the Chữ nôm form 𠊛山由, and the Vietnamese alphabet form Người Sán Dìu. The major religions are Mahayana Buddhism and Taoism, with elements of animism and veneration of the dead; about 400 people are adherents of the Catholic Church, and a few are evangelical Protestants.
