## Overview

The Kadu, also called Asak, are an ethnic group in Myanmar who speak the Kadu language. They mostly reside in the northwestern hills, centred around Katha, and primarily live in Sagaing Region, scattered across 98 villages. Their traditional territory includes the Chindwin River valley and the townships of Banmauk, Indaw, and Pinlebu in Katha District. The Kadu population is estimated at approximately 180,000. Kadu is a Sino-Tibetan language of the Sal branch, closely related to Ganan and Sak. The Kadu traditionally cultivate rice on irrigated terraces. Their documented folk-cultural significance in these sources lies chiefly in their language, historical settlement, ethnic relationships, and agricultural environment.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Kadu were recorded under different names in historical sources. In Old Burmese they were called the Kantu or Sak-Kantu; their endonym is Asak, commonly shortened to Sak. In the 13th century History of Yuan they were recorded as the Jiandu (建都), while the Travels of Marco Polo recorded them as the Caindu.

## Language & religion

Kadu, Kado, or Asak is a Sino-Tibetan language of the Sal branch spoken in Sagaing Region, Myanmar. Its documented dialects are Settaw, Mawkhwin, and Mawteik, with Mawteik described as extinct or nearly extinct. The language has 30,000 speakers in the source and is considered endangered. Many speakers have shifted to Burmese or are bilingual in Burmese. Kadu has eight monophthongs and a diphthong /ai/, 20 consonants, and three tones: high, mid, and low. It is an SOV language. Native numerals remain only for one, two, three, and four; other numerals have been replaced by Tai Laing and Burmese numerals. By the early 20th century, most Kadu had assimilated and adopted Burmese customs, including Theravada Buddhism.
