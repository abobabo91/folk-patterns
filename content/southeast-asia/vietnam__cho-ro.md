## Overview

The Chơ Ro, also called Chau Ro and Do Ro, are a Mon–Khmer people in Vietnam. Most Chơ Ro live in Đồng Nai and Ho Chi Minh City, and their population was 29,520 in 2019. Their language, Chrau, is a Bahnaric language of the South Bahnaric subgroup. Chrau is spoken in southern Vietnam, including areas around Biênhòa and Binh Tuy, and extends from the Langa River toward Tánhlinh, with communities described around Hàmtân, Vũngtàu, Longthành, Phướctuy, and the Đồngnai River. The Chơ Ro are documented through their language, its dialects and historical influences, and the New Year Festival called Yang Pa, whose purpose is worshipping the Rice God.

## Language & religion

Chrau, also known as Charuo, Chauro, Jro, Ro, Tamun, Cho Ro, Choro, Chíoro, and Doro, is a Bahnaric language in the South Bahnaric subgroup. It is spoken by some of the 22,000 ethnic Cho Ro people in southern Vietnam, while fewer than 20,000 native speakers are reported. The language has no lexical tone, although it has significant sentence intonation; the sources also describe influences from Chinese, Vietnamese, Laos, Cambodia, and Malaysia. Jro and Prâng are identified as dialects. Chrau is endangered, and Vietnamese is commonly used in markets and by much of the newer generation. The sources identify worship of the Rice God in *Yang Pa*.
