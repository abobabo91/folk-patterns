## Overview

The H're are an ethnic group of Vietnam who speak a language in the Mon–Khmer family. Most H're live in Quảng Ngãi, while minorities live in Bình Định and Kon Tum provinces. In 2015, Quảng Ngãi province had 132,745 H're: 48,852 in Ba Tơ District, 65,823 in Sơn Hà District, 13,478 in Minh Long District, and 4.592 in Quảng Ngãi city and other districts. Around 5,800 Hre lived in An Lão District of Bình Định Province in 1996. The sources document wet-rice cultivation, wickerwork, weaving, and the use of the chiêng Ba gong in traditional festivals.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Weaving** — Weaving is identified as one of the H're handicrafts, although it is now less developed and remains only in a few areas.

**Wickerwork** — Wickerwork is identified as an H're handicraft, but the sources provide no further description of its materials, techniques, forms, or visual characteristics.

## Music & performance

H're people use a special kind of gong called *chiêng Ba* in their traditional festivals. It is a trio consisting of a father, a mother, and a child gong. Performances based on four basic melodies are named Chinh Năng, Chinh K’oa, Chinh H’lay, and Chinh Tuguốc.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that H're people widely use the chiêng Ba in their traditional festivals.

## Foodways

The H're are mainly residents of wet-rice cultivation, while a small part cultivate slash-and-burn agriculture. Their popular livestock are buffalos, pigs, dogs, and chickens.

## Language & religion

The H're speak a language in the Mon–Khmer family. In earlier times, they called themselves according to the river name of their residence. During the Nguyễn dynasty, they were recognized by names including Chăm Rê, Chăm Quảng Ngãi, Thượng Ba Tơ, Man Thạch Bích, and Mọi Ðá Vách.
