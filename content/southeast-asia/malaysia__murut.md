## Overview

The Murut live across parts of Malaysia, Indonesia and Brunei. Their culture includes shifting cultivation, communal longhouses, beadwork, handicrafts, gong music, festivals, warrior traditions and dances. They speak several closely linked Murutic languages. Most are Christian, while some retain traditional beliefs.

## Material culture

Murut material culture is marked by woven mats, baskets, beadwork, decorated clothing, gongs, bamboo instruments and ancient jars.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Murut textile and basket traditions use bamboo, rattan, beads and strong black-and-white or floral designs.

- **Kelarai handicrafts** (*Kelarai*) — These Keningau and Tenom handicrafts use salingkawang, bamboo and rattan with white-and-black floral and geometric patterns.
- **Pinongkoloh dress** (*pinongkoloh*) — This Tahol women's dress is usually black and decorated with colourful floral motifs and beads.
- **Woven mats** (*apin lantungan*) — Tahol Muruts use apin and lantungan for woven mats, including apin lantungan, lantungan ulia and lantungan puaran.
- **Sampoi cloth** (*Sampoi*) — Woven Sampoi cloth is linked with Murut handicrafts and the Kalimaran festival.
- **Body armour** (*tavil*) — The former back-cover armour called tavil had a woven surface with sinusu motifs and crossing lines.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Murut dress commonly uses black cloth, beadwork, belts and distinctive head coverings.

- **Warrior headdress** (*lalandau*) — The lalandau was a feathered head covering whose number of Argus pheasant feathers showed death and enemies killed.
- **Rain and sun hat** (*silaung*) — The silaung, siraung or sirung protects people from rain and sunlight during farm work.
- **Tahol women's dress** (*pinongkoloh*) — The Tahol pinongkoloh includes a blouse, sarong, coin belt, beads, dangling headdress, necklace, wrist jewellery and waist decoration.
- **Coin belt** (*pipirot linggit*) — The pipirot linggit is a belt made from coins.

### Architecture

Murut communities formerly lived in communal longhouses, usually beside rivers.

- **Communal longhouse** — Longhouses were shared homes built near rivers, which served as transport routes and shaped settlement life.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Murut everyday objects include spirit jars, winnowing trays, baskets and tools made from local materials.

- **Spirit jar** (*tajau*) — Ancient Chinese pottery jars called tajau became places of spirits, and large ones were formerly used as coffins.
- **Winnowing tray** (*tikalis*) — The tikalis was used to winnow rice and as a wedding tray, and is now also a souvenir, decoration and dance prop.
- **Forest-produce basket** (*buyong*) — The buyong was formerly used to gather forest produce and has regional names including barait, sasaging, takiding and balait.
- **Baskets and mats** — Murut baskets include banso, bundasan, kalong, kapa, kapon, sasar, tapan, takinan and tinkapan.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Murut adornment centres on beads, coins, necklaces, wrist ornaments, feathers and dangling headpieces.

- **Necklace** (*rarangkol*) — The rarangkol is a necklace in the Tahol female dress.
- **Wrist jewellery** (*holong*) — The holong is wrist jewellery in the Tahol female dress.
- **Dangling headdress** (*sinikot*) — The sinikot is a dangling headdress in the Tahol female dress.
- **Waist decoration** (*bungkas*) — The bungkas is a white-bead waist decoration in the Tahol female dress.
- **Rattan bracelet** — British Museum records describe Murut bracelets made of rattan.

## Music & performance

Murut music uses gong ensembles, bamboo instruments and songs linked with ceremony, leisure, festivals and dance.

- **Gong ensemble** (*agung*) — Agung ensembles use large hanging shallow-sided gongs and are common among Murutic and Dusunic communities.
- **Two-string instrument** (*tangkung*) — The tangkung is a wooden, guitar-like instrument with two strings, usually played during leisure time.
- **Mouth organ** (*sompoton*) — The sompoton is a Murut instrument also owned by Dusuns and Kadazans.
- **Ceremonial instrument** (*kulundi*) — The kulundi is associated with Tangara Muruts of Inarad in religious or cultural ceremonies.

## Dance & theatre

Murut dance includes warrior dances, bamboo-pole dances, circle dances and the jumping heritage of Lansaran.

- **Bamboo-pole warrior dance** (*Magunatip*) — In Magunatip, dancers move their feet between clapping bamboo poles without getting trapped.
- **Jumping dance and game** (*Lansaran*) — In Lansaran, men and women jump on a bamboo floor to reach the suspended kinkilat, a tradition once used to welcome victorious warriors.
- **Tahol dances** (*sasayau*) — Tahol Muruts perform sasayau and alang ra illau.
- **Bookan dance** (*mangalai*) — Bookan Muruts perform mangalai.
- **Kolor dance** (*magalai*) — Kolor Muruts perform magalai and their Murut version of sumazau.

## Festivals & rituals

Murut festivals include the March harvest festival, beauty pageants, wedding and funeral feasts, and older warrior rites.

- **Kalimaran beauty pageant** (*Ralaa Kalimaran*) — The Ralaa Kalimaran pageant is held during the annual March harvest festival, often in Tenom District.
- **Young women's pageant** (*Unduk Ngadau*) — Unduk Ngadau is held in May with Murut, Kadazan, Dusun and Rungus contestants.
- **Young women contestants** (*ralaa*) — Ralaa are young women who compete for the pageant title.
- **Warrior headhunting rite** (*ngayau*) — Lansaran was formerly held to welcome Murut warriors returning victorious from ngayau.
- **Wedding and funeral feasts** — Murut wedding and funeral feasts can last several days and include tajau jars.

## Foodways

Murut foodways combine shifting cultivation with hunting and river fishing.

- **Hill paddy farming** — Muruts practised shifting cultivation of hill paddy.
- **Tapioca farming** — Tapioca was another cultivated food.
- **Hunting** — Hunting supplemented the Murut diet.
- **River fishing** — River fishing also supplemented the Murut diet.

## Oral tradition & literature

Murut oral tradition includes festival folklore, stories of sacrifice and beliefs about ancestral spirits.

- **Ralaa origin story** (*Ralaa*) — Murut folklore says Ralaa was a young woman whose sacrifice was linked with the production of mats, silaung and Sampoi cloth.
- **Ancestral-spirit belief** — During the 1915 drought, spiritual leaders said food shortages were caused by ancestral spirits angered by the taxation system.
- **Basketwork documentation** — George Cathcart Woolley recorded Murut basketwork, motifs and symbolic themes through interviews with Murut communities.

## Language & religion

Murutic languages form a closely linked family, while Murut religion includes Christianity, Islam and surviving traditional beliefs.

- **Murutic language family** (*Murutic*) — Murutic is a family of about six closely intertwined Austronesian languages.
- **Lowland lingua franca** (*Timugon*) — Timugon serves as a lingua franca for lowland Muruts.
- **Highland language** (*Tagol Murut*) — Tagol Murut is used among highland Muruts.
- **Traditional religion** — Muruts were originally pagan-animist, and some still retain traditional religion, especially in deeper Bornean jungles.
- **Head-hunting belief** (*ngayau*) — Headhunting formerly held an important role in Murut spiritual beliefs.
