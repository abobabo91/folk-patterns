## Overview

The Chin are highland Tibeto-Burman peoples living in ridgetop villages above 1,000 metres in western Myanmar. They comprise five main groupings (Asho, Daai, Khami, Laimi, Zomi) and over 50 subgroups, each speaking distinct Kuki-Chin languages. Their material culture is defined by women's backstrap-loom weaving, heavy body ornament including now-extinct facial tattoos, and a rich repertoire of tribal blankets, tunics, and skirts. Today most Chin are Christian, though feast-of-merit and guardian-spirit traditions persist.

## Material culture

Chin material culture emphasizes textiles and body ornament over ceramics and metalwork, with weaving the central craft and marking tribal identity.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Weaving on the backstrap loom by women produces narrow warp-faced cotton panels seamed into blankets, skirts, and tunics with supplementary-weft geometric patterns.

- **Chin blanket** (*puan (Zomi/Mizo), hnisan (other)*) — Large ceremonial cloths in deep indigo or madder-red with dense supplementary-weft bands, used as wedding gifts, shrouds, and feast-of-merit rewards.
- **Puan Ropui** — The most prized wrapped tube-skirt worn by women, shared with the Mizo, with heavy horizontal bands of red and white on black.
- **Hakha tunic** (*Cawnhnak Ngo*) — Women's long tunic from the Hakha-Laimi tract with dense diamond-and-lozenge supplementary weft.
- **Zomi Tedim blankets** — Northern blankets from Tedim, Sizang, and Sukte with narrow warp stripes in indigo, red, and cream, and discrete supplementary-weft motif bands.
- **Khumi shoulder bags and skirts** — Short striped skirts and small carrying bags woven by Khumi/Mro women with beadwork edging.

### Clothing & dress

Everyday dress is wrapped loincloths or lungis with plain jackets; tribal identity shows clearest in ceremonial dress worn at feasts of merit.

- **Facial tattoo** (*Muun, Uppriu, Dai, Yindu, Magan patterns*) — Applied to southern Chin women in adolescence with soot and thorn needles as marks of beauty and tribal affiliation; now virtually extinct.
- **Hornbill headdress** — Worn by Tedim chiefs at feasts of merit, made from hornbill tail feathers.
- **Silver hairpins** — Worn wound into a knot at the nape by married women in Laimi and Zomi tracts, often paired with heavy amber-and-glass bead necklaces.
- **Brass earplugs** — Large cylindrical plugs formerly worn by Khumi and Mro women as body marks.
- **Cane leg-rings** — Formerly worn by Khumi and Mro women as status and tribal markers.

### Architecture

Chin villages are ridge-top pile-built timber houses roofed with thatch or corrugated iron, with the northern longhouse type being diagnostic.

- **Northern Chin longhouse** — Two-level rectangular timber house with a lower livestock and storage floor on posts and an upper living floor entered by notched-log ladder, with open front veranda.
- **Mithun-skull rack** — Bamboo rack hung above the hearth storing meat, seed maize, and sacrificed mithun skulls, with number and arrangement marking household rank in the feast-of-merit cycle.
- **Carved house post** — Front posts of wealthy men's houses carved with human, tiger, and hornbill figures, or ancestor memorial posts in Hakha and Zophei villages.
- **Khua-hrum stone altar** (*khua-hrum*) — Stone altar or shrine at Laimi and Zomi village entrances, focus of the village guardian-spirit cult.
- **Skull post** — Formerly held at village entrances in headhunter-era Chin settlements as a display of prowess.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Chin are not a strong ceramic tradition; prestige is placed on imported stoneware jars and metalwork from household forges.

- **Martaban-type jar** — Dark-glazed Shan or Chinese stoneware jars used to ferment rice beer and passed down as heirlooms.
- **Dao machete** (*dao*) — Iron blade forged at village blacksmith shops, the signature hand tool of Chin life and warfare.
- **Carved rice-measure baskets** — Utilitarian carved wooden vessels for grain storage, representing a household and sculptural tradition.
- **Brass and bronze gongs** — Traded from Shan and Kachin smiths, used for rhythm at feasts, funerals, and New Year celebrations.
- **Zu-hna bamboo straw** (*zu-hna*) — Hollowed bamboo tubes used to drink communal rice beer at feasts and ritual occasions.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Chin women's ornament is heavy and stacked, with trade beads, brass and silver spacers, and distinctive pectoral discs.

- **Amber and carnelian necklaces** — Trade beads from Yunnan and Bengal strung with brass and silver spacers, worn by women as status markers.
- **Venetian chevron beads** — Glass beads of European origin found in older Zomi collections, strung into necklaces and ornaments.
- **Brass pectoral disc** — Flat disk worn on the chest by Laimi women, hammered from brass or silver.
- **Silver comb ornaments** — Ornamental combs worn wound into a knot at the nape, marking married status.
- **Brass or ivory ear plugs** — Large cylindrical ornaments worn by Khumi women as distinctive body adornment.

## Music & performance

Chin music is dominated by voice and the bronze gong, with gong sets sounding rhythm at all major feasts and strong antiphonal singing traditions.

- **Flat brass gong** (*dar, darkhuang*) — Traded from Shan and Kachin smiths, the most important instrument used in matched pairs at feasts of merit, funerals, and New Year, stored as household wealth.
- **Khuang Cawi** — A feast-of-merit dance-and-song complex among the Hakha and Zophei with days of gong music, ceremonial rice-beer drinking through bamboo straws, and communal dance.
- **Courtship songs** (*hla*) — Antiphonal songs exchanged between young men and women at village gatherings and work in swidden fields.
- **Funeral narrative songs** — Long antiphonal laments recounting the deceased's genealogy, hunts, and feasts to accompany the body.
- **Baptist choral singing** — Four-part harmony singing introduced by American Baptist missionaries after 1899, now the dominant public music tradition.

## Dance & theatre

Chin dance is communal and tied to the feast-of-merit and New Year cycles, with no mask or shadow-puppet drama tradition.

- **Khuang Cawi dance** (*khuang cawi*) — Feast-of-merit circle dance in which the host and his wife are carried on a decorated platform while the village circles them to gong rhythm.
- **Sarlamkai / Rallu Lam dance** (*sarlamkai, rallu lam*) — Ex-headhunter victory dance of the Zomi-Mizo continuum performed with dao and shield to gong rhythm.
- **Bamboo dance** (*cheraw*) — Pairs of dancers step between clapped bamboo poles to rhythm, best known from the Mizo but shared with several Chin subgroups.
- **Courtship line dances** — Harvest-time line dances of southern Chin (Cho, Asho) for courtship and celebration.

## Festivals & rituals

The old Chin ritual calendar centered on swidden agriculture and the guardian spirit khua-hrum; today secular and Christian festivals blend with feast-of-merit traditions.

- **Chin National Day** — Celebrated on 20 February, commemorating the 1948 Falam conference that abolished hereditary chieftainship.
- **Feast of merit** (*tong, khuang cawi*) — A graded sequence of mithun sacrifices conferring the right to new blankets, house-post carvings, and stone memorials, the life's-work ritual of a Chin big-man.
- **Khuado** — Two- to three-day post-harvest thanksgiving of the Zomi (Tedim and Sizang) with gongs, rice-beer, and communal dance.
- **Fang Rual** — Post-harvest feast of the Hakha people with ritual food and community celebration.
- **Secondary burial** — Northern Chin practice of wrapping the corpse in the finest household blanket; southern Chin (Cho, Khumi) practiced cremation.

## Foodways

Chin cuisine is a hill-swidden cuisine centered on rice, maize, and millet with greens, chilies, and mithun meat at major feasts.

- **Sabuti** — Signature Zomi stew of dried maize kernels simmered for hours with pork bones and greens.
- **Vok ril** — Pork sausage stuffed with rice and blood, a traditional celebration dish.
- **Chin chilli relish** — Pounded paste of roasted dried chilli, garlic, and dried fish that has crossed over into wider Burmese cuisine.
- **Zu rice beer** (*zu*) — Household fermented rice drink central to Chin feasts, drunk communally through bamboo straws from a shared jar and offered to khua-hrum.
- **Sathu fermented paste** (*sathu*) — Fermented soybean paste comparable to Naga akhuni, used as a condiment and flavoring.

## Oral tradition & literature

Chin oral literature centers on genealogy, migration origins, and feast-of-merit narratives; a written literature in Chin languages has flourished since the early 20th century.

- **Chinlung origin myth** (*chinlung*) — Shared emergence myth of ancestors from a rock, cave, or hole, anchoring the pan-Chin/Zomi/Mizo sense of common origin.
- **Khampat banyan legend** (*khampat*) — A sapling planted at the khua-hrum altar in the Kabaw Valley before westward migration into the hills, with a pledge to return when its branches touch the ground.
- **Sukte oral tradition** — Preservation of the deeds of Khan Thuam and Kam Hau in the 19th-century rise of Tedim.
- **Feast-of-merit songs** — Oral narratives preserving village and lineage genealogies and the exploits of feast hosts.
- **Modern Chin literature** — Active written literature in Hakha Chin, Tedim, Falam, and Mizo since the Baptist mission press at Hakha, including hymnody, scripture, ethnography, and fiction by authors like Sing Khaw Khai and Vumson.

## Language & religion

Chin languages are Kuki-Chin Tibeto-Burman with Roman-based orthographies from Baptist missionaries; traditional spirit religion has been overlaid by predominantly Baptist Christianity.

- **Kuki-Chin languages** (*kukish*) — Branch of Tibeto-Burman with main written standards in Hakha Chin, Falam, Tedim/Zomi, Mizo, Cho, and Asho.
- **Khua-hrum guardian spirit** (*khua-hrum*) — Village guardian spirit central to traditional Chin religion, receiving sacrifices and offerings that persist even in Christian communities.
- **Baptist Christianity** — Introduced by American Baptist missionaries from 1899 onward, now the majority religion of the Chin with post–World War I mass conversion.
- **Mithun sacrifice** (*sial (mithun)*) — Central to both traditional spirit propitiation and the feast-of-merit ritual cycle, marking rank and prestige.
- **Roman orthography** — All Chin languages use Roman-based writing systems devised by Baptist missionaries; there was no pre-colonial indigenous script.

## Glossary

- *puan* — Wrapped tube-skirt worn by women; also large ceremonial blanket
- *hnisan* — Ceremonial blanket (term used in some Chin languages)
- *Cawnhnak Ngo* — Women's long tunic from Hakha-Laimi tract with dense diamond patterns
- *khuang cawi* — Feast-of-merit dance-and-song complex; also funeral narrative songs
- *hla* — Courtship songs exchanged between young men and women
- *khua-hrum* — Village guardian spirit; also stone altar honoring it
- *tong* — Feast of merit with mithun sacrifice
- *khuado* — Post-harvest thanksgiving festival of Zomi peoples
- *zu* — Household fermented rice beer
- *zu-hna* — Hollowed bamboo straw for drinking communal rice beer
- *dao* — Iron machete forged at village smithies
- *sial* — Mithun (gayal); sacrificial animal and wealth marker
- *sabuti* — Stew of dried maize kernels simmered with pork and greens
- *sathu* — Fermented soybean paste used as condiment
- *cheraw* — Bamboo dance performed with clapped poles
- *chinlung* — Origin myth of emergence from a rock or cave
- *khampat* — Banyan legend marking the pre-migration territory
