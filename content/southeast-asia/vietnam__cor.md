## Overview

The Cor, also known as Co, Col, or Cùa, are an ethnic group of Vietnam whose Vietnamese name is Người Co. Most Cor live in Quảng Ngãi and Quảng Nam, in Vietnam’s South Central Coast region. They numbered 40,442 in 2019. In 1996, they formed a slight majority in Trà Bồng District, which then also included Tây Trà District, numbering around 18,000 there. The Cor speak Cua, a language in the Mon–Khmer family. Their documented folk-culture practices include village leadership, militia organization, spirit beliefs, long-house architecture, slash-and-burn agriculture, distinctive songs and dances, gong and drum performance, and the Xa a'ni New Year.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Formerly, the Cor lived in long houses built on stilts called *X'lup*. More recently, the Co has built shorter houses at ground level.

## Music & performance

Cor music includes songs, dances, beat drums, and gongs. Folk songs called *Xru*, *Klu*, and *Agioi* are described as very popular.

## Dance & theatre

The Cor New Year, called *Xa a'ni*, includes sword dancing and gong dancing. The same celebration also includes animal offerings and a wrestling competition.

## Festivals & rituals

The Cor New Year is called *Xa a'ni*. It is celebrated through animal offerings, sword dancing, gong dancing, and wrestling competition.

The Cor believe that all things have souls. Their spiritual practices include worship of the souls of rice grains, the spirit of cinnamon barks, and the souls of livestock. They distinguish between good spirits, called *garu*, and bad spirits.

## Foodways

The Cor live mainly from slash-and-burn agriculture. They grow rice, maize, cassava, cinnamon, and other plants.

## Language & religion

The Cor speak Cua, a language in the Mon–Khmer family. In their religious beliefs, all things have souls, including rice grains, cinnamon barks, and livestock. They recognize good spirits called *garu* and bad spirits.
