## Overview

The Mangyan are eight indigenous groups living in the highlands of Mindoro island, each with its own language and customs. They moved inland centuries ago to escape lowland settlement and raids, and now live along highland rivers. They are known for their indigo textiles with pakudos embroidery, a living syllabic script of Indic origin, and a sustained poetic tradition.

## Material culture

Mangyan material culture emphasizes textiles, bamboo work, and objects tied to daily life in the highlands.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Hanunó'o textiles are exceptional, dyed indigo and embroidered with a distinctive design.

- **Indigo-dyed cloth with pakudos embroidery** (*pakudos*) — Hanunó'o textiles are dyed indigo blue and carry an embroidery design called pakudos placed at the back of the garment and also appearing on woven bags.
- **Rice-harvesting basket** (*buri*) — A basket made of buri palm leaf with cane and bark for collecting rice at harvest.
- **Woman's belt** (*salatid tag ina*) — A vine belt woven in a continuous round to be worn by women.
- **Money belt** (*apdus*) — A woven vegetable-fibre belt with a double layer that forms the pocket itself.
- **Shoulder bag** — A bag combining buri palm and nitu vine worn across the shoulder.

### Clothing & dress

Men wear loincloths and women wear skirts with shirts, with the finest designs found among the Hanunó'o.

- **Loincloth** (*bahag*) — A loincloth worn by men covering the lower body.
- **Woman's skirt and blouse** — Textiles of probably cotton worn as the standard female dress.
- **Embroidered white jacket** — A collected garment showing embroidered decoration.
- **Woman's torso band** — A band of vegetable fibre worn around the torso.
- **Man's head cloth** — A head cloth of probably cotton worn by men.

### Architecture

Mangyan communities live in seclusion along highland rivers, with ancestral claims over extensive forest lands.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Bamboo, coconut shell, and clay form the main household items, with tools and weapons for hunting and farming.

- **Smoking-pipe bowl** (*Kwaku*) — A clay pipe bowl for smoking.
- **Lime container** — A bamboo lime container incised with geometric designs, often kept with a jew's harp inside.
- **Container lid** (*sukluban bangon*) — A bamboo-and-fibre lid for a container.
- **Fishing spear** — A spear so thin it is shot through the barrel of a ballpoint pen and drawn by a rubber band.
- **Fishing goggles** (*salamin*) — Goggles with glass lenses waxed into wooden frames and held by a red rubber head band with nylon twine.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ornaments are made from beads, plant fibre, and leaf, with no recorded tattooing or ritual function.

- **Bead necklace** (*siwayang*) — A necklace strung from blue, yellow and orange glass beads.
- **Man's armband** — An armband made of vegetable fibre worn by men.
- **Man's ear pendants** — Ear pendants of beads and cotton thread worn by men.
- **Neck-ring** — A ring combining vegetable fibre with beads worn around the neck.
- **Woman's ear plugs** — Ear plugs of leaf worn by women.

## Music & performance

Mangyan instruments are portable and often made of bamboo, while vocal performance centers on rhythmic poetry rather than songs.

- **Jew's harp** — A portable instrument of wood and resin played and sometimes kept inside an incised bamboo lime container.
- **Flute** — A bamboo flute used for musical performance.
- **Violin** — An instrument of wood and human hair played with a bow of bamboo and human hair.
- **Seven-syllable poetic form** (*ambahan*) — A rhythmic poetic expression practiced by the Hanunó'o, presented through recitation and chanting or inscribed on bamboo.
- **Riddles** — Mind games and comical entertainment told in the evening as part of oral tradition.

## Festivals & rituals

Ritual life centers on rice cultivation, with specific rites at each phase to appease rice spirits.

- **First planting rite** (*panudlak*) — A rite performed at the beginning of rice planting to appease the rice spirits.
- **Rice binding** (*magbugkos*) — The binding of rice stalks that forms part of the harvest rites.
- **Post-harvest rite** (*pamag-uhan*) — A rite performed after the harvest is complete.
- **Rice spirit caretaker** (*Binayo*) — A sacred female spirit married to Bulungabon who cares for the rice spirits.
- **Supreme Being** (*Mahal na Makaako*) — The Supreme Being who gave life to all human beings merely by gazing at them.

## Foodways

The Mangyan are subsistence farmers growing sweet potato, upland rice, and taro, supplemented by hunting and gathering.

- **Upland rice** — A crop grown through dry cultivation, with many varieties and extensive traditional knowledge.
- **Sweet potato** — A tuber crop grown by the Mangyan for subsistence.
- **Taro** — A root crop cultivated as part of Mangyan subsistence farming.
- **Winnowing tray** — A rattan winnowing tray used to process harvested rice.
- **Lime containers** — Containers for betel chewing, made of incised bamboo or gourd with vegetable fibre.

## Oral tradition & literature

Mangyan oral traditions were historically transmitted and preserved as syllabic script carved into bamboo and other materials.

- **Legend, folklore and fable** (*suyot*) — The oldest oral traditions, comprising legend, folklore and fable, tellable only after sunset.
- **Acquired stories** (*sugklanon*) — Stories acquired through Bisayan influence.
- **Historical accounts** (*tultulanon*) — Accounts of historical events.
- **Song poems** — Poems that carry taboos, beliefs and customs and account for the origins of practices.
- **Incised bamboo verse** — Poetry written in syllabic script and carved into bamboo with a sharp object, existing as both sound and text.

## Language & religion

Mangyan languages are Austronesian and mutually unintelligible, with a pre-Hispanic syllabic script still taught in schools.

- **Hanunó'o script** (*surat Mangyan*) — A pre-Hispanic syllabary of Indic origin, still practiced and taught in Mangyan schools in Oriental Mindoro.
- **Lowlanders** (*damuong*) — The term for lowland Filipinos in Mangyan languages.
- **Tawbuid language** — A Mangyan language with eastern and western dialects, unusual in having an /f/ phoneme and lacking glottal phonemes.
- **Buhid language** — A Mangyan language closely related to Tawbuid and unusual in having an /f/ phoneme.
- **Animistic worldview** — The traditional spiritual system, with around 10% Christian conversion and the New Testament published in six Mangyan languages.
