## Overview

The La Ha are an ethnic group of Vietnam. Most La Ha live in Yên Bái and Sơn La provinces, and their population numbered 10,157 people in 2019. They speak Laha, a language in the Tai–Kadai family; Laha is also classified as a Kra language and is spoken in Lào Cai and Sơn La provinces. Laha-speaking areas also include populations of Black Thai (Tai Dam), Kháng, Ksongmul (Ksingmul, Xinh-mun), Hmong, and Khmu speakers. The available sources document the group’s geographic distribution, language, a festival, and a historical account.

## Music & performance

The Laha people article states that the La Ha celebrate the Pang A Nụn Ban Festival to show deep gratitude to their doctors; during the festival they offer bitter bamboo and Mạ Rệ flower, both described as popular drugs.

## Festivals & rituals

The La Ha celebrate the Pang A Nụn Ban Festival as an expression of deep gratitude to their doctors. They offer bitter bamboo and Mạ Rệ flower, which the source describes as popular drugs.

## Foodways

They mention bitter bamboo and Mạ Rệ flower only in connection with the Pang A Nụn Ban Festival, where these are offered to doctors and described as popular drugs.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Black Tai Chronicle provides a historical account involving the Laha and the Black Tai. It states that the Black Tai had gained control of Muang Mouay in the Red River valley from the Laha through deceit and murder. The Laha chief Am Poi, also named Khoun Piên, fought the Black Tai for a long time. The Black Tai leader Lang Cheuang then asked to marry Am Poi’s daughter Hao and went to the wedding feast. Am Poi brought fifty bodyguards because he distrusted the Black Tai, but the bodyguards were persuaded to put down their weapons. Am Poi became drunk, was killed at the feast, and the other Laha escaped as the Black Tai took over the Red River valley.

## Language & religion

Laha is spoken by approximately 1,400 people out of a total population of 5,686 Laha. It is spoken in Lào Cai and Sơn La provinces, Vietnam. Laha dialects were documented in 1986 by Russian linguists and in 1996 by American linguist Jerold A. Edmondson. The Wet Laha dialect is also called Laha Ung, while the Dry Laha dialect is also called Laha Phlao. Wet Laha is associated with Lào Cai and Lai Châu, including Tà Mít Commune, Tân Uyên, Lai Châu; Dry Laha is associated with Sơn La, around the Sông Đà and Nậm Mu Rivers. Many Laha can also converse in Khmu.
