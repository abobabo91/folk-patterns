## Overview

The Nage are an indigenous people living on the eastern Indonesian islands of Flores, chiefly in the eponymous Nagekeo Regency, and Timor. They are descended from the indigenous population of Flores and are largely assimilated by neighboring people. The Nage speak Nage, one of the major languages in the Austronesian languages group. Their documented folk-culture practices include manual slash-and-burn farming, hunting and gathering, communal land ownership, settlement in cumulus-type villages surrounded by stone walls, agrarian rites, distinctive household arrangements, and clothing consisting of loincloths and skirts or *kain*. The sources also record a connection between Nage mythology concerning Ebu Gogo and renewed interest in the discovery of Homo floresiensis.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The clothes of the Nage people are described as loincloths and skirts or *kain*. Women fasten the garment over the breast, while men fasten it around the waist.

### Architecture

The Nage live in cumulus-type settlements located on mountain slopes and surrounded by stone walls. Houses are piled up in rectangular positions and connected by an open gallery into a single complex. This arrangement is intended for the joint residence of several large families.

## Music & performance

The Nage language is described as retaining respect through oral traditions despite an unfavorable attitude toward the language.

## Festivals & rituals

Agrarian cults have survived among the Nage and are still practiced. Before sowing, rites of cleansing the field and the grains of rice are performed on the first new moon, before the start of cultivating the fields.

## Foodways

Nage foodways are described as being dominated by plant-based foods, including cooked groats and tubers with spicy seasoning. Meat is eaten only on holidays. The Nage traditionally engaged in manual slash-and-burn farming involving tubers, rice, and corn, as well as hunting and gathering.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources describe oral traditions as supporting a continuing sense of respect for the Kéo language. Nage mythology includes Ebu Gogo, a creature associated with local stories. Gregory Forth hypothesized a possible connection between these stories and the discovery of Homo floresiensis, a possible species of extinct hominid.

## Language & religion

The Nage speak Nage, described as one of the major languages in the Austronesian languages group. The related Kéo or Nagé-Kéo is a Malayo-Polynesian dialect cluster spoken by the Kéo and Nage peoples in an area southeast of the Ebulobo volcano in the south-central part of Nusa Tenggara Timur Province on Flores, largely in Nagekeo Regency. Kéo belongs to the Malayo-Polynesian, Central-Eastern Malayo-Polynesian, and Bima-Lembata subgroups of the Austronesian language family, and the source gives approximately 40,000 speakers. Agrarian cults and field-cleansing rites are documented as continuing practices.
