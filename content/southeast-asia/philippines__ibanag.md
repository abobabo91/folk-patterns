## Overview

The Ibanag, also called Ybanag, Ybanak, or Ibanak, are an ethnolinguistic minority of the Philippines numbering a little more than half a million people. They inhabit the provinces of Cagayan, Isabela, and Nueva Vizcaya, and are especially associated with communities around the Cagayan River. The Ibanag language is an Austronesian language of the Northern Philippine languages subgroup. It is spoken principally in Isabela and Cagayan, including Tuguegarao, Solana, Abulug, Lal-lo, Cabagan, Ilagan, San Pablo, Santa Maria, Santo Tomas, and Tumauini. The name Ibanag comes from *I-* meaning “people of” and *bannag*, meaning “river.” The sources document Ibanag cultural preservation through language teaching and stage performances.

## Music & performance

The sources identify two Ibanag-language stage plays: *Zininaga Ta Bannag*, translated as “Heritage of the River,” and *Why Women Wash the Dishes*. In October 2012, the revival of Ibanag culture was described as part of the Mother-Tongue Based program of the Philippine government, which sought to preserve indigenous cultures, including their languages, for future generations. The plays were being performed in Ibanag as part of this setting of language and cultural preservation.

## Foodways

The sources provide examples of Ibanag food vocabulary and sentences but do not present a general account of Ibanag foodways. *Makan* means “food,” *kuman* means “to eat,” and *minum* means “to drink.” The examples mention *bavi*, “pig,” and *kazzing*, “goat,” as well as *danum*, “water.” One example states, “We cooked dinengdeng,” using the form *Nallutu kami tu dinengdeng*. Another says, “Go buy lechon later,” with the Ibanag forms *lichon* and *lechon*. These examples show words and sentences used in the language source.

## Language & religion

Ibanag is an Austronesian language belonging to the Northern Philippine languages subgroup. It is closely related to Gaddang, Itawis, Agta, Atta, Yogad, Isneg, and Malaweg. The language is spoken principally in Cagayan and Isabela, with speakers also documented in the Philippines, the Middle East, the United Kingdom, and the United States. Most Ibanag speakers can also speak Ilocano, described in the sources as the lingua franca of northern Luzon.

The sources describe Southern Ibanag and Northern Ibanag as the main dialects. Ibanag spoken in Tuguegarao is considered the standard dialect. The sources also discuss regional pronunciation differences, including the replacement of [p] with [f] in parts of Northern Cagayan. Earlier writing used Spanish-influenced spellings, while the modern orthographic system is more phonetic and has been adopted in public schools under the Department of Education’s Mother Tongue-Based Multilingual Education policy.
