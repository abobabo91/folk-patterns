## Overview

The Mlabri, also called Mrabri and Phi Tong Luang, are an ethnic group living in Thailand and Laos. In Thailand they live in the northern part of the country along the border with Laos, close to the Hmong and northern Thai; in Laos they live near other ethnic groups. About 400 or fewer Mlabris remain in the world, with some estimates as low as 100. Their language is usually classified as Khmuic, within the Austroasiatic languages. The Mlabri are notable in folk-culture studies as a historically nomadic hunter-gatherer group whose temporary shelters, bark or cloth clothing, gathering practices, spiritual beliefs, and later settlement in Thailand are documented in descriptions of Southeast Asian societies. Since the 1990s, Mlabri in Thailand have settled in more permanent villages in Phrae and Nan provinces.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Hammock weaving** — In their settled villages, Mlabri engage in hammock weaving.

### Clothing & dress

Traditionally, the Mlabri wore only a loin-covering made of bark or cloth. Most Mlabri now wear factory-made clothes obtained through trade with other hill tribes.

### Architecture

The Mlabri traditionally had no permanent houses. They moved frequently and made temporary shelters from palm leaves and bamboo-string. In Thailand, the houses in the permanent villages where many Mlabri now live are made of cinderblock and wood, with metal roofs and electricity.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They state that the Mlabri traditionally made temporary shelters from palm leaves and bamboo-string and that many now work as day laborers, engage in highland farming, and practice hammock weaving.

## Music & performance

They state that the Mlabri have few regimented social ceremonies and are said to have no formal religious system.

## Festivals & rituals

The Mlabri have few regimented social ceremonies. They are said to have no formal religious system, although they believe in forest spirits and other nature spirits. Marriages are made by simple request and there is no bride-price. Women traditionally give birth alone in the forest, and infant mortality used to be very high. The dead are buried near where they died, after which the group moves on.

## Foodways

The Mlabri are hunter-gatherers, with most of their food traditionally coming from gathering. The sources also state that they now engage in highland farming while continuing to hunt and gather.

## Oral tradition & literature

In 1938, the Austrian anthropologist Hugo Bernatzik published an ethnography of the “Yellow Leaf People” containing brief observations of the group in the early 20th century. The sources also mention the RWAAI Digital Archive and works concerning Mlabri language, history, demography, and material and spiritual life.

## Language & religion

Mlabri is spoken in the border area between Thailand and Laos and is usually classified as a Khmuic language, a subgroup of the Austroasiatic languages. The linguist Jørgen Rischel divided it into three varieties: one spoken by a small group in Laos and previously called Yumbri, and two spoken by larger groups in Thailand. These varieties differ in intonation and lexicon. Mlabri can be counted up to ten, although only the numerals one and two may modify a noun, and the word for “two” has uses closer to “pair” or “couple” in English than to a numeral. The Mlabri are said to have no formal religious system, but they believe in forest spirits and other nature spirits.
