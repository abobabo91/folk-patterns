## Overview

The Tidung are a native group originating from Tana Tidung, now part of North Kalimantan province in Indonesian Borneo. Their traditional territories extend along the Sembakung River and Sibuku River, from their headwaters to the estuary north of Tarakan Island, along the coast, south to the river-mouth of Bulungan River, and northward to Tawau in Sabah, Malaysia, including Cowie Harbour. A Tidung enclave is located at Labuk River, opposite the city of Klagan. Their population in Indonesia was estimated at about 27,000 in 2007, while Malaysia’s 2010 census recorded 28,515 Tidung. They speak Tidung, a North Bornean language within the Murutic family and the Western Malayo-Polynesian languages. Their folk culture includes named folktales, farming, fishing, and traditions shaped by the Muslim Kingdom of Tidung.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that the rise of the Muslim Kingdom of Tidung shaped the ethnogenesis of the Tidung people and that cultural and religious traditions contribute to their treatment as a common people group.

## Foodways

The Tidung are traditionally farmers practising slash-and-burn agriculture, while some are ocean fishermen. Their crops include sweet potatoes, cassava, lentils, fruits, and vegetables.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources name several Tidung folktales and fables. These include *Asal-usul Orang Tidung Tengara*, described as “The origin of Tidung Tenggara people”; *Lasedne sinan pagun*, “The sink of Jelutong village”; *Seludon Ibenayuk*, “The tale of Ibenayuk”; *Si Benua dan Si Sumbing*, “Benua and Sumbing”; *Seludon Yaki Yamus*, “The tale of Four-eyed king”; *Seludon Batu Tinagad*, “The logged stone”; and *Yaki Balak*, “The story of Aki Balak.”

## Language & religion

The Tidung language is a North Bornean language and is part of the Murutic languages, a family of closely related Austronesian languages spoken in the northern inland regions of Borneo by the Murut and Tidung. The Tidung language is also described as belonging to the Western Malayo-Polynesian languages. Tidung is spoken in several dialects: Nunukan, Penchangan, Sedalir, Tikal, Tarakan, Malinau, Sesayap, and Sibuku in Indonesia, and Tarakan and Sesayap in Sabah, Malaysia. Before the present-day Roman writing system, the Tidung used Jawi script. The sources describe the Tidung as a Malayised Dayak people and connect their collective identity with the Muslim Kingdom of Tidung.
