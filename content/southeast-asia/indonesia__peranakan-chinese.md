## Overview

The Peranakan Chinese are an ethnic group defined by genealogical descent from early waves of Southern Chinese settlers who established themselves in maritime Southeast Asia, including the Malay Peninsula and the Indonesian Archipelago. Their culture developed through centuries of transculturation and interracial marriage between Chinese settlers and Malay, Thai, Javanese, and other local populations. In Indonesia, Peranakan communities are found along the northern coastline of West Java, Central Java, and East Java, as well as in Special Region of Yogyakarta. Peranakan culture is distinguished by the combination of Chinese cultural elements with local cultures of the Nusantara region. The community is multiracial, and family histories and degrees of identification with Chinese identity vary widely.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Batik sarong** — The *batik sarong* is a wrap-around skirt worn with the Nyonya’s Baju Panjang. The sources describe it as part of Peranakan dress.

**Kain batik pesisiran** — *Kain batik pesisiran* is Javan coastal batik associated with Chinese settlements and with the kebaya encim. Its patterns incorporate symbols and motifs from China, and it is described as having vibrant colours.

**Baju Panjang** — *Baju Panjang*, meaning “Long Dress,” was adapted from the Malay Baju Kurung. It is worn with a batik sarong and three kerosang.

**Kasot Manek** — *Kasot Manek* are Peranakan beaded slippers. They were hand-made by stringing, beading, and sewing tiny faceted glass cut beads, called *Manek Potong*, onto canvas. Earlier designs often used European floral subjects, while their colours were influenced by Peranakan porcelain and batik sarongs.

**Kebaya encim** — *Kebaya encim* developed among Chinese Peranakans in Javan coastal cities with significant Chinese settlements, including Semarang, Lasem, Tuban, Surabaya, Pekalongan, and Cirebon. Compared with Javanese kebaya, it is described as having smaller and finer embroidery, lighter fabrics, and more vibrant colours.

**Batik patterns** — Peranakans in Indonesia developed batik patterns incorporating symbols from China.

**Manek Potong** — *Manek Potong* are tiny faceted glass cut beads used in the construction of Peranakan beaded slippers. The sources compare them with beads from Bohemia, identified in the source as present-day Czech Republic.

**Motif vocabulary.** Dragon, phoenix, peony, lotus, European floral subjects.

### Clothing & dress

Women’s clothing includes the Baju Panjang, adapted from the Malay Baju Kurung and worn with a batik sarong and three kerosang, or the kebaya encim developed by Chinese Peranakans in Javan coastal cities. The kebaya encim is distinguished from Javanese kebaya by finer and smaller embroidery, lighter fabrics, and brighter colours. It is associated with vibrant kain batik pesisiran carrying Chinese symbols and motifs.

Peranakan beaded slippers, called Kasot Manek, were made with tiny faceted glass beads sewn onto canvas. They could be flats or bedroom slippers; from the 1930s, modern shapes and heels became increasingly common. For men, the baju lokchuan, described as the Chinese men’s full costume, was worn by the Baba. The younger generation wore only its top, a long-sleeved silk jacket with a Chinese collar, or a batik shirt.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention three kerosang, or brooches, worn with the Baju Panjang. They also describe Kasot Manek slippers decorated with faceted glass beads. No further jewellery types, ritual functions, tattooing, henna, hair practices, or other body adornment are covered by the sources used.

## Festivals & rituals

Peranakans celebrate Chinese New Year, the Lantern Festival, and other Chinese festivals. At the same time, they adopt customs of the lands in which they settled and influences associated with their colonial rulers.

Peranakans generally retained elements of their ethnic and religious origins, including ancestor worship. Older generations continued to observe *pantang larang*, described as taboos; these could be strict and complex, although most Peranakans no longer maintain the more complex forms in modern times.

The sources describe marriage and family formation as historically important to the emergence of Peranakan society. Chinese settlers married Malay, Thai, Javanese, Batak, Balinese, Bugis, Nias, Dayak, and other local women in different parts of the region. Peranakan families also arranged marriages involving brides from China or newly arrived Chinese immigrants. In Java, Chinese men who married Javanese Muslim women sometimes converted to Islam, while Chinese Muslim Peranakan communities developed among the descendants of converts.

## Foodways

They state that Peranakan culture combines Chinese traditions with local cultures and that Peranakans adopted customs from the lands where they settled.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention Malay myths concerning the Peranakan Chinese in Malacca, Singapore, and Penang. These myths sometimes claim exclusive descent from the royal retinue of a princess named Hang Li Po, who was said in the Malay Annals to have married the Sultan of Malacca in the fifteenth century. The source states that modern historians disproved this princess marriage as a false myth of the Malay Annals.

The sources also mention the phrase “orang Cina bukan Cina,” translated as “a not-Chinese Chinese person,” as an expression of the complex relationship between Peranakan identity and Chinese identity. No Peranakan epic, folktale cycle, proverb collection, riddle tradition, literary revival, or preservation project is described.

## Language & religion

The Peranakan language is Baba Malay, also called Bahasa Melayu Baba or Peranakan Malay. It is a creole related to Malay and contains many Hokkien words. The language is described as dying, with contemporary use mainly limited to older generations. Malaccan Peranakan Malay is strongly based on Malay, while Peranakans in eastern Peninsula Malaysia are also associated with Hokkien, Thai, Kelantanese Malay, and Terengganu Malay. Penang Peranakan speech is strongly influenced by Penang Hokkien. In Indonesia, the Peranakan language is mainly based on Indonesian and Javanese and includes elements of different Chinese varieties, especially Hokkien. It is found along the northern coastline of West Java, Central Java, and East Java, and in Special Region of Yogyakarta.

Peranakans generally subscribed to Chinese belief systems including Taoism, Confucianism, and Han Buddhism; Roman Christianity is also present in the modern community. Ancestor worship remained part of their inherited religious origins. A significant number of modern Peranakans, particularly in Indonesia, have embraced Christianity.
