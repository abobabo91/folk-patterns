## At a glance

|---|---|
| Who | The Lisu are a Tibeto-Burman ethnic group. |
| Where | They live mainly in the mountains of Myanmar, southwest China, Thailand, and Arunachal Pradesh in India. |
| How many | About 600,000 live in Myanmar, 55,000 in Thailand, and 5,000 in India. |
| Language | Lisu is a tonal Tibeto-Burman language. |
| Religion | Many Lisu became Christian in the early 20th century; older traditions included animism and spirit worship. |
| Known for | Mountain agriculture · Colorful textile clothing · Silver jewelry · Bamboo and basket objects · Songs that carry history |

## Overview

The Lisu live in mountainous regions of Myanmar and nearby countries. Their culture includes clan organization, mountain farming, textile clothing, silver adornment, bamboo objects, sacred groves, ancestor altars, and ritual specialists. History has traditionally been passed down through songs. Many Lisu later adopted Christianity.

## Material culture

Lisu material culture includes textile clothing, silver adornment, bamboo containers and baskets, household tools, and simple bamboo-walled houses.

People wear their best clothes and much silver jewelry during the main festival and other celebrations.

### Architecture

Lisu villages are usually built near water, with houses commonly made from bamboo and built on the ground.

- **Bamboo-walled house** (*bamboo walls*) — Traditional houses usually have bamboo walls and dirt floors.
- **Wood house** (*wood*) — More affluent Lisu increasingly build houses of wood.
- **Concrete house** (*concrete*) — More affluent Lisu also increasingly build houses of concrete.
- **Village sacred grove** (*sacred grove*) — A sacred grove stands at the top of every traditional village.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Silver jewelry is especially important during festivals, weddings, and other celebrations.

- **Silver festival jewelry** (*silver jewelry*) — People wear large amounts of silver jewelry during the main festival.
- **Silver wedding jewelry** (*silver jewelry*) — Silver jewelry is also worn at weddings and other celebrations.

## Music & performance

Songs carry Lisu history across generations, and some performances last all night.

- **History songs** (*songs*) — Lisu history is traditionally passed down through songs.
- **All-night song** (*song*) — Some songs are so long that one performance can take an entire night.
- **Mission hymns** (*hymns*) — Missionaries used hymns when teaching and spreading Christianity among the Lisu.

## Festivals & rituals

The main festival follows Chinese New Year and combines music, feasting, drinking, fine clothes, and silver jewelry.

- **Main Lisu festival** (*Chinese New Year*) — The main Lisu festival corresponds to Chinese New Year and includes music, feasting, and drinking.
- **Wedding celebration** (*weddings*) — Weddings are associated with music, feasting, drinking, and silver jewelry.
- **Mountain-spirit offerings** (*nat-worship*) — Before Christianity, sacrifices were offered to spirits of the mountains.
- **Sacred-grove rite** (*sacred grove*) — The sky spirit, or in China the Old Grandfather Spirit, is honored with offerings in the village sacred grove.
- **Ancestor altar** — Each house has an ancestor altar at the back.

## Foodways

Lisu foodways center on rice, fruit, vegetables, trade, and swidden farming, with rice liquor and other stored goods recorded in the sources.

- **Mountain rice** — Mountain rice was part of Lisu subsistence.
- **Swidden farming** (*swidden*) — The Lisu practiced swidden, or slash-and-burn, agriculture.
- **Rice liquor** — The museum catalogue records rice liquor kept in a large earthenware jar.
- **Opium poppy** — The opium poppy became a cash crop in the early 19th century.
- **Betel nuts and tobacco** — Bamboo containers for betel nuts and tobacco are recorded among Lisu household objects.

## Oral tradition & literature

Lisu history is carried through songs, while early written works included Christian texts.

- **Historical songs** (*songs*) — Lisu history is transmitted through songs performed across generations.
- **Lisu alphabet publications** (*Lisu alphabet*) — Early published works in the Lisu alphabet included a catechism, parts of scripture, and a complete New Testament in 1936.
- **Catechism** — A catechism was among the early works published in the Lisu alphabet.
- **New Testament** — A complete New Testament was published in 1936.

## Language & religion

Lisu is a tonal language with several dialects and two main writing systems, while religious life includes both Christianity and older spirit traditions.

- **Lisu language** (*Lisu*) — Lisu is a tonal Tibeto-Burman language related to Lahu, Akha, Burmese, Jingphaw, and Yi.
- **Fraser alphabet** — The Fraser alphabet was developed by James O. Fraser and Ba Thaw and was officially recognized in China in 1992.
- **Chinese Lisu script** (*pinyin*) — The Chinese government developed a Lisu script based on pinyin.
- **Lisu syllabary** — Ngua-ze-bo invented a syllabary from Chinese characters, Dongba script, and Geba script between 1924 and 1930.
- **Yobin** — Lisu people in India are called Yobin.
