## Overview

The Buginese are an ethnic group from South Sulawesi, Indonesia. Their language is spoken by about 4 million people, mainly in southern Sulawesi. Buginese culture is represented here through writing, historical records, weapons, clothing, jewelry, and a few musical instruments. The profile does not describe their architecture, foodways, festivals, dances, or current religion.

## Material culture

The profile mainly documents Buginese textiles, weapons, belts, clothing, jewelry, and bamboo instruments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Buginese textile objects include black-and-white cotton sarongs and technically complex woven belts.

- **Buginese sarong** (*sarong*) — This narrow black-and-white cotton textile was sewn at the short ends and could also be worn by girls as a head covering.
- **Man’s ceremonial sword belt** — This blue cotton belt has Islamic calligraphy, floral patterns, a loop, padding, and very long fringes.
- **Kris sheath** — Kris sheaths were made from wood, gold and silver thread, or rattan and wood, with floral decoration on some examples.
- **Double-sided belt patterning** — Some belts used alternating colors and patterns woven at the same time on both sides, which the catalogue calls a considerable technical achievement.

### Clothing & dress

Recorded Buginese dress includes a black-and-white sarong, a distinctive fiber hat, and a ceremonial sword belt.

- **Buginese sarong** (*sarong*) — This cotton garment was patterned in black and white and could also be worn by girls as a head covering.
- **Early peci-style hat** (*peci*) — This dyed and interlaced vegetable-fiber hat had a shape based on the Middle Eastern fez and was an early form of the black velvet peci.
- **Ceremonial sword belt** — This padded cotton belt had floral patterns, fringes, and a loop.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The recorded Buginese objects are mainly kris, thrusting daggers, and knives.

- **Diamond-hilted kris** (*kris*) — This kris has a steel blade, a carved ivory hilt with diamonds, and a wooden sheath decorated with gold and silver thread.
- **Sepukal kris** (*sepukal*) — This kris has a blade shape called sepukal and a “water melon skin” blade pattern.
- **Silver-sheathed dagger** — This South Sulawesi dagger has a silver sheath and a silver handle.
- **Damascened dagger** — This dagger has a straight, single-edged blade of coarsely damascened steel and a white-metal handle with floral decoration.
- **Buginese knife** — A Buginese knife found in South Sulawesi is also recorded.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Buginese bracelets called ponto lola are gilded silver rings decorated with filigree and colored stones.

- **Pair of hollow bracelets** (*ponto lola; ponto sipapa*) — These somewhat triangular bracelets are made of gilded sheet silver, joined from two halves by a pin, and decorated with leaf and flower filigree and blue and green gemstones.
- **Beaded small bag** — The bracelet set includes a small bag with round green beads and a border of transparent beads.

## Music & performance

The sources record only the existence of two Buginese musical instruments made of bamboo.

- **Bamboo musical instrument** — Two British Museum objects are described as Buginese musical instruments made of bamboo, but their names and uses are not given.

## Festivals & rituals

The profile does not describe Buginese festivals, rites, or life-cycle ceremonies.

## Oral tradition & literature

Buginese written tradition includes the epic creation myth Sureq Galigo and the historical records known as Lontara.

- **Epic creation myth** (*Sureq Galigo*) — Sureq Galigo is identified as the earliest written record of Buginese and the epic creation myth of the Bugis people.
- **Historical records and script** (*Lontara*) — Lontara refers both to the traditional script and to historical records that date back to around the 17th century.
- **Buginese wall poem** — A Buginese poem was painted on a wall near the Royal Netherlands Institute of Southeast Asian and Caribbean Studies in Leiden.

## Language & religion

Buginese is an Austronesian language traditionally written in the Lontara script, while the profile does not describe current religion.

- **Buginese language** (*Basa Ugi*) — Buginese is called Basa Ugi and belongs to the South Sulawesi subgroup of the Austronesian language family.
- **Bugis people** (*To Ugi*) — The Bugis people are called To Ugi, meaning “the followers of La Sattumpugi” according to a Buginese myth.
- **Buginese script** (*Aksara Ugi*) — The Buginese Lontara script is locally called Aksara Ugi and is now often replaced by the Latin script.
- **Lontara script** (*Lontara*) — Lontara is a Brahmic script also used for Makassar and Mandar, and its name comes from the Malay word for the palmyra palm.
