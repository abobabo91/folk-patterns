## Overview

The Angkola, also known as Angkola Batak, are one of the sub-ethnic groups of the Batak people. They are found mainly in North Sumatra, Indonesia, and primarily inhabit the southern part of the Tapanuli region. Their name is believed to derive from the Angkola River, also called Batang Angkola. The river’s southern, downstream section is called Angkola Jae, while its northern, upstream section is called Angkola Julu. The Angkola language is similar to Mandailing and Toba, but is sociolinguistically distinct. Angkola society follows patrilineal kinship, with clans and surnames based on the patrilineal system. Most Angkola are Muslims, while a small minority are Christians.

## Language & religion

The Angkola language is similar to Mandailing and Toba but is sociolinguistically distinct. Most Angkola are Muslims, while a small minority are Christians. Angkola kinship is patrilineal, and marriage between people with the same surname is strictly prohibited.
