## Overview

The Savunese people, also known as Sawu, Sabu, or Hawu, are an ethnic group inhabiting Savu and the smaller island of Raijua in East Nusa Tenggara, Indonesia. Their language is Hawu, also called Lii Hawu, and belongs to the Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian language family. Hawu is most closely related to Dhao and the languages of Sumba. Linguistically and culturally, the Savunese are related to the Sumba people on the neighboring island of Sumba. Savu remained largely insular until the late-20th century. They document genealogy, weaving, architecture, music, dance, religion, farming, settlements, and language.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Savunese ikat** — Savunese ikat is a traditional weaving practice reflecting aspects of Savunese genealogy. Specific motifs and colours represent the weaver’s *hubu* and *wini*, social categories connected with Savunese descent and kinship.

### Clothing & dress

 They state only that dancers in the **Ledo Hawu** dance wear particular items: men wear bells, while participants in the **dho’a** dance wear *kedhu'e*, beans wrapped in palm leaf to create a rattle.

### Architecture

The Savunese house is built on poles and designed to resemble a proa boat. Its front beams resemble the boat’s bow, and anthropomorphic elements occur in the terminology used for parts of the house. Savunese housing is also described as framed columns with gable roofing.

Traditional settlements are cumulus structured. A temple and stone shrines stand in the central square, which is surrounded by stone walls. While the harvest is ripening, people live in temporary huts in the fields.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They mention *kedhu'e*, a rattle made from beans wrapped in palm leaf, as an object used in the **dho’a** dance.

### Jewelry & body adornment

 They state that men performing the **Ledo Hawu** dance wear bells.

## Music & performance

Savunese music is based on the gong and normally accompanies traditional dance.

The **dho’a** dance, known as **Padho'a** in Kupang Lingua Franca, is performed in a circle. Dancers hold hands and rotate their legs clockwise while wearing *kedhu'e*, beans wrapped in palm leaf to create a rattle. The name **Padho'a/pedho'a** is derived from Savunese *pe dheja dho'a*.

The **Ledo Hawu** dance is performed by mixed pairs, with the men wearing bells.

## Dance & theatre

Two named dances are documented. The **dho’a**, also called **Padho'a** in Kupang Lingua Franca, is a circular dance performed while holding hands. Its dancers rotate their legs clockwise and wear *kedhu'e*, beans wrapped in palm leaf that create a rattle.

The **Ledo Hawu** is performed by mixed pairs, and men wear bells.

## Festivals & rituals

 They state that the Savunese measure time in units ranging from six to 49 years, depending on the domain.

Genealogy is important in Savunese ritual life. Names are chosen to avoid repetition, and genealogies are recited during ritual performances such as funerals, where the connection of the departed person to his or her ancestors is memorialised. *Wini* and *hubi* have roles in weddings, funerals, and baptisms.

Marriage is between a man and a woman of the same *wini* as his mother, or at a minimum within the same *hubi*. There is non-reciprocal gift-giving from the groom’s family to the bride’s family. The conclusion of marriage is within the three-year union, and the marital residence of a Savu family is patrilocal.

The traditional religion of the Savu people is **Jingi Tiu**. Each domain was led by a Jingi Tiu Council of Priests.

## Foodways

The traditional occupation of the Savunese people is farming. Rice, corn, millet, and beans are usually grown, and irrigation systems are used in some places. Savunese people also fish and breed small cattle, buffaloes, and horses.

Betel nut is popular. *Tuak*, wine made from the sugar palm, is also consumed, as is the sap drunk fresh from the tree. The palm tree is treated with great respect, and the *apu lodo* priest, described as a descendant of the sun, supervises the palm-tapping season.

The Savunese make sweet syrup and wine from the juice of a lone palm tree. Cockfights are also mentioned as an activity.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They document genealogy as an important verbal and ritual tradition: genealogies are tracked through many generations and recited during ritual performances, especially funerals.

The Alan T. Walker Collection contains audio recordings, handwritten field notes, and narrative texts produced through Hawu language documentation. An accompanying Finding Aid and Inventory was created to help navigate the collection in the PARADISEC archive. Another PARADISEC collection contains audio recordings of Hawu conversations, narratives, elicitation, genealogies, and wordlists; several items are accompanied by video files.

## Language & religion

The Hawu language, or *Lii Hawu*, is spoken by the Savu people on Savu Island and on Raijua Island. It belongs to the Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian language family and is most closely related to Dhao and the languages of Sumba. Dhao was once considered a dialect of Hawu, but the two languages are not mutually intelligible.

The dominant dialect is Seba, called *Mèb'a* in Hawu. Timu, called *Dimu* in Hawu, is spoken in the east; Mesara, called *Mehara* in Hawu, in the west; and Liae on the southern tip of the island. Raijua is spoken on the island of the same name.

The traditional religion is **Jingi Tiu**, a polytheistic religion with gods of earth, sea, and sky, as well as minor spirits. Evangelism began in 1854 and increased after 1861. Since the 1970s, Protestantism has been in the ascendancy, while Jingi Tiu has declined; aspects of Jingi Tiu belief continue to influence Christian worship in Savu.
