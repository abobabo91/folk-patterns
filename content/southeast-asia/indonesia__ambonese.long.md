## Overview

The Ambonese (*Orang Ambon*) are an ethnic group of mixed Austronesian and Melanesian origin from Ambon Island and the southwest of Seram Island in Indonesia’s Maluku Province. As of the 2007 census, 258,331 Ambonese lived in Ambon, Maluku, and about 35,000 lived in the Netherlands. Ambonese communities are predominantly Christian, with a significant Muslim minority. Their culture combines rural settlement traditions, sago-based foodways, pottery, weaving, carving, shipbuilding, music, and a Malay-based creole language shaped by Malay, Dutch, Portuguese, and indigenous languages. Ambonese social organization includes *negeri* communities, *soa* territorial groups, *mata ruma* patrilineal clans, and the customary norms called *adat*.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Men adopted modern European-style clothing during colonization. On special occasions, men wore short jackets and black trousers. Women commonly wore thin blouses or small-patterned sarongs. Older women typically wore black, while younger women wore bright-colored cotton dresses reaching to the knee.

### Architecture

A typical Ambonese village consisted of about 1,500 people living in houses made from woven sago leaves or plastered bamboo, wood, and coral stones on stone foundations. Traditional rural settlements were located on the shore and had a linear layout; houses were built on stilts. The people cultivated the surrounding hillsides. Many Ambonese lived in traditional rural communities called *negeri*, headed by a leader called a *raja*.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ambonese craftsmen worked in pottery, blacksmithing, weapons making, shipbuilding, carving on tortoiseshell and mother of pearl, making ornamental crafts from carnation buds, and weaving boxes and mats from strips of palm leaves. The museum catalogue records include a pottery sago porridge bowl, a pottery basin, a pottery dish, a pottery cooking-pot or water-pot, and an earthenware cooking-bowl.

## Music & performance

The Ambonese have a musical folklore that absorbed European elements. The Ambonese quadrille, called *katreji*, is one named example. The songs of the lagoon are accompanied by a violin and lap steel guitar. Traditional musical instruments named in the sources are the 12 gongs, drums, bamboo flute (*efluit*), xylophone (*tatabuhan kayu*), and Aeolian harp.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe marriage ceremonies as being performed within sectarian groups, traditionally in the form of patrilocal marriages. Community relations are governed by *adat*, which today largely governs family matters, inheritance, land rights, and elections for leadership positions. Relations between Christian and Muslim Ambonese have been connected through *pela*, described as a type of blood-brother relationship.

## Foodways

The basis of the Ambonese diet is porridge made from sago starch, vegetables, taro, cassava, and fish. The inhabitants of Ambon Island also have access to imported rice. The museum catalogue records a pottery sago porridge bowl, a pottery cooking-pot or water-pot, and an earthenware cooking-bowl. Ambon is also described as a center of production of spices such as carnation and nutmeg. Fishery, agriculture, horticulture, and small trades are common means of earning a living.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources provide an Ambonese version of Article 1 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and several example sentences in Ambonese, including *Beta pung nama Ahmad* and *Ose su tau Ahmad pung maitua?*

## Language & religion

Ambonese Malay, or Ambonese, is a Malay-based creole spoken on Ambon Island in the Maluku Islands of Eastern Indonesia. It developed from Malay brought by traders from Western Indonesia and was later shaped by Dutch colonization and missionary use. Malay was taught in schools and churches in Ambon, helping Ambonese become a lingua franca in Ambon and its surroundings. It also became a lingua franca in Buru, Seram, Geser-Gorom, and the south-western Maluku Islands, with different accents. Ambonese Malay was influenced by Dutch, Portuguese, and indigenous languages. Christianity, including Reformed Christianity and Roman Catholicism, and Sunni Islam are the predominant religions. The sources also describe *pela* as a community bond between adherents of both faiths.
