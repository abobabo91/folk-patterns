## Overview

The Bru are an indigenous ethnic group living in Thailand, Laos, and Vietnam. In Laos, most Bru live in eastern Savannakhet Province, especially in Sepone District, and also in other parts of Savannakhet Province and in Khammouane Province. Their total population is estimated at 129,559 by Ethnologue. The Bru speak Bru, a Katuic Mon–Khmer language with several dialects. They settled mostly along waterways and traditionally live in small houses built on stilts, arranged around a central meeting building in a circle. Their folk culture includes swidden agriculture, basket-making, straw-mat making, music, dancing, orally transmitted myths and legends, and religious practices involving ancestors and spirits.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The Bru have clothing that combines Tai and Katuic elements. The sources state that clothing reflects the adoption of some elements of Tai culture.

### Architecture

The Bru traditionally live in small houses built on stilts. Their houses are arranged around a central meeting building in a circle, and the settlements are generally located along waterways.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention household objects among the sacred objects of the Bru, together with relics and fragments of ancient weapons. Basket-making and the making of straw mats are documented crafts.

## Music & performance

The Bru are described as socially outgoing and as people who love music and dancing. They are skilled with many instruments, including castanets, drums, gongs, zithers, and simple traditional instruments used to accompany singing tales. The sources name two song traditions: *cha chap*, described as singing tales, and *sim*, described as change songs.

## Dance & theatre

The Bru are described as people who love dancing. They also do not distinguish ceremonial dances from entertainment dances.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe religious practices involving the worship of ancestors, the spirits of rice, and fire spirits.

## Foodways

Rice is the main crop grown by the Bru and a main staple of their diet. Beans and maize are also grown. The Bru are involved in animal husbandry, fishing, and hunting.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Bru transmit many myths and legends orally, including several stories about animals. These stories portray the hare as the most intelligent animal and the tiger as the hero.

## Language & religion

Bruu, also spelled Bru, B'ru, Baru, and Brou, is a Mon–Khmer dialect continuum spoken by the Bru people of mainland Southeast Asia. Sô and Khua are identified as dialects. The language has various local and dialect designations, including So, Sô, Tri, So Tri, Chali, Van Kieu, Leu, Leung, Kaleu, Galler, Khua, and Katang; the source states that Katang is not the same as Kataang. The Bru mainly believe in Animism. Some, especially in Thailand, are adherents of Theravada Buddhism, observed along with Animism. Religious practice includes worship of ancestors, the spirits of rice, and fire spirits. Sacred objects include relics and fragments of ancient weapons and household objects.
