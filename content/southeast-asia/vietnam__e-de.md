## Overview

The Rhade or Êđê, also called Rade, are an indigenous Austronesian ethnic group of southern Vietnam. Their population was 398,671 in 2019. The Rade language belongs to the Chamic languages, a subfamily of the Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian language family. Êđê society is organized through matrilineal descent: family property is held and inherited by women, surnames pass down the female line, and residence is matrilocal. Their folk culture includes longhouse architecture, epics, distinctive gong ensembles, flutes, zithers, customary law, and forms of communal labor.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The typical Rade house is a longhouse made of bamboo and wood. Its length is measured by the number of collar beams, called *de*. When a girl living in the house marries, the house is lengthened by one compartment because the husband lives in his wife's house. Buildings are oriented North-South.

The longhouse is divided into two parts. The Gah part occupies 1/3-2/3 of the total area and functions as the living room; the other part contains bedrooms. The house has two doors: a front door for men and a back door for women. It also has two stairs, a male stair and a female stair. Longhouses can be 100 meters long and accommodate from three to nine families. A traditional description states: “The house is as long as the gong's echo.”

The inauguration of a new home occurs when a row of trees is planted along the wall, although people may move in before then. Women led by the female head of the matrilineal family are the first authorized to walk on the new floor, carrying water and fire to give coolness and heat to the new home.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention corporate property held by the matrilineage, including paddy land, cattle, gongs, and jars.

## Music & performance

Êdê music is described as diverse. Playing music is a way for Êdê people to communicate with other people and, according to their beliefs, with God, called *yang*.

Several gong sets are named. The *knah* gong set consists of six suspended gongs: *knah*, *hlinang* or *knah hliang*, *mdu khơk* or *knah khơk*, *hluê khơk* or *mong*, *hluê hliang*, and *hluê khơk điêt* or *k'khiêt*. The source also names *knah di*, *ching sar*, the largest gong, and two bossed gongs, *mđũ* and *ana*. A *h'gor* drum is also mentioned. Other gong instruments include *chinh k'ram*. The source states that Rade gong culture has been recognized by UNESCO as one of the Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, but the supplied UNESCO material contains no inscription.

Flutes, called *đing*, include *đing năm*, *ky pah*, *đing tak ta* or *đing buốt klé*, *đing buốt tút*, *đing buốt trok*, *đing rinh*, *đing téc*, and *đinh tút*. String instruments include the *brố* or *brok zither* and *goong*. Other named instruments are *chinh đing aráp*, *gông kram*, *đing pah*, *đing ktuk*, *đing pâng*, and *kni*.

*Kư- ứt* is a way of telling an epic accompanied by *đing buốt trok*. *Ayray* consists of love songs accompanied by *đing năm*.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe rituals and social rules connected with houses, marriage, kinship, and customary law.

Matrilineages and matrisibs are exogamous. Sexual intercourse and intermarriage within them are prohibited, while phratries impose further marriage restrictions. Couples who violate the restrictions must sacrifice a buffalo; violating phratry restrictions requires only the sacrifice of a pig and is generally not regarded as equally serious.

The inauguration of a new longhouse takes place when a row of trees is planted along the wall. Women led by the female head of the matrilineal family first walk on the new floor, carrying water and fire. The Rade also practice *H'rim zít*: when a new house is built, villagers help the family by collecting construction materials or contributing manual labor.

## Foodways

They mention paddy land as corporate property of the matrilineage but provide no further information about food production or consumption.

## Oral tradition & literature

Rade epics are called *klei khan*. Named examples include *Klei khan Y Dam San*, *H'Bia Mlin*, *Dam Kteh Mlan*, and *Mdrong Dam*. Epic tellers are called *po khan*. They tell the epics next to the fire through the night.

The source also records *Kư- ứt*, a form of epic telling accompanied by *đing buốt trok*.

## Language & religion

The Rade language is one of the Chamic languages, which belong to the Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian language family. Other Chamic languages are spoken in central Vietnam and in Aceh, Sumatra.

Êdê music is described as communication with God, or *yang*. The sources also mention Rade evangelical Protestants and a Rade evangelical Bible. Its translators adapted Rade gender prefixes for many biblical names. Female names are marked by the prefix *H'*, while masculine names are prefixed by *Y-*. Given names precede surnames, and the two most common surnames are *Niê* and *Mlô*. Surnames are passed down matrilineally.
