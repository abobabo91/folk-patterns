## Overview

The White Tai are an ethnic group living in Vietnam, Laos and China. In Vietnam they are called Tai Dón or Thái Trắng; in China they are also called Dai Duan. They are included among the Tái peoples, together with the Thái Đen, Thái Đỏ, Phu Thai, Tày Thanh and Thái Hàng Tổng. The Tái people are described as the third largest of the fifty-four ethnic groups recognized by the Vietnamese government. Approximately 280,000 White Tai lived in Vietnam in 2002, 200,000 in Laos in 1995, and 10,000 in Yunnan province, China, in 1995. The White Tai migrated from China to Laos several centuries ago and settled along the Red and Black Rivers. Their language, Tai Dón or Tai Khao, belongs to the Tai–Kadai family.

## Festivals & rituals

The White Tai believe in multiple personal souls and hold ceremonies to recall those souls in order to strengthen their character.

## Language & religion

The White Tai speak Tai Khao, also called Tai Dón or White Tai. It is a Tai–Kadai language, classified within the Tai languages and Southwestern Tai languages subgroups. Tai Dón is spoken in northern Vietnam, Laos and China. The language has nine short vowels and one long vowel; short vowels are phonetically realized as long in final position. It has six tones on a smooth syllable and two tones on a checked syllable. The sources describe writing-system materials for Tai Don, including a preliminary proposal to encode the Tai Khao script. Religious affiliation is given as Animism at 60 percent, Theravada Buddhism at 38 percent, and Christianity at 2 percent.
