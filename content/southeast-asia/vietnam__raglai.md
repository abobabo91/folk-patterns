## Overview

The Raglai are a Chamic ethnic group mainly living in Khánh Hòa of Central Vietnam. They have also lived for a very long time in the high and rugged mountains west of Khánh Hòa, Ninh Thuận and Bình Thuận provinces, next to Cham communities in the South Central Coast plains. Their population was 146,613 in 2019. They speak Roglai, a Malayo-Polynesian language, and the Roglai language has Northern, Du Long, Southern and Cac Gia dialects. The name *raglai* means “forest” in their language, while the autonym *radlai* means “forest people.” Their importance in the sources lies especially in their relationship with Cham communities, their history in the highlands, and their distinctive musical tradition.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They mention a publication titled *Trang phục cố truyền Raglai*, but give no catalogue of garments, materials, techniques, head coverings, belts, footwear or ceremonial clothing.

## Music & performance

The Raglai have a rich musical tradition. The sources describe their use of several instruments, including specific variants of *Đàn đá*, Gong, *Đàn nhị*, *Kèn bầu* and *Đàn bầu*. These variants are named PATƠU TILẼNG, CHHAR / CHĨNG, CANHĨ and CHAPI VILUAI in the source.

The sources connect Raglai musical culture with their broader relationship to Cham communities and with the cultural life of Vietnam. No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription is provided for Raglai music.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that the Raglai historically returned over time to the practice of worshiping “wooden statue” ancestors, in contrast with Cham movements toward beliefs that banned idols and mascots.

The Raglai community received administrative recognition connected with ethnic minority regulations in 1997, following administrative documents and cultural efforts since 1989. This is a modern administrative history rather than a festival or life-cycle ritual description.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources record a thousand-year-old remaining proverb, “Cam sa-ai Raglai adei,” glossed as “Cham oldest sister, Raglai youngest sister.” The proverb is presented as evidence of the historical relationship and blood relations between Cham and Raglai communities.

The sources also record several names for the Raglai community: Rànglai, Ràglai, Ràdlai, Rơglai, Ràng-chơk and Ràng-ngơk. They state that these were negative ways of referring to “mountainous people,” without other meanings. Nguyễn dynasty records used the name mọi Lá Vàng, glossed as “barbarians of yellow-leaf.”

## Language & religion

Roglai is a Chamic language of southern Vietnam spoken by the Raglai people, and Chamic belongs to the Malayo-Polynesian language family. The Roglai language has four dialects: Northern, Du Long, Southern and Cac Gia. Its autonym is *radlai*, meaning “forest people.” The sources mention that sounds /e, o/ may have more open variants as [ɛ, ɔ], and that glide sounds [j, w] may occur as a result of vowel off-glides.

The sources describe historical changes in religious practice. The Raglai were previously considered part of the Cham people in relation to language, costumes and some customs. While the Cham people moved toward beliefs that banned idols and mascots, the Raglai returned over time to worshiping “wooden statue” ancestors, compared in the source with the Central Highlands community.
