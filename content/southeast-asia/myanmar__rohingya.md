## Overview

The Rohingya are a stateless Indo-Aryan ethnolinguistic group who predominantly follow Islam and are associated with Rakhine State, Myanmar. Their population was estimated at 1.4 million in Myanmar before the Rohingya genocide in 2017, when over 740,000 fled to Bangladesh. Before the 2015 refugee crisis and the military crackdowns of 2016 and 2017, the population was chiefly concentrated in the northern Rakhine townships, which were 80–98% Rohingya. The region is connected to the historical coastal area of Arakan, near the Bay of Bengal, and includes places such as Akyab, Maungdaw and Buthidaung. Rohingya is an Eastern Indo-Aryan language in the Bengali–Assamese branch and is closely related to Chittagonian. The sources present Rohingya culture chiefly through language, history, religious identity and political experience.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention the Santikan Mosque, built in the 1430s in Arakan. Its court measured 65 ft from north to south and 82 ft from east to west, while its shrine was a rectangular structure measuring 33 ft by 47 ft.

## Festivals & rituals

The Rohingya are described as predominantly following Islam. The sources do record political and military events affecting the community, including armed insurrections since the 1940s and military crackdowns in 1978, 1991–1992, 2012, 2015 and 2016–2018. In 2017, the most recent mass displacement prompted the International Criminal Court to investigate crimes against humanity and the International Court of Justice to hear a case alleging genocide.

## Language & religion

Rohingya is an Indo-Aryan language belonging to the Indo-Iranian branch of the Indo-European language family. It is an Eastern Indo-Aryan language in the Bengali–Assamese branch and is closely related to Chittagonian, with a high degree of mutual intelligibility among Rohingya, Chakma, Noakhali and Chittagonian. The language has six vowels and several diphthongs, distinguishes three tenses and four aspects, and commonly uses Subject–Object–Verb word order.

The sources identify several writing systems. Rohingya was first written in the nineteenth century with a version of Perso-Arabic script. In 1975, an orthographic Arabic script was developed, and in the 1980s Mohammad Hanif and his colleagues created the Hanifi Rohingya script. A Latin-letter system was simplified by E.M. Siddique Basu in 1999 and is known as Rohingyalish or Rohingya Fonna. Rohingya people predominantly follow Islam. The sources also describe historical Muslim communities, Arab merchants, Bengali Muslims, Buddhist populations and Islamic influence in Arakan.
