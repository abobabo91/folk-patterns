## Overview

The Ukit, also known as *Bhuket*, are a small minority tribe found in Sarawak, Malaysia. Until recently, they were nomads in the rain forests of Borneo. Some were settled at Rumah Ukit on the upper reaches of the Batang Balui, or Balui River. Most are found in the Upper Rajom and Tatau rivers and in Baleh, Sarawak. The Ukit population is estimated to be only about 120, although an unknown number may continue to live as forest nomads. Their population has declined through killings in conflict, displacement, and intermarriage with other tribes. They were also regarded as a sub-group of the purported Klemantan people. Ukit is a Punan language of Sarawak, Malaysia, while *Punan Ukit* is a dialect of the related language Bukitan.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

A description recorded from Harry De Windt in 1880 states that the Ukit possessed no dwellings and were totally unclothed, while the museum catalogue records several images of Ukit men with tattoos on their upper bodies and legs. The catalogue does not identify garment types, head coverings, belts, footwear, or ceremonial clothing.

### Architecture

The sources used mention that some Ukit were settled at Rumah Ukit on the upper reaches of the Batang Balui or Balui River. The 1880 description states that the Ukit possessed no dwellings, in the context of their forest-nomadic way of life.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The museum catalogue records glass negatives showing Ukit men with tattoos on their upper bodies and legs, including full-length portraits and a view of the back of one man. The Wikipedia material states that the Ukit were described as the only people living at the head of the Rejang not tattooed; this contrasts with the museum catalogue descriptions of the photographed men.

## Language & religion

Ukit is identified as a Punan language of Sarawak, Malaysia. *Punan Ukit* is described as a dialect of the related language Bukitan.
