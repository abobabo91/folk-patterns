## Overview

The Khmer Krom are ethnically Khmer people living in or originating from the Mekong Delta, in the southwestern part of Vietnam known in Khmer as *Kampuchea Krom*. They are considered indigenous to parts of Southern Vietnam and are recognized in Vietnam as one of the country’s fifty-four ethnic minorities. Most live in Tây Nam Bộ, the southern lowland region of historical Cambodia, around modern-day Hồ Chí Minh City and the Mekong Delta. Vietnamese government figures from the 2019 census recorded 1,319,652 Khmer Krom in Vietnam. Their history is connected to the Khmer Empire, whose economic center in the region was Prey Nokor, now Hồ Chí Minh City. The sources describe linguistic, religious, customary, and cultural links to Cambodia.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe religious, customary, and cultural links between the Khmer Krom and Cambodia. They also record that Khmer Krom Buddhist monk Samouk Sen headed the “Front for the Struggle of Kampuchea Krom,” whose militia was nicknamed the “White Scarves”; this is presented in the context of the Vietnam War and subsequent conflicts rather than as a ritual tradition.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do identify several Khmer Krom-associated political figures, including Son Ngoc Thanh, who was born in Trà Vinh, Vietnam, and was active in Cambodia’s independence movement.

## Language & religion

In Khmer, *Krom* means “low” or “below” and distinguishes the group from Khmers in Cambodia. The source identifies Khmer as the state-recognized ethnonym in Vietnam and says that the Khmer Krom retain linguistic and religious links to Cambodia. It also describes Samouk Sen as a Khmer Krom Buddhist monk.
