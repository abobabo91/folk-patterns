## Overview

The Maranao, also spelled Meranaw, Maranaw, and Mëranaw, are a predominantly Muslim Filipino and Austronesian ethnic group native to the region around Lake Lanao on Mindanao. Their name means “people of the lake,” with *lanaw* or *ranaw*, and the archaic *danaw*, meaning “lake” in the Maranao language. Maranaos number 1,800,000 in 2020, representing 1.65% of the population. They are closely related to the Iranun and Maguindanao people, and all three groups speak languages belonging to the Danao language family. Maranao culture is associated with artwork, weaving, wood, plastic and metal crafts, epic literature, royal houses, gong music, dance, woodcarving, and Lake Lanao, which is described as the largest lake in Mindanao and the second-largest and deepest lake in the Philippines.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources identify textiles as popular Maranao crafts.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name *okir* as a group of scroll, leaf, and spiral motifs used in Maranao art, including the decoration of the Sarimanok.

### Architecture

Maranao architecture follows an Austronesian framework of wooden structures built on piles. Traditional buildings are divided into three tiers associated with social class: the *torogan* of royalty, the *mala a walay* of lesser nobility, and the common *lawig*, which is described as analogous to the *bahay kubo*. The *torogan* is identified as a Maranao royal house, and the sources describe its architecture as the most aesthetic in the Philippines.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Maranao are known for plastic and metal crafts, and *okir* is associated with wood and metal carvings. The sources also identify *tabo* as a drum used in mosques to call worshippers to prayer.

## Music & performance

Maranao *kulintang* music is a type of gong music. The sources also name *Sarunaay*, which is found among both Muslim and non-Muslim groups of the Southern Philippines. *Kobbing* is identified as a Maranao instrument, while *Biyula* is another popular instrument and is described as a string instrument. These instruments and musical forms are presented as elements of Maranao music.

## Dance & theatre

*Singkil* is a Philippine dance based on a scene from the *Darangən*. The sources also state that the dance was based on a local adaptation of the *Ramayana*. No choreography, costume, musical accompaniment, performance setting, ceremonial function, or distinction between courtly and popular performance is provided.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that dishes are intertwined with important cultural rituals across all aspects of Maranao culture, from birth to death, but provide no names or descriptions of those rituals.

The sources describe Maranao society as traditionally divided into two strata: *mapiyatao*, meaning “pure,” and *kasilidan*, meaning “mixed blood.” *Kasilidan* is further divided into *sarowang*, “non-Maranao”; *balbal*, “beast”; *dagamot*, “Sorcerer/Sorceress”; and *bisaya*, “Slave.” The *mapiyatao* are described as natives entitled to ascend to thrones through a pure royal bloodline, while the *kasilidan* are described as natives suspected of mixed bloodline. These social strata are said to be declining because of improving economic conditions among most Maranao families.

## Foodways

Maranao cuisine is described as spicier than that of most other regions of the Philippines, a trait shared with much of Mindanao. Traditionally cultivated spices, locally known as *palapa*, are common condiments. The sources associate *palapa* with Bontang, a native product of Gandamatu, and describe it as made from stewed *sakurab* scallion bulbs, ginger, and chillies in coconut oil.

The Maranao historically engaged in fishing and farming, cultivating wet rice, corn, and sweet potatoes. Lake Lanao supports a major fishery, linking the lake to the economic and food life of the region.

## Oral tradition & literature

The *Darangən* is an epic associated with the Maranao and is identified as an UNESCO Intangible cultural heritage in the supplied Wikipedia material. In 2005, the *Darangen Epic of the Maranao people of Lake Lanao* was selected by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.

The Maranao also have an adaptation of the *Ramayana* called the *Maharadia Lawana*. The sources connect *Singkil* to a local *Ramayana* adaptation and separately describe the dance as based on a scene from the *Darangən*.

## Language & religion

Maranao is an Austronesian language spoken in Lanao del Sur and Lanao del Norte, including the cities of Marawi and Iligan in the Philippines, and also in Sabah, Malaysia. It is spoken among the Moros within the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao. Within the Danao subgroup, it is described as closer to Iranun than to Maguindanao. Many Maranaos are also fluent in Cebuano; Tagalog, English, and Arabic are also spoken, with Arabic associated with its importance to Islam and Maranao culture.

Maranao was historically written in Perso-Arabic letters called *Jawi*, also known as *Batang-a-Arab* or *Batang Arab*. It is now written with Latin letters, although there is no officially proclaimed standard orthography. The sources describe the Maranao as predominantly Muslim and state that Islamization occurred under the influence of Arab-Malay missionaries, including Sharif Kabungsuwan and others from Borneo.
