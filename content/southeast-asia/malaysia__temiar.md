## Overview

The Temiar are an indigenous people of the Malay Peninsula whose name means 'people of the edge,' reflecting their traditional home on the rainforest fringe. Most of their material culture—pouches, baskets, mats, instruments—is made entirely from forest materials like bamboo and cane, worked by hand. They are traditional animists whose spiritual life centers on the Sewang dance. Today, while most still live on rainforest margins, a small number have moved to cities.

## Material culture

Nearly everything the Temiar make comes from the forest—bamboo, cane, and wood shaped by hand into practical and beautifully patterned objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Temiar express all their pattern vocabulary through plaiting bamboo fiber and cane rather than weaving cloth.

- **Plaited bamboo-fiber pouches** — Small containers for betel nut, leaf and tobacco made from split bamboo fibers in natural and dyed colors, often with a lid, body and carrying strap all from a single plaited piece.
- **Plaited straps** — Carrying straps decorated with woven double squares, circular shapes, diamonds and belt-buckle patterns, often left with natural-colored borders.
- **Cane and bamboo baskets** — Back baskets with cylindrical or square forms plaited from cane or bamboo, fitted with leaf-fiber or handle-loop shoulder straps.
- **Plaited sleeping mats** — Narrow rectangular mats of plaited cane or fiber, sometimes plain and sometimes patterned with dyed fibers creating crossed diagonal lines.
- **Rice-shaker plate** — A large shield-shaped winnowing plate of plaited bamboo fiber with a thick bamboo rim and brickwork ground weave.

### Clothing & dress

The only documented clothing item is a plaited bamboo-fiber headband, with no sources describing everyday or ceremonial dress differences.

- **Plaited headband** — A band plaited from undyed and pale green dyed bamboo fibers arranged to form repeating diamonds, with a white polythene frill sewn along one edge.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Household and forest equipment is made from bamboo, cane and wood, with metal used only for blades and binding.

- **Machete** — A metal-bladed tool with a wooden handle kept in a wooden sheath held together by a metal coil and plaited cane binding, sometimes finished with carved notches.
- **Axe** — Built from a bamboo handle with an inserted wooden pole, a molded metal blade set into a wooden head, and plaited cord wrapped around to bind everything together.
- **Blowpipe dart and quiver** — Darts carried by the dozen in a cylindrical bamboo quiver closed with a plaited fiber lid and a cord handle fixed with wire coil.
- **Fish trap** — An open-work cane cylinder tapering to a point with two sets of inward-facing pyramid spikes to hold the catch and a slatted cane cap.

### Jewelry & body adornment

No jewelry, tattooing or hair practice is documented; adornment is limited to functional items with decorative detail.

- **Comb** — Cut from a half-cylinder of red or stained wood or cane with three sharpened prongs and small incised hatched diamond and triangle designs.

## Music & performance

Two bamboo instruments survive: a tube zither and a sealed tube with a tongue-shaped projection, used in cultural practice.

- **Bamboo-tube zither** — A wide bamboo cylinder split along one side with two thin cane strings running its length across a wooden bridge, coiled at the far end.
- **Sealed bamboo tube instrument** — A sealed bamboo tube with a D-shaped opening at one end, set below a tongue-shaped projection on the top edge.

## Dance & theatre

The Sewang is a ceremonial dance performed as part of Temiar animist beliefs; no other theatrical tradition is documented.

- **Sewang dance** (*Sewang*) — A ceremonial dance performed by the Temiar as part of their traditional animist spiritual practices.

## Festivals & rituals

No festival calendar or life-cycle rites are documented; the Sewang dance is the recorded religious practice.

- **Sewang dance** (*Sewang*) — A ceremonial dance performed as part of Temiar animist beliefs and spiritual life.

## Foodways

Three dishes are named, all cooked in or with bamboo, and betel nut, leaf and tobacco are carried in special plaited pouches.

- **Nasi serempad** — Rice cooked inside bamboo.
- **Umbut bayas masak gulai** — A curry made from palm pith.
- **Pucuk paku peno'ol** — Young fronds of the pucuk paku fern combined with tapioca leaves and anchovies, cooked in bamboo.
- **Betel pouch set** — Three plaited pouches joined by one strap used to carry betel nuts, tobacco and leaves together.

## Language & religion

Temiar is a Central Aslian language with three allomorphic classes of pronouns, and the Temiar are traditional animists.

- **Temiar language** (*Temiar*) — A Central Aslian language of the Austroasiatic family spoken in western Malaysia, with around 30,000 speakers in 2017.
- **Stressed pronouns with demonstrative** (*na-doh, ʔun-tu:y*) — In Temiar, stressed third-person pronouns must appear with a demonstrative, as in na-doh 'he-here' and ʔun-tu:y 'they-elsewhere'.

## Glossary

- *Temiar* — An indigenous Senoic people of the Malay Peninsula; also their Central Aslian language
- *Senoic* — A branch of the Orang Asli, Malaysia's indigenous peoples
- *Central Aslian* — A language family within Austroasiatic spoken by various Malaysian indigenous groups
- *Austroasiatic* — A language family spanning Southeast and South Asia
- *Sewang* — A ceremonial dance performed by the Temiar as part of animist spiritual life
- *Animist* — A traditional belief system that attributes spiritual essence to natural things
- *Betel* — A plant whose nut and leaf are chewed together, often with tobacco, in Southeast Asian cultures
- *Plaiting* — Interlacing strips of material, as in bamboo fiber or cane, to create patterns and objects
- *Cane* — Flexible material from rattan or similar plants, used for baskets and traps
- *Pucuk paku* — A fern species whose young fronds are used in Temiar cooking
- *Umbut bayas* — Palm pith, used in Temiar curry dishes
- *Nasi serempad* — Rice cooked inside bamboo
- *Aslian* — A language family spoken by Malaysia's indigenous peoples, including Temiar
- *Demonstrative* — A word indicating location or direction, as in Temiar pronouns like doh 'here' and tu:y 'elsewhere'
- *Allomorphic* — Relating to different forms of the same grammatical element
