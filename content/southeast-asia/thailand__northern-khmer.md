## Overview

Northern Khmer people, also known as Khmer Surin or *Khamae Lue*, are ethnic Khmers native to Northeast Thailand. They live especially in the Thai provinces of Surin, Sisaket, Buriram and Roi Et, and some have migrated from this region into Cambodia. Approximately 1.4 million Khmers speak Northern Khmer, a dialect of the Khmer language. Northern Khmer differs from Central Khmer in vowel phonemes, consonantal distribution, lexicon, grammar and pronunciation, and speakers of standard Khmer may initially have difficulty understanding it. The group has maintained elements of Khmer identity through its language, Khmer form of Theravada Buddhism and Kantrum music, while its history reflects contact with Lao, Thai, Kuy and other Mon-Khmer groups.

## Music & performance

**Kantrum** is a popular musical genre in the southern part of Isan that originated within the Khmer community. Its musical accompaniment is similar to Cambodian-Khmer music and includes drums and fiddles. The lyrics are sung in Khmer. In Cambodia and among Cambodians overseas, Kantrum music karaoke DVDs are described as a context in which Northern Khmer is written in the Khmer script.

## Festivals & rituals

An annual festival is celebrated each year in front of Prasat Phanom Rung temple to celebrate the 1000-year-old Khmer heritage of the region. During the festivities, Apsara dancers parade in front of the temple.

## Language & religion

Northern Khmer is a dialect of the Khmer language, also called Surin Khmer. It is spoken in Surin, Sisaket, Buriram and Roi Et, and by people who migrated from this region into Cambodia. Its differences from Central Khmer include vowel phonemes, consonantal distribution, lexicon, grammar and the pronunciation of syllable-final /r/. Northern Khmer has been influenced by Lao, Kuy and Thai, and most speakers are bilingual in Thai. Thai is the sole language of education and mass communication, while Northern Khmer is largely restricted to domestic and village use. In Thailand it is written in the Thai script; within Cambodia it is written in the Khmer script. The Northern Khmer practice the Khmer form of Theravada Buddhism.
