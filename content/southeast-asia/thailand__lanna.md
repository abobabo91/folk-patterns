## Overview

The Lanna are a Tai people of northern Thailand whose identity was historically shaped by wet-rice cultivation in river valleys rather than descent. They settled the alluvial plains and plateaus of nine provinces, developing a distinct language, script, textile traditions, and cuisine that persisted through a century of state-driven assimilation into Thai nationality. Since the mid-1990s, a cultural revival has renewed the use of traditional dress, script, and performance traditions. Their Theravada Buddhism coexists with animist beliefs centered on spirit worship.

## Material culture

Lanna material culture is dominated by hand-woven cotton textiles with geometric patterning, supplemented by silver metalwork, woodcarving, and objects of household production and daily use.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Cotton cloth with supplementary-weft geometric patterning is the dominant textile technique, worked in colored yarns across natural cream or apricot grounds.

- **Supplementary-weft patterning** (*khit*) — Bands of geometric ornament worked in colored cotton yarns across the ends of plain tabby cloth, ranging from narrow accents to broad panels.
- **Bedsheet** (*pha lop*) — Two equal pieces of cream cotton joined together with patterned bands at one end and macramé tassels formed from grouped warps.
- **Striped and warp-ikat cloth** — Skirts woven as striped tubes with dark blue or turquoise borders and polychrome stripes separated by light-and-dark blue ikat bands.
- **Shoulder cloths** — Long cloths patterned identically at both ends with the center field left plain or carrying scattered white dotted patterning.
- **Textured striped blankets** — Two joined cream cotton pieces with red striped bands separated by fine tabby patterning that raises the cloth surface.

### Clothing & dress

Lanna garments are plain in cut and modestly decorated, with the shoulder cloth being the primary garment bearing sustained ornament.

- **Woman's tube skirt** — Plain striped cotton skirt, the basic lower garment.
- **Shoulder cloth** — Worn by both men and women, patterned at both ends and left undecorated across the body.
- **Classic northern dress** — Hand-made cotton dress of traditional design worn by Lanna women, especially those of middle and upper classes, since the mid-1990s revival on special occasions and Fridays in public institutions.
- **Abdominal tattoo** (*phung*) — Male body practice distinctive to the Lanna, so characteristic that Siamese called them Lao phung dam (black-bellied Lao); tattoos in Tai Tham script are believed to possess particular powers.

### Architecture

Lanna architecture centers on temples with tiered lacquered roofs and ornamental cresting, and on settlements built in river valleys and plains rather than mountains.

- **Temple building** — Wooden walls with woven rattan roofs lacquered in red and black with gilding, featuring three or more roof levels with ornamental cresting ending in flame shapes and decorated gables.
- **Settlement pattern** (*muang*) — Local communities organized in alluvial plains, river valleys and plateaus where wet-rice cultivation was possible, avoiding wooded mountains.
- **Spirit house** — Small shrine commonly found in private gardens, stocked daily with votive offerings to protective spirits.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Silver embossed goblets and objects of household production, especially cotton-working equipment and domestic items, comprise the documented metalwork and everyday objects.

- **Embossed silver goblet** — Hexagonal goblet part of a betel nut set with floral band at rim and six panels depicting mythical, animal and human figures including a prince with bow and arrow.
- **Cotton reeling stand** — Wooden and metal stand carved in the shape of a cat, used for reeling cotton fiber.
- **Prayer flag** — Ritual object assembled from wood, thread, reed, pith, bamboo, cloth and metal.
- **Cotton-gin** — Wooden machine for processing cotton seed from fiber.
- **Sleeping mat** — Woven from vegetable fiber for domestic use.

### Jewelry & body adornment

No jewelry traditions are documented; the sole body adornment practice is male abdominal tattooing.

- **Abdominal tattoo** (*phung*) — Male body practice above the hips, distinctive enough that outsiders called the Lanna 'black-bellied Lao'; designs in Tai Tham script are believed to possess particular powers.
- **Amulet** — Object inscribed with Tai Tham script and believed by the Tai Yuan to possess particular spiritual powers.

## Music & performance

The Lanna maintain a music tradition distinct from central Thailand, regularly performed since the mid-1990s revival.

- **Lanna music tradition** — Distinct musical tradition separate from that of central Thailand, performed publicly at cultural demonstrations and heritage events since 1996.

## Dance & theatre

The Lanna have their own dance tradition, explicitly distinct from central Thai dance and performed regularly in Chiang Mai since the post-1990s cultural revival.

- **Lanna dance tradition** (*Tai Yuan dance*) — Distinct dance repertoire separate from central Thai traditions, regularly performed in Chiang Mai as part of cultural heritage events.

## Festivals & rituals

The Lanna ritual landscape centers on animist spirit worship and the widespread cult of a protective city spirit, with spirits housed in sanctuaries and private gardens throughout the region.

- **Spirit sanctuaries** — Small shrines scattered throughout Lanna territory dedicated to animist belief, still frequented by devotees seeking protection from spirits.
- **Protective city spirit** (*Chao Luang Kham Daeng*) — The most widespread cult among the Tai Yuan, known through two competing legends: one as a human sent by god Indra to teach Buddhist precepts and placed at the head of Chiang Mai's protective spirits; another as the lord of ogres guarding treasure in the sacred cave of Chiang Dao.
- **Prayer flag** — Ritual object assembled from multiple materials and used in observances.

## Foodways

The Lanna cuisine is distinctly different from central Thai food and served as a marker of cultural identity that survived Thai assimilation, rooted in wet-rice agriculture of the river plains.

- **Wet-rice cultivation** — The material and cultural basis of Lanna foodways, practiced in alluvial plains and river valleys and fundamental to Lanna self-identity as distinct from mountain peoples.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Lanna literary tradition flourished in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries in the Tai Tham script, with classical works in Pali and vernacular Lanna, and is being revived through scholarship and public signage since the 1990s.

- **Tai Tham script** (*tua mueang*) — Traditional Lanna script adapted from Old Mon script, capable of transcribing all six Lanna tones; driven from schools by the Thai alphabet but still taught to Lao Buddhist monks and being revived on public signs.
- **Classical literary period** — Sixteenth century high point of Lanna literature, when works were written in Pali and vernacular Lanna interspersed with Buddhist Indic vocabulary on palm-leaf manuscripts.
- **Sermon manuscripts** — Old palm-leaf manuscripts in Tai Tham script that remain in active use in Buddhist temples today.
- **Khun Borom Rachathiriat legend** — Shared Tai legend of a possibly mythical king whose several sons settled and ruled city-states across Southeast Asia and southern China.

## Language & religion

Kam Mueang is a Southwestern Tai language with six tones, traditionally written in Tai Tham script; the Lanna practice Theravada Buddhism alongside animist spirit veneration.

- **Kam Mueang language** — Southwestern Tai language of the Kra-Dai family with six tones, divided into five dialect groups by tone split and merger, now typically written in Thai script.
- **Theravada Buddhism** — Adopted from the Mon and practiced for centuries, coexisting with animist beliefs centered on spirit worship.
- **Spirit cults** — Animist practices including sanctuaries, household spirit houses, and the widespread cult of Chao Luang Kham Daeng, the protective spirit of Chiang Mai.

## Glossary

- *Khon Mueang* — People of the cultivated land; the Lanna's self-designation
- *Kam Mueang* — The Lanna language, a Southwestern Tai language with six tones
- *Tai Tham* — The traditional Lanna script, also called tua mueang, adapted from Old Mon script
- *tua mueang* — Traditional Lanna script, another name for Tai Tham
- *khit* — Supplementary-weft patterning technique on textiles
- *pha lop* — Lanna bedsheet made of two joined cream cotton pieces with patterned ends
- *muang* — Local community, the settlement pattern of Lanna villages
- *phung* — Abdominal tattoo distinctive to Lanna men
- *Lao phung dam* — Siamese term meaning black-bellied Lao, referring to tattooed Lanna people
- *Chao Luang Kham Daeng* — The most widespread protective spirit cult among the Tai Yuan
- *Kra-Dai* — The language family to which Lanna belongs
- *Southwestern Tai* — The branch of Tai languages to which Kam Mueang belongs
- *Theravada Buddhism* — The form of Buddhism adopted by the Lanna and practiced alongside animism
- *spirit house* — Small shrine in a private garden stocked daily with votive offerings to protective spirits
- *tabby cloth* — Plain weave cotton fabric, the base for Lanna patterned textiles
- *ikat* — Resist-dye technique creating patterned stripes on cloth
- *macramé* — Knotted decorative edging made from grouped warps on textiles
