## Overview

The Katang, also called Kataang, are an ethnic group predominantly living in Laos, with a few people living elsewhere in Southeast Asia. In 2015, 144,255 Katang were living in Laos, making them one of the largest subgroups of the Lao Theung and one of the largest true ethnic groups in the country. Most live in the southern provinces of Savannakhet, Saravan, and Champasak. The sources describe Katang longhouses, ear-piercing traditions, crop rotation, irrigation, slash-and-burn agriculture, animist beliefs, and the continuing observance of some Buddhist festivals.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Katang are described as one of the few Lao Theung peoples who do not live in houses on stilts or in trees. Instead, they live in long wooden houses. When a Katang man marries a woman, he adds a room to the house for his new family. One such wooden longhouse is described as being 328 feet long and can still be seen just north of Saravan City.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do document the use of bamboo tubes in ear-piercing practices.

### Jewelry & body adornment

An older Katang tradition for both men and women involved piercing the ears and placing a bamboo tube in the opening to stretch the earlobes. The source states that this tradition has almost entirely died out. No other jewelry, tattooing, henna, hair practice, or ritual adornment is documented.

## Festivals & rituals

Most Katang follow old ethnic beliefs, and many people who do so also follow Buddhism. Some Buddhist festivals are commonly celebrated by the Katang. Traditional Katang animism includes belief in many forest spirits, together with taboos intended to avoid disturbing them. One of the most revered spirits is the house spirit, which is said to inhabit every house.

## Foodways

The Katang practice crop rotation as a common form of agriculture. Many also practice irrigation and slash-and-burn agriculture.

## Language & religion

Katang or Kataang can refer to the Katang dialect of the Ta’Oi language, an alternative spelling of Kattang or Gathang language, or one of the other languages spoken by the Katang people. The sources name Northern Katang and Southern Katang. Religiously, many Katang follow old ethnic beliefs, and many people who do so also follow Buddhism. Katang animism includes belief in forest spirits and taboos concerning them, including reverence for the house spirit said to inhabit every house.
