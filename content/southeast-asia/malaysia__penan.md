## Overview

The Penan live in eastern and central Borneo. They are divided into Eastern and Western groups. Most have settled since the 1950s, but a small number remain nomadic. Their culture is closely tied to hunting, gathering, forest knowledge and resistance to deforestation.

## Material culture

Penan material culture uses forest materials such as rattan, wood, animal parts, sago palm and tree latex.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Rattan mats, baskets and straps are important recorded Penan objects.

- **Woven rattan mat** — A rectangular rattan mat has dark and undyed corners with bird, blow-pipe and moon patterns.
- **Rattan basketry** — Recorded baskets are made from rattan with nylon or string.
- **Rattan strap** — A rattan strap is listed among Penan museum objects.
- **Penan fabric** — A 1957 eastern Penan textile was made from Musa textilis bast in brown, grey, black and blue-black.

### Clothing & dress

Animal hides and other animal parts are used for clothing and shelter.

- **Chawat loincloth** (*chawat*) — A poem refers to people wearing Chawats, described as loincloths.

### Architecture

Penan life shifted from forest nomadism to settled longhouse villages after 1950.

- **Forest shelter** — A British Museum image shows Penan men resting in a shelter in Borneo.
- **Longhouse village** — State programmes and Christian missionaries encouraged settlement in longhouse-based villages.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The blowpipe and forest knives are central recorded Penan tools.

- **Blowpipe** (*kelepud*) — A kelepud is a three-metre wooden blowpipe made from Belian wood and bored with a bone drill.
- **Blowpipe darts** — Darts are made from sago palm and tipped with poisonous Tajem-tree latex.
- **Bush knife** (*tongol*) — A tongol has a wooden handle, rattan binding, metal blade and tree latex called mat.
- **Parang** — A parang is made from metal, wood, rattan and gum.
- **Knife with bone handle** — One recorded knife has a metal blade set into a long bone handle.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe sacred plants and animals, destroyed burial sites and major religious change.

- **Sacred plants and animals** — Plants and animals may be seen as embodiments of powerful spirits and deities.
- **Christianisation** — Missionaries of the Borneo Evangelical Mission baptised many Penan and encouraged settlement.
- **Islamic conversion** — Half of the Penan community in Brunei has converted to Islam, even if only superficially.

## Foodways

Penan food comes mainly from hunting, gathering and the forest.

- **Sago** — Sago is starch from the sago palm and is part of the Penan diet.
- **Forest prey** — Hunted foods include wild boar, barking deer, mouse deer, monkeys, birds, frogs, monitor lizards, snails and locusts.
- **Reticulated python** (*kemanen*) — The reticulated python, called kemanen, is among the hunted animals.
- **Molong** — Molong means never taking more than necessary from the forest.
- **Feel good** (*jian kenin*) — Jian kenin means “feel good” and is used in settled communities as an equivalent to “thank you.”

## Oral tradition & literature

Penan communities use complaints, declarations, testimony and advocacy to defend their forests.

- **Long Sayan Declaration** — The 2002 Long Sayan Declaration stated Penan wants and requirements to the Sarawak State Government.
- **Forest testimony** — A Penan spokesman described the forest as the community’s money and bank because it provides food.
- **Rumah Nor** — Rumah Nor is a documentary mentioned in the sources.
- **Tawai: a Voice From The Forest** — Bruce Parry’s documentary Tawai: a Voice From The Forest is mentioned in the sources.

## Language & religion

Penan is a Kenyah-related language, while Penan religious life includes animism, Christianity and Islam.

- **Penan language** (*Punan-Nibong*) — Penan is also known as Punan-Nibong and is spoken as a language complex in Borneo.
- **Eastern and Western Penan languages** — Western Penan is closer to Sebop than to Eastern Penan.
- **Animism** — The Penan were originally animistic hunter-gatherers who could view plants and animals as sacred.
- **Borneo Evangelical Mission** — The Borneo Evangelical Mission Christianised, baptised and settled many Penan.
