## Overview

The San Chay people, also called *Sán Chỉ* and known in Vietnamese as *Người Sán Chay*, live in Tuyên Quang Province in the Northeast region of Vietnam and in some nearby provinces. Their population was 201,398 in 2019. The source describes Pinghua as a form of Chinese language. Many San Chay live in remote areas where the land is not flat enough for paddy rice production, and therefore use slash-and-burn agriculture. In folk culture, the source identifies their *sình cồ* love songs and the *Slếch thlin mảy* festival, described as a New Rice festival. The San Chay are related to the Shanzi People in Guangxi, China.

## Music & performance

The San Chay sing *sình cồ*, identified by the source as love songs.

## Festivals & rituals

The San Chay celebrate the *Slếch thlin mảy* festival, which the source identifies as the New Rice festival.

## Foodways

Many San Chay live in remote areas and use slash-and-burn agriculture because those areas are not flat enough for paddy rice production.

## Oral tradition & literature

The source records *sình cồ* as San Chay love songs.

## Language & religion

The source states that the San Chay use Pinghua, described there as a form of Chinese language. It also gives the Vietnamese name *Người Sán Chay* and the alternative name *Sán Chỉ*.
