## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| Who | The Semang, an ethnic-minority group of the Malay Peninsula. |
| Where | Malaysia, especially Perak, Pahang, Kelantan and Kedah, plus southern Thailand. |
| How many | Thailand's Semang population was estimated at 240 people in 2010; no general total is given. |
| Language | Aslian languages in the Austroasiatic family; most also speak Malay, and settled Semang in Thailand often speak Thai. |
| Religion | Animism, with halas mediating between people and spirits; some Semang were Muslim by the end of the 20th century. |
| Known for | Jungle hunting and gathering · Bamboo and rattan objects · Halas and spirit rituals · Bamboo musical instruments · Leaf and fibre adornment |

## Overview

The Semang traditionally moved through wet tropical jungles, foothills and lowlands as hunter-gatherers. Many now live in permanent villages or Malaysian towns. Their material culture uses bamboo, wood, cane, rattan, leaves and fibre. Music, dance, scarification and rituals are linked to festive occasions, healing, danger and death.

## Material culture

Semang material culture relies mainly on bamboo, wood, cane, rattan, leaves and plant fibre for tools, containers, clothing accessories and adornment.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not record a named textile tradition, but they describe many plaited leaf, rattan and fibre objects.

- **Leaf and fibre basketry** — Bags, baskets and a quiver used plaited leaf, cane, rattan and vegetable fibre.
- **Plaited rattan** — Rattan was used for basketry, bindings and armlets.
- **Root girdle** — A woman's girdle was made from rhizomorph and root.
- **Pandanus cordwork** — A man's head-band used plaited pandanus leaf and rhizomorph cord with leaf tassels.
- **Fungus waist belt** — A waist belt or waistband was made from fungus.

### Clothing & dress

The sources record accessories and body ornaments more fully than everyday clothing.

- **Rattan armlets** — Men and women wore armlets made from plaited rattan.
- **Leaf-tassel head-band** — A man's head-band was made from pandanus-leaf and rhizomorph cord with leaf tassels.
- **Dancing crown** — A man's dancing crown was made from pandanus leaf and rhizomorph.
- **Festive leaf decoration** — Both sexes decorated themselves with leaves on festive occasions.

### Architecture

The Semang traditionally moved between temporary camps, although many now live in permanent settlements.

- **Temporary camps** — Groups moved between temporary camps within customary territories.
- **Wind screens** — Museum records show Semang wind screens.
- **Camp shelters** — Shelters could be destroyed immediately when outsiders intruded.
- **State-built villages** — Many Kintaq, Jahai, Batek and Lanoh people came to live in state-built villages surrounded by secondary jungles and plantations.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources record no Semang ceramics or metalwork, but they describe many tools and containers made from natural materials.

- **Blow-pipes and poisoned darts** — Blow-pipes and poisoned darts were among the recorded hunting objects.
- **Wooden mortar** — Men used a wooden mortar and pestle for toothache.
- **Leaf smoke fan** — A leaf fan was used to waft smoke into rat's burrows.
- **Bamboo tobacco case** — A bamboo tobacco case was decorated with engraved patterns.
- **Thunder-stone bag** — A leaf bag was used for a thunder stone.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Semang adornment includes seed and tooth necklaces, leaf and fibre ear ornaments, armlets and scarification.

- **Seed necklace** — Some necklaces were made from seeds.
- **Seed-and-tooth necklace** — Another necklace was made from seeds and teeth.
- **Leaf ear ornaments** — Women's ear ornaments were made from leaf, rhizomorph and root.
- **Rattan armlets** — Men and women wore armlets made from plaited rattan.
- **Adolescent scarification** — A serrated sugarcane leaf cut the skin, and charcoal powder was rubbed into the cut to mark the end of adolescence.

## Music & performance

Semang music uses bamboo and cane instruments, while halas lead ritual performances through songs, spells and trance.

- **Jaw harp** — The Semang played a kind of bamboo jaw harp.
- **Nose flute** — The Semang played a bamboo nose flute.
- **Two-string instrument** — A bamboo instrument with two rattan strings is recorded.
- **Carved flute** — A flute made from cane and bamboo was carved.
- **Hala healing performance** (*hala*) — A hala could use songs, massage, herbal medicine, spells and trance in healing.

## Dance & theatre

Song and dance took place on festive occasions, but no named dance or theatre tradition is recorded.

- **Festive song and dance** — The Semang sang and danced on festive occasions while decorating themselves with leaves.
- **Dancing crown** — A man's dancing crown was made from pandanus leaf and rhizomorph.
- **Tourist-centre demonstrations** — A Thai tourist centre established in the early 1990s presented features of Semang life in theatrical form.

## Festivals & rituals

Rituals surround festive occasions, coming of age, healing, camp safety and death.

- **Coming-of-age scarification** — Scarification marked the end of adolescence for young boys and girls.
- **Burial in a mat** — The dead were buried the same day after being wrapped in a mat.
- **Grave bamboo rack** — The dead person's belongings were kept in a small bamboo rack over the grave.
- **Tree burial** — Only people of great importance, such as chiefs or great magicians, received a tree burial.
- **Smoke divination** (*capnomancy*) — Smoke divination was used to decide whether a camp was safe for the night.

## Foodways

Semang groups moved through customary territories and changed what they gathered or hunted as resources changed.

- **Jungle harvesting** — People harvested jungle produce, wild fruit and valuable vegetation resources.
- **Resource trading** — Trade included aromatic woods, camphor, rubber, rattan, rhino horns, elephant tusks, gold and tin.
- **Grain mortar** — A museum catalogue records a mortar for grain.
- **Food-pounding mortar** — Another mortar was used for pounding food.
- **Village crops** — State villages introduced rubber, durian, rambutan, oil palms and bananas.

## Oral tradition & literature

Semang cosmology describes spirits, supernatural beings, a sky paradise and Karey, the god of thunder.

- **Sky paradise** — The sky is described as a paradise filled with flowers and fruit trees.
- **Earth on a snake or turtle** — The earth is imagined as a disk lying on a huge snake or turtle underground.
- **Stone pillars** — One or several stone pillars connect the earth with the sky.
- **Dream-returning beings** — Some supernatural beings were once ordinary people and return in dreams.
- **Karey** — Karey is the cruel and evil god of thunder who punishes taboo violations with lightning or wildlife attacks.

## Language & religion

Semang languages belong to the Aslian branch of Austroasiatic, and Semang religion is described as animist.

- **Northern Aslian languages** — Northern Aslian is one of the language groupings identified for Semang languages.
- **Central Aslian languages** — Central Aslian is one of the language groupings identified for Semang languages.
- **Southern Aslian languages** — Southern Aslian is one of the language groupings identified for Semang languages.
- **Halas** — Halas mediate with supernatural beings through ritual, dreams and trance.
- **Animism** — Natural objects as well as people are believed to have souls.

## Glossary

- *hala* — shaman and ritual specialist
- *halas* — plural of hala
- *capnomancy* — divination by smoke
- *Karey* — god of thunder
- *Aslian* — language branch of Austroasiatic
- *Jahai* — Semang-related language or dialect
