## Overview

The Mentawai, also known as Mantawai, Mentawei and Mentawi, are an Austronesian people of the Mentawai Islands, principally Siberut, Sipura, North Pagai and South Pagai, about 100 miles from West Sumatra province, Indonesia. Their population is estimated at about 64,000. They have traditionally lived in coastal and rainforest environments as semi-nomadic hunter-gatherers, although modernization and government settlement policies have changed many aspects of daily life. The Mentawai language belongs to the Austronesian language family. Their folk-culture significance in the sources is associated especially with the uma longhouse, Arat Sabulungan spirituality, tattooing, tooth sharpening, hunting, sago-based foodways and ceremonial vocal and dance arts. The people are documented as having migrated from Nias and lived in isolation for centuries before encountering the Dutch in 1621.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no textile motifs.

### Clothing & dress

Men traditionally wear a loin cloth made from the bark of a gum tree. Women wear a cloth wound around the waist and small sleeveless vests made from palm or banana leaves. The sources also describe necklaces and flowers worn in the hair and ears. No named head coverings, belts, footwear or separate ceremonial garments are documented. Mentawai people may be covered in tattoos associated with traditional rituals, identity, social status and relationships with nature.

### Architecture

The principal traditional dwelling is the *Uma*, a longhouse whose walls are made by weaving bamboo strips together. Its roof is thatched with grass, its floor is raised on stilts, and the flooring is made of wooden planks. Each Uma is decorated with the skulls of animals hunted by its inhabitants. The sources state that an Uma can house three to four families, and elsewhere describe uma as housing around 5–10 nuclear families, with some accommodating up to 20 families. Families occupy an uma according to paternal descent, making it a patrilineal extended-family unit. A *Lalep* is a smaller house containing one family, while a *Rusuk* is a home for widows and bachelors. Villages are usually established along rivers. Each tribe generally inhabits a specific village called a *langgai*.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They identify the *Palitai* as the traditional knife and the *Kurabit* as the traditional shield. Hunting equipment includes bows and poisonous arrows; the poison is made from a local leaf mashed and mixed with water. The sources also describe rudimentary tools used when clearing forest for cultivation.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Mentawai people wear necklaces and flowers in their hair and ears. Body tattooing, called *Titi*, is a major form of adornment and identity. It is carried out with cane and coconut charcoal dye, a nail, a needle and two pieces of wood fashioned into a hammer-like stick. A shaman called a *Sikerei* prays for the charcoal before using it to make the tattoo. Tattoo designs and motivations differ between regions and clans. The tattoos identify role and social status, reflect personal or communal relationships with nature under Arat Sabulungan, and are believed to carry material wealth into the afterlife and help ancestors recognize people there. Mentawai people also sharpen their teeth with a chisel for aesthetic reasons, a practice associated with beauty ideals.

## Music & performance

The sources describe vocal arts and dance arts as connected with traditional ceremonies and daily life. Vocal performance develops according to ceremonial needs, including weddings and funerals. Singing and dance also form part of the healing process conducted by a shaman, or *sikerei*. Mentawai arts are generally based on appreciation of the surrounding natural world, including imitation of the movements or sounds of hunted animals. The development of these indigenous art forms is described as increasingly limited to the Lua people. The sources also state that the number of *Sikerei* still practicing the Arat Sabulungan lifestyle and fulfilling their traditional roles has diminished to a few small clans in the south of Siberut island.

## Dance & theatre

The sources identify Mentawai dance as an art associated with traditional ceremonies, weddings, funerals and the healing work of a *sikerei*. Some artistic forms imitate the movements or sounds of hunted animals. No named dance, theatre tradition, mask performance, shadow-play tradition or dramatic repertory is documented.

## Festivals & rituals

They describe weddings, funerals and traditional celebrations as important contexts for vocal arts, dance and food. Pork is stated to be a traditional food that is always present, especially at ceremonies and traditional celebrations. The traditional religious system is Arat Sabulungan, an animist belief in which everything has a spirit and soul. It gives reverence to ancestral spirits, the sky, land, ocean, rivers and all that is natural. When spirits are forgotten or not treated well, they may bring bad luck, illness or haunting. Sacred objects are also important. *Sikerei* act as teachers, healers and caretakers within this system. The sources describe government policies, including a 1954 decree prohibiting animist religions, as contributing to the suppression of Arat Sabulungan and associated ritual and ceremonial practices.

## Foodways

Sago and fish are identified as staple foods. Sago is obtained from swamp forests surrounding Mentawai homes and is made from ground palm medulla; it is usually grilled. Rice is also used as a daily staple. Pork is a traditional food associated especially with ceremonies and traditional celebrations. Mentawai people cultivate crops by clearing forest with rudimentary tools. Their crops include taro and sweet potatoes, which are used in daily meals. They also eat fruits found around their homes, including durian, banana and papaya. Men hunt warthogs, chicken, deer and primates, while women and children gather wild yams and other wild foods and fruits. Dogs are used to locate animals, which are then shot with bows and poisonous arrows. Small animals are hunted by women. Fishing takes place in rivers, swamps and the sea.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that vocal arts developed in relation to ceremonies and healing, and that artistic forms can imitate hunted animals. Preservation efforts described in the sources include community-driven indigenous educational programs intended to reconnect Mentawai people with important aspects of their cultural and environmental heritage. Another initiative, called “The Cultural and Ecological Education Program,” provides opportunities to learn aspects of indigenous education and lifestyle considered important for present and future prosperity. These programs are intended to operate alongside mainstream education and to be taught by the Mentawai for the Mentawai organization.

## Language & religion

The Mentawai language is an Austronesian language spoken by the Mentawai people of the Mentawai Islands, West Sumatra, Indonesia. One account identifies three dialects: the North Siberut dialect, the South Siberut dialect and the Sipora-Pagai dialect. The North Siberut dialect is spoken in Monganpoula Village, the South Siberut dialect in Maileppet Village, and the Sipora-Pagai dialect in Sioban Village and Makalo Village. The Sipora-Pagai dialect is described as the standard dialect because it has the widest geographical distribution and the largest number of speakers and is located in the district government center. Another source lists dialect names including Silabu, Sipura–Simalegi, Sakalagan, Saumanganja–North Siberut, South Siberut–Taikaku–Pagai, Sikapone, Togiiite, Pokai, Simajegi, Simatalu, Paipajet, Sakuddei, Sagulubbe, Sirileleu, Sikabaluan, Sempungan, Saibi & Sarabua, Silaoinan, Sarareiket and Sabirut. Before Christianity, the traditional religion was Arat Sabulungan. Many modern Mentawai people have adopted branches of Christianity or some Islam, while others continue to follow Arat Sabulungan.
