## Overview

The Lahu are an ethnic group spread across Yunnan, Myanmar, Thailand, and Vietnam. Their most distinctive feature is a subgroup system based on the traditional colors of their clothing: Black Lahu, Red Lahu, White Lahu, Yellow Lahu, and Lahu Shehleh. They speak a Tibeto-Burman language and practice a mix of traditional religion, Buddhism, and Christianity. Their material culture centers on textiles, silver ornaments, and bamboo crafts.

## Material culture

Lahu material culture emphasizes textiles and silver, with indigo-dyed appliquéd cotton appearing in most garments and silver dominating personal ornament.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Lahu textiles are built from indigo-dyed woven cotton and appliqué, with polychrome bands and fine stitching in red, blue, lilac, cream, and orange.

- **Indigo-dyed appliqué cloth** — Woven cotton cloth dyed dark with indigo and decorated with appliqué applied directly to the ground, used for jackets and bags.
- **Woman's tube skirt** (*phasin*) — Assembled from three to five unequal horizontal sections of tabby cotton with paired warps, topped with cream bands and broken by polychrome stripes on red or dark blue grounds.
- **Embroidered and appliquéd cotton** — Coats and caps combining embroidery and appliqué in cotton and wool, fastened with large metal buttons.
- **Woollen belt weaving** — Men's belts made as woollen bands finished with fringe.
- **Cloth-and-bamboo wall hangings** — Decorative hangings combining fabric with bamboo, including images such as elephants.

### Clothing & dress

Lahu dress is organized by subgroup color, with indigo-dyed appliquéd jackets, coats, skirts, and belts forming the core of recorded clothing.

- **Indigo jacket** — Men's and women's jackets made of indigo-dyed woven cotton with appliqué, fastened with large silver or metal buttons or buckles.
- **Woman's tube skirt** (*phasin*) — Lower garment assembled from horizontal banded sections of cotton in cream, red, blue, and lilac with applied red lines on dark ground.
- **Cotton coat** — Women's coat worn with silver brooches, sometimes embroidered and appliquéd.
- **Boy's cap** — Children's headwear combining cotton and wool with both appliqué and embroidery.
- **Man's woollen belt** — Fringed band completing the recorded male outfit.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Everyday objects are made from local plant and textile materials, with bamboo and cotton appliqué predominating; pottery and metal tools are not recorded.

- **Bamboo quiver** — Hunting equipment made of bamboo, the only recorded hunting gear.
- **Bamboo wall hangings** — Decorative hangings combining cloth and bamboo, including elephant imagery.
- **Appliquéd cotton bag** — Carrying gear decorated with appliqué using the same technique as garments.
- **Appliquéd passport holder** — Modern travel object showing appliqué technique applied to twentieth-century use, closed with nylon zip.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Silver dominates Lahu ornament, appearing as neck rings, bracelets, earrings, and brooches; beadwork and glass beads also occur.

- **Silver neck ornaments** — Rings worn around the neck, with at least two examples recorded.
- **Silver earrings** — Women's pairs of silver earrings in the collection.
- **Silver bracelets** — Women's single and paired silver bracelets.
- **Silver brooch** — Ornament worn with cotton coats and catalogued as part of the outfit.
- **Glass bead necklace** — Woman's necklace of glass beads representing beadwork traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

Lahu ritual practice centers on naming and spirit protection, with birth names incorporating gender and zodiac day markers.

- **Birth naming system** — Two-syllable names combine a gender marker with information on the zodiac day of birth; boys born on Ox day are called Zanu, girls Nanu.
- **Spirit protection gatha** (*gatha*) — Buddhist verbal formulas requested from forest monks around 1930–1940 to protect against ghosts and demons, showing traditional spirit concerns addressed through Buddhism.
- **Surname imposition** — The Lahu historically used only given names until the Chinese government assigned surnames; about 90% now bear Lee or Zhang.

## Oral tradition & literature

Three Latin-script alphabets exist for Lahu, developed by Protestant and Catholic missionaries and by the Chinese government, with extensive documentation in Bible translation and linguistic corpora.

- **Protestant alphabet** — Started in 1906 by American missionaries for Black Lahu in Myanmar; first book published 1908, tone marks added 1917, revised 1950 and 1962; remains the main alphabet in Myanmar and Thailand.
- **Catholic alphabet** — Developed by Catholic missionaries in Burma in the 1930s, differing mainly in finals; survives among Lahu Catholics in Myanmar.
- **Pinyin-based alphabet** — Begun in 1953 and approved in March 1957, marking tones with letters l, d, q, r, t, f after the syllable; official script in China, revived in 1989 after disuse during the Cultural Revolution.
- **Lahu Bible** — Bible translation introducing neologisms in hygiene, music, and education alongside loanwords from English, Latin, and Greek.
- **English-Lahu Lexicon** — James A. Matisoff's dictionary and collection of Lahu texts from 1960s Thailand, part of language documentation projects.

## Language & religion

Lahu is a Tibeto-Burman language with seven tones, subject-object-verb order, and no syllable-closing consonants; traditional religion is polytheistic, with Buddhism widespread in China and Christianity established in Myanmar and spreading.

- **Black Lahu** — The standard dialect in China and lingua franca among Thailand's hill tribes, based on traditional folk taxonomy.
- **Yellow Lahu** — A newer cluster originating in messianic movements, with speech notably divergent from Black Lahu; refugees built an orthography in 1982.
- **Lahu Shi** — A dialect notably divergent from other varieties, also arising from messianic movements.
- **Traditional polytheism** — The folk religion of the Lahu before Buddhism and Christianity.
- **Buddhist conversion** — Buddhism was introduced in the late 17th century and became widespread, particularly among Lahu in China.

## Glossary

- *Lahu Shi* — Yellow Lahu, named for traditional dress color
- *Lahu Shehleh* — Fifth Lahu subgroup, named after subgroup color classification
- *phasin* — Woman's tube skirt made of banded cotton sections
- *gatha* — Buddhist verbal formula for protection from spirits
- *Zanu* — Boy's name for those born on Ox day in the zodiac
- *Nanu* — Girl's name for those born on Ox day in the zodiac
- *Protestant alphabet* — Latin-script orthography begun in 1906 for Lahu by missionaries
- *Catholic alphabet* — Latin-script orthography developed by Catholic missionaries in Burma in the 1930s
- *pinyin-based alphabet* — Official script for Lahu in China, approved 1957, marking tones with l, d, q, r, t, f
- *appliqué* — Decorative technique of stitching fabric pieces onto a cloth ground
- *tabby* — Plain-weave cotton fabric
- *polychrome* — Decorated in many colors
