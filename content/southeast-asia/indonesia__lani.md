## Overview

The Lani are an indigenous people in Puncak, Central Papua, and Lanny Jaya, Highland Papua. They are usually labelled “Western Dani” by foreign missionaries, although they are inaccurately grouped with the Dani people of the Baliem Valley to the east. Their population was reported as around 200,000 in the 1980s. The Lani language is a Nuclear-Trans-New Guinea language spoken in Highland Papua and is described as the Papuan language with the most speakers in Indonesian New Guinea. The sources identify distinctive clothing and body adornment, particularly the *kobewak* or *kobeba* worn by men and bark skirts worn by women.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Sali koe** — The *sali koe* is a colourful skirt made of bark. The sources name green, yellow, red, and purple as its colours.

**Sali keragi** — The *sali keragi* is a skirt made of bark, described as brown with purple tips.

### Clothing & dress

Lani men wear *kobewak* or *kobeba*, described as thicker and larger penis gourds made from bottle gourd. They can have a diameter of 10 cm or more and are flat at the top. Large examples are used to hold tobacco and other valuables. The direction in which the *kobeba* is worn is associated in the source with the wearer’s social status: straight upright signifies a virgin male; leaning to the right signifies a brave, rich, and nobleman; and leaning to the left signifies descent from Panglima Perang or Apendabogur, described as a war chief.

Lani women wear two types of bark skirt. The *sali koe* is colourful, in green, yellow, red, and purple, while the *sali keragi* is brown with purple tips.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The most distinguishable feature of the Lani and Dani tribes is identified as their *kotekas*, or penis gourds made from the bottle gourd. Among the Lani, these are called *kobewak* or *kobeba*. The sources describe their size, flat top, use for holding tobacco and other valuables, and the social meanings associated with their direction.

## Festivals & rituals

 The source does associate the direction of the *kobeba* with social status and describes a left-leaning form as signifying descent from Panglima Perang or Apendabogur, a war chief.

## Foodways

 They mention tobacco only as one of the valuables held in large *kobewak* or *kobeba*.

## Oral tradition & literature

 The language source notes that the Baliem Valley tribes were called Oeringoep and Timorini in literature from the 1920s, but says those names are no longer used.

## Language & religion

Western Dani, or Lani, is described as a Nuclear-Trans-New Guinea language. It is spoken by the Lani people in Highland Papua and is identified as the Papuan language with the most speakers in Indonesian New Guinea. The source describes aspects of its phonology, including aspirated allophones of oral stops at the beginning of words, voiced allophones of voiceless consonants intervocalically, and contrastive vowel length. They state that Christian ministry among the Lani grew the Christian population to 13,000 under John “Tolibaga” and Helen “Tukwe” Dekker.
