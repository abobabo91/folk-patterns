## Overview

The Tasaday are an indigenous people of the Lake Sebu area in Mindanao, Philippines. They are considered part of the Lumad group, alongside other indigenous groups on the island. The Tasaday became widely known in 1971 after reports presented them as forest dwellers using apparent “Stone Age” technology and living in complete isolation from Philippine society. Later reports disputed their isolation and their status as a separate ethnic group, while further linguistic and anthropological work supported the view that they were a distinct group, probably isolated for several generations. They describe the Tasaday language as distinct from neighboring languages and relate it to the adjacent Manobo languages.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They mention that the Tasaday were reported as forest dwellers and that photographs and media accounts contributed to public attention.

### Architecture

The sources describe the Tasaday as having a secluded cave home site and ancestral caves in the forest. In March 1972, visitors met the Tasaday at their secluded cave home site. In April 1972, land surrounding the Tasaday’s ancestral caves was declared the Tasaday/Manobo Blit Preserve.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Tasaday were associated in reports with apparent “Stone Age” technology.

## Music & performance

The sources mention media attention, a National Geographic documentary titled *The Last Tribes of Mindanao*, and public presentations about the Tasaday.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources discuss the political and media history surrounding the Tasaday, including the creation of the Tasaday/Manobo Blit Preserve in April 1972 and the closure of the preserve to visitors in 1976, but they do not present these events as Tasaday festivals or rituals.

## Foodways

They state that the Tasaday were likely a separate group living as gatherers deep in the jungle and that they were probably unfamiliar with agriculture.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do describe disputes over accounts of Tasaday identity and history. After an unauthorized visit to the Tasaday caves, Oswald Iten and Joey Lozano reported that the caves were deserted and claimed that the Tasaday were members of other local tribes presenting themselves as Stone Age people under pressure from Manuel Elizalde. Lawrence A. Reid later analyzed Tasaday linguistic evidence and concluded that the language contained forms used in Manobo languages of Kulaman Valley.

## Language & religion

The Tasaday language is described as distinct from that of neighboring tribes. Linguists believe it probably split from adjacent Manobo languages 200 years ago. A related source identifies Cotabato Manobo, also called Dulangan Manobo, as a Manobo language spoken in Mindanao, the Philippines, with Tasaday and Blit among its dialects. It places Cotabato Manobo in the Kalamansig, Palimbang, and Ninoy Aquino municipalities of Sultan Kudarat Province and the T'Boli municipality of South Cotabato Province. The sources describe the phonology of Cotabato Manobo, including vowel realizations in closed and open syllables and changes involving /h/, /ʔ/, /ɡ/, and /k/.
