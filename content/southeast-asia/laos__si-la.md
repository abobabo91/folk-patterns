## Overview

The Si La, also called Sila or Syla, are an ethnic group living in northern Laos and in two villages, Nậm Sin and Seo Hay, in Lai Châu Province in the Northwest region of Vietnam. The source used describes about 3,151 Si La people in Laos and another 600 living in the two Vietnamese villages; it also records a population of 909 in Vietnam according to the 2019 census. The Si La speak a Tibeto-Burman language closely related to Hani. Their primary occupation is cereal cultivation, supplemented by hunting and foraging. In folk-culture terms, the available source specifically documents their language, subsistence practices, and the distinctive custom of painting teeth red for men and black for women.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They document only the distinction that Si La men traditionally painted their teeth red and women painted theirs black.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The documented Si La body-adornment practice is tooth painting: men traditionally painted their teeth red, while women painted theirs black. The source states that this custom is increasingly uncommon among the younger generation. No Si La jewelry, tattooing, henna, hair practice, or ritual adornment is described.

## Festivals & rituals

The only documented custom is the painting of men’s teeth red and women’s teeth black, a practice increasingly uncommon among the younger generation.

## Foodways

The sources used state that the Si La primarily cultivate cereals, with hunting and foraging as additional activities.

The Si La speak a Tibeto-Burman language closely related to Hani.
