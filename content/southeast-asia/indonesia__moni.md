## Overview

The Moni, also known as the Migani, Megani, Djonggunu, or Jonggunu, are an Indigenous people in Intan Jaya Regency, Central Papua, Indonesia, in Western New Guinea. They speak the Moni language. The Moni language is spoken by about 20,000 people, according to a 1991 figure, in the Paniai lakes region of the Indonesian province of Central Papua. The majority of Moni language speakers live in Kemandoga valley. The Moni revere the bondegezou, a large black-and-white whistling tree kangaroo, as an ancestor.

## Language & religion

Moni is a Papuan language spoken in the Paniai lakes region of the Indonesian province of Central Papua. It is also known as Migani or Megani, as given by locals, and as Djonggunu or Jonggunu. Awembak, also called Awembiak, is a dialect.
