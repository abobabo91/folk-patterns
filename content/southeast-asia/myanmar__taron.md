## Overview

The Taron, also called T’rung, are an ethnic group living in the Himalayan foothills of northern Kachin State, Myanmar. They settled in the lower Adunlaung River valley in Naung Mun Township, Putao District, an area of dense forests, torrential streams, difficult terrain, and snow-clad mountains. Their population is declining severely. A 1997 field trip reported that only eight individuals of pure Taron stock remained. The sources describe the Taron as very small in stature and as having been called the “East Asian pygmies.” They have been proposed as descendants of Derung people from China. Their importance in folk-culture terms is connected mainly with their endangered population, migration history, and changing religious practices.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe a former religious practice rather than an annual festival calendar. The Taron previously practised a form of animism, with rites of passage led by a shaman called *nam’sa*. Most Tarong, like their neighbouring Rawang, practise Christianity.

The sources also describe a population decision connected with reproduction. In the late 1990s, Dawi, described as the youngest remaining pure-blood Taron, said that Taron babies were being born with increasingly severe birth defects without any known cause. The Taron elders decided that their population would go extinct rather than risk more misshapen children.

## Language & religion

The sources identify the group as Taron or T’rung and give the Burmese form တရုမ်း, with the pronunciation [ta.rumː]. The sources state that most Tarong, like their neighbouring Rawang, practise Christianity. Earlier religious practice included a form of animism, with rites of passage led by a shaman called *nam’sa*. The sources also discuss a proposed connection with the Derung people, who are based in the Dulong Valley in Yunnan, China. They state that “Derung” is a Chinese term for Taron or T’rung and report observed genetic similarities between the two groups.
