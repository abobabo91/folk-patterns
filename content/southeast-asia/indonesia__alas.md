## Overview

The Alas, also called *ukhang Alas*, *kalak Alas*, or Batak Alas, are an indigenous Batak sub-ethnic group from Southeast Aceh Regency, Aceh, Sumatra, Indonesia. They are found around Gunung Leuser, Ketambe, and the Alas River. The Alas are agrarian people who cultivate rice on irrigated fields, while some grow cash crops in gardens. Fishing in the Alas River and nearby brooks was formerly an indispensable source of animal protein, and fishponds are becoming important. Their society is patrilineal, with each person belonging to an exogamous clan. The Alas language belongs to the northern branch of the Batak subgroup of the Austronesian language family, although Alas speakers generally do not identify as Batak.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that the Alas converted to Islam between the 15th and 17th centuries. They also identify *Payung mesikhat* as an Alas ritual umbrella. No annual festivals, seasonal calendar, life-cycle rites, wedding customs, funeral practices, or other rituals are described.

## Foodways

The Alas cultivate rice on irrigated fields, and some grow cash crops in gardens. Freshwater fishing in the Alas River and brooks formerly provided an indispensable source of animal protein, while raising fish in ponds is becoming important.

## Language & religion

The Alas use the Alas language, also called Cekhok Alas, on a daily basis. Alas–Kluet, Alas, or Batak Alas is an Austronesian language of Sumatra. The three dialects named in the sources are Alas, Kluet, and Singkil, also called Kade-Kade. Alas–Kluet belongs to the Batak subgroup; together with Karo and Dairi, it belongs to the northern branch of that subgroup. The language is most closely related to the language of the Kluet people in Aceh Selatan Regency, and it is entirely distinct from Acehnese. The language was estimated to have 80,000 speakers in 2000. The Alas converted to Islam between the 15th and 17th centuries.
