## Overview

The Palaung, also called Palong or Ta'ang, are an ancient Austroasiatic ethnic group found in Shan State of Myanmar, Yunnan Province of China, and Northern Thailand. In China they are known as the De'ang people. Most Palaung in Myanmar live in northern Shan State, especially in the Pa Laung Self-Administered Zone, whose capital is Namhsan. The principal groups are the Palé, Shwe, and Rumai; the Riang and Danau are also discussed in relation to the De'ang ethnic nationality. The Palaung languages form a dialect cluster, and many Palaung also speak Burmese, Shan, and Jingpo. Most Palaung follow Theravada Buddhism, while some maintain an animist religion based on nature spirits called *nats*.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They state only that Buddhist temples can be found in most Palaung towns.

## Music & performance

The language sources mention Pali texts and literacy work.

## Festivals & rituals

They do document religious and educational practices associated with Theravada Buddhism: Buddhist temples are found in most Palaung towns, and many children are sent to monasteries at the age of ten, primarily for education. Most later return to lay life. Some Palaung also maintain an ethnic animist religion based on nature spirits called *nats*.

## Foodways

They state that the Myanmar military is believed to have derived benefit from poppy cultivation, which has caused serious drug addiction among local people in the Palaung region, but provide no broader account of food production or consumption.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do document literary and orthographic work. The Ta’ang Committee for Literature and Culture published the first Burmese-Ta'ang dictionary in 2012. During British rule in Burma, Palaung speakers used the Shan script. An American Christian missionary introduced another script in 1912, and Paw San devised a new script in 1955. From 1967 to 1968, the Shan Council held a session in Taunggyi to devise a new script and settled on a Burmese-based form. A standardised form was adopted in 1972 and is used in non-formal education in Shan State and Mandalay. Community groups in Shan State and Mandalay have recently undertaken efforts to standardise Palaung orthography.

## Language & religion

Palaung, or Ta'ang, also known as De'ang, is an Austroasiatic dialect cluster spoken in Burma, Shan State, and neighboring countries. The principal languages are Palé, also called Ruching, Rumai, and Shwe, also called Ta'ang or De'ang. The Palaung are divided into Palé, Rumai, and Shwe, each with its own language; Riang languages are reported to be unintelligible, or understood only with great difficulty, by native speakers of the other Palaung languages. A total number of speakers is uncertain: the sources record 150,000 Shwe speakers in 1982, 272,000 Ruching speakers in 2000, and 139,000 Rumai speakers at an unrecorded date. Palaung was classified as a “severely endangered” language in UNESCO's Atlas of the World's Languages in Danger. The majority of Palaung are adherents of Theravada Buddhism, while some maintain animist beliefs concerning *nats*.
