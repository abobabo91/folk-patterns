## Overview

The Boholano people, also called *Bol-anon*, live primarily on the island province of Bohol in the Philippines, with additional communities in Southern Leyte and Mindanao, especially Northern Mindanao and Caraga. In 2010, their population was 2,278,495. Boholanos are considered part of the Cebuano people and the wider Visayan ethnolinguistic group, which is described as the second largest Filipino ethnolinguistic group. Their culture is associated with the wider culture of the Philippines, particularly the Visayas, and reflects Austronesian foundations alongside influences from indigenous Melanesian people, Spain, Mexico, China, and other Asian countries. Archaeological artifacts from Mansasa, Tagbilaran City, Dauis, and Panglao are cited as evidence of an established Boholano culture.

## Material culture

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention artifacts excavated at Mansasa, Tagbilaran City, Dauis, and Panglao.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The people of Bohol are described as descendants of the last group of inhabitants who settled in the Philippines called *pintados*, or “tattooed ones.”

## Festivals & rituals

The sources identify a historical blood compact alliance known as the *Sandugo*. It took place on March 16, 1565, between the native king Datu Sikatuna and the Spanish conquistador Miguel López de Legazpi, and is associated with the first international treaty of peace and unity between the native king and the Spanish conquistador.

## Language & religion

Boholano, also called *Binol-anon*, is a variant or dialect of Cebuano spoken in Bohol, a major portion of Southern Leyte, and parts of Mindanao, particularly Northern Mindanao and Caraga. It is sometimes described as a separate language, although the sources state that it originated as a dialect continuum of Cebuano. Central Bohol speech has several phonetic distinctions: Cebuano *y* can become *j*, *k* can become *h*, and intervocalic *l* can become *w* after *u* or *o*. The sources describe most Boholanos as Roman Catholic, with other Christian denominations and a minority professing Protestant faith; the former indigenous Boholano religion was animistic. They also mention traditional indigenous religions without describing their practices.
