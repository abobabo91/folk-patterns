## Overview

The Dani live in the Baliem Valley of Highland Papua, Indonesia. They call themselves Hubula. Their culture includes sweet-potato farming, pig feasts, shell ornaments, net bags, and stone tools. Traditional fighting and the victory dance edai are also recorded.

## Material culture

Dani material culture includes plant-fibre bags, shell ornaments, stone tools, pig tusks, and earth-oven cooking.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Dani fibre work includes net bags, pouches, shell bands, and ropes with plant-fibre patterns and shell decoration.

- **Net bag** (*Nätväska*) — These string bags may have yellow, reddish-brown, or brown-white orchid-fibre patterns.
- **Shell band** (*Snäckband*) — This plant-fibre band uses kauri shells, seeds, cuscus skins, and plastic thread, and can be a gift or payment.
- **Vegetable-fibre bag** — This plaited bag is decorated on one side with yellow orchid stems.
- **Shell-decorated rope** — This long plaited rope has cowrie shells, fur, and other attachments.

### Clothing & dress

The profile gives little information about everyday clothing but records shell necklaces and bound pig tusks worn as ornaments.

- **Bound pig tusk nose ornament** — Two bound pig tusks are used as nose ornaments.
- **Shell necklace** — A shell necklace is usually worn by men and sometimes by women.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Dani houses, buildings, roofs, or settlement layout.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The records contain no ceramics or metalwork but describe several stone tools and axe blades.

- **Sharp stone knife** — A grey, slate-like stone tool has a long sharp edge and can be used as a knife.
- **Thin stone knife** — A light-green stone tool is a very thin slice that can also be used as a knife.
- **Stone chisel** — A greenish stone tool is described as a chisel.
- **Stone axe blade** — Stone axe blades include a blue-grey blade with a pointed back and a convex-convex section.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Dani adornment uses shells, seeds, plant fibre, pig tusks, fur, feathers, and other natural materials.

- **Three-shell necklace** — This necklace uses three kinds of shells and is usually worn by men, sometimes by women.
- **Cane-backed shell necklace** — Small shells are sewn onto a woven cane or similar foundation.
- **Seed-shell necklace** — Grey-white seed shells are threaded onto string or arranged in small cup-like forms.
- **Pig tusk nose ornament** — Bound pig tusks are worn as nose ornaments.
- **Shell-decorated rope ornament** — A shell-decorated rope has fur, feathers, hair-like tails, and empty cocoons attached.

## Music & performance

The sources do not identify Dani instruments or song genres, but they record a victory performance with weapons and ornaments taken in battle.

- **Victory performance** (*edai*) — This two-day dance is held after an enemy is killed.
- **Battle trophies** (*wim*) — Weapons and ornaments taken from an enemy are displayed during the edai.

## Dance & theatre

The main recorded performance is edai, a two-day victory dance after an enemy is killed.

- **Two-day victory dance** (*edai*) — The Dani hold this communal dance after killing an enemy.
- **Dead Birds** — Robert Gardner made this 1965 film from his experience recording Dani communities.

## Festivals & rituals

Communal pig feasts, exchange, cremation gifts, and traditional warfare are the main practices described.

- **Pig feast** — Pig feasts celebrate events, and the number of pigs killed can show the standing of the organiser.
- **Sweet-potato barter** — Sweet potatoes are a major form of barter, especially in dowries.
- **Bridal and cremation gift** (*Snäckband*) — A shell band may be given at a marriage, a cremation, other occasions, or as payment.
- **Ritual village warfare** — Traditional fighting between rival villages focused on insulting enemies and wounding or killing token victims, and is no longer practiced.

## Foodways

Sweet potatoes are a staple and are cooked with pigs and other crops in a covered earth oven.

- **Earth-oven cooking** (*bakar batu*) — Hot stones cook food in a covered pit lined and topped with stones.
- **Earth-oven cooking** (*barapen*) — Meat, sweet potato, banana, and cassava are wrapped in banana leaves and steamed in the pit.
- **Sweet potato** — Sweet potatoes are a staple crop and an important means of barter.
- **Communal pig cooking** — Pigs are cooked for special occasions, especially communal feasts, because they are too valuable for regular meals.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources do not identify Dani folktales or oral literature, but they record battle expressions and books about Dani life.

- **Battle expressions** (*wim*) — The expressions wim, “dead birds,” and “dead men” refer to weapons and ornaments taken from enemies.
- **Gardens of War** — Robert Gardner’s book documents Dani life and death in the New Guinea Stone Age.
- **The Dugum Dani** — Karl G. Heider’s book documents a Papuan culture in the highlands of West New Guinea.
- **Grand Valley Dani** — Karl G. Heider’s book presents the Dani as peaceful warriors.
- **Under the Mountain Wall** — Peter Matthiessen’s book describes two seasons in Stone Age New Guinea.

## Language & religion

The Dani languages have several regional groupings and use two basic colour terms, while religion is not covered.

- **Cool and dark colours** (*mili*) — Mili refers to cool or dark shades such as blue, green, and black.
- **Warm and light colours** (*mola*) — Mola refers to warm or light colours such as red, yellow, and white.
- **Grand Valley Dani** (*Hupla*) — Hupla is listed as part of the Grand Valley Dani language grouping.
- **Western Dani** (*Lani*) — Lani is another name used for the people and language west of the Baliem Valley.
