## Overview

The Jahai, also called the Jehai, are an indigenous people belonging to the Semang people group. They live in Perak and Kelantan, Malaysia, and in parts of Thailand. Traditionally nomadic, they are hunter-gatherers who occasionally practice swidden agriculture. Jahai communities live in the montane rainforests of northern Peninsular Malaysia and southernmost Thailand, including permanent settlements in parts of Royal Belum State Park. Other listed settlements include RPS Air Banun in Gerik, Perak, and Sungai Rual in Jeli District, Kelantan. Their language is the largest Northern Aslian language and has a distinctive vocabulary for describing odors.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe a religious system involving *Karei*, a supernatural force understood to oversee actions and behaviours. Jahai people follow taboos and avoidance rules in order to avoid attracting Karei’s negative attention. They believe that Karei can be scared away or attracted by various odors according to Karei’s preferences, including the unpleasant smell of a burnt crayfish.

## Foodways

They mention hunter-gathering, occasional swidden agriculture, and the unpleasant smell of a burnt crayfish only in connection with beliefs about Karei.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Jahai DoReCo corpus contains audio recordings of narrative texts with transcriptions time-aligned at the phone level, translations, and, for some texts, time-aligned morphological annotations.

## Language & religion

Jahai, also called Jehai, is an aboriginal Mon-Khmer language spoken by the Jahai people in the montane rainforests of northern Peninsular Malaysia and southernmost Thailand. It is the largest Northern Aslian language and is spoken by a little more than 1,000 people. Jahai parents commonly pass the language to their children as their mother tongue, and the language does not appear to be in immediate danger of extinction. Its distinctive odor vocabulary is based on abstract qualities rather than specific odor sources. On the surface level, its maximal syllable is represented as CV(C), with an obligatorily required onset consonant; stress falls on the last syllable, and there is no tonal distinction. The sources describe Jahai religious beliefs concerning Karei, taboos, avoidance rules, and odor-related practices.
