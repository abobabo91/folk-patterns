## Overview

The Bimanese, also called *Dou Mbojo* or Orang Bima, are an Austronesian ethnic group native to the eastern part of Sumbawa Island in West Nusa Tenggara province of Indonesia. Their population approaches a million, making them the second largest ethnic group in West Nusa Tenggara. They live principally in the regencies of Bima and Dompu and in Bima City, as well as in parts of Sumbawa Regency, Lombok, East Nusa Tenggara, and western Flores. Rural livelihoods include wet rice farming, animal husbandry, fishing, and, among highland communities, swidden farming. Urban Bimanese work in trade, local administration, and other professions. Their culture reflects Austronesian language affiliations, Islamic practice, indigenous highland beliefs, and historical connections with the Sultanate of Gowa and the Sultanate of Bima.

## Festivals & rituals

Sunni Islam is the predominant religion of the Bimanese. Lowland communities are described as fervent adherents of Islam, while indigenous folk beliefs remain important in everyday life among the mountain communities of the *Dou Donggo*, meaning “mountain people,” and *Dou Wawo*. Some Dou Donggo communities converted to Catholicism in the mid-20th century, while many others adopted Christianity or Islam without abandoning indigenous religious beliefs.

## Foodways

They do identify wet rice, millet, and maize as crops associated with Dou Donggo subsistence farming, and peanuts and soybean as cash crops cultivated in the lowlands of Bima island.

## Oral tradition & literature

Local traditions attribute the introduction of Islam to Malay merchants.

## Language & religion

Bima, also called Bimanese or *Nggahi Mbojo*, is an Austronesian language spoken principally on the eastern half of Sumbawa Island by Bimanese and Dompuan peoples. The autochthonous name for the territory is *Mbojo*, while Bima is described as an exonym. The language is spoken in Bima Regency, Dompu Regency, Bima City, parts of Sumbawa Regency, and the islands of Banta, Moyo, Sangeang, Komodo, and Rinca, as well as in western Flores. Dialects listed in the sources include Bima, Dompu, Donggo, Kolo, Mbojo, Sangar or Sanggar, and Toloweri. Bima belongs to the Central–Eastern Malayo-Polynesian subgroup. Neither the Bima nor the Sumbawa people have alphabets of their own; they use the alphabets of the Bugis and Malay languages. Sunni Islam predominates, while indigenous beliefs remain influential among some mountain communities.
