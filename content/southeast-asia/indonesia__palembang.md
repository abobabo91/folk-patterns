## Overview

The Palembang people, also called Palembang Malay, are an ethnic group native to the city of Palembang and surrounding areas in South Sumatra province, Indonesia. Palembang lies on the Musi River and is one of the oldest cities in Southeast Asia. It was the capital of the ancient Srivijaya Empire in the 7th century, when the city was part of a Hindu-Buddhist maritime kingdom that controlled much regional trade. The 2010 census recorded 1,252,258 Palembang living in Indonesia. They speak Palembang, a Malayic variety of the Musi dialect chain, primarily in Palembang and nearby lowlands, and also as a lingua franca throughout South Sumatra. Their material and intangible culture documented here includes language registers, mosque architecture, customary ceremonies, royal sweets, freshwater-fish cuisine, and culinary forms shaped by Malay, Javanese, Arab, Indian, and Chinese influences.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Islamic influence on Palembang culture is evident in its architecture, particularly in the city’s numerous mosques and the Great Mosque of Palembang, described as one of the oldest mosques in Indonesia.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe the jero or alus register of Palembang language as being used in conversations with community leaders, elders, or respected individuals, especially during Palembang traditional ceremonies. The sources also mention customary ceremonies and Eid al-Fitr, and state that Kue Maksuba, Kue Delapan Jam, and Kue Bolu Kojo may be served to honourable guests, during customary ceremonies, or during Eid al-Fitr. Kue Maksuba is also sometimes served during Eid al-Adha. Malbi is usually served during Eid al-Fitr or at a Palembang traditional wedding.

## Foodways

Palembang cuisine is associated with the city of Palembang in South Sumatra and is described as the second most well-known cuisine from Sumatra after Padang. Freshwater fish and prawns are central ingredients because of the importance of the Musi River. Named fish include patin, baung, lais, lele, gabus, mas, gurame, and belido; seafood such as shrimp, tenggiri, kakap merah, and Spanish mackerel is also popular.

The cuisine favours sour and sweet flavours. **Pempek** is a fishcake made from deboned fish flesh and tapioca flour, served with a dark, sweet, and spicy sauce called Cuko. It can be boiled, fried, or grilled. Named forms include lenjer, keriting, kapal selam, and pistel. **Tekwan** consists of small pempek balls served with fresh prawn soup, cellophane noodles, and ear mushrooms. **Model**, **Laksan**, **Celimpungan**, **Mie Celor**, **Burgo**, **Lakso**, and **Ragit** are other named dishes described in the sources.

Rice dishes include **Nasi gemuk**, a coconut rice dish, and **Nasi minyak**, cooked with minyak samin and spices. **Pindang Patin** is spicy iridescent shark boiled with spices and served with sliced pineapple, while **Pindang Tulang** consists of spicy beef ribs. **Malbi** is beef stewed with sweet soy sauce and spices. **Tempoyak** is fermented durian stir-fried with onion and chili pepper, and **Brengkes Tempoyak Ikan Patin** combines iridescent shark and tempoyak.

Named snacks include **Kemplang** and **Kerupuk**. Sweets and desserts include **Kue Maksuba**, **Kue Delapan Jam**, **Kue Bolu Kojo**, **Kue Bolu Suri**, **Kue Srikayo**, **Engkak Ketan**, **Gulo Puan**, and **Srikaya Tape**.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They do identify written and language-preservation efforts. The *Kitab Undang-Undang Simbur Cahaya* is described as a written source believed to have been compiled by Queen Sinuhun, the wife of Palembang ruler Prince Sido ing Kenayan, around the 17th century. It was written in Classical Malay with some influence from Javanese.

The sources also state that the government of South Sumatra, supported by the Indonesian Ministry of Religious Affairs, launched the Quran with a Palembang language translation in 2019. The refined register of Palembang, known as jegho/jero or alus, has been included as a local content subject for elementary and secondary schools in the Palembang area since 2021.

## Language & religion

Palembang, also known as Palembang Malay or Baso Pelémbang, is a Malayic variety of the Musi dialect chain. It is primarily spoken in Palembang and nearby lowlands and functions as a lingua franca throughout South Sumatra. Palembang is significantly influenced by Javanese, including in its core vocabulary, and also has influences from Arabic. The Palembang subcluster includes Palembang Lama, Palembang Pasar, and Pesisir. Palembang Lama is the traditional variety of ethnic Palembang communities; Palembang Pasar is a koiné used for interethnic communication; and Pesisir is spoken north and east of Palembang toward Jambi Province and the waters of Bangka Strait.

Traditional speakers use the jero or alus register and the sari-sari register. Jero or alus is used with community leaders, elders, and respected individuals, especially during traditional ceremonies, while sari-sari is used in everyday conversation. The language uses the Latin alphabet, with optional use of é, and some sources use diacritics. The sources state that most Palembang people are Muslims, with a small percentage following Buddhism, Hinduism, and Christianity.
