## Overview

The Ot Danum, also known as Dohoi, Malahoi, Uud Danum or Uut Danum, are an ethnicity of the Dayak peoples. They dwell at the upper reaches of the south Kapuas River and along the Schwaner range, bordering West Kalimantan and Central Kalimantan, Indonesia. They are the most important group of the upper Melawi River. The sources describe them as culturally and linguistically distinct from the Malay people, and linguistically distinct from the Ngaju people of the middle reaches of Central Kalimantan’s great rivers. Their name is explained through *Ot*, meaning “people” or “upstream,” and *Danum*, meaning “water.” The sources connect their culture with living alongside nature and maintaining ancestral traditions through care for the balance between mankind and surrounding nature.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that a majority of Ot Danum people practice the Kaharingan religion and that they revere the traditions of their ancestors by taking care of the balance between mankind and surrounding nature.

## Language & religion

Ot Danum is a Barito language of central Borneo, Indonesia, spoken by the Ot Danum people. Its dialects include Cihie and Dohoi. The sources distinguish the Ot Danum language from the languages of the Malay and Ngaju peoples. A majority of the Ot Danum people practice the Kaharingan religion. The sources also describe the group as closely tied to living with nature and revering ancestral traditions through care for the balance between mankind and surrounding nature.
