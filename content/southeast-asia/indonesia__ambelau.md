## Overview

The Ambelau people, also called *Orang Ambelau*, are an ethnic group forming the majority of the population of the Indonesian island of Ambelau. They also live on nearby Buru and other islands, including Ambon, other Maluku Islands, and Jakarta. Their largest community outside Ambelau is in Wae Tawa, a village south-east of Buru. The group numbers about 8,260 and speaks Ambelau, an Austronesian language traditionally classified in the Central Maluku branch of the Malayo-Polynesian languages. Most Ambelau people are Sunni Muslims. Their documented material culture includes bamboo houses, distinctive holiday clothing, and a subsistence economy based on farming, sago, hunting, and fishing.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Ambelau clothing is described as similar to clothing worn by many other Indonesian peoples. Men wear a sarong, described as a kind of kilt, together with a long-skirted tunic. Women wear a sarong and a shorter jacket. The sources identify a specific preference for red in holiday attire. Holiday clothing also includes distinctive hats: men wear a peaked cap with a plume, while women wear headwear described as having panache.

### Architecture

Traditional Buru houses, also associated in the source material with the Ambelau cultural context, are made from bamboo and are often built on stilts. Their roofs are covered with palm leaves or reeds, although tiles are becoming progressively popular.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that the majority of Ambelau people are Sunni Muslims and that Islam is deeply rooted in their culture and daily lives, but provide no further ritual detail.

## Foodways

Most Ambelau people are engaged in farming. Because the mountainous terrain of Ambelau hinders the cultivation of rice, maize, sago, sweet potato, cocoa, coco, allspice, and nutmeg are grown instead in coastal areas. Some residents work at the sago plantation on Buru. Hunting the wild pig Buru babirusa is common, while tuna fishing is mostly localized to the villages of Massawa and Ulima.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Ambelau language has no writing system.

## Language & religion

Ambelau is an Austronesian language. It was spoken by about 5,700 Ambelau people in 1989, more than 5,000 of them on Ambelau and most of the others in Wae Tawa on Buru. It has traditionally been classified in the Central Maluku branch of the Malayo-Polynesian languages, while more recent classifications place it with the Sula–Buru languages. The language has no dialectal variation and no writing system. Most Ambelau people also speak Indonesian or Ambonese Malay, a creolized form of Malay. The majority of Ambelau people are Sunni Muslims, and Islam is deeply rooted in their culture and daily lives.
