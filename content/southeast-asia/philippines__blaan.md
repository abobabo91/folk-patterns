## Overview

The Blaan are one of the indigenous peoples of Southern Mindanao in the Philippines. They live near Lake Sebu and Tboli municipalities of South Cotabato, Sarangani, General Santos, the southeastern part of Davao, and around Lake Buluan in Cotabato. Blaan is an Austronesian language belonging to the Bilic microgroup of the Philippine language subgroup. The Blaan are neighbors of the Tboli and are known for brasswork, beadwork, and *tabih* weaving. Their cultural life includes Indigenous rituals, abaca-fiber weaving, brass and copper work, bead decoration, and the use of their native language alongside Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Tagalog, and, to some extent, Ilocano.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Mabal tabih** — *Mabal* or *mabal tabih* is the Blaan art of weaving abaca fiber. The cloth produced through this process is called the *tabih*. Weavers join strands of abaca fiber by hand and do not use spinning wheels.

**Igêm** — *Igêm* are traditional mats associated with Blaan weaving. Estelita Bantilan is identified as a master weaver of these mats and received the National Living Treasures Award in 2016.

**Takmon** — *Takmon* refers to women's blouses and trousers decorated with plastic beads or shell sequins. The beadwork creates intricate designs.

**Msif** — *Msif* refers to men's pants and shirts decorated with geometric and other designs depicting the environment or the Solar System. These designs are sewn with cotton yarns.

**Motif vocabulary.** Geometric designs, designs depicting the environment, designs depicting the Solar System.

### Clothing & dress

Blaan clothing includes colorful embroidered native costumes and beadwork accessories. Women particularly wear heavy brass belts with brass tassels ending in tiny brass bells. Women's blouses and trousers may be decorated with plastic beads or shell sequins and are called *takmon*. Men's pants and shirts may carry geometric designs and designs depicting the environment or the Solar System, sewn with cotton yarns; these garments are called *msif*.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Blaan have a tradition of creating art from brass and copper. They smelt these metals to produce small bells and handles for long knives. The knives, called *fais*, are made with intricately designed brass.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Blaan women wear heavy brass belts with brass tassels ending in tiny brass bells. Beadwork accessories are part of the colorful native costumes, and plastic beads or shell sequins are sewn onto women's blouses and trousers.

## Festivals & rituals

The Blaan practice Indigenous rituals while adapting to the way of life of modern Filipinos.

## Foodways

The Blaan tribe at the foot of Mount Matutum Protected Landscape supports livelihoods by collecting the feces of the Philippine palm civet for processing into wild civet coffee. Sitio 8 village, Barangay Kinilis, Polomolok is described as a civet-coffee-producing settlement.

## Language & religion

Blaan, also known as Bla'an, is an Austronesian language spoken by an indigenous ethnic group of the same name. It belongs to the Bilic microgroup of the Philippine language subgroup, together with Giangan Manobo, Tiruray, and Tboli. The two major varieties are Koronadal Blaan, also called Tagalagad, and Sarangani Blaan, also called Tumanao. Blaan speakers have additionally developed literacy in Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Tagalog, and, to some extent, Ilocano.

Blaan Indigenous religion includes a pantheon containing Melu, the Supreme Being and creator; Fiuwe, a spirit who lived in the sky; Diwata, a spirit who joined Fiuwe in the sky; Tasu Weh, the evil spirit; Fon Kayoo, the spirit of the trees; Fon Eel, the spirit of water; Fon Batoo, the spirit of rocks and stones; Tau Dilam Tana, the spirit who lives in the underworld; Sawe, who joined Melu to live in the world; and Loos Klagan, the most feared deity.
