## Overview

The Mongondow, also called the Bolaang Mongondow people, are an Austronesian ethnic group native to the western part of North Sulawesi. They have traditionally been concentrated in North Sulawesi and Gorontalo and are predominantly Muslim. Their historical political unity was expressed through the Kingdom of Bolaang Mongondow, which became part of the western regencies of North Sulawesi after Indonesian independence. Mongondow, Bolango and Bintauna belong to the Greater Central Philippine languages branch, alongside Gorontalo. The Mongondow language has more than 200,000 speakers and historically served as the official language of the Bolaang Mongondow Kingdom.

## Festivals & rituals

The group is described as predominantly Muslim.

## Foodways

The sources describe hunting, fishing, processing of sago and harvesting tubers in the jungle as sources of income in earlier periods, before the Mongondow had learned farming.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that the Mongondow people believed their ancestors originated from the offspring of Gumalangit and Tendeduata, and from Tumotoiboko and Tumotoibokat, who lived on Mount Komasan in what is now Bintauna, North Bolaang Mongondow Regency.

## Language & religion

Mongondow, or Bolaang Mongondow, is one of the Philippine languages spoken in Bolaang Mongondow Regency, Kotamobagu city and neighbouring regencies of North Sulawesi and Gorontalo Provinces, Indonesia. Mongondow, Bolango and Bintauna belong to the Greater Central Philippine languages branch. Mongondow has lexical influence from Malay, Ternate and the Indonesian national language, and younger generations are shifting toward Manado Malay. The language is described as threatened, while Lolak is described as moribund. The Mongondow people are predominantly Muslim and also use Manado Malay for communication with other North Sulawesi people.
