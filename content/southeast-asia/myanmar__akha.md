## Overview

The Akha live in small, high mountain villages. Their culture centers on rice, family, ancestors, land, and the natural world. They are known for spirit gates, colorful women’s clothing, large headdresses, hunting equipment, and oral traditions. Their yearly rituals include ancestor offerings and the four-day Swing Festival.

## Material culture

Akha material culture uses cotton, indigo, bamboo, wood, cane, iron, silver, shell, glass, seeds, and feathers in clothing, tools, houses, ornaments, and ritual objects.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Akha women spin cotton, weave it on foot-treadle looms, dye it with indigo, and add embroidery and beads.

- **Indigo-dyed cloth** — Women weave cotton cloth and dye it with indigo.
- **Embroidered women’s clothing** — Women’s clothing includes a black jacket with heavily embroidered cuffs and lapels, a short black skirt, and a white beaded sporran.
- **Beaded sash** (*jejaw*) — The jejaw is worn by girls and hangs down the front of the skirt.
- **Ulo Akha headdress** (*Ulo Akha*) — This headdress has a bamboo cone covered with beads, silver studs, and seeds, with coins and chicken-feather tassels.
- **Pamee Akha headdress** (*Pamee Akha*) — This trapezoidal cap has silver studs, coins, beaded side flaps, and long chains of linked silver rings.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional women’s clothing combines broad leggings, a short black skirt, a white beaded sporran, and a loose black jacket with heavy embroidery.

- **Woman’s black cotton coat** — Museum records include a woman’s black cotton coat.
- **Pleated black cotton skirt** — Museum records include a woman’s pleated woven black cotton skirt.
- **Appliquéd and embroidered apron** — Museum records include an appliquéd and embroidered apron.
- **Appliquéd cotton sash** — Museum records include an appliquéd cotton sash.
- **Cotton, hemp, silver, glass, and shell ensemble** — A museum record identifies an ensemble made from cotton, hemp, silver, glass, and shell dating to about 1900.

### Architecture

Akha villages use wooden spirit gates and houses made from logs, bamboo, and thatch.

- **Low house** — A low house is built on the ground.
- **High house** — A high house is built on stilts.
- **Spirit gate** — A carved wooden gate divides the village world of people and domestic animals from the outside world of spirits and wildlife.
- **Carved gate figures** — Village gates may show carved male, female, and bird figures.
- **Roof carvings** — Carvings on house roofs are used to control the flow of spirits.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Akha everyday objects include baskets, weaving tools, bamboo arrows, and ironwork made from local materials.

- **Poisoned iron-and-bamboo arrow** — Museum records include poisoned arrows made from iron and bamboo.
- **Poisoned bamboo arrow** — Museum records include poisoned arrows made from bamboo.
- **Unpoisoned bamboo arrow** — A museum record identifies an unpoisoned bamboo arrow associated with the Akha.
- **Rice basket** — Rice baskets are made from wood and cane.
- **Ironworker** (*pa jee*) — The pa jee is an expert in ironwork and holds the second most important position in society.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Silver, beads, shells, feathers, seeds, and plant fibers decorate Akha headdresses and other ornaments.

- **Silver geometric bracelet** — Museum records include silver bracelets with incised geometric patterns.
- **Glass-bead necklace** — Necklaces may use glass beads, fibre, carnelian, and shell.
- **Rattan armlet** — An armlet is made from rattan, a yellow leaf, fibre cord, coix seeds, and hair.
- **Dragon-head silver armband** — Older Akha men wear a silver armband with dragon heads and a hollow bracelet containing a silver bead called the heart.
- **Headdress ornaments** — Women’s headdresses may include silver coins, monkey fur, dyed chicken feathers, silver rings, balls, pendants, and inscribed plates.

## Music & performance

Akha music is heard in festival singing, traditional dances, courtship songs, and modern radio listening.

- **Swing Festival songs** — Akha women perform traditional songs for villagers during the Swing Festival.
- **Courtship songs** — Village squares were once filled with courtship songs.
- **Jew’s harp** — A museum record identifies a Jew’s harp made of wood and thread, but gives no Akha name or performance context.

## Dance & theatre

Women perform traditional dances during the Swing Festival while displaying clothing, ornaments, age, and marriageable status.

- **Swing Festival dance** — During the Swing Festival, women dress in traditional clothing and ornaments and perform traditional dances for villagers.

## Festivals & rituals

Akha ritual life includes ancestor offerings, rice rites, village-gate building, and the annual Swing Festival.

- **Blessings** (*guivlahav*) — Many rituals seek guivlahav, or blessings, from ancestors for fertility and health.
- **Akha Swing Festival** (*Yehkuja*) — Yehkuja is a four-day festival in late August, held on the 120th day after rice planting and known as “eating bitter rice.”
- **Women’s New Year** — The Swing Festival is especially important for women and is also called Women’s New Year.
- **Men’s New Year** — The traditional New Year in late December is called Men’s New Year.
- **Village swing** (*dzoeuh mah*) — An elder called a dzoeuh mah builds the village swing each year for an ancestor offering linked to rice fertility.

## Foodways

Rice is the main staple, and Akha foodways combine farming, gathering, fishing, beekeeping, and hunting.

- **Highland rice** — Highland rice is the main staple and is important in Akha culture and ritual.
- **Dry-land rice farming** — Most Akha grow dry-land rice that depends on rainfall.
- **Forest gathering** — Women gather plants, eggs, and insects from the forest.
- **Bee gums** — Some villages build bee gums to attract colonies and harvest honey.
- **Barking deer hunting** — Hunting is a male activity, and the barking deer is a favorite prey.

## Oral tradition & literature

Akha beliefs and history are passed down through oral recitation, especially religious teachings and long male genealogies.

- **Akha Way** (*Akha Zang*) — The Akha Zang, or Akha Way, gives guidelines for life.
- **Origin account** — The origin account says spirits and people were born of the same mother until a quarrel separated them.
- **Patrilineal genealogy** — Akha men recite their family line back more than 50 generations to the first Akha, Sm Mi O.
- **Marriage genealogy rule** — A man and woman who share a male ancestor within their last six generations may not marry.

## Language & religion

Akha is a tonal language with several writing systems, and zahv emphasizes ancestors, souls, family, land, and nature.

- **Akha language** (*Akha*) — Akha is spoken across southern China, eastern Burma, northern Laos, and northern Thailand.
- **Baptist script** — Paul Lewis created the commonly used Latin-based Baptist script in 1950.
- **Portaluppi transcription** — Father Portaluppi devised the first documented Akha transcription system in 1927.
- **Akha religion** (*zahv*) — Zahv combines animism and ancestor worship, although Akha often reject that simple label.
- **Soul belief** — People and rice are considered to have souls, and the flight of the soul causes disease.
