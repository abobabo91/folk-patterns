## Overview

The Mee, also known as Bunani Mee, Ekari, Ekagi, Kapauku, and Tapiro, are a people of the Paniai Lakes area of Central Papua, Indonesia. They speak Ekagi, a Trans–New Guinea language spoken by about 100,000 people in the Paniai lakes region, including Enarotali, Mapia, and Moanemani. The name Mee is shortened from *Makado Mee*, meaning “true human.” Their history includes oral traditions concerning an ancestor from a place east of the Baliem Valley called *Pupupapa* or *Pagimo Peku*, trading relationships with surrounding peoples, and contact with European explorers during the 1909–1911 expedition by the British Ornithologists' Union.

## Language & religion

Ekari, also called Ekagi, Kapauku, and Mee, is a Trans–New Guinea language spoken by about 100,000 people in the Paniai lakes region of the Indonesian province of Central Papua. It is spoken in villages including Enarotali, Mapia, and Moanemani. The language has vigorous use, while its documentation is described as quite limited. Ekari has pitch accent: one syllable in a word may have a high tone contrasting with words without a high tone.
