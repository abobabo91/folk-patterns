## Overview

The Urak Lawoi are an aboriginal Austronesian people residing on the islands of Phuket, Phi Phi, Jum, Lanta, Bulon, Lipe and Adang, in the Adang Archipelago, off the western coast of Thailand. Their population is approximately 6,000. They are also known as Orak Lawoiʼ, Lawta, Chao Tha Le, Chao Nam and Lawoi, and are one of several Austronesian ethnicities referred to as “Sea Gypsies,” or *chao leh* in Thai. Their local way of life has been changing rapidly because of the encroachment of the market economy and the opening of Tarutao National Marine Park. They have largely not benefited from tourism on Ko Lipe, where families have been forced from their land.

## Festivals & rituals

The **Ari Pajak ceremony** involves setting adrift a boat about three metres long, divided into three compartments, carrying ancestral spirits towards Gunung Jerai in Kedah. Named ancestors are associated with animals, including Toq Bu Rong with the bird and Toq Bi Kong with the shark. The sources state that approximately a thousand years of evidence are missing between the last makers of the region’s rock art and the recorded ritual. They also state that no DNA comparison has been made between prehistoric skeletons from the area and the sea people, so the proposed relationship is not established.

## Language & religion

Urak Lawoiʼ, also written Urak Lawoc, is a Malayic language spoken in southern Thailand. It is related closely to Malay but influenced by Thai. The language spoken by the Orang Laut between Sumatra and the Malay Peninsula consists of divergent Malayic lects with connections to various Sumatran Malay varieties. Urak Lawoiʼ has no tones except in Thai loans; words are usually stressed in the penultimate syllable, and interrogative sentences have rising intonation while negative sentences have lower-pitch intonation.
