## Overview

The Rakhine, also known as Arakanese, are a Southeast Asian ethnic group forming the majority along the coastal region of present-day Rakhine State, formerly called Arakan, in Myanmar. Communities also live in the Ayeyarwady and Yangon Regions, Bangladesh’s Chittagong Hill Tracts, India’s Northeastern states, Singapore, Japan, Malaysia and Thailand. The Rakhine people consist of seven ethnic groups: Rakhine, Kamein, Kwe Myi, Daingnet, Maramagyi, Mru and Thet. They constitute approximately 4.61% or more of Myanmar’s total population. Their language is closely related to Burmese and belongs to the Tibeto-Burman language family. Rakhine culture includes distinctive clothing, festivals, cuisine, wrestling, literature, Buddhist practice and a long history of kingdoms, scripts and architecture.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Rakhine men mostly wear a sarong called *lungyi*, while women also wear a sarong called *thabein*. The sources describe Rakhine traditional dress as one way of distinguishing a Rakhine person from a Bamar person.

### Architecture

Rakhine historical architecture includes the temples of the Mrauk U period, including **Shite-thaung Temple** and **Htukkanthein Temple**. The sources state that more than six million shrines and pagodas flourished in Mrauk-U and describe the architecture as notable for durability, architectural skill and ornamentation. The sources also mention the remnants of Dhanyawadi’s architecture and artifacts.

## Music & performance

The sources state that Indian influence remains in aspects of Arakanese culture, including literature, music and cuisine. No specific musical tradition can therefore be described from the sources used.

## Dance & theatre

They mention that participants in Kyin wrestling perform warming-up dances called *kyin kwin* before matches, but provide no further description of the dance.

## Festivals & rituals

The Rakhine celebrate several festivals, including **Sangrain** and **Buddha Purnima**. Sangrai is described as a traditional ceremony of the Marmas and Rakhines marking the New Year. Its main attraction is the water sport called Marmara “Ri Long Poye,” held on the last day of the festival. Sangrai means saying goodbye to the old year and welcoming the new year; in the Chittagong Hill Tracts, the new Jhum farming season begins afterward.

The Rakhine also observe **Buddha Purnima**, or Vesak, known as the Buddha’s Birthday. It commemorates the birth, enlightenment and death of Buddha and is observed on the full moon of Vaisakha, usually in May. **Pavarana Purnima** is a Buddhist holy day celebrated by the Rakhine on the Aashvin full moon. It marks the end of the three lunar months of Vassa, sometimes called Buddhist Lent. At night, lanterns are blown into the sky to honor the heritage of Gautam Buddha.

## Foodways

Rakhine cuisine is associated with the coastal nature of Rakhine State and is described as bold and flavorful, with seafood playing an important role. **Rakhine mont di** consists of rice vermicelli noodles and is popular across Myanmar. **Mont di** is also described as a spicy rice noodle soup with fish broth, while **Rakhine Moti** is a pungent noodle soup.

Other foods named in the sources include prawns, crab and fish, prepared in curries or grilled. **Rakhine Moat Te Thoat** is a salad made with rice vermicelli, fish cake and fried beans. Rakhine food is distinguished in the source by its emphasis on spiciness, sourness and locally sourced ingredients.

## Oral tradition & literature

The history of the Arakanese can be traced through chronicles including **Maha Razawin**, **Rakhine Razawin**, **Rakhine Razawin Haung**, **Dhanyawaddy Ayedawbon** and **Rakhine Razawin Thit**. These texts remain untranslated into English.

Rakhine oral traditions and written records describe several alternative origin myths. One traces the Rakhine to an intermarriage between a highland Mro and a lowland queen; another traces Rakhine monarchs to Mahasammata, described as the legendary first monarch of the world. Arakanese legends also claim that Rakhine people are Aryans who came from Shakya in India, while other accounts describe mixed Indo-Aryan and Tibeto-Burman origins.

## Language & religion

Rakhine, also known as Arakanese, is a Tibeto-Burman language spoken primarily in Rakhine State, parts of south-eastern Bangladesh and parts of southern Tripura in India. It is closely related to Burmese and is spoken by the Rakhine and Marma peoples. The sources estimate around one million native speakers and a further million second-language speakers. Its three dialects are Sittwe–Marma, Ramree and Thandwe.

Rakhine is written using the Burmese script, which descends from Southern Brahmi. The first extant Arakanese inscriptions include the Launggrak Taung Maw inscription and the Mahathi Crocodile Rock inscription, dated to the 1300s. Arakanese inscriptions transitioned from square letters associated with stone inscriptions to rounder letters during the early 1400s.

The Rakhine are predominantly Theravada Buddhist. Rakhine State contains many Buddhist temples, and Rakhine religious observances include Buddha Purnima and Pavarana Purnima.
