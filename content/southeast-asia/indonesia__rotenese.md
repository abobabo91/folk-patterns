## Overview

The Rotenese are native inhabitants of Rote Island, with other communities in West Timor and on surrounding islands including Ndao, Nuse, Pamana, Doo, Heliana, Landu, and Manuk. They are associated with the Austronesian language family and with the Southwest Malayo-Polynesian languages. Their livelihoods include agriculture, animal farming, fishing, tapping Neera, palm crafting, small trades, and textile work. Their folk-culture profile includes traditional weaving, woven Pandan leaves, pottery, distinctive clothing, the *ti'i langga* straw hat, patriarchal kinship and exogamy clan marriage customs, and religious traditions centered on Lamatuan or Lamatuak.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Traditional weaving** — Rotenese women work on traditional weaving handicrafts.

**Woven Pandan leaves** — Woven Pandan leaves are among the handicrafts associated with Rotenese women.

**Palm crafting** — Palm crafting is included among Rotenese livelihoods.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Rotenese clothing includes a *kain*, a cloth up to 2.5 meters long wrapped around the waist and reaching the knees or ankles. Clothing also includes jackets and shirts. A specific straw hat called *ti'i langga* is part of the clothing tradition. The sources do not distinguish everyday from ceremonial dress, identify separate men's and women's garments, or describe belts, footwear, jewelry, or other forms of adornment.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Pottery is identified as one of the handicrafts associated with Rotenese women.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that traditional Rotenese beliefs center on a creator known as Lamatuan or Lamatuak, regarded as a creator, predestinator, and one who blesses, and symbolized by a three-branched pillar. The sources also describe exogamy clan marriage customs and a patrilocal family arrangement in which the wife resides in her husband’s community, but provide no further ritual details.

## Foodways

The common Rotenese diet consists of tubers, porridge, and fish with spicy seasonings. Agriculture produces rice, corn, cassava, sorghum, millet, pepper, peanuts, vegetables, and coffee.

## Language & religion

Rotenese is described as an Austronesian language belonging to the Southwest Malayo-Polynesian languages, with several dialects. The main dialects named are Lole (Loleh), Ringgou, Termanu, Bilba, Dengka, Tii (Thie), Oenale, and Dela (Delha). The related discussion of Timoric languages places them within the Central–Eastern subgroup of Austronesian languages and describes languages spoken on Timor, neighboring Wetar, and, depending on classification, Southwest Maluku.

Traditional Rotenese beliefs center on Lamatuan or Lamatuak. Today, most Rotenese people practice Protestant Christianity, Catholic Christianity, or Islam.
