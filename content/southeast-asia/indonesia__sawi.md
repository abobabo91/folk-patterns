## Overview

The Sawi, also known as the Sawuy, are an ethnic group of South Papua, Indonesia. They speak the Sawi language, which belongs to the Awyu language family. A separate disambiguation page identifies Sawi language as a language of West Papua, Indonesia, while also listing another language with the same name in Afghanistan. The sources describe the Sawi in relation to major changes during the twentieth century: they were known as cannibalistic headhunters as recently as the 1950s, and many later converted to Christianity. Their cultural significance in the supplied material is connected especially to this history, to missionary encounters, and to the construction of a large Christian meeting place.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources record one major Sawi building project. In 1972, the Sawi constructed what the source describes as the world’s largest circular building made strictly from un-milled poles. It was built as a Christian meeting place. The supplied material does not provide a Sawi name for the building, details of its roof or interior, information about domestic houses, or descriptions of architectural decoration.
