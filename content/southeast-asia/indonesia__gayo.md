## Overview

The Gayo people, also called *Urang Gayo* in Gayo and *Orang Gayo* in Indonesian, are an Austronesian ethnic group living predominantly in the mountains and highlands of Aceh, Sumatra, Indonesia. Their principal areas are the regencies of Bener Meriah, Central Aceh, and Gayo Lues, although Gayo communities also occur in East Aceh, Nagan Raya, North Aceh, Southeast Aceh, and Aceh Tamiang. The Gayo homeland lies in the Barisan Mountains, whose elevations exceed 12,000 feet. The population is given as 336,856. Gayo culture includes oral traditions, poetry, named dances, performance genres, foods, the *umah* house, and the textile *Upuh Ulen-Ulen*.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Upuh Ulen-Ulen** — *Upuh Ulen-Ulen* is the only named Gayo textile in the supplied sources. The sources identify it as a traditional cloth.

**Motif vocabulary.** The supplied sources name no motifs.

### Clothing & dress

The supplied sources mention *Upuh Ulen-Ulen* as a traditional Gayo cloth.

### Architecture

The traditional Gayo house is called *umah*.

## Music & performance

The supplied sources name several Gayo performance traditions. The named forms are *Didong*, *Didong Alo*, *Didong Sesuk*, and *Didong Niet*. The sources also mention *Melengkan* and *Dabus*, without explaining their performance contexts, texts, instruments, or social functions. No information is supplied about whether these forms are performed at weddings, funerals, courtly occasions, religious gatherings, or other events.

## Dance & theatre

The Gayo dance traditions named in the supplied sources are **Saman dance**, **Bines dance**, **Guel dance**, **Munalo dance**, **Sining dance**, **Turun ku Aih Aunen dance**, and **Resam Berume dance**.

## Festivals & rituals

They do state that ancient pre-Islamic practices remain extant among the Gayonese, including belief in good and bad spirits and in holy men, both dead and alive. People formerly gave ritual offerings and sacrifices to spirits, holy men, and ancestors.

## Foodways

The Gayo foods named in the supplied sources are *Masam Jaeng*, *Gutel*, *Lepat*, *Pulut Bekuah*, *Cecah*, *Pengat*, *Gegaloh*, *Gelame*, *Duekali*, and *Dedah*. They do state that during Dutch colonization the Gayonese developed a cash-crop economy based on vegetables and coffee.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Gayo language has no writing system, and folk tales, stories, and poetry are passed down through oral tradition. They mention an open-access collection of Gayo recordings containing traditional stories, historical narratives, and conversation through Paradisec. The history of the Linge Kingdom is presented through accounts attributed to Raja Uyem, Raja Ranta, and Zainuddin, including traditions concerning Raja Linge I and his children.

## Language & religion

Gayo, alternatively rendered Gajo, is an endangered Austronesian language spoken in the mountainous region of Aceh. It is classified within the Western Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian languages and is described as distinct from other languages in Aceh. The dialects named in the sources are Bukit, Dëret, Lues, Lut, and Serbejadi-Lukup. Gayo has no writing system. In 1907, G.A.J. Hazeu wrote a Gayo–Dutch dictionary for the colonial authorities of the Dutch East Indies.

The Gayonese are Sunni Muslims who practise a local form of Islam, while traces of pre-Islamic traditions remain. Local traditions attribute conversion to Islam to an Acehnese religious scholar during the early 17th century; Muslim merchants from West Sumatra and the expansion of the Aceh Sultanate also contributed to the process.
