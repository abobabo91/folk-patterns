## Overview

The Lamet are an ethnic group in Thailand and Laos. Approximately 22,000 live in Louang Namtha Province, Oudomxai and Bokeo Provinces of Laos, while approximately 100 live in Chiang Rai and Lampang Provinces of Thailand; there are also 90 Lamet in the United States and 30 in France. In Laos, the Lamet are a hill tribe. They claim to be an indigenous population of northwestern Laos and are a Palaungic ethnic group. Their language is Austroasiatic and related to Palaung and Wa. Their documented folk-culture practices include slash-and-burn agriculture, hunting, gathering, fishing, marriage payments, and the keeping of buffalo, chimes, and bronze drums as measures of wealth.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources state that, after marriage, a newly married Lamet man moves into his wife's family home and works for her father; later, the couple either moves in with the husband's parents or makes a new household.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources identify chimes and bronze drums as items included in Lamet men's inventories of wealth.

## Music & performance

The sources mention chimes and bronze drums as measures of wealth among male Lamet.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources identify Animism as the religion of the Lamet. Marriage customs are documented: after marriage, the newly married Lamet man moves into his wife's family home and works for her father. He then begins making marriage payments up to a fixed amount, after which the couple either moves in with the husband's parents or makes a new household.

## Foodways

The sources state that the Lamet practice slash-and-burn agriculture, hunting, gathering, and fishing, and trade with the Lao and Thai to obtain their necessaries.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that Lamet claim to be an indigenous population of northwestern Laos, but provide no further account of oral tradition.

## Language & religion

Lamet is an Austroasiatic language and a Mon–Khmer language of Laos. The Lamet language is related to Palaung and Wa, and most adult males also speak Tai Yuan. Lamet speakers call their language [χəmɛːt], or less commonly [kʰəmɛːt]. In Lampang Province, Thailand, it is known as Khamet; there are one hundred speakers there. A closely related variety called Lua' is spoken in Ban Pang Chok (Ban Lua), Wiang Pa Pao District, southern Chiang Rai Province, Thailand. Lamet has two tones, high and low. The Lamet religion is identified as Animism.
