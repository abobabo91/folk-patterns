## Overview

The Manggarai are an ethnic group living mainly in western Flores, in East Nusa Tenggara Province, Indonesia. Their settlements are concentrated in the Greater Manggarai area, comprising West Manggarai, Manggarai, and East Manggarai regencies. Their settlements cover more than 6,700 square kilometers, almost a third of Flores. Manggarai people are also found on Komodo Island, in Bima Regency and Ngada Regency, and among migrants in Kupang and Jakarta. As of the late 20th century, there were about 500,000 Manggarai people. They sometimes call themselves *Ata Manggarai*, meaning “people of Manggarai.” Their language, Tombo Manggarai, belongs to the Austronesian family and has around 43 sub-dialects divided into 5 dialect groups. Folk-cultural life described in the sources includes circular settlements, weaving, ritual celebrations, ancestral beliefs, harvest observances, dance, and the whip-fighting performance called caci.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources identify weaving as a Manggarai handicraft.

### Clothing & dress

The sources describe Manggarai traditional clothing as initially consisting of two pieces of fabric, reinforced at the front and back with a cord at the waist and hips. Modern clothing is described as being of the same type as mainstream Indonesian clothing.

### Architecture

The traditional Manggarai settlement has a circular layout. At its center is a round public space with a large tree, usually of the Ficus genus, and megalithic structures. In the past, a settlement could consist of one large house holding up to 200 people. Modern settlements, called *beo*, generally contain from 5 to 20 round or oval houses built on stilts. These houses have high, conical roofs descending to the ground; the roof is described as about 9 meters high. Free spaces in Manggarai settlements are paved with huge stones.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention carving, metalworking, and weaving among Manggarai handicrafts. The sources also state that large stones are used to pave free spaces in settlements and that stones close the round graves described for the city of Ende.

## Music & performance

 They do describe performance traditions connected with ritual, dance, and competition. In celebrations associated with the cult of the supreme creator god, Mori Karaeng, priests sacrifice buffalos called *ata Mbeki*, accompanied by ritual dances and battles between two groups of men in military garb.

Caci is described as a traditional folk sport and war dance. It is a form of whip fighting in which two young men strike and parry with a whip and a shield, usually in a large field. A caci performance commonly begins with danding dance performances, after which the caci warriors demonstrate their abilities to hit and parry.

## Dance & theatre

The sources name several dance-related traditions. Caci is a traditional folk sport and war dance performed by two young men using a whip and a shield. Before the competition, danding dance performances commonly take place. Another dance, called *Tandak Manggarai*, is performed on stage to predict the outcome of the caci competition.

Ritual dances also accompany buffalo sacrifice in celebrations associated with Mori Karaeng.

## Festivals & rituals

The principal named harvest observance is *Penti Manggarai*, a ceremony of harvest thanksgiving. Other rituals form part of the Penti cycle or are associated with village, spring, household, and spirit observances.

*Barong Lodok* is a ritual inviting the guardian spirit to the center of the *Lingko*, described as the middle farm. *Barong Wae* invites ancestral spirits to watch over springs. *Barong Compang* summons a village guardian spirit at night. *Wisi Loce* is conducted so that all invited spirits can wait before the climax of the Penti ceremony. *Libur Kilo* gives thanks for the welfare of each family in its home.

Traditional beliefs in the central part of Flores include the cult of Mori Karaeng and a form of ancestral worship. Priests hold massive celebrations involving buffalo sacrifice, ritual dances, and battles between two groups of men in military garb.

## Foodways

The main food is corn porridge with vegetables and pork. Pork is consumed only by non-Muslim Manggarai people according to the source. Palm wine, called *tuak*, is also part of the described diet. Rice is served at the table only as a festive meal.

The sources state that Manggarai people practice manual tropical farming. They grow upland rice, legumes, vegetables, tobacco, coffee, and corn. They also keep animals: buffaloes are bred for socially significant animal ceremonies, horses are kept for pack transportation, and pigs and chickens are raised. The sources state that Manggarai people do not hunt or fish. No further cooking methods, named dishes, tea or coffee ceremonies, or food restrictions are documented in the supplied material.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They do state that the Manggarai language is used throughout the region and that most Manggarai speakers also speak Indonesian for official and commercial purposes and for communication with non-Manggarai Indonesians.

## Language & religion

The Manggarai language is called *Tombo Manggarai*, also identified as Indonesian *bahasa Manggarai*. It is an Austronesian language related to Indonesian and other Malay varieties. The language has around 43 sub-dialects divided into 5 dialect groups: Western Manggarai, Central West Manggarai, Central Manggarai, East Manggarai, and Far East Manggarai. The latter is separated from the other dialects by the Rembong language and is distributed in the north-central part of Flores. The language is spoken by about 300,000 people in the description of the Manggarai people, while another source reports more than 730,000 native speakers in East Nusa Tenggara Province based on statistical data from 2009. Rongga is spoken by about 5,000 people in three settlements in southern East Manggarai Regency and is considered by many Manggarai people to be part of the Manggarai language.

More than 90% of Manggarai people are Catholics. Some people in the coastal west profess Sunnism, with an approximate number of 33,898. The central part of the island maintains traditional beliefs later mixed with Catholicism. The sources name Mori Karaeng as the supreme creator god and describe ancestral worship, spirit guardians, buffalo sacrifice, and ritual dances. They also state that the Manggarai people are divided into aristocrats called *karaeng*, community members called *ata-leke*, and descendants of slaves.
