## Overview

The Stieng people are an ethnic group of Vietnam and Cambodia. Most Stieng live in Bình Phước Province, where 81,708 people were recorded in 2009. In Cambodia, they are classified under the name Khmer Loeu, a designation for non-Khmer peoples. They speak Stieng, a language of the South Bahnaric grouping within the Mon–Khmer languages and the Austroasiatic language family. The sources identify the Stieng through their settlement in southern Vietnam and adjacent areas of Cambodia, their distinct language, and their musical-instrument documentation.

## Music & performance

The sources identify a publication titled *Nhạc khí dân tộc S'Tiêng* (“Musical instruments of the S'Tiêng people”), by Phùng Đăn Quang, published in Hanoi by Nhà xuất bản trẻ in 2005.

## Language & religion

Stieng is spoken by the Stieng people of southern Vietnam and adjacent areas of Cambodia. It is classified with Chrau and Mnong in the South Bahnaric grouping of the Mon–Khmer languages within the Austroasiatic language family. The Bahnaric languages are often cited as being most closely related to Khmer. The sources describe several Stieng dialects, including Bulo Stieng, Bu Dek, and Bu Biek. Bulo Stieng is associated with Bình Phước, Lâm Đồng, Tây Ninh, Kratié, and Mondulkiri; Bu Dek and Bu Biek are spoken in the lowlands.
