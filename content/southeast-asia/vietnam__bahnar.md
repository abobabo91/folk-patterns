## Overview

The Bahnar, also called Ba-Na, are an ethnic group of Vietnam and the indigenous people of the Central Highland provinces of Gia Lai and Kon Tum, as well as the coastal provinces of Bình Định and Phú Yên. They speak Bahnar, a Central Bahnaric language in the Bahnaric branch of the Mon-Khmer, or Austroasiatic, language family. The group includes sub-ethnicities such as Bahnar Jơlơng, Rơngao, Gơlar, KonKde, Kriem, Tơlô, Bơnâm, and Roh. Bahnar cultural expression documented in the sources includes the epic tradition of *H'amon*, ensembles of pitched gongs and bamboo string instruments, group dancing, ceremonial rice-wine drinking, and the Koh Kpo festival.

## Music & performance

The Bahnar play many traditional musical instruments, including ensembles of pitched gongs and string instruments made from bamboo. These instruments can be played in concert during special occasions. Such occasions may also include ceremonial *rượu cần*, identified in the source as rice wine, and group dancing.

## Dance & theatre

The sources mention group dancing in connection with occasions when Bahnar musical instruments are played. No theatrical tradition is documented in the sources used.

## Festivals & rituals

Koh Kpo, also called Groong Kpo Tonơi, is a festival expressing gratitude toward Yang, identified in the source as the god. Its main activity is the stabbing of a water buffalo. Contemporary Bahnar refer to the ceremony as “Gong Kpo.” The source also gives a possible Chamic reconstruction of the name as Gleng Ka Ppo Tanguei, glossed as “Dedication to the God of the Corn.”

## Foodways

The sources mention *rượu cần*, described as rice wine, which is consumed ceremonially during occasions involving traditional musical performance and group dancing.

## Oral tradition & literature

Bahnar epics, called *H'amon* in Bahnar, represent centuries-old aspirations of Bahnar people. *Dam Noi* is identified as an example of this epic tradition.

## Language & religion

Bahnar, also called Ba-Na, is a Central Bahnaric language with nine vowel qualities and phonemic vowel length. The sources describe several Bahnar subgroups and dialect-related varieties, including Bahnar Kon Tum, Bahnar Jơlong, Bahnar Golar, Bahnar Tosung, Bahnar Konkođe, Bahnar Alatang, Bahnar Alakông, Bahnar Tolo, Bahnar Bơnâm, Bahnar Roh, Bahnar Krem, Bahnar Chăm, and Bahnar But. Other listed varieties include Tơ Lô, Krem, Vân Canh, Thồ Lồ, Gơ Lar, Kon Tum, Jơ Lơng, and Rơ Ngao. The Bahnar follow traditional religions described as animism and polytheism, and there is also a large Christian population among them.
