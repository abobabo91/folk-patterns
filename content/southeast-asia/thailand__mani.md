## Overview

The Mani, also known as the Maniq, are a Negrito ethnic group of Thailand and the only Negritos in the country. They live in the southern provinces of Yala, Narathiwat, Phatthalung, Trang, and Satun, in two broad population areas: the Titiwangsa Mountains and the Banthat Mountains. Current population estimates range from 300 to 350 people. The Mani speak related Aslian languages, primarily Kensiu and Ten'edn, neither of which has a standard writing system. They are a hunting and gathering society whose temporary forest camps, foraging practices, knowledge of medicinal herbs, and oral traditions distinguish their folk-culture profile. Their history also reflects long isolation, changing forest environments, conflict, and continuing challenges concerning land rights, cultural preservation, and environmental change.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The Mani wear simple clothes made from materials such as bamboo leaves. The sources do not distinguish men's and women's clothing, ceremonial dress, head coverings, belts, footwear, or named garments.

### Architecture

The Mani build temporary huts of bamboo with roofs made of banana leaves. Their living environment includes forest camps and rock shelters. Field campaigns in 1995 and 1996 recorded ten rock shelters and nine forest camps, all belonging to a wider system of up to one hundred living sites inhabited by Mani groups during one year in the forested region of Trang, Satun, and Phatthalung Provinces.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They describe bamboo huts, banana-leaf roofs, bamboo-leaf clothing, hunting, gathering, and the use of medicinal herbs.

## Music & performance

They state that the Mani have oral traditions, but provide no names or descriptions of songs, instruments, dances, or dramatic forms.

## Festivals & rituals

They record that Mani groups move between living sites and that hunters navigate the terrain to find a place for their clan to establish a new camp.

## Foodways

The Mani are a hunting and gathering society. They hunt many types of animals and consume many different kinds of vegetables and fruits. They also state that the Mani are familiar with many different species of medicinal herbs.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that the Mani preserved oral traditions while remaining relatively isolated. No contemporary literary revival or preservation project is described beyond references to documentation resources concerning Mani and Austroasiatic intangible heritage.

## Language & religion

The Mani speak a variety of related Aslian languages, primarily Kensiu and Ten'edn. These languages do not have standard writing systems.
