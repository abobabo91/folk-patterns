## Overview

The Ngái are an ethnic group found in Vietnam, largely descended from the Hakka people of southern China. The Vietnamese government classifies the Ngái separately from the Hoa when considering ethnic minority groups, although the Ngái have lived in the modern-day lands of Vietnam for centuries. They speak Hakka, a Sinitic language, and some use the endonym *San Ngai*, meaning “mountain Ngai.” The officially recorded population was 4,841 in 1999, 1,035 in 2009, and 1,649 in 2019. These figures are affected by ethnic-registration issues, while the total number of Hakka speakers in Vietnam is estimated at around 150,000 to nearly 200,000, most of whom are classified as Hoa.

## Language & religion

The Ngái speak Hakka, which the source describes as a Sinitic language. The Vietnamese government classifies them separately from the Hoa, or urban “ethnic Chinese,” and the Ngái are described as having lived in the modern-day lands of Vietnam for centuries.
