## Overview

Malay Indonesians (*Orang Melayu Indonesia*; Jawi: اورڠ ملايو ايندونيسيا) are ethnic Malays living throughout Indonesia and are one of the country’s indigenous peoples. The 2010 census states that there are 8 million Malays in Indonesia, using a classification associated with Malays in East Sumatra and the coast of Kalimantan. Malay civilization developed along the east coast of Sumatra, Singapore, the Malay Peninsula, and the coast of Kalimantan. Indonesian, the national language and lingua franca of Indonesia, is a standardized form of Riau Malay. Malay states and kingdoms were associated especially with Sumatra and Borneo, including Srivijaya, the Melayu Kingdom, Dharmasraya, the Sultanate of Deli, the Sultanate of Siak Sri Indrapura, the Riau-Lingga Sultanate, the Sultanate of Bulungan, Pontianak Sultanate, and the Sultanate of Sambas.

## Festivals & rituals

They do describe the historical association of Islam with Malay society. In the Malacca Sultanate, Islam became the state religion and a primary instrument in the development of a common Malay identity. The three core values of Kemelayuan (“Malayness”) were identified as the Classical Malay language, the Islamic faith, and Malay Muslim culture. ## Oral tradition & literature

The *Malay Annals* associates the etymological origin of “Melayu” with a small river named *Sungai Melayu* (“Melayu river”) in Sumatra. The source states that the epic incorrectly placed the river’s course in the Musi River, whereas it actually flowed to the Batang Hari River in Jambi. The term is thought to derive from the Malay word *melaju*, combining the verbal prefix “me” with the root *laju*, meaning “to accelerate,” in reference to the river’s strong current.

Malay has a literary record dating as far back as the 7th century AD. The Kedukan Bukit Inscription is described as an early Malay inscription and as written in Old Malay, a possible ancestor of today’s Malay language and its variants.

## Language & religion

The Malay languages have Sumatra as their homeland and today extend throughout Southeast Asia. Indonesian, the country’s official language and lingua franca, was based on Riau Malay. The source distinguishes this from the vernacular Malay dialects of the Riau Islands, stating that Riau Malay represents a form of Classical Malay used in the 19th and early 20th centuries in the Riau-Lingga Sultanate.

Most Malay languages and dialects spoken in Indonesia are mutually unintelligible with Standard Indonesian. The source names Palembang Malay, Jambi Malay, Bengkulu Malay, and Banjarese among the most widely spoken varieties, while also identifying Minangkabau, Kerinci, and Kubu as languages closely related to Malay. The Old Malay language was the official language and lingua franca of Srivijaya, replacing Sanskrit, the language of Hinduism. The sources describe Islam as central to Malay identity.
