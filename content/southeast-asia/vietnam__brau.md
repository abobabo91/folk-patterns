## Overview

The Brau are an ethnic group living in Laos, Cambodia, and Vietnam. In Vietnam, most Brau live in Đắc Mế village, Bờ Y commune, Ngọc Hồi district, Kon Tum province, where their population was 525 in 2019. Their ancestors came from southern Laos and northeastern Cambodia and migrated to Vietnam around 150 years ago. They speak Brao, a Mon–Khmer language. The available source presents Brau culture through named stories, a Creator god, instruments, body practices, and elephant-related knowledge. Their traditions are linked to nature and hunting. The source also records the Brau role in caring for elephants in Cambodia through the mahouts of the Airavata Elephant Foundation in Ratanakiri Province.

## Material culture

### Jewelry & body adornment

The Brau have traditional body practices including *uốt bưng*, the filing of teeth; *síp tiêu*, the straining of ears; and *chingkrackang*, a tattoo on the forehead.

## Music & performance

The Brau play *Táp đinh bố*, described as a kind of K'lông pút, and *Tha*, described as a special kind of gong. The source identifies these instruments but gives no further information about their construction, playing technique, musical structure, songs, performance settings, or occasions such as weddings, funerals, courts, or festivals.

## Festivals & rituals

They do record the *Un cha đắc lếp* story, in which the Brau talk about the Great Flood, and identify the Creator god as Pa Xây.

The source records an event at the Airavata Elephant Foundation in Ratanakiri Province in Cambodia: on the 26th of December, the first elephant baby born in 30 years was born there. It states that the last four elephants belonging to the foundation are cared for by mahouts of the Brau people.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Brau talk about the Great Flood in their *Un cha đắc lếp* story. The source also names Pa Xây as the Creator god. No further details are provided about the narrative, its characters, performance context, transmission, or relationship to other Brau oral forms.

## Language & religion

The Brau speak Brao, a Mon–Khmer language. It names Pa Xây as the Creator god and reports that the Brau speak about the Great Flood in the *Un cha đắc lếp* story. No broader account of Brau religious institutions, denominations, syncretism, clergy, ritual specialists, or other spiritual practices is given.

The Brau have only two surnames: Thao for men and Nang for women. The source presents these names as part of Brau social identification.
