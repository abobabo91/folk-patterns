## Overview

The Sikka people, also known as Sikkanese or Sika, are an Austronesian-speaking ethnic group native to east central Flores, between the Bloh and Napung rivers. Their main settlement is Maumere, the center of the region and capital of Sikka Regency, where they occupy a separate block. Around 180,000 people of the Sikka ethnic group speak Sikka on Flores island in East Nusa Tenggara province, Indonesia. Sikka is described as belonging to the Central Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian language family, while the Sikka people article places it among the Bima–Sumba languages. The group’s documented cultural life includes slash-and-burn agriculture, weaving and braiding, coastal fishing, distinctive mountain and coastal settlements, Roman Catholicism, and continuing traditional ancestor worship practices.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Weaving* is well developed among the Sika people.

**Braiding** is also described as well developed.

### Clothing & dress

Clothing differs between interior and coastal communities. In the interior, clothing consists of a skirt or loincloth. In coastal villages, people wear *kain*—a cloth—and a jacket or shirt. The supplied sources do not distinguish men’s and women’s clothing, ceremonial dress, head coverings, belts, footwear, or other forms of body dress.

### Architecture

Mountain villages are small, circular settlements located on steep mountain slopes, where their position served as protection against attacks. At the center of a settlement is an area with a temple and sacred megalithic shrines. Coastal settlements have a linear plan and are located along a road or river. In the mountains, dwellings have a frame-and-pillar structure and are designed for large families; in coastal areas, dwellings are designed for a small family.

## Festivals & rituals

The Sikka people are predominantly Roman Catholic, while many people living in the interior continue to maintain traditional ancestor worship practices. The sources also state that the first *raja* of the Kingdom of Sikka ruled from 1607 after introducing Roman Catholicism, although prior rulers had established themselves at Sikka Natar and spread their rule around modern Sikka.

## Foodways

Food crops include rice, corn, cassava, and millet. Other commodities include peanuts and coconut palm. The dietary description identifies vegetables and cereals from wheat and corn, together with spices, fruit, and juice; fish and meat are eaten on holidays. Sika people obtain food through slash-and-burn agriculture with short forms of shifting cultivation, while irrigation is used in the west of the coastal area. Coastal fishing is common, and people also raise horses, small cattle, and poultry.

## Oral tradition & literature

The supplied sources mention local tradition concerning the first *raja* of the Kingdom of Sikka and identify two works concerning the origin and history of the Rajadom of Sikka: *The Stranger-Kings of Sikka: With an Integrated Edition of Two Manuscripts on the Origin and History of the Rajadom of Sikka* and a study of precedence in the formation of the Domain of Wai Brama and the Rajadom of Sikka.

## Language & religion

Sikka, also called Sikkanese or Sika, is spoken by around 180,000 people of the Sikka ethnic group on Flores island in East Nusa Tenggara province, Indonesia. It is described as a Central Malayo-Polynesian language within the Austronesian language family and as a member of the Bima–Sumba languages. At least three recognized dialects are named: *Sikka Natar*, generally perceived as the most refined and prestigious variety; *Sara Krowe*, spoken in the central hills; and *Ata Tana ’Ai* or *Sara Tana ’Ai*, used by outsiders and insiders for the people and language of the region and also used as a ritual language. Most Sikka people are Roman Catholic, while many interior communities maintain traditional ancestor worship practices.
