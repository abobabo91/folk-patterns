## Overview

The Mante people, also spelled Mantir and known as Manti in Gayonese, are described as one of the earliest ethnic groups associated with Aceh, Indonesia. They are regarded as part of the Proto-Malay people group that initially settled around Aceh Besar Regency and in the interior jungle, and were thought to have migrated to Aceh through the Malay Peninsula. According to Acehnese legend, the Mante and Batak peoples descended from Kawom Lhèë Reutōïh, meaning “the people of three hundred.” The Mante are described as one of the indigenous peoples whose history contributed to the formation of the existing Acehnese people. They are considered extinct or disappeared through intermarriage with later-arriving non-indigenous groups, and there is no strong scientific evidence for their existence today.
