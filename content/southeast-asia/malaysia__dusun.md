## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| Who | The Dusun are an Austronesian people of Sabah, Malaysia. |
| Where | Sabah, including the West Coast, Interior, Sandakan and Tawau divisions, and Labuan. |
| How many | About 555,647 Dusuns mixed with Kadazans, together forming the larger Kadazan-Dusun grouping. |
| Language | Central Dusun, also called Bunduliwan or Boros Dusun. |
| Religion | Historically animist and Momolianist; many Dusuns later became Christian or Sunni Muslim. |
| Known for | Barkcloth clothing · Rattan hats and baskets · Momolianism · Kaamatan harvest festival · Rice farming |

## Overview

The Dusun live across Sabah and include many sub-ethnic and dialect groups. Central Dusun is one of their widely spoken languages. Their culture includes barkcloth clothing, rattan work, musical instruments, oral traditions and rice farming. Kaamatan is their main widely shared harvest tradition.

## Material culture

Dusun material culture includes barkcloth garments, rattan hats and girdles, feathered ornaments, baskets, fish traps, tools, jars and musical instruments.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources mention barkcloth and decorated garments.

- **Barkcloth tunic** — A British Museum record describes a Dusun tunic made of barkcloth.
- **Velvety garments** — Two Dusun girls are recorded wearing velvety garments decorated with metal sewing.

### Clothing & dress

Recorded Dusun dress includes barkcloth, rattan and bamboo hats, rattan girdles and feathered head ornaments.

- **Women’s feathered head ornament** (*Siwot*) — Siwot is made of feathers, cloth and bamboo.
- **Rattan and brass woman’s girdle** — A woman’s girdle is recorded as made of rattan and brass.
- **Rattan hat** — British Museum records include hats made of rattan.
- **Bamboo hat** — British Museum records include hats made of bamboo.
- **Priests’ ceremonial garments** — Dusun priests are recorded wearing ceremonial garments with high head ornaments.

### Architecture

Dusun houses are linked with communal living, fruit trees and farming.

- **Long communal house** — The sources compare Dusun long communal houses with houses on the Burmese border.
- **Fruit-tree house setting** — A Dusun house is often within or surrounded by trees such as bambangan, breadfruit, cempedak, jackfruit, mango and tarap.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded Dusun objects include rattan baskets, farm tools, fish traps, knives and jars.

- **Rattan basket** — Some recorded baskets are made of rattan.
- **Bark and cord basket** — Other baskets are made of rattan, bark and cord.
- **Wooden harrow** — A wooden harrow is recorded among Dusun-associated tools.
- **Bamboo fish trap** — A bamboo fish trap is recorded as a Dusun-associated object.
- **Martaban jar** — One recorded Martaban jar has lugs and animal masks.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Dusun adornment includes bead, shell, bamboo, rattan, brass, feather and fibre ornaments.

- **Feathered head ornament** (*Siwot*) — Siwot is a woman’s ornament made of feathers, cloth and bamboo.
- **Shell-bead necklace** — A necklace made of shell beads is recorded.
- **Bamboo bracelet** — One recorded bracelet is made of bamboo.
- **Beaded chest ornament** — A bracelet or possible chest ornament is made of beads and vegetable fibre.
- **Beaded carrying belt** — A beaded carrying belt is associated with a sword.

## Music & performance

Dusun musical instruments include mouth organs, flutes, lutes and a guitar-like instrument.

- **Mouth organ** (*Sumpotan*) — Sumpotan is a pipe instrument made of wood with cane binding and a gourd resonator.
- **Two-stringed lute** (*Sundatang*) — The Sundatang is made of wood and has brass wire strings.
- **Nose flute** — A Dusun nose flute is made of bamboo.
- **Guitar-like instrument** — A guitar-like instrument is made of wood and vegetable-fibre string.
- **Emergency signal gong** (*canang*) — Gana Dusun people formerly hammered canang instruments to signal an emergency.

## Festivals & rituals

Dusun traditions connect ancestral origins, harvest, customary law and mutual cooperation.

- **Ancestral banyan tree** (*Nunuk Ragang*) — Nunuk Ragang is a giant red banyan tree where ancestors are said to have first settled in northern Borneo.
- **Harvest festival** (*Kaamatan*) — Kaamatan is an annual harvest festival celebrated by Dusuns and Kadazans.
- **Customary punishment** (*sogit*) — Sogit is described as tribal punishment under customary law.
- **Mutual cooperation** (*mitatabang*) — Mitatabang is practised when clearing fields, preparing weddings, building houses and responding to a family loss.
- **Tobilung cosmology** (*tinumaru*) — Tinumaru is considered a god and protector living in the seventh heaven.

## Foodways

Rice farming, fishing and trade in farm and forest products are central to Dusun foodways.

- **Upland rice variety** (*Kembulaung*) — Kembulaung is one of four traditional Minokok upland rice varieties.
- **Upland rice variety** (*Lantai*) — Lantai is one of four traditional Minokok upland rice varieties.
- **Upland rice variety** (*Sepulut*) — Sepulut is one of four traditional Minokok upland rice varieties.
- **Upland rice variety** (*Tesik*) — Tesik is one of four traditional Minokok upland rice varieties.
- **Fish-stunning plant juice** (*tuba*) — Tuba, made from surinit roots, is used to momentarily stun fish in rivers.

## Oral tradition & literature

Dusun oral traditions preserve origin stories, local histories and explanations of group names.

- **Origin story** (*Nunuk Ragang*) — The Nunuk Ragang story tells of the ancestors’ first settlement in northern Borneo.
- **Momolianist origin narrative** (*Huminodun*) — The story names Kinoingan, Suminundu and their daughter Huminodun and links Huminodun with Kaamatan.
- **Labuk oral tradition** (*kurilang*) — Kurilang is one of the oral traditions maintained by the Labuk Dusun.
- **Labuk oral tradition** (*tangon*) — Tangon is one of the oral traditions maintained by the Labuk Dusun.
- **Group-name explanation** (*gana-gana*) — Gana is connected with gana-gana, meaning a flat land area.

## Language & religion

Dusun languages belong to the Austronesian family, while Dusun religion includes Momolianism, Christianity, Islam and continuing minority animism.

- **Central Dusun language** (*Bunduliwan*) — Central Dusun is also called Bunduliwan or Boros Dusun.
- **Writing system** (*Pimato*) — Pimato is the name for the 21 Latin-alphabet characters used to write Dusun.
- **Creator pair** (*Kinoingan and Suminundu*) — Kinoingan and Suminundu are identified as creators in the Momolianist origin story.
- **Cosmological spirit** (*rineet*) — Rineet is named in the Tobilung cosmological system.
- **Seventh heaven** (*lingkogung*) — Lingkogung is described as the seventh heaven where tinumaru lives.
