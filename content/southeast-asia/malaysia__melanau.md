## Overview

The Melanau are an indigenous people of Sarawak on Borneo, among the earliest settlers of the region, historically living in stilt houses and longhouses along coastal swamps and river tributaries. Their culture centers on sago—both food and trade commodity—and distinctive ritual objects carved from sago palm pith. Today most are Muslim or Christian, though animist beliefs and practices like the Kaul festival remain strong. Their language, distinct from Malay despite heavy influence, is at risk as younger generations shift to Malay.

## Material culture

Melanau material culture is dominated by objects made from plant materials—plaited palm, bamboo, and rattan—and carved ritual figures from sago palm pith, with little pottery or metalwork beyond the distinctive parang latok.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Melanau pattern appears on plaited and carved objects rather than woven cloth, with named motifs on spirit figures.

- **Plaited hat (conical)** (*terindak or matu terindak*) — Conical hats plaited from nipah palm leaf, rattan and bamboo, with cotton lining, dark fibre bands, and glass-bead flowers with metallic-thread knobs.
- **Plaited baskets and lids** — Octagonal baskets over wooden frames with patterned plaited sides in brown, red and black, with matching lids and rattan or beaded decoration.
- **Chevron pattern** (*betek binawa'*) — Tiers of chevrons carved on a rounded surface on spirit figures.
- **Hair pattern** (*siret*) — Chevrons covering an outstretched human figure on spirit carvings.
- **Teeth pattern** (*nyipen*) — Triangle or teeth-like motif carved on the lower body of a figure.

### Clothing & dress

Melanau dress is documented mainly through headgear, particularly the plaited terindak hat; infant skull flattening was a recorded body practice.

- **Plaited hat** (*terindak*) — Conical hat plaited from nipah palm leaf, rattan and bamboo, with glass-bead ornaments and a metallic-thread knob on fine examples.
- **Wooden hat** — Men's hats made of wood alone or wood and cane, some circular with painted decoration.
- **Skull-flattening device** — Wooden and cloth instrument with cords and metal used on infants to flatten the skull during development.

### Architecture

The Melanau shifted from tall stilt houses and longhouses to individual Malaysian kampung-style houses; their most distinctive built form is the burial pole.

- **Burial pole with hanging coffin** (*kelidieng, jerunei, or lejeng*) — A funerary structure shared with Punan Bah, Kejaman and Sekapan peoples, found in Melanau districts like Mukah and Dalat and as far as East and South Kalimantan.
- **Ancient burial site** — An archaeological Melanau burial site known at Niah, Sekaloh, with graves in the Rajang District documented in 19th-century photographs.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Melanau household equipment is overwhelmingly of plant material, with the heavy parang latok the key metal tool.

- **Chopping sword for sago harvest** (*parang latok*) — A heavy, thick chopping sword with a blade that bends sharply at its middle, ivory hilt with bone pommel, used by both Malay and Melanau for harvesting sago palm and personal protection.
- **Food covers** — Nipah palm leaf covers for food storage in the kitchen.
- **Baskets and boxes** — Cane and palm-spathe boxes, and plaited baskets for holding goods.
- **Model sailing boat** — A plank-built boat carved from a single piece of wood with incised planking, bitumen-filled seams, crossbeams, decking, oars, stone anchor and mast.
- **Darts and quiver** — Darts with sheaths and a bamboo quiver for hunting or warfare.

### Jewelry & body adornment

No Melanau jewellery types are documented; glass beads appear as ornament on hats and baskets rather than personal adornment.

- **Glass beads on hat** — Red, yellow and white glass beads sewn onto the terindak hat as flower ornaments with metallic-thread details.

## Music & performance

Only a single flute fragment is documented; no information on Melanau instruments, songs or performance occasions survives in the sources.

- **Flute fragment** — A partial flute from Melanau peoples held in the British Museum collection.

## Dance & theatre

No Melanau dance or theatre is documented, but a modern horror film draws on Melanau burial ritual.

- **Sumpahan Jerunei** — A Malaysian horror film released in 2023, set around Melanau noble burial rituals of the 13th century and filmed in Mukah and other Sarawak locations.

## Festivals & rituals

The Kaul festival marking the Melanau New Year in March is the strongest surviving ritual, celebrated across religious lines; the richest ritual material is carved spirit figures called belum.

- **Kaul festival** (*Kaul*) — The annual New Year festival beginning in March, celebrated even by Melanau who are Muslim or Christian, and understood as a reminder of older Melanau animist religion.
- **Earth spirit: kneeling female with necklace** (*Durig Ateng*) — A carved sago-palm-pith figure of a kneeling female earth spirit with carved hair and necklace, hands on her stomach.
- **Earth spirit: crouching male in pointed hat** (*Tujah Pemarau*) — A carved sago-palm-pith figure of a crouching male earth spirit in a pointed hat with triangle motifs on the lower body.
- **Air spirit: seated with flame-like head-dress** (*Dalong Langit*) — A carved sago-palm-pith figure of an air spirit seated cross-legged with scales on the torso, wings, and a raised right hand holding a spear.
- **Water spirit: four identical males forming a canoe shape** (*Bilum Salui*) — A set of four identical seated males in round hats whose touching elbows and knees form the shape of a canoe, representing water spirits.

## Foodways

Melanau food rests on sago and river and swamp products, with sago eaten in multiple forms and the parang latok used to harvest the sago palm.

- **Sago biscuit** (*tebaloi*) — A sago-and-coconut biscuit that the Melanau are best known for.
- **Sago balls** (*linut*) — Sago eaten in ball form as a staple food.
- **Palm weevil larva** (*siet*) — The edible larva of the sago palm weevil, a protein source.
- **Jungle fern dish** (*umai*) — Jungle ferns and fresh fish products eaten as part of the Melanau diet.

## Oral tradition & literature

Melanau oral tradition centers on Raja Tugau, a king who appears in the Bruneian epic poem Syair Awang Semaun; the language and traditions face extinction as younger generations shift to Malay.

- **Melanau king in oral tradition** (*Raja Tugau*) — A legendary king in Melanau tradition whose lands were conquered by fourteen brothers in the Bruneian epic Syair Awang Semaun, possibly the same figure as the oppressive Datu Makatunao of the Philippine Maragtas Code.
- **Bruneian founding epic** (*Syair Awang Semaun*) — An epic poem telling of the founding of the Brunei kingdom by fourteen brothers who expanded by conquering Melanau lands, surviving in at least six versions committed to manuscript in the 19th century.

## Language & religion

Melanau is a North Bornean Austronesian language with multiple dialects, now threatened by Malay; most Melanau are Muslim or Christian, with animist traditions continuing.

- **Melanau language** (*Melanau*) — An Austronesian language of the North Bornean branch, spoken in the Rajang delta and Brunei, with dialects including Mukah-Oya, Balingian, Bruit and Bintulu (Vaie), some barely intelligible to other speakers.
- **Religion: layered belief system** — Most Melanau are Muslim, with Christian minorities in Mukah and Dalat; some families hold all three religions, with Christian and Muslim children and parents who keep animist beliefs.
- **Spirit figures** (*belum*) — Carved sago-palm-pith images of tew (malevolent spirits) used in animist ritual, representing earth, air and water spirits.
