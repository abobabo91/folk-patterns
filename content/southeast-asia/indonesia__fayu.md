## Overview

The Fayu are an ethnic group living in swampland in Waropen Regency, Papua Province, and Puncak Regency, Central Papua, Indonesia. They are found in Kampung Otodemo, Inggerus District, and in Kampung Dirou, Kampung Kawari, Kampung Dairi, and Kampung Subohiri, as well as in Kirihi District south of Otodemo. When first contacted by westerners, they numbered about 400, compared with about 2000 before violence within the group reduced their population. Today, the Fayu number up to 1,470, and the majority are Christians. Fayu, also known as Sehudate, is a Lakes Plain language spoken by about 1,400 Fayu people. The sources describe their social organization as based generally on single-family groups, with gatherings once or twice a year to exchange brides.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe gatherings of several single-family groups once or twice a year to exchange brides.

## Oral tradition & literature

Two books are identified as accounts of living among the Fayu. Sabine Kuegler spent most of her childhood growing up with them, and her account is associated with *Child of the Jungle: The True Story of a Girl Caught Between Two Worlds*. Jared Diamond’s *Guns, Germs, and Steel* uses the Fayu as an example of a band type society.

## Language & religion

Fayu, also known as Sehudate, is a Lakes Plain language of Papua Province, Indonesia. It is spoken by about 1,400 Fayu people in Foida and other nearby villages. The language has a relatively minimal phonological index, described as typical of Lakes Plain languages, and has an extensive system of allomorphy. The majority of the Fayu people are Christians.
