## Overview

The Iranun are an Austronesian ethnic group native to southwestern Mindanao in the Philippines. They are closely related ethnically and culturally to the Maranao and Maguindanaon, and all three groups speak Danao languages. Iranun communities are also found in Malaysia and Indonesia, including Sabah and Riau. In the Philippines, they live in parts of Maguindanao del Norte, Cotabato, Lanao del Norte, Lanao del Sur, Bukidnon, and Zamboanga del Sur. Their communities have also spread to other parts of the Philippines, especially urban areas. Traditionally sailors, the Iranun were known for ship-building, maritime activity, and the mapping and control of waters in Southeast Asia.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They state that the historically important Carta Indigena Filipina map demonstrates Iranun geographical knowledge and control over many of the seas and coasts of Southeast Asia.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They identify ship-building as an important traditional skill and mention the Carta Indigena Filipina as a material object of historical importance.

## Music & performance

They identify the Iranun as traditionally sailors and describe their maritime activity, including travel and raiding across the South China Sea, Sulu Sea, Moro Gulf, and Celebes Sea.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that most Iranuns are Muslim and that Iranun communities in Kudat and Likas, Kota Kinabalu, assimilated with the Sama-Bajau partly because of shared Muslim religious beliefs and ancestral roots in the Bangsamoro region of the Southern Philippines.

## Foodways

They state that most Iranuns are Muslim but provide no further information about foodways.

## Oral tradition & literature

The origin of the name “Iranun” is described as contested. The form “Iranun,” also recorded as the archaic “Iranaoan,” may have been the original endonym of an ancestral group that later divided into the Iranun, Maranao, and Maguindanao peoples.

## Language & religion

Iranun is an Austronesian language belonging to the Danao languages. It is spoken in Maguindanao del Norte, parts of Lanao del Sur and Lanao del Norte, coastal municipalities of Zamboanga del Sur from Tukuran to Dumalinao, Cotabato, and the Malaysian state of Sabah. It is the second most spoken language in Maguindanao del Norte after the Maguindanao language. The language is also known as Iranon or Illanun, and its Jawi form is إيراناونساي. Iranun is most closely related to the language of the Maranao people of Lanao. The sources state that most Iranuns are Muslim. Historically, the British colonial-era exonym “Ilanun,” with variant spellings including “Illanun,” “Illanoan,” “Illanoon,” and “Ilanoon,” gave rise to the Malay term “Lanun,” meaning “pirate.”
