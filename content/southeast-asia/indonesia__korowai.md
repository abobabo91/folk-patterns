## Overview

The Korowai, also called the Kolufo, live in southeastern Papua in the Indonesian provinces of South Papua and Highland Papua. Their territory is divided among Boven Digoel, Mappi, Asmat, Pegunungan Bintang, and Yahukimo regencies. They number about 4,000 to 4,400 people. The Korowai language belongs to the Awyu–Dumut family and the Trans–New Guinea phylum; Korowai, also called Kolufaup, is spoken along the Becking River. Korowai cultural life is documented through tree-house settlement, shifting cultivation, hunting, fishing, sago preparation, oral traditions, spirit practices, clan organization, and ceremonial feasts.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The majority of Korowai clans live in tree houses in isolated forested territory. The sources describe the building of a tree house 35m high for the BBC documentary *Human Planet*, and a Smithsonian Institution study documented Korowai treehouse construction in the Dayo village area. The sources also state that houses at a particular high altitude have been financed by Western film crews.

Since 1980, some Korowai have moved into villages including Yaniruma on the Becking River banks, Mu, Mbasman, Manggél, Yafufla, Mabül on the Eilanden River, and Khaiflambolüp. Village absenteeism remains high because settlements are relatively distant from sago resources.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They identify a traditional smoking pipe called *depon nagel*, made from bamboo with a diameter of 3–4 cm. Sago-shoot shavings called *fiop* are used as its filter. Other documented activities include fire making, horticulture, hunting, fishing, and the preparation of sago.

## Music & performance

They do describe traditional displays performed for tourist groups and religious ceremonies in which only male adults are involved. The Korowai have a rich oral tradition comprising myths, folktales, magical sayings, charms, and totem traditions.

Tourism has provided some moderate cash income since the early 1990s. Activities connected with the tourist industry include hosting groups in villages for tourist-sponsored sago feasts, carrying luggage, and performing traditional displays.

## Dance & theatre

They mention traditional displays for tourist groups and documentary representations of Korowai life, including treehouse construction and the reported practice of cannibalism as a form of criminal justice.

## Festivals & rituals

Once in a lifetime, a Korowai clan must organize a sago grub festival intended to stimulate prosperity and fertility through ritual action. In times of trouble, domesticated pigs are sacrificed to the spirits of the ancestors. Religious ceremonies are among the gender-specific activities of Korowai society, and only male adults participate in the ceremonies described by the source.

The Korowai universe is populated by various spirits. Particular reverence is paid to the red headed creator god Gimigi, and the Korowai also honor their “One God,” understood as a prime deity from whom other beings descend or to whom they pay homage.

## Foodways

The Korowai are horticulturalists who practice shifting cultivation and have excellent hunting and fishing skills. Sago is a central food resource: the distance between villages and sago resources contributes to village absenteeism, and sago preparation is a gender-specific activity. Tourist-sponsored sago feasts are also documented.

The sources mention sago shoots, whose shavings called *fiop* are used as filters in the traditional smoking pipe *depon nagel*.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Korowai have a rich oral tradition that includes myths, folktales, magical sayings, charms, and totem traditions. Their beliefs concerning death and the afterlife include reciprocal reincarnation: people who have died can be sent back to the land of the living by their kinsmen in the land of the dead, returning as a newly born infant of their own clan.

A dictionary and grammar book have been produced by a Dutch missionary linguist. The sources also identify documentary and research projects concerning Korowai life, including films by Dea Sudarman, the documentary *Lords of the Garden*, and the BBC documentaries *First Contact* and *Human Planet*.

## Language & religion

The Korowai language, also called Kolufaup, is spoken in South Papua, Indonesia, along the Becking River. It belongs to the Awyu–Dumut family and the Trans–New Guinea phylum. The sources state that a dictionary and grammar book have been produced by a Dutch missionary linguist.

Korowai religious life includes spirits, reverence for the red headed creator god Gimigi, and honoring the “One God.” Ancestor spirits receive sacrifices of domesticated pigs in times of trouble. The sources describe Christian contact beginning with Dutch Protestant Christian missionaries in the late 1970s. A local Christian community was established in 1996, and the first Korowai converts to Christianity were baptized by the end of the 1990s. A small team of Bible translators from Wycliffe/SIL moved to Yaniruma in the autumn of 2003.
