## Overview

The Chut, also written Chứt, are a collective name for five Vietic-speaking groups in Vietnam’s Quảng Bình Province: the Arem, Rục, Maliêng or Mã Liêng, Mày, and Sách. They are located mainly in the Minh Hóa and Tuyên Hóa districts, and the Chứt population in Vietnam was 7,513 according to the 2019 census; 6,572 lived in Quảng Bình Province. The Chut are one of four main groups of Vietic speakers in Vietnam. Their name refers to the cave and rock dwellings in which they once lived. Traditionally nomadic and resident in temporary villages, they have adopted a sedentary lifestyle. Their folk-culture record includes hunting, gathering, trapping, fishing, farming, bark clothing, and forms of exchange with Lao and Viet people.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The Chut do not traditionally grow and weave cotton. In the past, traditional clothing was made from bark and consisted of simple loin-cloths and skirts. The sources do not distinguish documented men’s and women’s clothing, ceremonial dress, head coverings, belts, footwear, or named garments. They state that Chut people now wear modern clothing.

### Architecture

The name “Chut” refers to the cave and rock dwellings in which the people once lived. Before 1954, they traditionally lived as nomads in temporary villages; they have since adopted a sedentary lifestyle.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They state that the Chut traditionally hunted small animals with crossbows and different traps, used fishing rods and fishing nets, sowed seeds with a stick, and raised buffalo and cattle for plowing.

## Foodways

The sources describe subsistence practices. Traditionally, the Chut hunted small animals with crossbows and traps, gathered fruit and fungi, and caught fish with fishing rods, fishing nets, and poisonous leaves. Traps were used for monkeys, civets, foxes, porcupines, birds, and mice. They now grow rice, corn, and tobacco, and raise buffalo and cattle for plowing. When a large animal is caught collectively, the hunter receives the back meat and the remainder is divided equally among the others.

## Language & religion

The Chứt languages are a Vietic language cluster within the Austroasiatic family, spoken by Chứt peoples in Vietnam and Khammouane Province in Laos. Chut sub-groups speak the Chut language with dialects, except the Arem, which speaks the Arem language. The sources tentatively identify the Mày, Rục, and Sách; Arem; and Kri and Maleng or Malieng. The Chut language article states that Kri and Maleng are listed as Western Vietic rather than part of the Chut phylogenetic group by Alves and Sidwell.
