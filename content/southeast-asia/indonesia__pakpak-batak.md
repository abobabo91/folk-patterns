## Overview

The Pakpak people, also called Batak Pakpak or Pakpak DAIRI, are an ethnic group found mainly in North Sumatra, Indonesia, with communities in Aceh. They are distributed across Dairi Regency, Pakpak Bharat Regency, Humbang Hasundutan Regency, Central Tapanuli Regency, Aceh Singkil Regency, and Subulussalam. Most settled in Dairi Regency, which on July 28, 2003 was divided into Dairi Regency and Pakpak Bharat Regency. The sources describe Pakpak as both a culture and a language, and identify five sub-ethnic groups: Pakpak Klasen, Pakpak Simsim, Pakpak Boang, Pakpak Pegagan, and Pakpak Keppas.

## Festivals & rituals

Pakpak traditional ceremonies are described as “working” terms, although “festival” is also frequently used. They are divided into two major categories: ceremonies involving joyous occasions, called “good works,” and ceremonies involving sorrowful occasions, called “bad works.” Examples of good works include *merbayo*, a wedding ceremony; *menanda tahun*, a paddy planting ceremony; and *merkottas*, which initiates a risky task. Bad works include *mengrumbang* and the *mate ncayur ntua* ceremony, a funeral.

Pakpak social organization is called *sulang silima*. It consists of five elements: *Sinina tertua* or *Perisang-isang*, referring to descendants or older generations; *Sinina penengah* or *Pertulan tengah*, referring to descendants or middle generations; *Sinina terbungsu* or *Perekur-ekur*, referring to the youngest generation; *Berru*, kinsmen who receive women into their family; and *Puang*, kinsmen who give women into another family. These five elements participate in decision making, kinship, and traditional ceremonies. Their involvement is required for a decision to be valid in customary terms, whether within a single surname-clan community called *Lebbuh* or a village-based community called *Kuta*.

## Language & religion

Pakpak is both a spoken and written language. It is part of the Northern Batak language and has its own writing system and alphabet, called *Surat Batak Pakpak*. The source states that fewer Pakpak people use this system today.
