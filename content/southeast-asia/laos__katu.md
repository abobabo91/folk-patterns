## Overview

The Katuic people, also known as Co Tu and Ca Tang, are an ethnic group of about 102,551 living in eastern Laos and central Vietnam. In Laos, the Katu live in Sekong Province along the upper Sekong River and in the highland basin of the Song Boung river watershed along the border with Vietnam's Quảng Nam Province and Huế city. There were 28,378 Katu in Laos in 2015. They speak a Mon-Khmer language and are numbered among the Katuic peoples. In Vietnam, the official name is “Cơ Tu,” and Katu people live in Thừa Thiên–Huế, Quảng Nam, and Da Nang city. Their documented folk-culture expressions include stilt houses, jute growing and weaving, bamboo-stem cooking, carrying baskets, music, and dance.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources used identify jute growing and weaving among Katu living on the Laotian border.

**Carrying baskets** — Katu peoples are known for developing a wide variety of Austroasiatic carrying baskets.

### Architecture

Traditional Katu homes are built on stilts.

## Music & performance

The Katu play *h'roa* in ordinary life. No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription concerns this ethnic group in the supplied material.

## Dance & theatre

The Katu dances named in the sources are *tung tung*, performed by males, and *ya yá*, performed by females.

## Foodways

The Katu typically serve rice cooked in bamboo stems. The sources name the preparations *zăr*, *aví hor*, *koo dep*, *koo g'đhoong*, and *cha chắc*, and identify *tavak* as a beverage.

## Language & religion

Katu, or Low Katu, is a Katuic language of eastern Laos and central Vietnam. Katuic languages are Mon-Khmer languages. In Vietnam, Katu is spoken in Huế city, including A Lưới district. Some 15,000 Katu in Thừa Thiên–Huế speak Phuong, a Katuic dialect often recognized as a separate language. The sources describe Katu phonology, including the diphthongs /ia, ɨa, ua/.
