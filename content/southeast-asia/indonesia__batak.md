## Overview

The Batak are an Austronesian people centered on North Sumatra's Lake Toba highlands, divided into six subgroups with related languages and customs. They are known for a highly developed weaving tradition centered on the ritual cloth ulos, monumental stone tombs, distinctive timber architecture, and one of Southeast Asia's most elaborate ancestor-and-soul cosmologies. Their material culture, music, and ceremonies reflect a complex kinship system called dalihan na tolu that governs exchanges of cloth, bride-price, and ritual obligations across three categories of relations. Christianity (especially Protestantism) and Islam now coexist with older practices of soul-veneration and secondary burial.

## Material culture

Batak material culture emphasizes textiles, wood carving, and objects charged with ritual meaning through the kinship system.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The ulos, a warp-faced cotton cloth woven on a backstrap loom, is the center of Batak textile life and passes between kinship groups at every major life event.

- **Ulos Ragidup** — The most sacred Toba cloth of life, distinguished by three separately woven panels with a distinct central pattern, given by the bride's father to the groom's mother.
- **Ulos Ragi Hotang** — The rattan-pattern cloth with dark ground and warp stripes, given to newly married couples to bind them like tightly wound rattan.
- **Ulos Sadum** — The most colorful Batak cloth with polychrome supplementary weft, used as a cloth of blessing in Angkola and Toba communities.
- **Ulos Sibolang** — A mourning and widow's cloth in indigo with tie-resist white bands, used at funerals and to mark widowhood.
- **Karo uis** (*uis*) — The Karo counterpart to Toba ulos, generally narrower with a restrained palette of indigo, madder, and undyed cotton.

### Clothing & dress

Ceremonial dress centers on draped ulos cloths worn as wraps, shoulder cloths, and head-cloths, with folded head-cloth styles identifying the wearer's subgroup.

- **Tali-tali** — The Toba men's folded head-cloth, tied with peaked ends that identify the wearer's subgroup.
- **Bulang-bulang** — The Karo men's folded head-cloth, equivalent to the Toba tali-tali.
- **Tudung** — A heavy folded red cloth pinned into a broad crown, worn by Karo women as the most recognizable head covering.
- **Ulos hela** — The cloth in which the bride and groom are wrapped together at weddings.

### Architecture

The signature Batak house is a saddle-roofed timber pile-dwelling with steeply sloping thatch, walls that lean outward, and a front gable covered in deeply carved and painted scrollwork called gorga.

- **Ruma (Jabu)** (*ruma or jabu*) — The Toba house, a saddle-roofed timber dwelling on massive hardwood posts with a facade of carved gorga patterns.
- **Gorga** — Deeply incised curvilinear scrollwork painted in ritual red, black, and white on house facades, with named panels like gorga singa-singa and ipon-ipon.
- **Karo rumah adat** (*rumah adat*) — A much larger Karo multi-family longhouse accommodating up to eight extended families under a double-tiered roof crowned by projecting buffalo-horn finials.
- **Sopo** — A rice granary on posts whose upper storey served as a guest and sleeping space in traditional villages.
- **Stone tomb** (*sarkopagus, tugu, or tambak*) — Monumental stone sarcophagi carved with human and singa faces, placed in Toba villages as permanent dwellings for honored ancestors.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Batak created no fine ceramics but excelled at wood and bone carving for ritual and everyday use, with the bark-book pustaha being the most iconic object.

- **Pustaha** — A folded bark-book with wooden covers inscribed in Surat Batak script by a datu, containing divinatory, medical, and magical knowledge.
- **Tunggal panaluan** — A tall carved magic staff stacked with human and animal figures, topped with hair and feathers, concealing ritual substance.
- **Medicine container** (*naga morsarang or guri-guri*) — A buffalo horn container carved into a singa or dragon shape and stoppered with a wooden rider figure, used to hold healing substances.
- **Divination calendar** (*porhalaan*) — Bamboo strips incised with divination marks for consulting the ritual calendar.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Batak jewelry features massive neck and ear ornaments in silver and gold, historically worn to mark status and life-stage changes.

- **Padung-padung** — A massive spiral silver ear ornament worn by married Karo women, weighing over a kilogram and looped over the head-cloth for support.
- **Rante** — Heavy neck chains in gilded silver worn as part of Karo bridal dress.
- **Sertali** — Forehead ornaments in gilded silver worn as part of Karo bridal regalia.

## Music & performance

The gondang ensemble—a set of tuned drums, gongs, and the double-reed sarune bolon—is the core of Batak music and accompanies all major ceremonies.

- **Gondang sabangunan** — The great outdoor Toba ensemble built around five tuned single-headed taganing drums, a bass gordang, gongs, and the sarune bolon reed.
- **Gondang hasapi** — An indoor chamber ensemble featuring the two-stringed hasapi boat-lute, sulim transverse flute, garantung wooden xylophone, and small sarune.
- **Andung** — A Toba ritual lament sung at funerals and ceremonies.
- **Batak trio** — A distinctive 20th-century close-harmony guitar-and-voice style that became standard across Indonesia, exemplified by artists like Nahum Situmorang.

## Dance & theatre

The tortor, a stylized restrained dance performed to gondang music with outturned palm gestures, is the paradigmatic Batak dance and ritual form.

- **Tortor** — A ritual dance with restrained upper-body and hand movements, performed at weddings, funerals, and house consecrations to address kinship groups.
- **Sigale-gale** — A life-sized wooden funerary puppet articulated with strings and made to dance the tortor at the funeral of a man who died without male heirs.
- **Guro-guro aron** — A Karo courtship dance performed by young unmarried people.

## Festivals & rituals

Batak ceremonial life is organized around life-cycle events (birth, marriage, funeral) governed by the dalihan na tolu kinship triangle and adat customary law.

- **Pesta unjuk** — A wedding ceremony extending over days, with the exchange of ulos cloths, bride-price (sinamot), and the sequential invitation of gondang musical pieces.
- **Mangongkal holi** — The exhumation of ancestral bones some years after death and their transfer to a monumental family stone tomb, often accompanied by buffalo sacrifice.
- **Saur matua** — A festive funeral for an elder who has reached full status with married descendants, featuring tortor dance and gondang music.
- **Horja bius** — A village-level community feast to petition the high god Mula Jadi Na Bolon for agricultural success.

## Foodways

Batak cooking is emphatic, sour, and hot, seasoned with andaliman pepper, and uniquely among Muslim Sumatra includes pork and dog as ceremonial meats.

- **Saksang** — Minced pork or buffalo cooked with andaliman, coconut milk, turmeric, and in full form pig blood.
- **Arsik** — Carp from Lake Toba braised with andaliman, torch ginger, and asam cikala until the bones soften, the ritual dish given by wife-givers to a newly married couple.
- **Naniura** — Raw carp cured only in asam jungga citrus juice with andaliman and shallots, a Toba dish once reserved for kings.
- **Mie gomak** — A thick spaghetti-like noodle from Tapanuli tossed with andaliman sambal.

## Oral tradition & literature

Batak oral tradition centers on the tarombo genealogy tracing descent from the mythic ancestor Si Raja Batak and cosmological narratives preserved in bark-books and still recited by ritual specialists.

- **Tarombo** — The recited genealogy tracing every person's descent from Si Raja Batak, the mythic ancestor said to have settled at Sianjur Mula-Mula on Lake Toba.
- **Umpasa** — Proverbs delivered in obligatory couplets at every ceremonial speech, still an active oral form.
- **Turi-turian** — Cosmological narratives centered on Mula Jadi Na Bolon and his descendants, traditionally recited by the datu and inscribed in pustaha bark-books.

## Language & religion

Batak languages form a coordinate branch of Austronesian with the historical Surat Batak script; contemporary religion is majority Christian (HKBP Lutheran) with Muslim communities and survivors of the old agama si dekah.

- **Surat Batak** — The historical Batak script, likely derived from southern Sumatran Kawi, written on bamboo, bone, and tree bark and now largely ceremonial.
- **HKBP** — The Huria Kristen Batak Protestan (Batak Protestant Church), founded by 19th-century mission, the largest Protestant church in Indonesia.
- **GBKP** — The Gereja Batak Karo Protestan (Karo Protestant Church) serving the Karo subgroup.
- **Agama si dekah** (*agama si dekah or pemena or parmalim*) — The old religion still practiced by a minority, with parmalim being a codified Toba revivalist form founded in the early 19th century.
- **Tondi/tendi** (*tondi or tendi*) — The Batak soul-concept, central to spiritual life and still operative across all religious confessions.

## Glossary

- *ulos* — Ritual warp-faced cotton cloth passed between kinship groups at major life events
- *dalihan na tolu* — The kinship system organizing social relations in three categories: hulahula (wife-givers), boru (wife-takers), dongan tubu (agnates)
- *gorga* — Deeply incised curvilinear scrollwork carved on house facades and painted in ritual red, black, and white
- *ruma or jabu* — The traditional Batak house with saddle roof and carved facade
- *sopo* — A rice granary on posts whose upper storey served as guest quarters
- *pustaha* — Bark-book with wooden covers inscribed in Surat Batak script containing ritual knowledge
- *datu* — A ritual specialist or priest who creates pustaha and performs ceremonies
- *gondang* — The core Batak musical ensemble built around tuned drums and gongs
- *sarune bolon* — The double-reed wind instrument that plays melody in the gondang sabangunan ensemble
- *tortor* — The ritual dance with restrained upper-body movements performed to gondang at ceremonies
- *sigale-gale* — A life-sized wooden puppet made to dance the tortor at special funerals
- *mangongkal holi* — The secondary burial rite exhuming bones and transferring them to monumental stone tombs
- *adat* — Customary law and ritual practice governing social obligations
- *andaliman* — A Sumatran pepper related to Sichuan pepper, the signature seasoning of Batak cuisine
- *tarombo* — The recited genealogy tracing descent from the mythic ancestor Si Raja Batak
- *umpasa* — Proverbs recited in obligatory couplets at ceremonial speeches
- *tondi or tendi* — The Batak soul-concept, central to cosmology and ancestor veneration
- *singa* — A stylized lion-like guardian face motif in carving and weaving
- *Surat Batak* — The historical Batak script derived from Kawi, written on bamboo and bark
- *sinamot* — Bride-price given by the groom's family to the bride's family
