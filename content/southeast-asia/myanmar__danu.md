## Overview

The Danu are a government-recognized ethnic group in Myanmar, predominantly populating areas near the Pindaya Caves in Shan State. They speak Danu, a southern Burmish language closely related to Burmese, the national language. Danu has 93% lexical similarity with standard Burmese, and both Danu and Intha are spoken by about 100,000 people each. The name Danu derives from the Pali term *dhanu*, meaning “archer” or “bow,” referring to the legend of Prince Kumarabhaya, whose bow and arrow rescued seven princesses trapped in caves by a giant spider. Oral traditions trace Danu ancestry to intermarriages of Burmese men and Shan women in the 16th century.

## Language & religion

Danu and Intha are southern Burmish languages of Shan State, Burma. The Government of Myanmar considers both to be dialects of Burmese. Danu and Intha are written using the Burmese alphabet. Between 2013 and 2014, the Taunggyi branch of the Danu Literature and Culture Committee invented a new alphabet inspired by Pyu and Burmese scripts found on stone inscriptions. Adoption of this script remains divisive within the Danu Self-Administered Zone, and the script is not accepted by its administration.
