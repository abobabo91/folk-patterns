## Overview

The Idaʼan live on the east coast of Sabah in Malaysian Borneo and speak a Malayo-Polynesian language. They are notable for converting to Islam in 1408, one of the earliest documented conversions in Malaysia. The material culture shows skill in working bamboo, rattan, and bark into clothing, weapons, musical instruments, and everyday objects. Much of their traditional knowledge is at risk as younger speakers shift to Malay.

## Material culture

Idaʼan material culture is built from natural materials — bamboo, rattan, bark, bone, and brass — crafted into functional forms without elaborate ornament.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Textiles were made from bark, grass, and spun fibre, with no named weaving traditions recorded.

- **Barkcloth garments** — Beaten bark was used for sleeveless jackets, men's dresses combined with vegetable fibre, and hats made with twine.
- **Grass cloth** — A fringed cloth made of grass rather than spun thread.
- **Woven cloth with metal** — A woman's dress fitted with a metal chain, making metalwork part of the garment itself.

### Clothing & dress

Idaʼan dress used barkcloth and woven textile, with the most distinctive items being plaited hats in a standard conical form.

- **Conical bamboo hat** — Plaited from bamboo with three vertical stripes of dark plaiting alternating with three strips of red-dyed bamboo, held in shape by a thick bamboo rim.
- **Rattan hat** — Plaited from rattan in a simple style.
- **Barkcloth jacket** — A sleeveless jacket made of beaten bark.
- **Man's dress** — Made of barkcloth combined with vegetable fibre.
- **Woman's dress** — Made of woven textile fitted with an attached metal chain.

### Architecture

House forms and building practices are not documented in available sources.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The strongest craft tradition is in arms and tools made from bone, metal, bamboo, and rattan; everyday objects were plaited or carved from plant materials.

- **Brass sword** — Made of brass, wood, and rattan with a scabbard decorated with hair.
- **Bone knife** — Made of bone with metal, stored in a wooden and rattan sheath bound with cord or sinnet.
- **Rattan shield** — A round shield made entirely of rattan.
- **Bamboo quiver** — Made of bamboo and rattan with a cylindrical bamboo lid decorated with rattan strips and tied with cord.
- **Sacred pottery jar** — A ceramic vessel classified as ritual equipment rather than ordinary storage.

### Jewelry & body adornment

No jewellery, tattooing, or body adornment practices are documented, except for a metal chain attached to women's dress.

## Music & performance

Music was played on bamboo and cane instruments, both intimate and close-range types suited to solo performance.

- **Bamboo jaw harp** — Made of bamboo with string, possibly with gum and wax, a plucked lamella type found across island Southeast Asia.
- **Cane mouth organ** (*mouth organ*) — Built of cane tubes sealed with gum and rattan, an aerophone assembled from plant materials.

## Dance & theatre

No dance or dramatic traditions are documented.

## Festivals & rituals

No annual festival cycle is documented, but objects point to sacred and mortuary practices.

- **Sacred pottery jar** — A pottery jar set apart from ordinary storage vessels and classified as religious or ritual equipment.
- **Shroud cloth** — A cloth of ticking and thread, possibly used in handling the dead, though this is uncertain.
- **Islamic conversion (1408)** — A manuscript records Abdullah of Darvel Bay embracing Islam, after which the Idaʼan community converted while Begak and Subpan peoples maintained traditional religion.

## Foodways

No staple crops, cooking methods, or dishes are documented, but tools show food was obtained and carried by plough, trap, and basket.

- **Fish trap** — Made of cane and bark.
- **Wooden plough** — A tool for cultivation.
- **Back-carried basket** — Made of bamboo and vegetable fibre with a loose wooden disc inside as a platform.
- **Bamboo lime-box lid** — Associated with betel preparation rather than cooking.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Idaʼan have a documented literary history beginning with a 1408 manuscript in Jawi script recording a conversion to Islam, though no epic, folktale, or storytelling traditions are named.

- **1408 Jawi manuscript** — An account of Abdullah of Darvel Bay's conversion to Islam, one of the earliest known works in the language.
- **Begak dialect grammar** — A full-length linguistic study by Nelleke Goudswaard (2005) documenting one of the three Idaʼan dialects.

## Language & religion

Idaʼan is a Malayo-Polynesian language with three dialects corresponding to three ethnic groups; the Idaʼan proper are Muslim while Begak and Subpan maintain traditional religion.

- **Idaʼan proper** (*Idaʼan*) — The main dialect spoken around Sagama and villages west of Lahad Datu, spoken by the Muslim Idaʼan community.
- **Begak dialect** (*Begak*) — Spoken in Ulu Tungku and east of Lahad Datu, threatened with extinction as younger speakers shift to Malay.
- **Subpan dialect** (*Subpan*) — Spoken in Kinabatangan and Sandakan districts.
- **Jawi script** (*Jawi*) — The historical script for writing Idaʼan, used in the 1408 manuscript.

## Glossary

- *Jawi* — Arabic-based script used historically to write Idaʼan
- *Idaʼan* — Malayo-Polynesian language and main ethnic group of Sabah's east coast
- *Begak* — One of three Idaʼan dialects, now threatened with extinction
- *Subpan* — One of three Idaʼan dialects
- *Barkcloth* — Fabric made from beaten bark, used for garments and hats
- *Sinnet* — Cord made by plaiting or twisting plant fibres
- *Rattan* — Palm stem material used for plaiting baskets, hats, and shields
- *Quiver* — Container for carrying arrows
- *Darvel Bay* — Location on Sabah's coast associated with early Islamic conversion in 1408
- *Sagama* — Settlement in Sabah where Idaʼan proper is spoken
- *Lahad Datu* — District on Sabah's east coast in the Idaʼan region
- *Kinabatangan* — District in Sabah where Subpan dialect is spoken
- *Sandakan* — District in Sabah where Subpan dialect is spoken
- *Malayo-Polynesian* — Language family spanning Southeast Asia and the Pacific
- *Abdullah* — Idaʼan man who converted to Islam in 1408, leading his community to do the same
