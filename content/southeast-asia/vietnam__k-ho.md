## Overview

The K'Ho, also known as Cơ Ho, Kaho, or Koho, are an ethnic group living in Lâm Đồng province in Vietnam's Central Highlands. They speak the K'Ho language, which belongs to the southern Bahnaric branch of the Mon–Khmer language group. They are related to the Cho Ro and Mạ people. The K'Ho are described as the oldest ethnic group in the southern Central Highlands of Vietnam. The Lạch, a subgroup of K'Ho, are the indigenous group of Lâm Đồng. The name of the provincial capital, Da Lat, originated from Đà Lạch, literally “water of the Lạch people”; the name refers to the Cam Ly stream, which flows into the Đồng Nai river to the south-west of the city. The sources identify K'Ho musical, culinary, religious, and literary traditions.

## Music & performance

The K'Ho have a musical instrument called *kăm boat*. No K'Ho song genres, named repertories, professional performers, or performance occasions are described. The supplied UNESCO material lists no Intangible Cultural Heritage inscriptions for Vietnam concerning this ethnic group.

## Festivals & rituals

K'Ho folk religion worships a pantheon of gods, including Yang N'Du, identified as the Supreme God, as well as a forest god, water god, and fire god. The majority of the Koho people now identify as Christian.

## Foodways

The sources identify *sour gruel* as a K'Ho dish.

## Oral tradition & literature

The K'Ho are described as having vernacular literature that includes the epic *Gơ Plom kòn Yồi*, literally “The child of Plom is Yồi.”

## Language & religion

The K'Ho language belongs to the southern Bahnaric branch of the Mon–Khmer language group. The sources refer to K'Ho, Cơ Ho, Kaho, and Koho as names associated with the people, and identify the Lạch as a subgroup. They also list subdivisions including Srê, Nộp, C'don, Cil, Lach, and T'ring. In Đưng K'nớ commune, Lạc Dương district, Lâm Đồng province, the sources list clans including Bon Dơng, Rơ Ông, K'Long, Kơ Liêng, Krajan, Pang Ting, Da-gout, Bon Niêng, Cil, Liêng Hót, Phi Srônh, M'bon, Kơ Să, Đơng G, and Bon Đing. Religious life is described as involving a pantheon of gods, while the majority of Koho people now identify as Christian.
