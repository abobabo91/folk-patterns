## Overview

The Bunak, also known as Bunaq, Buna' and Bunake, are an ethnic group living in the mountainous region of central Timor, divided between West Timor in Indonesia and East Timor. In Indonesia they are concentrated particularly in Lamaknen District, with settlements also found in Belu Regency, Malaka Regency and surrounding areas. Their language is Papuan rather than Austronesian; the Bunak language belongs to the Timor–Alor–Pantar language family. One source estimates about 100,000 speakers, divided evenly between the two countries, while 55,837 East Timorese speak Bunak as their native language. Bunak communities often live beside Tetun communities and commonly speak at least one surrounding Malayo-Polynesian language.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Bunak settlements in the mountains were relatively isolated from neighboring communities. In the Lamaknen region, Bunak people historically lived in fortified villages on high ground; after the traditional conflicts ended in 1897, they gradually built houses closer to water supplies. Clan members continued to visit their clan houses for ceremonies after settlements became more dispersed.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Bunak origin narrative does mention a golden blowpipe, a golden arrow, bamboo arrows, betel pouches and an oil from the Underworld, but it does not present these as catalogueable material traditions.

## Music & performance

Bunak narrative traditions were historically transmitted orally rather than through a written tradition. They were recited with repetition, rhyme and alliteration, techniques that helped performers remember the verses. The sources describe an oral-literature genre called *Zapal* in the Bunak Lamaknen area. No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription concerns the Bunak.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe ceremonies associated with clan houses in the Lamaknen region, where dispersed clan members continued to gather after moving from fortified settlements on high ground to houses near water supplies. The Bunak origin narrative is connected to ideas of ancestry: Mau Ipi Guloq is presented as one of the main ancestors of the Bunak people after his conflict with Asa Pharan, encounters with the Underworld and marriages to his brother’s wives.

## Foodways

The origin narrative mentions water buffalo, sows, oranges and betel. It also refers to a betel pouch in the story of Mau Ipi Guloq.

## Oral tradition & literature

Bunak history and traditions were transmitted by word of mouth before European colonization. Narrative traditions were performed with repetition, rhyme and alliteration. One recorded legend tells of Mau Ipi Guloq, who first domesticated the water buffalo, followed a crow into the Underworld, restored its ruler to health, received two oranges that became princesses, and later became one of the main ancestors of the Bunak people. Another legend contrasts Bunak and Kemak character through a story about the size of their ears: Bunak people are associated with a short-tempered and impatient temperament, while Kemak people are represented as calm and patient. An oral-literature genre called *Zapal* is associated with the Bunaq Lamaknen area.

## Language & religion

Bunak is a Papuan language spoken in central Timor and belongs to the Timor–Alor–Pantar language family. It is surrounded by Malayo-Polynesian languages, including Uab Meto and Tetun. Bunak distinguishes animate and inanimate noun classes. The language has dialect differences associated with areas including Lamaknen, the northeast and southwest, Lolotoe, Zumalai, Ainaro and Manufahi. Bunak communities commonly speak surrounding Malayo-Polynesian languages; in East Timor, Tetum functions as a lingua franca, and many Bunak people in contact with Mambai are bilingual in Mambai.
