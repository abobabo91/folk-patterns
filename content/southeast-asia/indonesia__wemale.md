## Overview

The Wemale are an ethnic group of Seram Island, Indonesia. They number over 7,500 and live in 39 villages in the central area of the island. They originate from a common ancestral group called the Patasiwa, shared with the Alune people of western Seram. Traditionally, Wemale subsistence relied on forest products, especially the sago palm, together with shifting cultivation. Their material culture included large houses made from wood, sticks and palm leaves, tall conical baskets, long knives, and rattan girdles. Wemale culture has changed substantially during the last few decades under the impact of consumerism, political and religious unrest, and conflict in the Maluku area.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Wemale men and women traditionally wore little clothing because of the humid environment. Women wore rattan girdles around their waists. Men carried long knives.

### Architecture

The Wemale built large and elaborate houses from wood, sticks and palm leaves. These houses were skillfully constructed to keep their interiors dry and comfortable.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They do record long knives carried by Wemale men and tall conical baskets used by women to collect products from the forest.

### Jewelry & body adornment

They record women’s rattan girdles as an element of dress.

## Festivals & rituals

Coming-of-age celebrations for girls were important occasions among the Wemale, as among the Alune.

## Foodways

Traditionally, the Wemale lived from forest products, and much of their diet was based on the sago palm. They also practiced shifting cultivation.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Hainuwele legend is an origin myth from Wemale and Alune folklore. It was recorded by the German ethnologist Adolf Ellegard Jensen during a 1937–1938 expedition to the Maluku Islands.

## Language & religion

Wemale is an Austronesian language of Malayo-Polynesian origin spoken on western Seram Island in Indonesia. Collins (1983) classified it as a member of the Central Maluku subgroup. The language is divided into northern and southern varieties or dialects. Northern Wemale is spoken by about 5,000 people and Southern Wemale by about 3,700 people. Variants or dialects named in the sources include Horale, Kasieh, Uwenpantai, Honitetu, Kawe, Oemale, and Tala. They mention political and religious unrest.
