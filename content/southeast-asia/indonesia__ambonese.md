## Overview

The Ambonese come from Ambon Island and southwest Seram Island in Indonesia. Their culture combines rural communities, sago foods, pottery, weaving, carving, shipbuilding, and music. Ambonese Malay was shaped by Malay, Dutch, Portuguese, and indigenous languages. Society includes negeri communities, soa groups, mata ruma clans, and adat customs.

## Material culture

Ambonese material culture includes sago-leaf and bamboo houses, pottery, palm-leaf weaving, carving, blacksmithing, weapons making, and shipbuilding.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not document named Ambonese textile or pattern traditions.

### Clothing & dress

Ambonese dress includes European-style clothing for men and blouses, sarongs, and cotton dresses for women.

- **Short jackets and black trousers** — Men wore these on special occasions.
- **Small-patterned sarongs** — Women commonly wore small-patterned sarongs.
- **Bright cotton dresses** — Younger women wore bright-colored cotton dresses reaching to the knee.

### Architecture

Traditional Ambonese villages stood by the shore, with houses on stilts and made from local materials.

- **Negeri village** (*negeri*) — A negeri was a traditional rural community often led by a raja.
- **Stilt houses** — Houses in traditional shore settlements were built on stilts.
- **Woven sago-leaf houses** — Some houses were made from woven sago leaves.
- **Plastered bamboo, wood, and coral houses** — Other houses used plastered bamboo, wood, and coral stones on stone foundations.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ambonese craftspeople made pottery, metalwork, weapons, ships, carvings, palm-leaf goods, and ornamental crafts.

The sources do not document Ambonese jewelry or named body adornment, and tattooing has largely disappeared through assimilation.

## Music & performance

Ambonese music absorbed European elements and includes katreji, lagoon songs, and several named instruments.

- **Ambonese quadrille** (*katreji*) — Katreji is an Ambonese quadrille.
- **Songs of the lagoon** — The songs of the lagoon are accompanied by violin and lap steel guitar.
- **Bamboo flute** (*efluit*) — The efluit is a named traditional bamboo flute.
- **Wooden xylophone** (*tatabuhan kayu*) — The tatabuhan kayu is a named traditional xylophone.
- **Twelve gongs** — Twelve gongs are among the traditional instruments named in the sources.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not document Ambonese dances, theatre forms, or dramatic traditions beyond katreji as a quadrille.

## Festivals & rituals

Ambonese community life is shaped by marriage customs, adat, and bonds between Christian and Muslim communities.

- **Patrilocal marriage** — Marriage ceremonies were traditionally performed within sectarian groups as patrilocal marriages.
- **Customary law** (*adat*) — Adat governs family matters, inheritance, land rights, and leadership elections.
- **Blood-brother bond** (*pela*) — Pela connects Christian and Muslim Ambonese as a type of blood-brother relationship.

## Foodways

The Ambonese diet centers on sago porridge, vegetables, root crops, and fish, with imported rice also available.

- **Sago porridge** — Sago starch porridge is a basic Ambonese food.
- **Taro** — Taro is part of the Ambonese diet.
- **Cassava** — Cassava is part of the Ambonese diet.
- **Fish** — Fish is part of the Ambonese diet and a common livelihood.
- **Carnation and nutmeg** — Ambon is a center of production for spices such as carnation and nutmeg.

## Language & religion

Ambonese Malay is a Malay-based creole and a regional lingua franca used by mostly Christian and Sunni Muslim communities.

- **Ambonese Malay** (*Ambonese*) — Ambonese Malay developed from Malay and was shaped by Dutch, Portuguese, missionary use, and indigenous languages.
- **Regional lingua franca** — Ambonese Malay is used in Ambon and its surroundings, Buru, Seram, Geser-Gorom, and the southwestern Maluku Islands.
- **Christianity** — Reformed Christianity and Roman Catholicism are predominant religions among the Ambonese.
- **Sunni Islam** — Sunni Islam is also a predominant religion among the Ambonese.
- **Community bond** (*pela*) — Pela is a bond between followers of both faiths.
