## Overview

The Madurese are native to Madura Island and nearby eastern Java. Many communities also live elsewhere in Indonesia. Their life includes farming animals, salt making, fishing, trade, batik, bull racing, and Islam. Their material culture includes bamboo houses, the celurit sickle, and the kris.

## Material culture

Madurese material culture combines bamboo houses, cattle spaces, batik, simple work trades, weapons, boats, and salt production.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Madurese batik is an important craft, with colors that carry cultural meaning.

- **Madurese batik** (*batik*) — Madurese craftsmen make batik, using black, red, yellow, white, green, and blue as cultural symbols.
- **Red-and-white striped cloth** — Men wear shirts with broad red and white stripes, linked to the Majapahit naval flag.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional dress uses dark coats, striped shirts, sarongs, and the celurit.

- **Black long coat** — Men traditionally wear a completely black long-skirted coat with a wide belt.
- **Red-and-white shirt** — Men wear a shirt with broad red and white stripes beneath the coat.
- **Checkered sarong** — Men wear a checkered sarong with their traditional clothing.
- **Women’s dark jacket** — Women wear a dark blue or mottled jacket over a sarong.
- **Crescent knife** (*celurit*) — The celurit is a common peasant weapon and can also be part of traditional male dress.

### Architecture

Madurese villages are scattered, and houses are usually bamboo structures on low stilts.

- **Bamboo house** — Traditional houses are made of bamboo and usually have a frame and a veranda.
- **Low stilts** — Many traditional houses stand on low stilts.
- **Palm-leaf roof** — Roofs are covered with palm leaves or reeds, though roof tiles became more common from the late 20th century.
- **Cattle paddock** — Villages commonly contain paddocks for cattle.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Madurese craftspeople work in pottery, tanning, blacksmithing, boat building, and weapon making.

- **Pottery** — Pottery is one of the occupations listed for Madurese craftsmen.
- **Boat building** — Boat building is a Madurese craft occupation.
- **Kris with sheath** (*kris*) — Museum records include kris with sheaths linked to Madurese collections and materials such as ivory, wood, steel, silver, brass, copper, hair, and pelet wood.
- **Winged-horse kris** (*kris*) — One record describes a Madurese kris with an old ivory winged-horse hilt, a Minangkabau sheath, and a Madurese blade.
- **Spear** — Museum records include spears associated with Indonesian collections.

## Music & performance

Gamelan is shared with eastern Javanese culture and accompanies bull races.

- **Gamelan** — Gamelan performances accompany Karapan sapi races and their folk festivities.

## Dance & theatre

The Madurese share dance and shadow theatre with eastern Javanese culture.

- **Shadow theatre** (*wayang*) — Wayang is a form of shadow theatre shared with eastern Javanese culture.

## Festivals & rituals

Karapan sapi bull racing is the best-known Madurese public tradition, while family life is shaped by Islam and marriage customs.

- **Bull racing** (*Karapan sapi*) — Locally bred bulls pull light carts in annual races, with winners traditionally competing in a final round in Pamekasan.
- **Bull-race festivities** — Races in different localities are held in August and October and usually include gamelan and folk festivities.
- **Bride price** — After an accepted marriage proposal, the bride’s parents usually receive cattle as a bride price.
- **Family residence after marriage** — Newly married couples often live with the bride’s family.

## Foodways

Madurese food is often saltier than eastern Javanese food and is known for satay.

- **Madurese satay** (*sate madura*) — Sate madura usually uses mutton or chicken and a black sauce made with sweet soy sauce, palm sugar, garlic, shallots, peanut paste, shrimp paste, candlenut, and salt.
- **Chicken satay** (*sate ayam*) — Sate ayam is a named Madurese dish.
- **Goat satay** (*sate kambing*) — Sate kambing is a named Madurese dish.
- **Madurese soup** (*soto madura*) — Soto madura is a named Madurese dish.
- **Black-seasoned duck** (*bebek bumbu hitam*) — Bebek bumbu hitam is a named Madurese duck dish.

## Oral tradition & literature

Madurese folklore is shared with eastern Javanese culture, and Sumenep forms the basis of literary Madurese.

- **Folklore** — The Madurese share forms of folklore with eastern Javanese culture.
- **Literary Madurese** — The Sumenep dialect is the basis of literary Madurese.

## Language & religion

Madurese is a Malayo-Sumbawan language, and Sunni Islam is central to social life.

- **Madurese language** — Madurese belongs to the Malayo-Sumbawan group within the Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian family.
- **Sumenep dialect** (*Sumenep*) — Sumenep is the standard dialect and underlies literary Madurese.
- **Bangkalan variety** (*Bangkalan*) — Bangkalan is the most common variety and often works as a lingua franca.
- **Speech levels** (*Èngghi-Bhunten, Èngghi-Enten, Enjâ'-Iyâ*) — Madurese has the speech levels Èngghi-Bhunten, Èngghi-Enten, and Enjâ'-Iyâ.
- **Pesantren schools** (*Pesantren*) — Muslim theologians and Pesantren schools play important roles in spiritual and social life.
