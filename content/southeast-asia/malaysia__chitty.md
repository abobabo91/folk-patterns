## Overview

The Chitty, also known as the Chetty or Chetti Melaka, are an ethnic group of primarily Tamil descent found mainly in Melaka, Malaysia, where they settled around the 16th century. Chitty communities later developed in Singapore, after migration from Melaka during the 18th and 19th centuries. Also called “Indian Peranakans,” they combine Tamil ancestry and Hindu heritage with strong cultural influences from the Nusantara region and, to a lesser extent, Chinese, Dutch and Portuguese communities. In the 21st century, their population stands at 2,000. Their cultural identity is closely associated with the traditional settlement at Kampung Tujuh, their temples, festivals, Malay-influenced clothing and cuisine, and a community identity now described as nearly lost.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no textile motifs.

### Clothing & dress

Most Chitty people have adopted Malay costume. Men may wear a comfortable *sarong* and Malay shirt, and may also wear a *songkok*. Women wear a costume similar to that of the Peranakan Nonya. Clothing is presented as part of a broader cultural assimilation into Malay life, while elements of Indian culture remain visible elsewhere in marriage practices, religious observance and household decoration.

### Architecture

The traditional Chitty settlement is located at Kampung Tujuh along Jalan Gajah Berang in Melaka. Chitty families live alongside Chinese of Tamilian ancestry and Malays in *Kampong* houses. A typical Chitty home is marked by mango leaves arranged in a row and dangling above the front door; Chitty temples are decorated in the same manner. The Chitty temple is an important building type. The Sri Poyatha Moorthi Temple was built by Thaivanayagam Pillay, the leader of the Chitty people, in 1781, after the Dutch colonial government gave him a plot of land. The temple demonstrates simplified Tamil architecture and some Dutch colonial architectural influence. A Chitty neighbourhood probably formed around it in Goldsmith Street. Another temple in the village was built in 1827.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention Chitty ceramics as an area influenced to some extent by Chinese culture. They also state that Chinese cultural influence is visible in the use of religious objects for ancestral-worship rituals. An exhibition of Chitty history, antiques and culture can be seen at the Chitty Museum in Chitty Village, Melaka, Malaysia.

### Jewelry & body adornment

They do describe mango leaves as a form of household and temple decoration, but do not present them as personal adornment.

## Music & performance

They state that Chitty religious ceremonies remain important and that gods such as Ganesha and Shiva are worshipped in full gaiety. No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription concerns this ethnic group.

## Festivals & rituals

The Chitty are practising Saivite Hindus and worship in three temples. Their religious calendar includes Deepavali, Ponggal, the Hindu New Year, Navratri and other traditional Hindu festivals celebrated by Hindu groups in Malaysia. The Chitty do not participate in Thaipusam at a grand level like most Hindu groups; instead, during May they observe a similar festival at their local temple called *Mengamay*.

The *Parchu* festival is unique to the Chitty community and is celebrated twice a year. *Parchu Ponggal*, also called Bhogi, is observed on the day before Ponggal in January. *Parchu Buah-buahan* takes place during the fruit season between June and July. The sources describe marriage customs in which offerings of fruits and the burning of incense are used. Hints of Taoist and Islamic influence are evident in Chitty religious rituals.

## Foodways

Chitty Melaka has an Indian–Peranakan fusion cuisine that combines Tamil spices with Malay ingredients such as *belacan* and lemongrass. Malay spices, ingredients and methods of cooking have largely replaced Indian styles. A well-known Chitty cooking method is *Pindang*, a Malay-language method of boiling ingredients in brine or acidic solutions.

Food is associated with Chitty festivals, including Parchu Bhogi. The available description emphasizes the combination of Tamil spices with Malay ingredients and cooking practices.

## Oral tradition & literature

They describe historical records concerning Tamil traders from Panai in Tamil Nadu who settled in Melaka during the sovereignty of the Sultanate of Malacca, but they provide no Chitty oral-literary tradition.

## Language & religion

The Chitty speak a Malay patois proper to their community, mixed with many Tamil loan words. Despite their Tamil ancestry, the Chettis do not speak Tamil.

Religiously, the Chitty are a tightly knit community of Saivite Hindus. They worship in three temples, including the Sri Poyatha Moorthi Temple, and worship Ganesha and Shiva. Their rituals show hints of Taoist and Islamic influence. They observe Hindu festivals including Deepavali, Ponggal, the Hindu New Year and Navratri, as well as the Chitty festivals Mengamay and Parchu. The community retains its Hindu faith and heritage while showing substantial Malay, Chinese, Dutch and Portuguese cultural influence.
