## Overview

The Bantenese, also called *Orang Banten*, *Orang Sunda Banten*, or *Urang Wahanten*, are an indigenous Sundanese sub-ethnic group native to Banten in the westernmost part of Java island, Indonesia. Their region corresponds more or less with the former Banten Sultanate, a Bantenese nation state that preceded Indonesia. In the 2010 Population Census, the Bantenese and Baduy people were categorized together as Native Bantenese people, with a total of 4,657,784 people. Most Bantenese are Sunni Muslim and speak Banten Sundanese, also called *Basa Sunda Banten* or *Basa Wewengkon Banten*, a Sundanese variety without a general linguistic register. Their folk culture includes distinctive performance forms, cuisine, historical landmarks, and a batik tradition whose motifs refer to Banten Sultanate places, titles, buildings, and historical artifacts.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Bantenese batik** — Bantenese batik uses decorative designs reconstructed from excavations associated with traditional Bantenese houses. The designs were studied by the National Archaeology and the Faculty of Literature, Universitas Indonesia, beginning in 1976, and were decided by the Banten governor in 2003. The batik has grayish colors, storytelling identities, and motifs adopted from historical artifacts. Its motifs draw on ancient village names, noble or sultan titles, and the name of the court palace of Banten.

**Surosowan** — The *Surosowan* motif refers to the spatial layout facing the king or sultan of the Banten Sultanate.

**Pasulamam** — The *Pasulamam* motif refers to the place of embroidery craftsmen within the Banten Sultanate.

**Pasepen** — The *Pasepen* motif refers to the palace place where Sultan Maulana Hasanuddin of Banten would meditate.

**Sebakingking** — The *Sebakingking* motif records Panembahan Sultan Maulana Hasanuddin of Banten’s title in the spreading of the Islamic religion.

**Srimanganti** — The *Srimanganti* motif refers to the place where the commoner’s porch connects to the Banten Sultanate’s *pendopo*, the king or sultan waiting area.

**Panjunan** — The *Panjunan* motif refers to a village where pottery and ceramic craftsmen were found in the Banten Sultanate.

**Motif vocabulary.** Surosowan, spatial layout facing the king or sultan; Pasulamam, embroidery craftsmen’s place; Pasepen, meditation place in the palace; Sebakingking, title associated with spreading Islam; Srimanganti, waiting-area connection; Panjunan, pottery and ceramic craftsmen’s village.

### Clothing & dress

The sources describe Bantenese batik as material for clothing and state that its motifs carry philosophical and intellectual meanings for the wearer.

### Architecture

The sources connect Bantenese decorative designs with traditional Bantenese houses and identify historical buildings associated with the Banten Sultanate. Named structures include the Great Mosque of Banten, the Sacred Long Mausoleum, the Royal Palace of Banten, and the *pendopo*. The *Kaibon* motif refers to *Kaibonan*, a walled building surrounding the Royal Palace of Banten. *Datulaya* refers to Sultan Maulana Hasanuddin of Banten and his family’s residence in the Banten Sultanate.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention pottery and ceramic craftsmen in relation to the *Panjunan* motif, bamboo and mat craftsmen in relation to *Wamilahan*, and *keris* and *keris* adornment craftsmen in relation to *Pamaranggen*.

## Music & performance

The sources name several Bantenese performance forms. Listed forms include Pencak Silat martial arts, Debus, Rudad, Umbruk, Saman dance or Dzikir Saman, Tari topeng, Dog-dog, Angklung Gubrag, Rampak Bedug, Walijamaliha dance, Silat Pandeglang dance, Palingtung, Lojor, and Beluk. The sources specifically associate Rampak Bedug with Pandeglang Regency and describe the artistry of the Banten region as portraying Islamic activities of its society.

## Dance & theatre

The sources identify Saman dance, also called Dzikir Saman, Tari topeng, Walijamaliha dance, and Silat Pandeglang dance among the distinctive art forms of the Bantenese community. They also list Rudad, Umbruk, Dog-dog, Palingtung, Lojor, and Beluk.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that most Bantenese are Sunni Muslim and connect Bantenese cultural activity with a strong Islamic cultural background.

## Foodways

Bantenese culinary traditions named in the sources include *Sate Bandeng*, *Rabeg Banten*, *Pasung Beureum*, *Ketan Bintul*, *Nasi Belut*, *Kue Cucur*, *Angeun Lada*, *Balok Menes*, *Sate Bebek Cibeber*, and *Emping Menes*. They do state that most Bantenese are Sunni Muslim, but do not explicitly connect that religious identity to food preparation.

## Oral tradition & literature

They mention a news program entitled *Beja ti Lembur*, aired by the regional CTV Banten network in Bantenese as part of a regional-government effort to preserve the dialect. The sources also identify Eman Sulaeman as a historian and actor, and Misbach Yusa Biran as a film director.

## Language & religion

Banten Sundanese, or Bantenese, is a Sundanese dialect or a distinct language closely related to Sundanese. It is classified under the Sundanesic language family and is spoken predominantly in Banten, including Lebak, Pandeglang, parts of Tangerang Regency, Tangerang City, and South Tangerang City. It is also spoken in western Bogor Regency, northwestern Sukabumi Regency, and by Bantenese transmigrants in Central Lampung Regency and South Lampung Regency, Lampung. The language does not employ a general sociolinguistic register, unlike the Priangan Sundanese of the Parahyangan region. Most Bantenese are Sunni Muslim. The sources also mention the Baduy people’s practice of Sunda Wiwitan in Kanekes, Leuwidamar, Lebak Regency.
