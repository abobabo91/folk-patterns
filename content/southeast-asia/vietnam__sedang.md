## Overview

The Sedang people, also called Xê Đăng or Xơ Đăng, are an ethnic group of Vietnam. They mainly inhabit Kon Tum province, Quảng Nam province—especially Trà My and Phước Sơn districts—and Quảng Ngãi province, particularly Sơn Tây district. They consist of five main groups: Xteng, also called Xơ Teng, Kayong, Halang or Hà Lăng, Monom, and Todrah. Their language belongs to North Bahnaric, a branch of the Mon–Khmer language family. Sedang communities are largely animistic and Roman Catholic. Their folk culture includes stilt-house architecture, weaving, slash-and-burn and other agricultural practices, blacksmithing, livestock keeping, seasonal festivals, and distinctive language features including an extensive vowel system.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Jute, wild ramie, and garden-grown ramie weaving** — Except for part of the Kayong group, the Sedang groups know how to weave. Historically, weaving used jute and wild or garden-grown ramie; the Monom and Xteng groups still maintain this tradition.

**Cotton spinning and weaving** — The Todrah and Halang groups grow cotton for spinning and weaving.

### Architecture

Sedang people live in small, rectangle-shaped stilt houses with thatched roofs. The upper story is used by people, while the lower story is used for livestock and wood storage.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The Todrah group has a developing blacksmithing tradition. Its members know how to make iron from ore and forge metalwork. Agricultural tools described in the sources include axes, knives, sharpened sticks, sticks with iron blades, small hoes, scrapers with bent blades, and wood or iron hoes.

## Music & performance

The Sedang language is described linguistically as having 24 pure vowels, with seven vowel qualities that may be plain, nasalized, or creaky; /i a o/ may also be both nasal and creaky. The language has between 33 and 55 vowel sounds altogether, and the sources state that Sedang is sometimes claimed to have the largest vowel inventory in the world. A sound sample showing the distinction between clear and creaky vowels is listed among the further resources for the language.

## Festivals & rituals

Sedang folk festivals follow the agricultural production cycle. Around the beginning of July, the **Ondrô Lo Chôi** festival is held to sow seeds in upland fields and/or plant young rice plants in paddy fields. When the rice plants have turned green, the **Tra Ke Ton** festival is held at the beginning of August for eating leftover rice seeds.

The sources describe Sedang religious life only as largely animistic and Roman Catholic.

## Foodways

The sources describe food only in relation to agricultural work and the **Tra Ke Ton** festival, which is held at the beginning of August for eating leftover rice seeds. Rice is cultivated through both slash-and-burn agriculture and other farming methods. The Monom group tills land by driving buffalo herds to stomp it before using wood or iron hoes to excavate. Other groups cut trees with axes and knives, burn the vegetation, sow seeds by poking holes with sharpened sticks or sticks with iron blades, weed with small hoes and scrapers, and harvest rice by hand.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that Sedang groups no longer remember stories of their long migrations and have attached their legends to locations in the northern Central Highlands. A myth of ethnic origin connects the North Bahnaric groups with Hmong–Mien inhabitants and some Sino–Tibetan groups, while linguistic and cultural closeness to ancient Vietic peoples is also described.

## Language & religion

Sedang is an Austro-Asiatic language spoken in eastern Laos and Kon Tum Province in south central Vietnam. It is the language with the most speakers among the North Bahnaric languages, a group known for its range of vowel phonations. Sedang has 24 pure vowels, seven vowel qualities, and three phonation types—plain, nasalized, and creaky—with additional nasal-and-creaky combinations for /i a o/. It has more diphthongs than other North Bahnaric languages described in the source, with between 33 and 55 vowel sounds altogether.

The Sedang people are largely animistic and Roman Catholic.
