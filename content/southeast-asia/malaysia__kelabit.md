## Overview

The Kelabit are a small highland people of Borneo whose isolation in mountains over 1,200 meters high shaped distinct traditions in textiles, pottery, and music until recent decades. They live in single large communal longhouses inherited through generations, farming wet and dry paddy rice as their central food and cultural anchor. Their society divides into three castes with strict inheritance rules that bind families through expensive commemorative celebrations. Christianity arrived in the 1940s alongside World War II, when they fought as armed allies in the Allied campaign to liberate Borneo.

## Material culture

Kelabit material culture centers on bamboo, rattan, grass, and local wood shaped into everyday objects and prestige goods that signal rank and wealth.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Pattern comes from plaiting, binding, and beading rather than loom-weaving, worked into named basketry and mat forms.

- **Plaited floor mat** (*liling*) — A coarse grass mat laid as bedding or flooring inside the longhouse, made from berpah grass.
- **Carrying basket** (*bekang dechur*) — A rattan backpack worn from a headstrap, with woven back and a hinged flap patterned with twisted rattan.
- **Knife-binding pattern** (*kal belad*) — Rattan lashing that wraps a knife blade into its handle, fixed with tree latex and beeswax.
- **Sun hat** (*ilad palm leaf hat*) — Broad-brimmed hat on a rattan frame patterned with concentric circles, zigzags, and colored thread pompoms or beads.
- **Beaded bamboo hat** — Paired hats of finely split bamboo decorated with beads and cloth in stars, stripes, and abstract patterns.

### Clothing & dress

Men traditionally wore loincloths and women wrapped skirts of bark cloth; elaborate beadwork and patterned headgear signaled rank and wealth.

- **Tapa skirt** — Women's skirt woven from the inner bark of a tree.
- **Beaded necklace** — Worn by both sexes, combining fiber with black, white, yellow and red glass beads as a prestige marker.
- **Ear rings** (*abe tawak*) — Men's rings made from bent brass tube with slightly overlapping ends.
- **Shell bangles** — Bracelets cut from shell, worn as adornment.
- **Beaded belts** — Belts decorated with beadwork as prestige display.

### Architecture

A Kelabit village is a single large inherited longhouse shared by the entire community, reaching up to 75 meters long with no internal room divisions.

- **Longhouse** — The communal dwelling housing an entire village, inherited through generations and worked as a collective farming unit.
- **Megalith** — Stone monuments erected before Christianization to honor notable individuals.
- **Commemorative ditch** — Earthworks dug before Christianization to mark important people.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Large earthenware pots and cooking vessels, hand-forged metal tools, and carved wood and rattan objects fill daily life.

- **Large storage jar** (*kudin*) — Round-bottomed earthenware vessel glazed with damar resin, sometimes ruled with lines and blackened by use.
- **Cooking pot** (*tuning*) — Round-bottomed pot either resin-glazed or left unglazed, decorated with incised lines or v-shaped patterns.
- **Spear** (*boso*) — Weapon with a hardwood shaft bound with rattan to a forged metal head.
- **Knife sheath** (*binan tongol*) — Large sheath of labakan wood holding two blades, the smaller sheath (labuh io) cased in reman belabo bark.
- **Carved wooden stool** (*laan*) — Low seat carved from a single piece of belaban wood.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Kelabit prestige relies on beaded necklaces, brass ear-rings, shell bangles, Chinese porcelain vases, and gongs, with former practices including tattooing and tooth-dulling.

- **Brass ear-ring** (*abe tawak*) — A tube of brass bent into a ring with overlapping ends, worn by men.
- **Beaded necklace** — Necklaces combining fiber with multicolored glass beads, worn by both sexes.
- **Shell bangle** (*akap*) — Bracelet cut from shell material.
- **Gong** — An ancient prestige object held by leaders.
- **Leg tattoo** — Documented tattoos on women's legs, a practice now less common.

## Music & performance

Kelabit instruments are carved almost entirely from bamboo sections, each with strings, stops, or slits that produce sound from the tube itself.

- **Two-string bamboo lute** (*pagang*) — A telang bamboo section with two strings carved from its wall, raised by bamboo bridges over a hole.
- **Four-string bamboo lute** (*lotong*) — A wide-bore betong bamboo with strings raised from both sides of a decorative slit.
- **Bamboo flute** (*selingut*) — A telang bamboo flute with three or six triangular stopping holes and an incised, blackened end.
- **Nose flute** (*kelingut*) — A telang bamboo flute played through the nose with three triangular stops.
- **Carved wooden lute** (*sape*) — A single-piece menubun wood instrument with two metal strings on wooden bridges, beeswax frets, and ornamental carving.

## Dance & theatre

No documented Kelabit dance or theatre traditions are recorded in the sources.

## Festivals & rituals

The annual Bario Food and Culture Festival anchors community life; life events bind rank, inheritance, and expensive commemorative obligations.

- **Bario Food and Culture Festival** (*Irau Nukenen Bario*) — Three-day annual gathering held in late July to celebrate food and culture.
- **Adulthood status** (*lun merar*) — Literal 'big person': requires a spouse, children, and enough rice to feed them.
- **Marriage bridewealth** — Traditionally seven buffalo paid for an upper-class bride, demonstrating wealth and alliance.
- **Commemorative celebration** — Expensive feasts staged by heirs to honor those from whom they inherit, creating family tension over obligation.

## Foodways

Rice is the central human food and crop; fields yield maize, tapioca, coffee and tropical fruits, supplemented by forest gathering, hunting and fishing.

- **Mashed rice in leaf** (*Nubak Layak*) — Rice mashed and wrapped in Isip leaf.
- **Bamboo chicken** (*Manuk Pansuh*) — Seasoned chicken cooked inside a bamboo tube.
- **Tapioca leaf stir-fry** (*Udung Ubih*) — Tapioca leaves stir-fried with lemongrass.
- **Shredded beef with bamboo shoots** (*Labo Senutuq*) — Shredded beef stir-fried with bamboo shoots and served with Kantan flower salad.
- **Salt trade good** — Salt extracted from the highlands and traded for goods the Kelabit cannot produce.

## Oral tradition & literature

Kelabit narrative and language documentation appear in published sources; a 1965 creation story of Guma Nepeled was collected by Balang Galih.

- **Creation story** — A Kelabit–Murut narrative about how Guma Nepeled made the world, published in the 1965 Sarawak Gazette.
- **Kelabit–English vocabulary** — Dictionary by Robert Blust published in the Sarawak Museum Journal.
- **Voiced aspirate research** — Historical and comparative work by Blust, Douglas and others on Kelabit's rare phonological feature.
- **Language documentation archive** — ELAR, Kaipuleohone, and Bario Kelabit Language Wiki preserve materials on the language.
- **Doctoral thesis on Kelabit voice** — Charlotte Hemmings' 2016 dissertation examines Kelabit voice and syntax.

## Language & religion

Kelabit is a North Bornean language marked by rare voiced aspirates; the people converted to Christianity in the 1940s and fought as Allied forces in World War II.

- **Kelabit language** — A remote North Bornean language with voiced aspirates between vowels, a feature also reconstructed for Proto-Indo-European.
- **Lepu Potng dialect** — One of three recorded Kelabit dialects.
- **Padas dialect** — One of three recorded Kelabit dialects.
- **Trusan dialect** — One of three recorded Kelabit dialects.
- **Borneo Evangelical Mission** — Organization that reached the highlands after World War II and led mass Christian conversion.
