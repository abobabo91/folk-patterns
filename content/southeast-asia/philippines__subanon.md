## Overview

The Subanon, also spelled Subanen or Subanun, are an Indigenous people of the Zamboanga peninsula, especially the mountainous areas of Zamboanga del Norte, Zamboanga del Sur, and Misamis Occidental on Mindanao Island, Philippines. Their name derives from *soba* or *suba*, meaning “river,” together with the suffix *-nun* or *-non*, indicating locality or place of origin; Subanon therefore means “a person or people of the river.” The 2010 census recorded 220,165 Subanon in Zamboanga del Norte, 148,402 in Zamboanga del Sur, 58,069 in Zamboanga Sibugay, 49,897 in Misamis Occidental, 657 in Zamboanga City, and 342 in Misamis Oriental. Subanen languages are closely related Austronesian languages of the Greater Central Philippine subgroup. Folk-culture documentation in the sources emphasizes agriculture, kinship, houses, food crops, trade, oral tradition, and Indigenous religious practice.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

 They state that forest products, including abaca fiber, were used for making ropes and weaving cloth, and that cloth was obtained through barter.

### Architecture

Subanon houses are built along hillsides and ridges overlooking family fields. They are usually rectangular, raised on stilts, and covered with thatched roofs. Their location reflects the agricultural household, since families cultivate fields and traditionally move from one location to another to clear additional forest for cultivation.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources describe pottery making as a likely part of the ancestors’ dry-agricultural economy. Archaeological finds associated with Subanon history include earthen and glazed burial jars, Chinese celadons, shell bracelets, beads, and gold ornaments; many ceramic wares came from the Yuan and Ming periods. Household and exchange goods included Chinese jars, porcelain, gongs, blades, axes, betel boxes, ornaments, and cloth. Family property covered by inheritance consisted mainly of acquired Chinese jars, gongs, jewelry, and, in later times, currency.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention shell bracelets, beads, gold ornaments, and jewelry among archaeological and inherited objects. Jewelry could form part of family property, together with Chinese jars and gongs.

## Music & performance

 They mention gongs as traded goods and inherited household property, but provide no information about how gongs were played or what musical roles they had.

## Festivals & rituals

 They do describe marriage, birth, illness, and Indigenous religious practices.

Marriage traditionally involved parental arrangement, which could occur before the parties reached puberty. The two families negotiated the bride-price, which could consist of cash, goods, or both, through a go-between unrelated to either family. After the marriage ceremonies and wedding feast, the newlyweds stayed with the girl’s household. The husband was expected to serve his wife’s parents, mainly in producing food, before the couple later selected their own residence.

The sources state that there is a general belief that all human beings should marry. Polygamy and polyandry are practiced, while separation is not allowed and marriage between nearest relatives is prohibited. After divorce, abduction of the wife, or the death of either spouse, the family as a corporate unit could be reconstituted through remarriage. A surviving widow could marry a brother of her deceased husband, while the parents of a deceased wife could marry one of their unmarried daughters or nieces to the widower.

Pregnancy and birth were surrounded by regulations and practices. Pregnant women had to follow various rules, including placing a piece of wood across their path before entering a doorway. Birth spacing and limiting the number of children involved herbs called *benayan*, while a midwife could “manipulate” the woman after delivery.

## Foodways

Rice is the most important Subanon crop and staple. Along the coast, wet agriculture using a plow and carabao produces rice; beyond the coast, both wet and dry agriculture occur, while swidden farming is the norm in the interior uplands. Mountain rice and corn are principal crops. Root crops include *camote* or sweet potato, cassava, *gabi* or taro, and *ubi* or yam. These may be roasted, boiled, or made into preserves and sweets.

The people also grow coconuts, tobacco, hemp or abaca, and a range of garden and orchard crops. Squash, eggplant, melons, bananas, papayas, pineapples, jackfruit, and lanzones provide additional food. Subanon supplement food and income through fishing, hunting, and gathering forest products. During crop failures caused by drought or pests, they gather *buri* and *lumbia* or *lumbay*, palms whose pith provides starchy flour. They also gather sago along riverbanks and collect wild edible roots.

Before the rice harvest in September, the Subanon of Sindangan subsist on root crops and bananas. Rice, sugarcane, yams, fish, and other products also appear in the Subanon account of early trade with coastal people.

## Oral tradition & literature

An old Subanon legend attributes the origins of ancient trade to the first Subanon chieftain, a giant named *Tabunaway*. He ruled before the Moros and the Spaniards appeared on Subanon land and lived near *Nawang*, which later became Zamboanga. According to the legend, the Moros sailed upriver and exchanged fish for the Subanon’s rice, sugarcane, and yams by placing goods on rocks between the coast and the hills.

The sources also describe Subanon traditional instruction. Education was formerly limited to teaching by the *timuay* to a future husband and wife concerning love, respect, and treatment of one another, parents, and in-laws.

## Language & religion

Subanen languages, also called Subanon and Subanun, form a group of closely related Austronesian languages belonging to the Greater Central Philippine subgroup. They are spoken in Zamboanga Sibugay, Zamboanga del Norte, Zamboanga del Sur, Misamis Occidental, Misamis Oriental, and Lanao del Norte. Important varieties include Western Subanon, Lapuyan, Sindangan, Tuboy, and Salug. Subanon speakers have also become fluent in Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Tagalog, and, to some extent, Ilocano.

Indigenous Subanon belief includes veneration of a supreme being called *Diwata Migbebaya*. The supernatural realm includes *gimuud* or souls, *mitibug* or spirits, *getautelunan* or demons, and *diwata* or deities. A *balian* is a medium or shaman who can perceive and address beings invisible to ordinary mortals. Spirits and deities are associated with notable natural features, including large trees, unusual rocks, caves, earth mounds, and high mountain peaks. Today Subanon people have adopted Islam, Catholicism, or Evangelical Protestantism. Muslim Subanon are known as Kalibugan or Kolibugan.
