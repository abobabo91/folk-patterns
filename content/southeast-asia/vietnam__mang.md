## Overview

The Mảng are an ethnic group living primarily in Vietnam and China. In Vietnam, they are one of the country’s 54 officially recognized ethnic groups and mainly reside in Hua Bum and Nậm Ban communes of Nậm Nhùn district in Lai Châu province. The 2009 Population and Housing Census recorded about 3,700 Mảng in Vietnam, while about 500 Mảng live in Yunnan province of southern China, where they are officially termed an undistinguished nationality. The Mảng language belongs to the Pakanic branch of the Austroasiatic language family. Their documented folk-cultural profile includes temporary leaf-built houses, a slash-and-burn nomadic lifestyle, and chin-tattooing for women who reach puberty.

### Architecture

The Mảng used forest leaves to build temporary houses. The sources connect this architecture with a slash-and-burn nomadic lifestyle: when the leaves turned yellow, they moved to another place. No further house forms, roof types, structural features, or decorative traditions are described.

## Language & religion

Mảng is an Austroasiatic language spoken mainly in Lai Châu Province, Vietnam, and across the border in Jinping County, China. It was first documented only in 1974. In Vietnam, it is spoken by 2,200 people in the districts of Sìn Hồ, Mường Tè, Phong Thổ, and nearby areas, including Nậm Ban Township, Sìn Hồ District, Lai Châu Province. In China, Mảng speakers numbered 606 people in 1999. Mảng is an analytic SVO language that retains Austroasiatic derivational morphology and case-marking on nouns.
