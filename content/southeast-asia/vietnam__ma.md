## Overview

The Mạ or Maa are a Mon–Khmer indigenous people of Vietnam. In 2019, their population was 50,322. They are concentrated mainly in Lâm Đồng and Đồng Nai provinces, particularly around the upper Đồng Nai River. The Mạ are very close to the Koho people. Before the 19th century, the Mạ achieved some political unity and a stratified society under the influence of Funan. This political organization later broke down through French colonization, inter-group warfare, and slave trading. Their culture includes oral traditions, myths, parables, legends, named language varieties, and distinctive clothing.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Mạ women wear colorful traditional attire, including skirts that reach well below the knees. Men customarily wear loincloths.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Mạ have rich oral traditions, and their culture includes myths, parables, and legends. These forms are described as integral to the group. Jean Boulbet provided a detailed description of Mạ beliefs, customs, ethno-geography, and botany after living in what are now the Cát Tiên and Bảo Lâm districts during the 1950–60s, before the extensive influx of lowland people into the region.

## Language & religion

The Mạ or Maa language belongs to the Mon–Khmer Group. The Mạ language and the Koho language are sometimes considered different dialects only. The sources describe several Mạ subgroups and language varieties: Mạ Ngăn, Mạ Tô, Mạ Krung or Mạ Đa Gui, and Mạ Xốp. Mạ Ngăn is identified as the main Mạ subgroup.
