## Overview

The Tenggerese are a sub-ethnic group of Javanese centered in the isolated Tengger mountains around Mount Bromo, within the Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park in eastern Java, Indonesia. Communities also occur in the Pasuruan, Probolinggo, Malang, and Lumajang regencies. The Tenggerese speak Tenggerese, also called Tengger Javanese, a dialect of Javanese in the Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian language family. Their cultural life is marked by customs associated with the Majapahit Empire, Java Hinduism incorporating Buddhist and Animist elements, ancestral worship, distinctive religious offerings, and the annual Yadnya Kasada festival at Mount Bromo.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They state only that, during rituals for spirits, small doll-like figures representing the spirits are clothed in batik cloth and presented with food and drink.

### Architecture

The Poten is a sacred area of ground at Mount Bromo’s sand sea and the focus of the annual Kasada Ceremony. It contains several buildings and enclosures arranged in a composition called the Mandalas, or zones.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention small horses used by nomadic herders living at higher altitudes, but provide no further description of their equipment or material culture.

## Music & performance

They do state that traditional ceremonies and rituals use Tenggerese, particularly in the recitation of mantras and oral literature such as folktales.

## Festivals & rituals

The main Tenggerese festival is **Yadnya Kasada**, which lasts about a month. On the 14th day of Kasada, the Tenggerese go to Poten Bromo and ask for blessings from Hyang Widi Wasa and Mahadeva, described as the God of the Mountain, Mount Semeru. Offerings include rice, fruit, vegetables, flowers, livestock, and other local produce. The festival also includes the examination of medicine men who memorize prayers; the medicine man who passes the examination is chosen as the spiritual leader of the Tengger tribe.

The festival’s origin is associated with a legend from the Majapahit kingdom during the reign of King Brawijaya. Roro Anteng and Jaka Seger are described as ancestors of the Tenggerese. According to the legend, they prayed at Mount Bromo for children and were told that their youngest child had to be sacrificed in the volcano’s crater. After the birth of 25 children, their 25th child, Kesuma, was thrown into the crater. An alternate version says that Kesuma was consumed by fire from the crater while trying to flee. The Tengger people then began commemorating the festival.

Tenggerese religious practice also includes offerings and rites for deities, ancestors, village guardian spirits, and other spirits. **Sajenan** is presented by a priest to guardian deities in formal liturgy. At weddings, a cone of rice called *Tumpeng Walagara* is offered as a source of blessing for the couple and the whole village. **Suguhan** are offerings made by ordinary Tengger Hindus to ancestral spirits. **Tamping** are offerings to evil spirits intended to ward off bad luck; they typically consist of meat, rice, and bananas wrapped in leaves and placed at cemeteries, bridges, and road intersections.

## Foodways

The sources describe food primarily through religious offerings. During Yadnya Kasada, the Tenggerese offer rice, fruit, vegetables, flowers, livestock, and other local produce at Poten Bromo. At weddings, *Tumpeng Walagara*, a cone of rice, is offered in the Sajenan ceremony. Suguhan are offerings to ancestral spirits, while Tamping typically consist of meat, rice, and bananas wrapped in leaves.

## Oral tradition & literature

Tenggerese oral tradition includes folktales, mantras, and the legends associated with Yadnya Kasada. The principal origin story concerns Roro Anteng and Jaka Seger, who are described as ancestors of the Tenggerese and as rulers of the Tengger region under the title Purbawisesa Mangkurat Ing Tengger. The story connects their descendants with the Majapahit kingdom, the spread of Islam in Java, Mount Bromo, and the sacrifice of Kesuma.

Tenggerese is used in traditional ceremonies and rituals, especially in the recitation of mantras and oral literature such as folktales.

## Language & religion

Tenggerese, or Tengger Javanese, is a Javanese dialect in the Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian language family. It preserves many features of Kawi, or Old Javanese, particularly in phonology and vocabulary. The language is also known locally as *Cārabasa Tengger* or *Piwākyan Tengger*. The Tenggerese call it *basa dhuwur*, or “high language,” while Arekan Javanese is called *basa ngisor*, or “low language.” Tenggerese has its own written Kawi script based on the old Javanese Brahmi type.

Most Tenggerese profess Java Hinduism, incorporating Buddhist and Animist elements. They worship Ida Sang Hyang Widi Wasa and also Hindu and Buddhist deities including the Tri Murti—Shiva, Brahma, and Vishnu—and Buddha. Places of worship include the Punden, Poten, and Danyang. Ancestor and spirit worship includes the cikal bakal, roh bahurekso, and roh leluhur. Priests are called *Dukun* or *Resi Pujangga* and are assisted by Legen, Sepuh, and Dandan.
