## Overview

The Ati are a Negrito ethnic group and Indigenous people of the Visayan Islands in the Philippines. Their small numbers are concentrated principally on Boracay, Panay, and Negros. On Panay, Ati communities have also lived in Barotac Viejo, the island of Guimaras, Igkaputol in Dao, Tina in Hamtic, and Badiang in San Jose de Buenavista. Boracay remains regarded as ancestral land, including the area called Takbuyan between Tobias Fournier and San Joaquin on Panay’s southwestern coast. The Ati speak Inati; Hiligaynon and Kinaray-a are also commonly used. Their folk-culture significance in the sources lies in their clothing, jewelry, herbal medicine, mobility, animist practices, oral traditions, and their representation in the Ati-atihan and Dinagyang festivals.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no Ati motifs.

### Clothing & dress

Ati clothing was formerly described as simple. Women wore wraparound skirts, sometimes made from bark cloth, while men wore loincloths. In contemporary daily life, T-shirts, pants, and rubber sandals are common.

### Architecture

They state only that the Ati traditionally lived as nomadic people, with the Ati of Panay described as the most mobile, and that many now live in more permanent settlements.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention boats called *balangays* in a legend concerning the ancestors of the Bisaya.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ati jewelry is described as simple. Some jewelry objects involve plants, including flowers, while others use animal bones, particularly pig teeth.

## Music & performance

They do state that the Ati are represented in festival performance traditions. In the Dinagyang festival of Iloilo City on Panay, performers are painted to look like Ati and organized into “tribes,” also called *tribus*. They perform dances with drums, reflecting the idea that the Atis danced with drums when the Austronesians arrived and bought Panay from the Ati.

The Ati-atihan festival also places the Ati at the center of public performance. The festival is named in their honor, and the sources state that it commemorates the first appearance of the Roman Catholic Church and the Spaniards in the province of Aklan. According to oral tradition, the Ati helped the Spaniards conquer the native Visayans and received a statue of the Santo Niño as a reward. In 1967, a tribe from the Ati-atihan festival was invited to Iloilo when the Santo Niño statue arrived there.

## Dance & theatre

The sources document festival dancing but no named Ati theatrical or dramatic tradition. In Dinagyang, performers paint themselves to look supposedly like Ati, organize themselves into *tribus*, and dance with drums. The performance is linked to the arrival of the Austronesians, the purchase of Panay from the Ati, and the arrival of the Santo Niño statue in Iloilo.

## Festivals & rituals

The **Ati-atihan festival** is named in honor of the Ati. The sources state that it is said to commemorate the first appearance of the Roman Catholic Church and the Spaniards in Aklan. An oral tradition says that the Ati helped the Spaniards conquer the native Visayans and were rewarded with a statue of the Santo Niño.

The **Dinagyang festival** takes place in Iloilo City on Panay. Its performances represent the Ati through painted bodies, organized *tribus*, dances, and drums. The festival celebrates the purchase of Panay and the arrival in Iloilo of the Santo Niño statue. A tribe from Ati-atihan was invited to Iloilo in 1967 to mark the statue’s arrival.

Another historical tradition concerns the Ten Bornean Datus and the Binirayan Festival. According to the legend, at the beginning of the 12th century, Datu Puti and Datu Sumakwel led people from Borneo to Panay in *balangays*. They met an Ati headman named Polpolan and his son Marikudo near the river Suaragan, on the southwest coast of Panay, in the place then known as Aninipay. The land was bartered for a necklace and one golden *salakot*. The hills were left to the Atis and the plains and rivers to the Malays. The meeting is commemorated through the Ati-atihan festival, although the source notes that some historians challenge the legend.

## Foodways

They state that Ati farmers on land granted through the national land reform program grow dragon fruit, banana, lemongrass, papaya, and vegetables.

## Oral tradition & literature

Ati-related oral tradition appears in accounts of the Ati-atihan festival and in the legends of the Ten Bornean Datus and the Binirayan Festival. The story of the meeting between the Ati headman Polpolan, his son Marikudo, and the Bornean migrants explains the division of hills, plains, and rivers on Panay and the later commemoration through Ati-atihan. The festival tradition also says that the Ati helped the Spaniards conquer the native Visayans and received the Santo Niño statue.

The source notes that the land-purchase legend is challenged by some historians.

## Language & religion

The Ati speak a distinct language known as Inati. A census in 1980 recorded about 1,500 speakers. Hiligaynon and Kinaray-a are also commonly used.

Ati religion includes a form of animism involving good and evil spirits. These are nature spirits associated with rivers, the sea, the sky, and mountains. They may cause disease or provide comfort. Ati people from Negros call such spirits *taglugar* or *tagapuyo*, terms glossed as “inhabiting a place.” Christianity has also been adopted in connection with reduced isolation and increased contact with “outsiders.”
