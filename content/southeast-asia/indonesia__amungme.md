## Overview

The Amung, also known as Amungme, Amungm, Amui, Amuy, Hamung, or Uhunduni, are a group of about 17,700 people living in the highlands of the Central Papua province of Indonesia. Most Amungme live in Mimika and Puncak, including the valleys of Noema, Tsinga, Hoeya, Bella, Alama, Aroanop, and Wa. A related group, the Damal people, lives in Beoga Valley, Puncak. The Amungme language is Uhunduni, which has several dialects and symbolic forms associated with sacred areas. Their folk culture is closely connected with animistic beliefs, shifting agriculture, hunting, gathering, ancestral land, and sacred mountains.

## Festivals & rituals

The traditional beliefs of the Amungme people are animistic. They did not have the idea of “gods” separate from nature; spirits and nature were understood as the same. The Amungme see themselves as the eldest child of God Nagawan Into, and therefore as conqueror and ruler of the world Amungsa.

## Foodways

The Amungme practice shifting agriculture and supplement their livelihood through hunting and gathering.

## Language & religion

The Amungme language is Uhunduni and has several dialects. Amung-kal is spoken mostly in the southern regions by the Amungme, while in the north it is called Damal-kal. The people also have symbolic languages called Aro-a-kal and Tebo-a-kal; Tebo-a-kal is spoken only in sacred areas. Their traditional beliefs are animistic, with spirits and nature understood as the same. They regard themselves as the eldest child of God Nagawan Into and consider the surrounding mountains sacred.
