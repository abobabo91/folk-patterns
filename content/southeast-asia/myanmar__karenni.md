## Overview

The Karenni, also known as the Kayah or Kayah Li, are a Karenic-speaking ethnic group native to Kayah State of Myanmar. The name Karenni means “red Karen,” while Karenni is also used to represent the groups native to the region of the so-called Kayah State. According to a 1983 census, these groups included Kayah, Geko, Geba, Lahwi, Bre, Manu-Manau, Yintale, Yinbaw, Bwe and Pa'O. The article also describes approximately 90000 people as Karenni or Kayah/Kayah Li. Karenni States were formerly a collection of small states, including Kantarawadi, Kyebogyi, Bawlake, Nammekon and Naungpale.

## Language & religion

Karenni or Red Karen, also known in Burmese as Kayah, is described as a Karen dialect continuum spoken by Kayah people in Burma. Eastern Kayah and Western Kayah are divergent varieties. Eastern Kayah was reported as spoken in Shadaw township of Kayah State and Langkho district of Shan State, while Western Kayah was spoken in Kayah State and Kayin State and also in Pekon township in southern Shan State. The sources name Northern and Southern dialects of Western Kayah, as well as Yintale, Kawyaw, Dawtama, Dawnnyjekhu, Sounglog, Chi Kwe and Wan Cheh. Manumanaw Karen did not yet have a standardized script in the account cited; Latin and Burmese-based writing systems were both used or developed. The sources mention Catholic missionaries, Catholics and Baptists.
