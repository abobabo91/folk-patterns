## Overview

The Orang Laut, also known as the Orang Suku Laut, are several seafaring ethnic groups and tribes living around Singapore, Peninsular Malaysia and the Indonesian Riau Islands. They are commonly identified with the Orang Seletar, also known as Orang Selat, from the Straits of Johor. The term can also refer to Malayic-speaking people living on coastal islands, including the Mergui Archipelago in Myanmar and Thailand. Their population in the 21st century is estimated at 420,000 people. They live and travel in boats and historically made their living from fishing, foraging and trading sea products.

## Material culture

### Architecture

 They state that communities settled in semi-nomadic fashion along rivers, coastlines and smaller islands, and that they live and travel in boats.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 The sources mention boats and the sampan panjang, identified as an Orang Laut racing boat.

## Music & performance

 They mention an Orang Laut racing boat, the sampan panjang, but provide no information about music associated with it.

## Dance & theatre

 The sources mention only a literary representation in Rudyard Kipling’s story *The Disturber of Traffic*, in which a character called Fenwick misrenders “Orang Laut” as “Orange-Lord.”

## Festivals & rituals

 They describe historical relationships with rulers, including prestigious titles and gifts given to Orang Laut leaders.

## Foodways

The sources state that the Orang Laut made their living from fishing, foraging and trading sea products.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They mention the group’s appearance in Rudyard Kipling’s story *The Disturber of Traffic*, where the term “Orang Laut” is corrected after being rendered as “Orange-Lord.”

## Language & religion

The Malay term *orang laut* literally means “sea peoples,” while *Orang Selat* means “Straits people.” The sources describe the Orang Laut as Malayic-speaking people in some uses of the term.
