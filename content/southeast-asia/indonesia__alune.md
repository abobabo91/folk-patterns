## Overview

The Alune live in western-central Seram Island in 27 villages. Their culture was historically tied to forest products, sago, shifting cultivation, bark-fibre clothing, necklaces, and carpentry. Important traditions include girls’ coming-of-age celebrations and the Hainuwele origin myth. Consumerism, political and religious unrest, and conflict in Indonesia have changed Alune life.

## Material culture

Alune material culture uses bark fibre, wood, sticks, palm leaves, bamboo, coconut shell, rattan, metal, and shell.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The main recorded textile is a patterned bark-fibre loincloth.

- **Bark-fibre loincloth** — Alune adults traditionally wore a short bark-fibre loincloth that reached above the knees and often had decorative patterns.
- **Tapa cloth** (*tapa*) — The Alune loincloth is compared with Polynesian tapa cloth, but the sources give no separate Alune term or production details.

### Clothing & dress

Traditional dress was light because of the humid environment, with special warrior clothing for men.

- **Bark-fibre loincloth** — Adults traditionally wore a short bark-fibre loincloth from the waist down.
- **Warrior clothing** — On special occasions, Alune men wore elaborate warrior clothes and carried long swords.
- **Women’s necklaces** — Women wore necklaces, but the sources do not give their name, design, or ritual use.

### Architecture

Ancestral Alune houses were large, elaborate wooden structures.

- **Ancestral house** — The houses were built from wood, sticks, and palm leaves.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded everyday objects were made mostly from plant materials, with some metal objects traded into Alune life.

- **Palm-sugar ladle** — A ladle for preparing palm sugar was made from coconut shell, wood, and rattan.
- **Wooden tongs** — The museum records tongs made of wood.
- **Bamboo strainer** — A strainer for preparing palm sugar was made of bamboo.
- **Bamboo palm-sugar ladle** — Another ladle for preparing palm sugar was made from bamboo, wood, and rattan.
- **Metal bracelet** — The catalogue records a traded bracelet made of metal.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Women wore necklaces, and museum records include shell and metal bracelets.

- **Necklace** — Alune women wore necklaces, but the sources give no further details.
- **Shell bracelet** — The museum catalogue records a female’s bracelet made of shell.
- **Traded metal bracelet** — The catalogue records a traded bracelet made of metal.

## Music & performance

The only recorded musical instrument is a bamboo slit gong.

- **Bamboo slit gong** — The museum catalogue records a slit gong made of bamboo, but gives no playing method or performance context.

## Dance & theatre

The sources do not document named Alune dances or theatre traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

Girls’ coming-of-age celebrations were important, but their details are not recorded.

- **Girls’ coming-of-age celebration** — The sources describe this as an important occasion but give no name, age threshold, rites, participants, or religious meaning.

## Foodways

Traditional subsistence centered on forest products, sago, and shifting cultivation.

- **Sago** — The Alune diet was based on the sago palm.
- **Shifting cultivation** — The Alune also practiced shifting cultivation.
- **Palm sugar preparation** (*palm sugar*) — Recorded tools show that palm sugar was prepared with ladles, tongs, and a strainer.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Hainuwele legend is an origin myth from Alune and Wemale folklore.

- **Hainuwele legend** (*Hainuwele*) — The legend was recorded by Adolf Ellegard Jensen during a 1937–8 expedition to the Maluku Islands.

## Language & religion

Alune is a Malayo-Polynesian language with several dialects, including the prestigious Rambatu variety.

- **Alune language** (*Alune*) — Alune is an Austronesian language of west Seram and is also known as Sapalewa or Patasiwa Alfoeren.
- **Sapalewa** — Sapalewa is another name for both the Alune people and their language.
- **Sapolewa** — Sapolewa is another name for the Alune people.
- **Patasiwa** — The Alune and Wemale originated in a common group called Patasiwa.
- **Patasiwa Alfoeren** — Patasiwa Alfoeren is another name for the Alune language.
