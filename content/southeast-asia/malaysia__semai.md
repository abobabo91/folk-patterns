## Overview

The Semai are the largest of the Senoi peoples, semi-sedentary horticulturalists of the interior Malay Peninsula. They are known for a strict ethic of non-violence and deference, and for creating their material culture almost entirely from natural fibers and plant matter. Their social world is built around community harmony, food sharing, and the resolution of disputes without coercion. Semai religion is animist, centered on a thunder deity and protective community bonds.

## Material culture

Semai material culture is made almost entirely from bamboo, cane, rattan, bark and wood, with no ceramics and almost no metal.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The Semai worked bark and plant fiber rather than woven cloth, creating barkcloth, cordage, basketry and plaited caps.

- **Barkcloth-beater** — A grooved wooden mallet used to pound bast into sheet cloth.
- **Barkcloth bag** — A long narrow container made from beaten bark rather than woven textile.
- **Leaf basket** — A plaited container made from plant leaves.
- **Cane fish trap** — A plaited cane trap used by women to catch fish by scooping.

### Clothing & dress

Semai dress was minimal and made of plant fiber and bark, with no tailored garments or distinct ceremonial costume.

- **Fibre waist belt** — A belt of fibre, grass and leaf worn by women.
- **Black twine girdle** — A girdle of black twine carrying a loosely attached wreath of leaves worn as ornament.
- **Bark head-dress** — A head-dress made of bark and string.

### Architecture

Semai settlements of 30 to 200 people live in wooden and bamboo dwellings with woven walls, thatched roofs, and a single open main hall divided only by a beaded curtain.

- **Household dwelling** — A wood and bamboo house with woven walls, palm-thatched roof, and interior sleeping in a single social space.
- **Beaded curtain** — A wooden-bead curtain screening the parents' chamber, which when drawn signals privacy and when open admits entry.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Semai everyday objects are bamboo, cane, wood and horn, dominated by the blowpipe complex and domestic tools for hunting, fishing and household work.

- **Bamboo quiver** — A bamboo tube bound with rattan and fitted with cane tubes to hold darts, closed by plaited basketry caps.
- **Blowgun dart** — A wooden projectile used with the blowpipe to hunt game.
- **Poison dart** — A reed dart tipped with poison used in hunting.
- **Fire-piston** — A wood tool with a horn receptacle used to start fire.
- **Wooden hair comb** — A carved wooden comb for grooming hair.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ornament is carved bamboo at the ears, nose and hair, with one brass wire armlet as the only metal piece recorded.

- **Cylindrical bamboo ear-ring** — An ear-ring of bamboo with incised linear decoration.
- **Bamboo nose stick** — A bamboo ornament incised with encircling lines worn in the nose.
- **Bamboo hair-pin** — A carved bamboo pin for holding hair.
- **Seed and teeth necklace** — A necklace strung from seeds and teeth.
- **Brass wire armlet** — A woman's armlet of brass wire, the single metal ornament recorded.

## Music & performance

Semai instruments are bamboo, wood and shell, with flutes forming the largest group, and the Sewang dance performed at life-cycle events lasting up to six days.

- **Bamboo flute** — A cylindrical bamboo flute with one end open above the embouchure, one end pierced by a single hole, some with incised decoration.
- **Jew's harp** — A jaw-plucked instrument made of wood, wood with vegetable-fibre string, or turtle-shell.
- **Bamboo horn** — A wind instrument made from bamboo.
- **Sewang** — A dance tradition performed at births, funerals, circumcisions, healings and other ritual occasions lasting three to six days.

## Dance & theatre

The Semai are known for the Sewang dance performed at life-cycle events, and for the becharaa, a public dispute assembly where the community works through conflict.

- **Sewang** — A dance tradition performed at births, funerals, circumcisions, healings of sickness and related occasions, lasting three to six days.
- **Becharaa** — A dispute assembly held at the headman's house where the community works through the causes and resolution of a quarrel over days.

## Festivals & rituals

The Semai have no annual festival cycle; their ritual life is occasional and life-cycle bound, centered on the Sewang dance.

- **Birth celebration** — An occasion marked by the Sewang dance, lasting up to six days.
- **Funeral** — An occasion marked by the Sewang dance, lasting up to six days.
- **Circumcision ceremony** — An occasion marked by the Sewang dance.
- **Healing of sickness** — An occasion marked by the Sewang dance, lasting up to six days.
- **Chuntah** — A weather rite performed during a storm in which a man collects rain in a bamboo container, gashes his skin, and lets his blood run into it to drive evil spirits away.

## Foodways

The Semai are semi-sedentary swidden farmers growing rice and cassava root, supplemented by hunting, fishing and raising chickens, goats and ducks, with food sharing governed by the principle of punan.

- **Swidden cultivation** — The practice of clearing forest with machetes, burning it, planting, and moving to a new plot after two or three harvests when the ground is worn.
- **Rice and cassava** — The staple crops grown and harvested by the Semai.
- **Women's fishing** — Women scoop fish with basket traps from streams and water sources.
- **Men's hunting** — Men hunt large game — deer, pigs, pythons, binturong — with blowpipes, poison darts and spears, shared across the community.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Semai use narrative to raise children, teaching them the virtue of mengalah and fear of spirits through stories of forest sprites and natural retribution.

- **Mambang stories** (*mambang*) — Stories of sprites and forest spirits that exact retribution when their sanctity is violated, used to teach children about consequences.
- **Tarlaid warning** (*Tarlaid*) — A shout used when a child is angry to warn that an act that is tarlaid can rouse the anger of spirits and bring on natural calamity.
- **Mengalah virtue** (*mengalah*) — The cherished virtue of giving in or giving way to others to keep the peace of the village, taught to children young.

## Language & religion

Semai is an Austroasiatic language closely related to Khmer, with an animist religion centered on a thunder deity and a worldview of the surrounding jungle as full of terrifying forces requiring community harmony for safety.

- **Enku** — A thunder deity who presides over the Semai spiritual world.
- **Thunder's headband** — A small eyeless snake associated with the thunder deity.
- **Nāga** — Huge subterranean dragons associated with rainbows that ravage villages during thunder-squalls.
- **Punan** — The taboo on making another person unhappy by imposing one's own wishes or denying theirs; refusing food or asking for more than one can spare is punan.

- *becharaa* — A dispute assembly where the community works through conflict and its resolution
- *mambang* — Sprites or forest spirits that exact retribution when their sanctity is violated
- *Tarlaid* — An act that can rouse the anger of spirits and bring on natural calamity
- *mengalah* — The virtue of giving in or giving way to others to keep village peace
- *punan* — The taboo on making another person unhappy by imposing one's wishes or denying theirs
- *Enku* — The thunder deity presiding over Semai spiritual life
- *Nāga* — Huge subterranean dragons associated with rainbows and destructive storms
- *Chuntah* — A weather rite performed during storms to drive evil spirits away
- *Senoi* — The indigenous grouping of the Malay Peninsula to which the Semai belong
