## Overview

The Sundanese are Indonesia's second-largest ethnic group, inhabiting the volcanic highlands and coastal plains of western Java. Their culture preserves an older, less courtly stratum of Javan civilization rooted in dry-field rice farming and village life. They are known for distinctive batik traditions, bamboo music, wooden puppet theatre, and rituals honoring ancestral and agricultural spirits, blended with Islam brought by 15th-16th century sultanates.

## Material culture

Sundanese material culture is centered on bamboo and handwoven textiles, with restrained aesthetic values emphasizing simplicity, harmony with the landscape, and honest exposure of natural materials.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Batik Cirebon from the north coast and Batik Priangan from the highlands each developed distinct styles reflecting their geography and influences.

- **Batik Cirebon** (*pesisir batik*) — Coastal batik from Cirebon's Trusmi village with bright palettes of deep indigo, cream, and red, absorbing Chinese influence from Ming-dynasty consorts of the sultans.
- **Megamendung** — The signature Cirebonese cloud motif in graduated bands of colour, traced to Chinese cloud-collar iconography representing the seven layers of heaven.
- **Batik Priangan** (*Batik Sunda*) — Inland batik from the Priangan highlands using softer ochre, indigo, and rust palettes with motifs drawn from mountain-forest environments.
- **Batik Garutan** — The Garut sub-tradition distinguished by a warm cream-yellow ground and diagonal lattice fills, often with floral and peacock motifs.
- **Checkered and striped wraps** (*kain poleng, kain kebat*) — Handwoven cotton wraps used in village dress and for ceremonial bundling of sacred objects.

### Clothing & dress

Everyday dress reflects village practicality with black cotton jackets and wraps; ceremonial dress for brides and grooms is elaborate with gilded crowns and ritual weapons.

- **Men's jacket and trousers** (*pangsi*) — A loose black cotton jacket and matching wide trousers worn with an iket kepala head-cloth, the foundation of traditional male village dress.
- **Bridal crown** (*siger*) — A tall gilded openwork diadem worn by Sundanese brides to frame the forehead, distinct from Javanese and Sumatran variants.
- **Women's blouse and wrap** (*kebaya, sinjang, samping*) — The kebaya blouse worn over a batik wrap and secured with a stagen sash forms traditional female village dress.
- **Groom's ritual jacket** (*jas takwa*) — A patterned jacket worn by grooms with a sinjang wrap and bendo headpiece, typically paired with a keris tucked at the back.
- **Baduy dress code** (*Baduy Dalam, Baduy Luar*) — Inner Baduy wear undyed handwoven white or indigo-and-white; outer Baduy wear all black, enforcing stricter dress standards than surrounding villages.

### Architecture

Traditional Sundanese houses are timber-frame stilt structures with bamboo walls and thatch roofs, each roof type named for an animal or object it resembles.

- **Soaring-bird house** (*Julang Ngapak*) — A roof with an overhanging gablet resembling outstretched wings, common around Tasikmalaya and Kuningan.
- **Rice barn** (*leuit*) — A small stilted structure with a steep thatched roof placed beside the house, treated as sacred and central to Seren Taun harvest ritual.
- **Yawning-rhinoceros house** (*Badak Heuay*) — One of several named roof types in Sundanese domestic architecture, each distinguished by a characteristic silhouette.
- **Baduy village compound** (*Kanekes*) — The Baduy village preserving archaic house-and-leuit compounds built entirely without nails, arranged in rows on ridge tops.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Bamboo and wood are the primary working materials; the kujang ritual blade is the signature Sundanese metal object.

- **Ritual blade** (*kujang*) — A small asymmetric blade with a curving spine and perforations, historically associated with the Pajajaran court and now a heraldic symbol of West Java.
- **Cooking tube** (*lodong*) — A bamboo vessel used for cooking, one of many household items crafted from bamboo.
- **Rice mortar and pestle** (*jubleg, halu*) — Wooden tools used for hulling rice, central to household food preparation.
- **Fish trap** (*bubu*) — A woven bamboo device used to catch fish in waterways and ponds.
- **Rice basket and steamer** (*boboko, aseupan*) — A woven-bamboo rice basket paired with a conical steamer for preparing rice.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Jewelry is concentrated in bridal regalia and is less elaborate than traditions in other Indonesian regions; gold is the primary precious metal.

- **Bridal crown** (*siger*) — A tall gilded openwork diadem worn by brides, often paired with ear ornaments and trembling hair-flowers.
- **Ear ornaments** (*sumping*) — Gold ear ornaments worn as part of elaborate bridal regalia.
- **Hair ornaments** (*kembang goyang*) — Trembling flower ornaments worn in the bride's hair during wedding ceremonies.
- **Ritual dagger** (*keris*) — A wavy-bladed dagger worn at the small of the back by grooms in wedding regalia and by pencak silat fighters.
- **Gold bangles** (*kalung*) — Simple gold bangles and ear studs common in daily wear among women.

## Music & performance

Sundanese music is defined by bamboo instruments and a distinctive pentatonic tuning, with the angklung ensemble recognized by UNESCO.

- **Bamboo rattle ensemble** (*angklung*) — Tuned bamboo rattles shaken by an ensemble where each player holds one or two notes; inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2010.
- **Courtly gamelan** (*gamelan degung*) — A smaller, more intimate Sundanese gamelan built around bonang kettle-gongs, saron metallophones, suling flute, and kendang drum.
- **Chamber music pair** (*kacapi suling*) — A boat-shaped plucked zither paired with a bamboo flute, used to accompany tembang Sunda sung poetry, especially the refined Cianjuran style.
- **Drum battery** (*rampak kendang*) — Multiple barrel drums played in unison, a vigorous ensemble form.
- **Bamboo xylophone** (*calung*) — A percussion instrument struck with mallets, made from tuned bamboo.

## Dance & theatre

Sundanese dance ranges from village forms to 20th-century choreographed styles; wayang golek puppet theatre with wooden rod-puppets is the dominant theatrical form.

- **Modern hip-dance** (*Jaipongan*) — Developed in Bandung in the 1970s from older village entertainments, featuring fast kendang drumming and vigorous hip and shoulder movements.
- **Peacock dance** (*Tari Merak*) — Choreographed by Raden Tjetje Somantri in the 1950s, dramatizing courting peafowl in a shimmering trained skirt.
- **Circumcision parade** (*Sisingaan*) — A Subang tradition in which a boy about to be circumcised is paraded on the shoulders of four men carrying a wooden lion effigy.
- **Wooden puppet theatre** (*wayang golek*) — Three-dimensional carved and painted wooden rod-puppets performed by a solo dalang with gamelan accompaniment, drawing on Hindu epics with a Sundanese and Islamic overlay.
- **Court-derived dances** (*Tari Ratu Graeni, Tari Dewi*) — Dances showing Mataram Javanese influence, performed in formal contexts.

## Festivals & rituals

The agricultural and Islamic calendars structure Sundanese ritual life, with the rice harvest thanksgiving Seren Taun central to village identity.

- **Rice harvest thanksgiving** (*Seren Taun*) — An annual celebration in which sheaves of rice are ritually carried into leuit barns with offerings to the rice goddess Nyai Pohaci Sanghyang Asri.
- **Pre-Ramadan feast** (*Munggahan, Cucurak*) — Communal feasts held with extended family in the days before Ramadan begins, Munggahan in Priangan and Cucurak in Bogor.
- **Seventh-month prenatal bathing** (*Nujuh Bulanan*) — A bathing ceremony performed during the seventh month of pregnancy.
- **Birth and circumcision rite** (*akekahan*) — A ceremonial animal sacrifice performed after birth (one goat for a girl, two for a boy) and circumcision (celebrated with the Sisingaan lion parade).
- **Wedding sequence** (*siraman, seserahan, akad nikah, saweran, huap lingkung, bakakak hayam*) — A fixed sequence including ritual bathing, gift presentation, Islamic vows, showering of coins and petals, mutual feeding with entwined arms, and tearing a grilled chicken in half.

## Foodways

Sundanese cooking is defined by freshness and the central role of raw vegetables, unlike any other Indonesian cuisine.

- **Raw vegetable plate** (*lalab*) — A plate of raw cucumber, basil, eggplant, papaya leaf, and bitter melon eaten with sambal terasi shrimp-paste chili, central to Sundanese meals.
- **Packed rice portion** (*nasi timbel*) — Steamed rice wrapped in banana leaf for portability and freshness.
- **Raw-vegetable salad** (*karedok*) — A raw-vegetable salad dressed in raw-peanut sauce.
- **Banana-leaf parcels** (*pepes, pais*) — Spiced fillings of fish, meat, tofu, or vegetables wrapped in banana leaf and steamed then grilled.
- **Fermented peanut-cake** (*oncom*) — A peanut-press-cake fermented with orange Neurospora mold, a signature Sundanese ingredient without a real counterpart elsewhere in Java.

## Oral tradition & literature

The core oral form is pantun Sunda, long sung narrative poems chanted through the night by a juru pantun accompanying himself on the kacapi zither.

- **Enchanted monkey tale** (*Lutung Kasarung*) — A legendary cycle about a black monkey who is a disguised prince, a standard repertoire piece of pantun Sunda.
- **Mount formation legend** (*Sangkuriang*) — An origin story in which a spurned son unwittingly courts his own mother and, when refused, kicks over the boat he was building, forming Mount Tangkuban Perahu.
- **King cycle** (*Siliwangi*) — Sung narratives recounting the deeds of King Siliwangi and the fall of the Pajajaran kingdom.
- **Old Sundanese didactic text** (*Sanghyang siksakanda ng karesian*) — A 1518 manuscript in Old Sundanese preserving Hindu-Buddhist and pre-Islamic moral instruction.
- **Sundanese literary magazine** (*Manglé*) — A magazine publishing Sundanese literature since 1957, carrying modern novels and poetry.

## Language & religion

Sundanese is the second-largest regional language in Indonesia; the majority are Sunni Muslims, with pre-Islamic animism surviving in particular villages.

- **Regional dialects** (*Priangan, Banten, Bogor, Cirebon*) — Major Sundanese dialects, with Priangan regarded as the most refined, particularly around Cianjur.
- **Archaic Baduy speech** (*undak-usuk basa*) — The Baduy speak an archaic form of Sundanese that predates the borrowing of Javanese-style speech levels marking rank.
- **Old script revival** (*Aksara Sunda Kuno*) — The historical Sundanese script used from the 14th to 18th centuries, now undergoing state-sponsored revival and appearing on street signs in Bandung and other cities.
- **Pre-Islamic beliefs** (*Sunda Wiwitan*) — Animist reverence for ancestral karuhun spirits, the rice goddess Nyai Pohaci Sanghyang Asri, and the supreme Sang Hyang Kersa, surviving among the Baduy and in specific villages.
- **Traditional villages** (*Kanekes, Kampung Naga, Cigugur*) — Communities where pre-Islamic Sunda Wiwitan beliefs and practices continue alongside Islam.

## Glossary

- *Tatar Sunda* — Historical name for the Sundanese heartland; also called Pasundan
- *Nyai Pohaci Sanghyang Asri* — The rice goddess venerated in Sundanese animist belief
- *pesisir* — Coastal; refers to north-coast batik traditions
- *megamendung* — Layered clouds; the signature Cirebonese batik motif
- *iket kepala* — Square head-cloth folded into a distinctive Sundanese men's headwrap
- *kebaya* — A fitted blouse worn by women over a batik wrap
- *siger* — A tall gilded bridal crown worn by Sundanese brides
- *kujang* — A small asymmetric ritual blade with curving spine and perforations
- *leuit* — A small stilted rice barn with steep thatched roof, treated as sacred
- *angklung* — A set of tuned bamboo rattles shaken to sound single pitches in ensemble
- *kacapi* — A boat-shaped plucked zither with wire strings used in chamber music
- *suling* — A bamboo flute used in Sundanese music
- *wayang golek* — Three-dimensional carved wooden rod-puppets performed by a solo dalang
- *Seren Taun* — The annual rice harvest thanksgiving ritual offering to the rice goddess
- *lalab* — A plate of raw vegetables eaten with shrimp-paste chili; central to Sundanese meals
- *pantun Sunda* — Long sung narrative poems chanted through the night by a juru pantun
- *oncom* — A fermented peanut-press-cake with orange mold, a signature Sundanese ingredient
- *Baduy Dalam* — The inner Baduy community adhering to strict traditional dress and customs
- *karuhun* — Ancestral spirits revered in Sunda Wiwitan animist belief
