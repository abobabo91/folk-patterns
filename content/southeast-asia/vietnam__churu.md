## Overview

The Churu, also called Chru, are a Chams-related ethnic group living mainly in Lâm Đồng and Bình Thuận provinces of Central Vietnam. Their language, Chru, is a Malayo-Polynesian and Chamic language spoken especially in southern Lâm Đồng Province, including Đơn Dương District, and in Ninh Thuận Province. The Churu population was 23,242 in 2019. Some villages have close ties with the Kaho people and speak Koho fluently.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Weaving** — The sources state that the Churu adopted economic innovations from the Chams in weaving in 1907.

**Pottery making** — Churu ancestors are described as bringing pottery making associated with the Chams when they moved to a new land.

**Plowing** — The Churu adopted innovations from the Chams in plowing in 1907. This concerns agricultural practice rather than a documented textile or pattern tradition.

### Architecture

They do mention irrigation structures: dams, large ditches, and auxiliary ditches leading to the fields of families, clans, and villages. Retention dams are built with soil, stones, and wood to block streams or tributaries and regulate water for irrigation.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Churu pottery making is associated in the sources with the Chams. Metalworking and woodworking were practiced to produce agricultural tools, including *lơngar* plows and *sơkăm* harrows. Water buffaloes, cattle, and horses supplied draft power and manure as well as serving in sacrificial rites.

## Music & performance

The largest folk festival named is Bok Bơmung, described as a festival in a temple rather than through its music or performances.

## Festivals & rituals

Bok Bơmung, described as a festival in a temple, is the biggest folk festival of the Churu. It is held in the second month of the lunar Year and lasts three days. The traditional religion is animism, with deities divided into two groupings: Yàng, the land deities, and Pô, the sky deities.

The sources also connect animals with ritual practice. Water buffaloes, cattle, and horses are used for sacrificial rites, as well as for agricultural work and manure.

Islam was first recorded among the Churu during the Katip Sumat uprising in 1833–4. Sumat, described as a Muslim Cham leader, had visited Kelantan; after his return, Islam was introduced to the Churu and Jarai peoples and contributed to the Jihad movement against Đại Việt. A number of Churu villages converted to Christianity in the 1950s with the arrival of missionaries.

## Foodways

The Churu are mainly residents of wet-rice cultivation. Their agricultural system includes regularly repaired and upgraded dams and ditches that store and regulate irrigation water. Livestock support farming through draft power and manure.

Churu men fish by pounding tree roots containing poisonous resins and mixing them with spring water. During idle farming months, men hunt in the forest using poison arrows, traps, and crossbows. Animals named in the sources include pig, deer, monkey, weasel, and other small animals. Collective hunting was organized across villages but is no longer popular today.

Women traditionally gather while carrying Austroasiatic baskets on their backs. Wild vegetables and field vegetables form the main part of the dishes; bamboo shoots and some wild fruits are also commonly used. *Dioscorea hamiltonii*, identified in the source with the Vietnamese name *củ mài*, is used as a main food source when crops fail. Other collected forest products include *Auricularia auricula-judae*, mushrooms, honey, and *Wurfbainia villosa*.

## Language & religion

Chru is a Chamic language of Vietnam and belongs to the Malayo-Polynesian language family. It is also called Cru, Kru, Chrau Hma, Chu, Chu Ru, Churu, Cho Ru, and Choru. It is spoken by the Churu in southern Lâm Đồng Province, especially in Đơn Dương District, and in Ninh Thuận Province. Use of Chru is declining as native speakers are generally bilingual in Vietnamese, which is used in official and public settings such as schools. Some Churu villages speak Koho fluently and may prefer Koho to Chru.

Chru is an analytic, or isolating, language without morphological marking of case, gender, number, or tense. It has subject-verb-object word order. Its traditional religion is animism, with Yàng land deities and Pô sky deities. Islam and Christianity are also documented among Churu communities.
