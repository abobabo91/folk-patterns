## Overview

The Ngada people, also called *Ata Ngada*, Ngadha, Nad'a, Nga'da, Bajawa, or Rokka, are an ethnic group inhabiting the central part of Flores Island, especially in Ngada Regency. They are the indigenous inhabitants of the Bajawa region and mostly reside in Ngada Regency, although nearby communities including the Riung, Rongga, Nage, Keo, and Palue are sometimes considered related population groups. Their population is estimated at around 155,000. The Ngada belong to the Bima–Sumba-speaking group, while their language belongs to the Austronesian family. Their folk-culture profile includes agriculture, weaving, hunting, gathering, livestock farming, metalworking, and a matrilineal family system. Coastal inhabitants have been influenced by Malay, Bimanese, Buginese, and Makassarese culture.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

Weaving crafts are widespread among the Ngada.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Some Ngada are involved in metalworking.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that meat is consumed during festive occasions. The majority of the Ngada adhere to Roman Catholicism, while some mountain dwellers maintain traditional beliefs.

## Foodways

Ngada agriculture traditionally includes rice, corn, millet, beans, squash, peanuts, vegetables, and spices. Their main commodities are primarily plant-based foods, while meat is consumed during festive occasions.

## Language & religion

The native language of the Ngada people is the Ngada language, including the Namut–Nginamanu dialect chain. It belongs to the Austronesian language family and is related to languages spoken on Flores Island and its surroundings, including Nage–Kéo, Ende, Lio, and Palue, as well as the Manggarai language. Ngadha is also spelled Ngada, Ngadʼa, or Ngaʼda. It is one of six languages spoken in the central stretch of Flores: Ngadha, Nage, Keo, Ende, Lio, and Palu'e. These form the proposed Central Flores group of the Sumba–Flores languages.

The language has a retroflex implosive /ᶑ /. It uses no prefixes or suffixes, and its sound system includes glottal stops, velar fricatives, and a short trill. In the 20th century, the missionary Paul Arndt conducted research in the region. Most Ngada adhere to Roman Catholicism, while some mountain dwellers maintain traditional beliefs.
