## Overview

The Kedayan live mainly in Brunei and nearby parts of Malaysian Borneo. Their life is associated with agriculture, Islam, Malay culture, medicinal plants, traditional medicines and spiritual healing. The sources give detailed records of some household, hunting and adornment objects. They provide little information about buildings, textiles, music, dance, food or oral traditions.

## Material culture

The recorded material culture includes palm-leaf and rattan household objects, bamboo blowpipe equipment, and bead, bronze, silver and cloth adornment.

### Textile & pattern traditions

The sources do not document a Kedayan textile or pattern tradition.

### Clothing & dress

The sources do not describe everyday or ceremonial clothing.

- **Woman’s hair ornament** — This ornament is made of bronze, cotton cloth, string and beads.
- **Woman’s necklace** — This necklace is made of glass beads and possibly nuts and seeds.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Kedayan houses, settlements or named building types.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Recorded household and hunting objects use palm leaf, rattan, bamboo, wood, bark, beads and metal.

- **Dish cover** — The dish cover is made of palm leaf.
- **Rice basket** — The rice basket is made of rattan.
- **Blowpipe-dart quiver** (*selongan*) — The quiver is made from telang bamboo with kusah rattan binding, nato wood and kilid bark string, and it has beads, a tooth, a carved hook and a metal chain.
- **Blowpipe dart** (*langan*) — The darts are kept in a squirrel-skin pocket and have sago-pith flights called ra’o.
- **Poisoned dart tip** (*parir*) — The darts are tipped with poison called parir.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Recorded adornment includes bead, bronze, cloth, cane and silver objects.

- **Childbirth charm** — This woman’s charm is associated with childbirth and the ankle and is made of beads.
- **Spiral silver bracelet** — Each bracelet is dark cane wound with a thin silver band and covered partly by punched silver plate with floral decoration.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do not provide a Kedayan festival calendar or describe named ceremonies.

- **Childbirth charm practice** — A museum record links one woman’s bead charm with childbirth and the ankle, but gives no further details.
- **Medicinal plant practice** — Kedayans are known for cultivating medicinal plants used to treat ailments and make tonics.
- **Spiritual healing** — The sources describe Kedayans as experts in various spiritual healings.

## Language & religion

Kedayan is close to Brunei Malay, and most Kedayans have adopted Islam and Malay culture.

- **Forest** (*hutan*) — Kedayan hutan means “forest,” while Brunei Malay uses utan.
- **House** (*umah*) — Kedayan umah comes from Malay rumah because Kedayan does not have the /r/ sound.
- **Kedayan and Banjar vocabulary** (*Banjar*) — The Banjar language in Kutai shares more than 90% of its vocabulary with Kedayan.

## Glossary

- *selongan* — blowpipe-dart quiver
- *telang* — bamboo used for the quiver
- *kusah* — rattan binding
- *nato* — wood used in the quiver
- *kilid* — tree whose bark provides string
- *langan* — blowpipe dart
- *parir* — poison on dart tips
- *hutan* — forest in Kedayan
- *utan* — forest in Brunei Malay
- *rumah* — Malay word for house
- *umah* — Kedayan word for house
- *Banjar* — language of the Banjar people
- *Kedayan* — name of the ethnic group and language
- *Kadayan* — another name for the Kedayan
- *Kadaian* — another name for the Kedayan
- *Kadyan* — another name for the Kedayan
