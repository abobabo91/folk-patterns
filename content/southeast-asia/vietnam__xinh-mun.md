## Overview

The Ksingmul, also known as Xinh Mul, Con Pua, Puộc, and Pụa, are an ethnic group in Vietnam and Laos. In Vietnam, they live primarily in the northwest, especially in the provinces of Sơn La and Lai Châu. The group numbers approximately 29,503 people, including 21,939 in Vietnam and 3,164 in Laos. Their language belongs to the Khmuic languages group of the Mon–Khmer language family; Khmuic languages are Austro-Asiatic. The available source identifies several festivals associated with the Ksingmul: Muong A Ma, Ksaisatip, and Mạ Ma. No further information on their material culture, performance traditions, foodways, religious practices, or historical significance is provided by the sources used.

## Festivals & rituals

The Ksingmul celebrate several festivals, including Muong A Ma, Ksaisatip, and Mạ Ma.

## Language & religion

The Ksingmul speak the Ksingmul language, which is a Khmuic language. The Khmuic languages are Austro-Asiatic and belong to the Mon–Khmer language family. The group is divided linguistically into three subgroups: the Kháng of Vietnam, the Phong-Kniang of Laos, and the Puoc of Vietnam and Laos.
