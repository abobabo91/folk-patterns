## Overview

The Lampung live mainly in Lampung and nearby parts of southern Sumatra and Banten. Society is conventionally divided into coastal Saibatin Lampungs and interior Pepadun Lampungs. Their culture includes ceremonial textiles, distinctive values, dialects, and the Rencong script. Kris are also associated with Lampung and South Sumatra.

## Material culture

Lampung material culture is best documented through gold-decorated textiles, ceremonial banners, beads, shells, and kris.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Lampung ceremonial textiles use cotton, gold-wrapped thread, beads, shells, and other bright decorations.

- **Woman’s ceremonial skirt** (*tapis*) — This nineteenth-century skirt uses cotton with gold-wrapped thread, silk paillettes, metal, paillettes, or lead.
- **Ceremonial textile** (*tampan*) — A nineteenth-century tampan is recorded in cotton as a Lampung ceremonial textile.
- **Ceremonial banner** (*palepai*) — Palepai banners use materials such as glass and ceramic beads, fiber, cloth, nassa shells, cotton, and metallic-wrapped yarns.
- **Lampung motif vocabulary** — Named motifs include temple angles, suns, sun wheels, birds, mica mirrors, angles, loops, and squares.

### Clothing & dress

Lampung ceremonial clothing includes decorated sarongs and women’s skirts.

- **Ceremonial clothing** (*högtidsklädnad*) — Museum records use this term for Lampung ceremonial clothing.
- **Ceremonial skirt** (*tapis*) — Women’s ceremonial skirts called tapis are recorded from the nineteenth century.
- **Ceremonial sarong** (*sarong*) — One sarong is yellow, blue, and white cotton with gold embroidery and woven suns, sun wheels, and birds.

### Architecture

The sources do not describe Lampung architecture.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mainly document Lampung-linked kris and do not describe ceramics or ordinary household tools.

- **Small kris** (*kris*) — A small South Sumatran kris from Lampung has an unusual ganja, described as the top of the blade.
- **Bird-hilted kris** (*kris*) — An early Sumatran kris has a bird or Garuda hilt, a damaskered steel blade, a wooden handle, brass fittings, and a Palembang-Lampung-style sheath.
- **Jawa Demam kris** (*kris*) — A Lampung kris has a Jawa Demam stylized bird hilt and a blade with five ondulations.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources do not describe Lampung jewelry or body-adornment practices.

- **Mica mirrors** — Mica mirrors appear as decorations on Lampung textiles.
- **Paillettes** — Paillettes are recorded as materials or decorations on Lampung textiles.
- **Filigree** — Filigree appears on brass fittings associated with a kris.

## Music & performance

The sources do not describe Lampung music, but they record adi-adi poems expressing Lampung values.

- **Value poems** (*adi-adi*) — Adi-adi are poems expressing characteristic Lampung values.

## Festivals & rituals

The profile records social values and community practices but no annual festivals or named life-cycle rites.

- **Welcoming visitors** (*Nemui-Nyimah*) — Nemui-Nyimah means keeping contact by visiting one another and gladly receiving guests.
- **Communal fellowship** (*Nengah-Nyampur*) — Nengah-Nyampur means active participation in communal fellowship rather than individualism.
- **Mutual assistance** (*Sakai-Sambaian*) — Sakai-Sambaian means giving mutual assistance in the community.
- **Title and character** (*Juluk-Adok*) — Juluk-Adok concerns having a personality that fits one’s customary title.
- **Sense of honor** (*Piil-Pusanggiri*) — Piil-Pusanggiri describes embarrassment at doing despised work in relation to religion and self-esteem.

## Foodways

The sources do not describe Lampung foods, cooking, drinks, or dietary rules.

## Oral tradition & literature

Lampung history and values are preserved in chronicles, a manuscript, and adi-adi poems.

- **Philosophical manuscript** (*Kuntara Raja Niti*) — The Kuntara Raja Niti manuscript is identified as the source containing Lampung philosophical life and values.
- **Value poems** (*adi-adi*) — Adi-adi poems express Piil-Pusanggiri, Juluk-Adok, Nemui-Nyimah, Nengah-Nyampur, and Sakai-Sambaian.
- **Sekala Brak lineage** (*Sekala Brak*) — The sources state that the Lampung people’s original lineage came from the Sekala Brak kingdom.
- **Early place name** (*Nampang*) — Seventh-century accounts associate Nampang with a place in the south.
- **Tolang Pohwang kingdom** (*Tolang Pohwang*) — The Tolang Pohwang kingdom is said to have covered present-day Tulang Bawang Regency and areas along the Tulang Bawang river.

## Language & religion

Lampung is an Austronesian language cluster with two major dialects and a traditional script called Rencong.

- **Lampung language** (*Lampung*) — Lampung is an Austronesian language or dialect cluster in the Malayo-Polynesian branch.
- **Lampung Api dialect** (*Api*) — Api is one of the two major Lampung dialects and is linked to several Saibatin and Pepadun regions.
- **Lampung Nyo dialect** (*Nyo*) — Nyo is one of the two major Lampung dialects and is used by Abung and Tulangbawang communities.
- **Rencong script** (*Rencong*) — Rencong is the traditional Lampung script, also called had Lampung or aksara lampung.
- **Lampung script** (*aksara lampung*) — Aksara lampung is described as an abugida with 20 main characters and 13 diacritics.
