## Overview

The Lu, also called Tai Lue or Tai Lü, are a Tai ethnic group living in China, Laos, Thailand, Burma and Vietnam. In Laos, they are part of a wider Southeast Asian population speaking Tai Lue, a Southwestern Tai language. Tai Lue is spoken by about 700,000 people, including 134,000 in Laos. The group is also known as Xishuangbanna Dai, Sipsongpanna Tailurian and Tai Sipsongpanna. In China, the Lu are officially classified as part of the Dai ethnic group, while in Vietnam they are recognised as the Lự ethnic minority. Their documented folk-culture significance includes the Tai Lue language, its writing systems, the song form *khap lue*, and the bamboo free-reed instrument *pi mae*.

## Music & performance

The Tai Lue people sing *khap lue* and play *pi mae*, a free-reed wind bamboo instrument.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that the religion of the Vietnamese Lu is Theravada Buddhism.

## Language & religion

Tai Lue is a Southwestern Tai language of the Lu people, spoken by about 700,000 people in Southeast Asia. It is closely related to Kham Mueang, also known as Northern Thai, and has limited intelligibility with Shan and Tai Nua. Tai Lue is written in the Fak Kham script, the Tham script, and New Tai Lue. Fak Kham was also used in Kengtung, Northern Thailand and Northern Laos centuries ago. The Tham script was reformed in the 1950s and is readable by most people in Burma, Laos, Thailand and Vietnam. New Tai Lue is a modernization of the Lanna alphabet. The sources state that the religion of the Vietnamese Lu is Theravada Buddhism.
