## Overview

The Krashovani (Romanian: Carașoveni; Croatian: Krašovani) are a Croat community in Carașova and Lupac, in Caraș-Severin County within Romanian Banat. They are Catholic by faith and speak a Torlakian dialect, which they identify as Croatian. Their settlements include Carașova, Nermed, Iabalcea, Clocotici, Rafnic, Vodnic and Lupac. Their ancestors first settled Carașova in the 13th and 14th centuries from northwestern Bosnia. The community formed in the northern plateau of the Caraș river, in seven villages. In 1992, their number was recorded as 2,775. Their history, language, settlement pattern and contested origins make them a distinct Croat community in Romania.

## Language & religion

The Krashovani adhere to the Catholic Church and identify their language as Croatian. Their dialect is regarded as a sub-dialect of the Torlak dialect, a transitional dialect spoken in southeastern Serbia, westernmost Bulgaria and northeastern Macedonia. In Romanian, the community is commonly known as Carașoveni, with variants including Carșoveni, Cârșoveni, Cotcoreți and Cocoși. In Croatian, it is commonly known as Krašovani, with variants including Karašovani, Krašovanje, Karaševci and Koroševci.

The community’s origins are disputed in the sources. Their ancestors are described as having settled Carașova from northwestern Bosnia in the 13th and 14th centuries. Jovan Cvijić concluded that they were “very old settlers with origin in Crna Reka who were Catholicised,” while Stanko Žuljić claimed that their origin was in Turopolje, in Croatia. Some Bulgarian scientists considered the Carașoveni Bulgarians in the first half of the 20th century, partly because they regarded Torlakian speakers as ethnically Bulgarians.
