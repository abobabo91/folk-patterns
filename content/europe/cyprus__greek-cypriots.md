## Overview

Greek Cypriots are the ethnic Greek population of Cyprus and form the island’s largest ethnolinguistic community. According to the 2023 census, 719,252 respondents recorded their ethnicity as Greek, representing almost 99% of 737,196 Cypriot citizens and over 77.9% of the 923,381 total residents of the area controlled by the Republic of Cyprus. Greek Cypriots are concentrated in Cyprus, while substantial diaspora communities live in Greece and the United Kingdom. Their everyday language is Cypriot Greek, a dialect of Modern Greek. Greek Cypriot identity has been shaped by Greek Orthodox Christianity, the Byzantine era, Ottoman and British rule, and the division of Cyprus following the 1974 coup, Turkish invasion, and occupation of the northern part of the island.

### Textile & pattern traditions

## Music & performance

The sources name several modern Greek Cypriot musicians and composers, including Alkinoos Ioannidis, Anna Vissi, George Kallis, Manos Loïzos, Marios Tokas, Michalis Hatzigiannis, Mihalis Violaris, and George Michael. They also identify Anthony Skordi, Michael Cacoyannis, George Eugeniou, and Sotiris Moustakas as actors or cinema and theatre figures.

## Dance & theatre

George Eugeniou is identified as the founder and artistic director of Art Theatre, London, while Michael Cacoyannis is identified as a cinema director and Sotiris Moustakas as an actor.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe Greek Orthodox Christianity as an integral part of Greek Cypriot identity and state that the majority of Greek Cypriots are members of the Church of Cyprus, an autocephalous Greek Orthodox Church within Orthodox Christianity. They also describe the role of the Archbishop of the Church of Cyprus, including Makarios III’s election as the first president of the Republic of Cyprus in 1960.

## Foodways

Cypriot cuisine was shaped by spices and herbs made common through extensive trade links within the Ottoman Empire. The names of many dishes reflected the sources of their ingredients from many lands. Coffee houses spread throughout the island into all major towns and countless villages.

## Oral tradition & literature

The epic *Cypria*, dating to the 7th century BC, may have originated in Cyprus. Stasinos is identified as its poet and author. The sources also identify Leontios Machairas and Georgios Boustronios as 15th-century historians, and Vasilis Michaelides as a poet.

## Language & religion

The everyday language of Greek Cypriots is Cypriot Greek, a dialect of Modern Greek. It shares characteristics with varieties of Crete, the Dodecanese and Chios, and Asia Minor. Greek Cypriots are generally educated in Standard Modern Greek, although they tend to speak it with an accent and preserve some Greek Cypriot grammar. Most Greek Cypriots belong to the Church of Cyprus, an autocephalous Greek Orthodox Church. The Byzantine era profoundly shaped Greek Cypriot culture, and Greek Orthodox Christianity became an integral part of Greek Cypriot identity. Under Ottoman rule, the Greek Cypriot community was administered through the millet system on behalf of Constantinople by the Archbishop of the Church of Cyprus.
