## Overview

Hungarian Slovenes are an autochthonous ethnic and linguistic Slovene minority living in Hungary. The largest group is the *Rába Slovenes* (*porabski Slovenci*, also called *vogrski Slovenci*, *bákerski Slovenci*, and *porábski Slovenci* in dialect), who live in the Rába Valley between Szentgotthárd and the borders with Slovenia and Austria. They speak the Prekmurje Slovene dialect. Outside the Rába Valley, Slovenes mainly live in the Szombathely region and in Budapest. In 2001, around 5,000 Slovenes lived in Hungary; around 3,000 remained in their original settlement zone in western Vas County. Their history in the western Carpathian basin reaches back at least to the 6th century AD. Their folk-culture record includes local religious life, wedding customs, cuisine, language, and communal traditions.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Knitted wedding robe** — In the wedding custom of Tarany and in traditions of the Rába Valley, the guest caller wears a robe decorated with knitting.

**Hedgehog-fur wedding stick** — The guest caller carries a stick with hedgehog fur wrapped around its bottom.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name no motifs.

### Clothing & dress

The sources document only ceremonial clothing connected with the wedding guest caller. In both Tarany and the Rába Valley, he wears a robe decorated with knitting and carries a stick with hedgehog fur wrapped around its bottom.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The wedding guest caller’s stick is the only everyday object described, and the source specifies only that hedgehog fur is wrapped around its bottom.

## Festivals & rituals

Almost all Rába Slovenes are Roman Catholic, and religion plays an important role in their local traditions and communal life. The sources describe customs connected with weddings, deathbeds, vigils, and funerals. In Tarany, a wedding guest caller remains part of local Slovene tradition; he wears a knitted robe and carries a stick with hedgehog fur wrapped around its bottom. The customs at the deathbed, the vigil, and funerals are described as similar to those of the Rába Valley and the Prekmurje region in Slovenia. The descendants of Slovenes in Somogy do not observe the typical Hungarian Easter Monday custom in which men and boys douse women and girls with Eau de Cologne.

## Foodways

The sources identify local cuisine as the most evident surviving indication of the historic presence of Slovenes in Somogy County. They provide no dish names, ingredients, staple grains, cooking methods, ceremonial foods, beverage traditions, or dietary rules. The sources therefore do not permit a more detailed account of Hungarian Slovene foodways.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources name several prominent Slovenes in Hungary connected with writing and scholarship: Mihály Bakos, József Kossics, István Küzmics, and Ágoston Pável. Their listed occupations include priest, author, educator, writer, ethnologist, linguist, historian, poet, and teacher.

## Language & religion

Hungarian Slovenes speak a specific dialect of Slovene, the Prekmurje Slovene dialect, which is almost identical to the dialect spoken in the Prekmurje region of Slovenia. The traditional Magyar name for the Slovenes was *Vendek* or *Vends*, while in their own dialect they referred to themselves as *Slovenes* (*Slovenci*). During the last decades of the 19th century, and especially during the Horthy regime, “Wends” was used to emphasize a difference between Hungarian Slovenes and other Slovenes. Almost all Rába Slovenes are Roman Catholic.
