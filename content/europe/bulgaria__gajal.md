## Overview

Gajals or Gadzhals are a Turkic ethnic group living mainly in the Eastern Balkans and Turkey. They mainly settle in the northeastern Ludogorie region of Bulgaria and in Eastern Thrace. Balkan-Gagauz is their main language, and their total number is about 300,000 people, including about 20,000 native speakers. Because of their Turkic language and Islamic faith, they are usually ranked as a subgroup among Turks. They are believed to be descendants of Pechenegs and Cumans and are closely related to the Gagauz people. The sources describe disagreement over their origins and over whether the term Gajal is used by the people themselves.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources mention Islamic faith and a claim that an Alevi population of Balbunar, Silistra and Varna gave sacrifice for St. Elijah.

## Foodways

The historical discussion mentions that an Alevi population of Balbunar, Silistra and Varna were said to drink and not hide their faces.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do record historical statements and theories about the group in works including Lehçe-i Osmani, The Yearbook of the Varna State High School, and The Old Bulgarian Population in Northeastern Bulgaria.

## Language & religion

Gajals are described as a Turkic ethnic group whose main language is Balkan-Gagauz. The sources also describe Deliorman Turks as having a different language and dialect, while Stefan Bobchev argued that this was a dialect difference rather than evidence of racial or tribal difference. Gajals are associated with the Islamic faith and are usually ranked as a subgroup among Turks for that reason and because of their Turkic language. The sources also discuss an Alevi population of Balbunar, Silistra and Varna.
