## Overview

Poles, also called the Polish people, are a West Slavic ethnic group and a nation associated with Poland in Central Europe. The Polish nation is defined in the Constitution of the Republic of Poland as comprising all citizens of Poland, regardless of heritage or ethnicity. The population of self-declared Poles in Poland was estimated at 37,394,000 out of an overall population of 38,512,000 in the 2011 census, including 36,522,000 who declared Polish alone. The largest urban concentrations are in the Warsaw metropolitan area and the Katowice urban area. Polish developed from the West Slavic and Lechitic linguistic environment and is the sole official language of the Republic of Poland. Polish culture includes regional cuisines, folklore, dialects, costumes, music, dances, papercutting, woodworking, needlework, and embroidery.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Stroje ludowe* are traditional Polish folk costumes. They commonly feature rich embroidery, vivid colours, beads, ribbons, and lace. Women’s clothing includes embroidered blouses, while men’s clothing often includes embroidered shirts and sashes.

**Łowicz garb** is a named regional costume tradition. The source describes it as one of the best-known regional forms of Polish dress.

**Goral clothing** is associated with the Goral people of the Tatra Mountains. The source identifies it as a regional costume.

**Kraków costume** is another well-known regional costume and is often considered Poland’s national dress.

Polish folk art includes colourful flower motifs, woodworking, papercutting, and needlework.

### Clothing & dress

Polish folk costumes are described as richly embroidered and vividly coloured, with beads, ribbons, and lace among their decorative elements. Women’s attire typically includes long skirts, aprons, embroidered blouses, corsets or vests, and headscarves or wreaths. Men’s outfits often include embroidered shirts, sashes, hats, and high boots. Regional forms include the Łowicz garb, Goral clothing from the Tatra Mountains, and the Kraków costume. The *rogatywka*, also called a *konfederatka*, is a type of hat that originated in Poland and is worn by the military.

## Music & performance

Traditional Polish music is described as having distinctive regional styles and using the fiddle, accordion, and clarinet. Highland bagpipe and fiddle music from the Tatra Mountains is particularly notable for its dynamic rhythms and expressive melodies. Poland’s classical music tradition is represented by the pianist and composer Frédéric Chopin, whose compositions remain central to the Romantic repertoire.

Latin songs and religious hymns, including *Gaude Mater Polonia* and *Bogurodzica*, were once chanted in places of worship and during festivities, although the source states that this tradition has faded. Sung poetry, disco polo, and jazz remain part of Poland’s musical identity. Jazz is supported by a tradition dating back to the mid-20th century, while hip-hop has become one of the most influential genres among younger audiences and is often connected with urban culture.

## Dance & theatre

Polish folk dances include the polonaise, mazurka, krakowiak, oberek, and kujawiak. They have varied rhythmic structures, tempos, and choreographic patterns. The polka also resonated with Polish dance traditions and was incorporated into local repertoires. Chopin helped popularise Polish dance tunes in Europe, while the Polish-American community popularised them in North America.

## Foodways

Polish meals are typically structured around breakfast, dinner, and supper. Breakfast is called *śniadanie*, dinner *obiad*, and supper *kolacja*. The largest meal of the day is dinner, while a second breakfast, *drugie śniadanie*, and evening snacks are also characteristic of Poland.

Popular everyday foods listed by the source include pork cutlets, *kotlet schabowy*, schnitzels, kielbasa sausage, potatoes, coleslaw, salads, soups, *barszcz*, tomato or meat broth, pierogi dumplings, and several types of bread, including kaiser rolls, rye bread, and bagels. ## Oral tradition & literature

The national epic of Poland is *Pan Tadeusz*, also known in English as *Master Thaddeus*, written by Adam Mickiewicz. Polish literature includes works by Joseph Conrad, who wrote in English, Stanisław Lem, and Andrzej Sapkowski. The source identifies *Heart of Darkness*, *Lord Jim*, *Solaris*, and *The Witcher* among the works associated with these writers.

The earliest examples of Polish literature date from the 10th and 11th centuries and were primarily written in Latin. Religious texts and chronicles, including *Gesta principum Polonorum* by Gallus Anonymus, provided an account of Poland’s early rulers. The Holy Cross Sermons are described as the oldest surviving prose texts in Polish, dating from the 14th century and preserved only in fragments. Saint Florian’s Psalter is a trilingual manuscript in Latin, Polish, and German, while the Bible of Queen Sophia is described as the first complete translation of the Bible into Polish. Polish-language literary writing expanded during the Renaissance through figures including Mikołaj Rey and Jan Kochanowski.

## Language & religion

Polish is a West Slavic language of the Lechitic group and the sole official language in the Republic of Poland. Its written form uses the Polish alphabet, based on the Latin alphabet with six additional diacritic marks, for a total of 32 letters. Polish has been influenced by Latin, German, and other languages. Nearly 97% of Poland’s citizens declare Polish as their mother tongue.

Polish is generally used uniformly throughout most of Poland, although regional dialects and a vernacular language coexist with standard Polish. Silesian is spoken in Upper Silesia, while Kashubian is widely spoken in historic Eastern Pomerania and has the status of a separate language. The Goral people of the mountainous south use their own nonstandard dialect, with distinctive accenting and intonation.

The majority of Poles adhere to Roman Catholicism.
