## Overview

Valencians are the native people of the Valencian Community in eastern Spain. The community is divided into Alicante, Valencia and Castellón, with Valencia as its capital. Valencians traditionally concentrated in fertile lowlands and localities along the Júcar, Turia, Segura and Vinalopó rivers, as well as in harbour cities associated with agricultural trade. Their native languages are Valencian, a Romance language also known as Catalan in about two-thirds of the territory, and Spanish in the rest; both are official. Valencian culture is represented in the sources through regional language varieties, historical literature, music, dance, festivals, cuisine and urban and coastal patterns of settlement. Since 2006, Valencians have been officially recognised in the Valencian Statute of Autonomy as a nationality “within the unity of the Spanish nation.”

## Material culture

### Architecture

 They do describe settlement geography: population traditionally concentrated in fertile cultivation and growing lowlands by the Júcar, Turia, Segura and Vinalopó rivers, and in harbour cities important to agricultural trade. Industrial activity and commerce also affected noncoastal cities including Alcoy, Elda, Ontinyent, Petrer, Villena and La Vall d’Uixó.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

 They do identify the *paella* as a large pan whose name is used for the rice dish cooked in it.

## Music & performance

Music in the Valencian Community is described as traditional and contemporary. Little is known about ancient music; prehistoric pictorial remains from Valencia and Alicante are identified as testimonies of musical activity. After the Muslim conquest of the Iberian Peninsula, the Córdoba musical tradition spread to Valencia and Xàtiva from the 9th century. The oldest preserved musical documents from the Land of Valencia are Christian, including filled epistles and the Song of the Sibyl, a liturgical drama of Catalan origin. The Mystery of Elx is another medieval liturgical drama.

The Land of Valencia has its own variety of *Jota* and a *dolçaina* tradition; the *dolçaina* is identified as a shawm. Brass bands are found in almost every village and originated in the 19th century. Religious and work songs are common, often with accompaniment, while *havaneres* are popular along the Valencian coast. *El Misteri d’Elx* is an old religious musical play dating to mediaeval times.

The contemporary sources describe *bakalao* as the dominant dance music in the club scene of the city and surrounding community in the 1980s. Techno remained prominent in the 1990s, with a heavier *schranz* influence from northern Europe emerging in the early 2000s. Minimal techno and trip-tek are also identified, alongside Valencian/Catalan rock and ska and newer urban, jazzy and funky music.

## Dance & theatre

Named Valencian dances include **Fandango of Albaida**, **El Ball del Danzants**, **El Ball dels Oficis**, **Los Alcides**, **Els Bastonets**, **Els Porrots**, **Paloteo of Requena** and **Valencian Jota**. The *Muixeranga* is described as a street festival and an important tradition originating in Valencia.

Theatrical and liturgical traditions include the **Song of the Sibyl**, a liturgical drama of Catalan origin, and **El Misteri d’Elx**, an old religious musical play dating back to mediaeval times.

## Festivals & rituals

The *Muixeranga* is identified as a street festival originating in Valencia. The sources also associate *Falles* with particular foods: *Figues albardaes* are traditional during Falles, and *bunyols* are typical of Falles. No date is supplied for the festival in the sources.

The Valencian musical sources describe religious and work songs as common and identify *El Misteri d’Elx* as an old religious musical play. The cuisine sources identify seasonal associations for some foods: *bunyols* are typical of Falles, while *gazpacho* is described as a quintessential summer dish.

## Foodways

Valencian cuisine is described as Mediterranean and based on vegetables, seafood and meat. It is especially known for rice dishes and citrus fruits. *Paella* originated near lake Albufera, a lagoon in eastern Spain, and is usually considered native to Albufera and Ribera, just south of Valencia. It has two main varieties, with chicken and rabbit or with seafood, and the name comes from the large pan in which it is cooked.

Other named rice dishes include *arròs negre*, made with squid and squid ink; *arròs amb conill i caragols*, with rabbit and snails, typical of the south of Valencia and especially the Vinalopo districts; *arròs al forn*, baked with ingredients including sausages, chickpeas and potato; *arròs amb costra*, covered with egg and typical of much of the south, especially Elche; and *arròs a banda*, a coastal preparation with rice, fish and garlic. *Fideuà*, a noodle and seafood dish, is typical of the Valencian coast and originated in Gandia.

Other dishes include *all i pebre*, made with peppers, garlic, potatoes and eels around Valencia city; *suquet de peix*, an eel, potato, garlic and fish stew; *esgarrat* or *pericana*, made with roasted peppers and salted cod; *espencat*, made from roasted vegetables; and *coques*, ancient local salty pastries. Sauces include *allioli*, *mullador* and *picada*.

Sweets and drinks include *arnadí*, typical of Xàtiva; *fartons*, designed to be dipped in *orxata*; *torró*, traditional to Xixona and Alicante; *peladillas*, typical of Casinos; and *bunyols*, an Arab-inspired fried pastry whose Valencian variety habitually includes pumpkin. *Orxata de Xufa*, made with tigernuts, is described as quintessentially Valencian and produced in Alboraya, north of Valencia. Valencian wines have the Protected Designations of Origin Alicante, Utiel-Requena and Valencia.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They do document an important literary history in Valencian. Works associated with the Valencian Golden Age include Joanot Martorell’s chivalric romance *Tirant lo Blanch* and Ausiàs March’s poetry. The first book produced with movable type in the Iberian Peninsula was printed in the Valencian variety, and the Valencian poem *Scachs d’amor* contains the earliest recorded chess game with modern rules for the queen and bishop.

## Language & religion

Valencian is a Romance language and the official, historical and traditional name used in the Valencian Community for the language also known as Catalan, either as a whole or in Valencia-specific forms. The Valencian Community’s 1982 Statute of Autonomy officially recognises Valencian as the name of the native language. The language belongs to the Western group of Catalan dialects; named varieties include Alacantí, Southern Valencian, Central Valencian or Apitxat, Northern Valencian or Castellonenc and Transitional Valencian. Valencian and Spanish are official languages of the Valencian Community.

Valencian is regulated by the Acadèmia Valenciana de la Llengua, following the Castelló Norms. Its relationship to the wider Valencian-Catalan linguistic system is the subject of political and cultural controversy, including debate over autonomy or heteronomy. The sources describe Christian and Muslim historical populations, Christian liturgical traditions and the Muslim conquest of the Iberian Peninsula.
