## Overview

The Mokshas, also called Mokshans or the Moksha people, are a Mordvinian ethnic group whose language belongs to the Volgaic branch of the Uralic language family. They live mostly near the Volga and Moksha rivers, a tributary of the Oka River, especially in the central and western parts of the Republic of Mordovia, neighbouring Tambov Oblast, and the western and central parts of Penza Oblast. Other populations live in Orenburg Oblast, Bashkortostan, Tatarstan, Altai Krai, Estonia, Kazakhstan, the United States, and Australia. According to a 1994 Russian census, more than 180,000 people identified themselves as Mokshas among the autochthonal Finnic population in Mordovia. The sources document Mokshan language, mythology, folklore, historical experience, and distinctive archaeological evidence.

## Festivals & rituals

They state that most Mokshas belong to the Russian Orthodox Church, while Lutheranism and paganism are also practised. The sources further describe traditional mythology in which the world was created by Ińe Narmon, the Great Bird, also called Akša Loksti, the White Swan in folklore. The first creation was water; Yakśarga, the Duck, brought sand from the bottom of the sea, and Ińe Narmon formed the earth and placed Ińe Šufta, the Great Tree, upon it. Akša Kal, the White Sturgeon, carried the earth with the roots of Ińe Šufta on its back.

## Foodways

In the mythology described by the source, the souls of heroes, clan elders, and warriors slain in battle travel after death to the emerald green isle of Usiya, where they sit at a long table with the great King Ťušťen and drink pure mead. This is a mythological description rather than an account of Mokshan foodways.

## Oral tradition & literature

Mokshan folklore preserves creation narratives involving Ińe Narmon, Akša Loksti, Yakśarga, Ińe Šufta, Akša Kal, Tsofks, Kuku, Ožarga, and Mešavane, the Mother Bee. Ińe Narmon’s three nestlings chose different environments: Tsofks chose bushes and willows, Kuku settled in the forest, and Ožarga went to the meadows. Since the Christianization of the Mokshans, the Mokshan Supreme God has usually been called Värden Škai, the Supreme Creator.

Later legends describe Idemevs, the Devil, hiding sand in his mouth after being asked by God to bring it from the bottom of the great sea. When Värden Škai began creating the earth, the hidden sand grew in Idemevs’s mouth; after he spat it out, chasms and mountains appeared on the previously level earth. The first humans created by Värden Škai were said to live for 700–800 years and to be 99 arshins tall. Mastoratia ruled the underworld, while Usiya received the souls of heroes, clan elders, and warriors slain in battle. The source also lists Mokshan folklore, Mokshan names, a Moksha–English–Moksha online dictionary, and a Mokshen Pravda newspaper.

## Language & religion

The Mokshas speak Moksha, also called Mokshan, a language in the Mordvinic branch of the Uralic language family and the Volgaic branch of the Uralic languages. The source gives several local names: Мокшет or Мокшень ломатть in Moksha; Мокшане or Мордва-Мокша in Russian; Muqşılar in Tatar; Мăкшăсем in Chuvash; and Мокшот in Erzya. The term “Moksha” begins to appear in Russian sources in the 17th century.

Most Mokshas belong to the Russian Orthodox Church. Lutheranism and paganism are also practised. Traditional Mokshan mythology includes Ińe Narmon, Akša Loksti, Mešavane, Mastoratia, and Värden Škai.
