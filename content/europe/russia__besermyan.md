## Overview

The Besermyan, also called Biserman, Besermans, or Besermens, are a numerically small Permian people in Russia. They live in the districts of Yukamenskoye, Glazov, Balezino, and Yar in the northwest of Udmurtia. Russia has ten villages of pure Besermyan ethnicity and 41 villages with a partial Besermyan population. The 1897 Russian census listed 10,800 Besermans; there were 10,000 in 1926, while the 2002 Russian census recorded 3,122. The Besermyan are of mixed origin and are described as probably resulting from the assimilation of a group of Tatars by the Udmurts. Their distinct dialect of Udmurt has Tatar influences.

## Oral tradition & literature

In the 13th century, during his travel to Mongolia, the papal envoy Plano Carpini claimed that the Besermyan were subjects of the Mongols. Russian chronicles sometimes mentioned the Besermyan, although the sources state that it is unclear whether the term referred to the modern group, since it was also a common derivation of “musulman” (Muslim).

## Language & religion

The Besermyan language is a dialect of the Udmurt language with Tatar influences. Although they speak a dialect of Udmurt, the Besermyan consider themselves a distinct people. The Besermyan historically practiced their own indigenous religion. According to scholar Shirin Akiner, most current Besermyan practice Sunni Islam, while some also practice Christianity. The Russians began converting the Besermyan to Christianity around the middle of the 18th century. Genetic research has examined Besermyan from villages including Yozhovo, Yukamenskoye, Yozhevo, and Shamardan. The sources describe Eastern Eurasian haplogroups, primarily haplogroup C, haplogroup N, Srubnaya-like admixture, and Nganasan-like admixture.
