## Overview

The Lovari are a subgroup of the Romani people. The name *Lovari* means “horse-dealer” and derives from the Hungarian word “ló,” meaning horse. They speak their own dialect, influenced by Hungarian and West Slavic dialects, and live predominantly throughout Central Europe, including Hungary, Poland, Slovakia, the Czech Republic, and Germany, as well as in Southeastern Europe, including Romania, Croatia, and northern Serbia. Their language is classified under Vlax Romani. The sources identify the Lovari as a distinct Romani people.

## Language & religion

The Lovari speak a Romani dialect influenced by Hungarian and West Slavic dialects. Their language is classified under Vlax Romani. The Lovari are further divided into the Machvaya, named after the Mačva region, which they settled from modern day Hungary.
