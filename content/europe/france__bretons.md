## Overview

The Bretons are a Celtic people who settled in Brittany from the 3rd to 9th centuries, originally from south-western Great Britain. Their traditional language is Brezhoneg, a Brittonic Celtic language related to Cornish and Welsh. Brittany has been counted as one of the six Celtic nations, and its festival, pilgrimage and music traditions remain among the most actively practised folk cultures in France. France does not collect statistics on ethnicity, so the exact number of Bretons cannot be established.

## Material culture

Breton material culture is documented mainly through 19th- and early-20th-century garments of wool decorated with thread, beads and metal.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Breton textiles are known from 19th-century wool garments worked with coloured thread, metal and glass beads.

- **Beaded and metal-thread woollens** — Jackets, vests, bodices and skirts of wool decorated with wool thread, metal thread and glass beads.
- **Silk-lined jackets and vests** — 19th-century jackets and vests pairing wool with silk, without beadwork.
- **Decorative ribbons** — Silk ribbons worked with metal spangles that trimmed garments.

### Clothing & dress

Women's dress consists of matching ensembles of wool bodices, skirts and jackets with metal and beaded decoration, worn in ceremonial contexts.

- **Leather and brass belt** (*gouriz*) — A belt of leather and brass dated about 1834.

### Architecture

The main built features documented are the shrines and parish churches of the seven founder saints, which anchor the Tro Breizh pilgrimage.

- **Saint-Pol-de-Léon** (*Kastell-Paol*) — Shrine of one of the seven founder saints.
- **Tréguier** (*Landreger*) — Shrine of one of the seven founder saints.
- **Saint-Brieuc** (*Sant-Brieg*) — Shrine of one of the seven founder saints.
- **Saint-Malo** (*Sant-Maloù*) — Shrine of one of the seven founder saints.
- **Dol-de-Bretagne** (*Dol*) — Shrine of one of the seven founder saints.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The only documented metalwork object is the gouriz belt, though metal appears widely as costume decoration.

- **Triskele** — A Celtic symbol Bretons use to assert their Celtic descent.
- **Ermine** — Heraldic and symbolic device of the Duchy's blazons and the chivalric L'Ordre de l'Hermine.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Breton body adornment is documented only through metal thread, paillettes and spangles applied to garments and ribbons.

## Music & performance

Breton music features a cappella choral singing and instrumental music with characteristic bagpipes and oboe-like instruments.

- **Choral singing tradition** (*kan ha diskan*) — A choral a cappella tradition that is a main type of Breton music.
- **Oboe-like instrument** (*bombard*) — A characteristic traditional instrument similar to an oboe.
- **Bagpipe** (*veuze*) — One of two kinds of traditional Breton bagpipe.
- **Small bagpipe** (*binioù kozh*) — A traditional Breton bagpipe displaced in many settings after the Second World War.
- **Large bagpipe** (*binioù bras*) — Became commonplace after the Second World War through Breton pipe bands.

## Dance & theatre

Breton dances are performed mainly in circles and chains at fest-noz festivals, with some reaching back to the Middle Ages.

- **Gavotte dances** (*gavottes*) — Among the best-known Breton dances.
- **Circle dance** (*An Dro*) — One of the best-known Breton dances performed in circles.
- **Half circle dance** (*hanter dro*) — One of the best-known Breton dances.
- **Round dance** (*plinn*) — One of the best-known Breton dances.
- **Processional walk** (*troménie*) — A twelve kilometre procession walked by large numbers of people in traditional costume at Locronan.

## Festivals & rituals

The Breton festival year centres on pardons, the feast days of parish patron saints, and the major pilgrimage tour of Brittany.

- **Festival of collective dancing** (*fest-noz*) — A festival built around dancing, dating as an institution from the 1950s and inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage on 5 December 2012.
- **Pardon at Sainte-Anne d'Auray** (*Santez-Anna-Wened*) — One of the three most celebrated pardons.
- **Pardon at Tréguier** (*Landreger*) — Pardon in honour of St Yves, one of the three most celebrated.
- **Pardon at Locronan** (*Lokorn*) — Pardon in honour of St Ronan with a twelve-kilometre troménie, one of the three most celebrated.
- **Tour of Brittany pilgrimage** (*Tro Breizh*) — The major pilgrimage in which pilgrims walk from the grave of one of the Seven Founder Saints to the next, usually completed over several years.

## Foodways

Breton cooking draws from French tradition with distinctive local specialities of crêpes, pastries, puddings and regional drinks.

- **Thin pancakes** (*krampouezh*) — Crêpes or galettes made from wheat or buckwheat flour, usually eaten as a main course.
- **Butter pastry** (*kouign-amann*) — A traditional Breton pastry.
- **Suet pudding with prunes** (*fars forn*) — A sweet suet pudding with prunes, also called far breton.
- **Salted butter caramel** (*caramel au beurre salé*) — A Breton sweet.
- **Apple eau de vie** (*lambig*) — A traditional Breton drink.

## Oral tradition & literature

Breton tradition centres on the Ankou, a figure from pre-Christian belief, and hagiographic legends of the seven founder-saints.

- **Reaper of Death** (*Ankou*) — The most powerful folk figure surviving from pre-Christian tradition in Brittany.
- **National anthem** (*Bro Gozh ma Zadoù*) — Based on the Welsh Hen Wlad Fy Nhadau.

## Language & religion

Breton is a Brittonic Celtic language with four dialects, spoken by around 206,000 people as of 2013, and Bretons are predominantly Catholic.

- **Vannes dialect** (*Gwenedeg*) — One of four main dialects of Breton.
- **Cornouaille dialect** (*Kerneveg*) — One of four main dialects of Breton.
- **Léon dialect** (*Leoneg*) — One of four main dialects of Breton.
- **Trégor dialect** (*Tregerieg*) — One of four main dialects of Breton.
