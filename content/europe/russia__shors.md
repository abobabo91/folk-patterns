## Overview

The Shors, also called Shorians, are a Turkic ethnic group native to Kemerovo Oblast of Russia. Most live in the Tom basin along the Kondoma and Mras-Su Rivers, in a region historically called Mountainous Shoria; Shor communities also live in Khakassia and Altai Republic. According to the 2002 census, there were 13,975 Shors in Russia. They speak their own Shor language. The Shors formed through a long process of intermixing among Turkic, Ugric, Samoyedic, and Yeniseian tribes, and their culture and origins are similar to those of northern Altaians and some ethnic groups of the Khakas. Their folk culture includes hunting, fishing, pine-nut picking, ironworking, throat-singing, epic performance, clan institutions, and shamanic traditions.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

* Nettle-fibre clothing* — The use of nettle fibre for clothing continued into the 20th century.

* Wild-hemp clothing* — Wild hemp was also used for clothing, alongside primitive implements and techniques.

### Clothing & dress

The sources state that nettle fibre and wild hemp were used for clothing and that primitive implements and techniques continued into the 20th century.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Ironworking was historically important to the Shors. They were suppliers of ironware to the Yenisei Kirghiz and Oirats, and their ability to smelt iron from ore was described as an achievement shared by only one other indigenous Siberian people, the Yakuts, before the Russian advance into the area. Russian traders later eroded this occupation, and Russian products effectively ended Shor blacksmithing.

## Music & performance

Throat-singing is described as an ancient and traditional form of music in Shor culture. Shor epic stories about heroes and deities are often accompanied by singing and by the use of a *kai-komus*, a two-stringed wooden instrument.

Music is also connected with Shor spiritual practice. The god Ul'gen is said to grant people anything when addressed through *kamlanie*, a shamanic ritual. Shor shamans, called *kam*, traditionally held an important place in religious life, although shamanism was persecuted during Russian colonization and the Soviet era. Graves, drums, ritual clothing, and other objects were burned or desecrated during that period.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe Shor religious and communal practices. Traditional Shor society was organized into clans, each led by a chief. Chiefs were elected in large clan gatherings, where important decisions, conflicts, and other issues were settled. Clans also organized communal hunts and shared the spoils equally among themselves.

Shor spirituality traditionally included shamanism and animism. The *kam* conducted shamanic rituals, and clan elders led the Prayer to the Sky directed to Tengri, the ancient Central Asian god of the sky. *Che ezi*, or master spirits of places, were believed to dwell in specific locales such as mountains, rivers, and forests. These protective spirits had to be respected when entering their domains.

## Foodways

The sources identify hunting, fishing, some primitive farming, and pine-nut picking as important Shor economic activities.

## Oral tradition & literature

Epic stories about heroes and deities are popular in Shor culture and are often accompanied by singing and the use of a *kai-komus*. Shor mythology describes a universe divided into three levels: the Heavenly Realm, the Middle Earth, and the Underworld. The Heavenly Realm belongs to Ul'gen, the Middle Earth is the physical world, and the Underworld belongs to Erlik and his minions. These worlds are linked by the World Tree or World Mountain.

The creation myth tells how Ul'gen created the three skies, while Erlik created mountains, birds, and other animals. Ul'gen created man but required Erlik to place the soul into the body. Other mythological figures include Chaashin, Paktan, Aina, Ker palyk, Ak pozat, and Samchi.

Shor intellectual culture expanded through education in the 1920s and 1930s. Shor writers, journalists, and clerks became prominent after the use of Shor in education and the growth of literacy. The resurgence of Shor identity beginning in the 1980s led to a revival of traditional culture and new educational programs for native teachers, students, and the younger generation.

## Language & religion

The Shors are a Turkic ethnic group and speak their own Shor language. Their population formed through intermixing among Turkic, Ugric, Samoyedic, and Yeniseian tribes. The region was Turkicized under the influence of the Yenisei Kyrgyz during the medieval period, and the Shors adopted Turkic speech as a result.

The Shors were originally practitioners of shamanism and animism. During Russian rule beginning in the early 17th century, they were converted to Russian Orthodox Christianity, and conversions occurred rapidly after the establishment of the Altai Spiritual Mission in the 1830s. Most present Shors are Russian Orthodox Christians. Shamanism nevertheless remained important and influential, and its revival beginning in the late 1980s made it a vital part of modern Shor identity. Traditional beliefs include Ul'gen, the god of the Heavenly Realm; Erlik, the god of the Underworld; Tengri; Umay; Cher-Sug; and numerous local *che ezi*.
