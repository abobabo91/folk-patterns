## Overview

Dalmatian Italians are the historical Italian national minority of Dalmatia, now part of Croatia and Montenegro. Italian-speaking Dalmatians accounted for 12.5% of the population in 1865, 5.8% in 1880, and 2.8% in 1910. Today, some 500–2,000 people in Dalmatia identify as Italians. The main communities are in the coastal cities of Zadar, Split, Trogir, Šibenik, Kotor, Perast, and Budva. The community is represented in Croatia and Montenegro by the Italian National Community, or *Comunità Nazionale Italiana*. Historically, Dalmatian Italians exerted significant cultural and architectural influence on Dalmatia. Their history also records the interaction of Romance-speaking urban populations with Slavic-speaking communities in the hinterland and islands.

## Language & religion

Dalmatian Italians speak or historically spoke Italian in a region where Romance and Slavic languages interacted. Roman Dalmatia was fully Latinized by 476 AD, and the Romance-speaking population of the coast and islands spoke Dalmatian, a now-extinct Romance language that developed from Vulgar Latin. The Dalmatian cities retained Romanic culture and language in places such as Zadar, Split, and Dubrovnik. During Venetian rule, Venetian was adopted by the Latin Dalmatians as their vernacular language. Italian replaced Latin as the language of culture in Venetian Dalmatia and the Republic of Ragusa from the 15th century. Italian lost its status as an official language of Dalmatia in 1909, when Croatian became the sole official language. The sources mention Catholic and Orthodox Slavic populations.
