## Overview

The Goryuns, also called Horiuns or Horyuny, are a little-documented ethnic group of East Slavs living around Putyvl, in the Sumy Oblast of north-eastern Ukraine. In the past, they lived in Kursk Governorate of the Russian Empire. They are considered a tiny sub-ethnos of Russians, but distinguish themselves from other Russians and from Ukrainians living in the same region. The Goryuns speak a dialect of Russian with some Belarusian and Ukrainian features. Their cultural importance in the available material lies chiefly in their distinct local identity, their unique style of polyphonic singing, and the documentation of Horyun culture in Nova Sloboda.

## Material culture

### Architecture

In 2017, a museum of Horyun culture opened in the village of Nova Sloboda. Its exposition recreates the estate of the Horyuns of the late nineteenth—early twentieth centuries.

## Music & performance

The Goryuns have a reputation for their unique style of polyphonic singing. No UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription concerns the Goryuns.

## Oral tradition & literature

Chronicles first mention Goryun villages in the sixteenth century, shortly after the annexation of the region to Muscovy. The Belarusian scientist Fiodar Klimchuk concludes from this that the Goryuns might have lived in the region before the year 1500.

## Language & religion

The Goryuns are an East Slavic ethnic group. Their dialect of the Russian language has some features of Belarusian and Ukrainian.
