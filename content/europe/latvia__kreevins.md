## Overview

The Kreevins (Latvian: *krieviņi*) were Votes who lived near the Latvian town of Bauska and spoke their own dialect of Votic. Their ancestors originally lived in Ingria. During an attack on Ingria in 1444–1447, Vincke von Overbeg of the Teutonic Order took about 3,000 Votic prisoners of war and transferred them to work on the construction of Bauska Castle. After the castle was finished, the Votes settled in the area and became farmers. The first written record dates from 1636. In the middle of the 19th century, the Kreevins merged with the surrounding Latvians, although aspects of Votic culture remained preserved.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Men’s embroidered clothing** — Men’s clothing was embroidered with blue and red thread.

**Woolen jackets** — Men wore jackets called *viita*, *viite*, or *viiten*, made of woolen fabric. The sources state that these garments were possibly copied from Estonian immigrants who also lived in the area.

**Clothing shared with Ingria** — Many clothing types used by Kreevins were also in use in Ingria.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name blue and red embroidery thread but provide no motif names.

### Clothing & dress

Kreevin men wore clothing embroidered with blue and red thread, and their shirts were fastened with ribbon instead of buttons. They also wore woolen jackets called *viita*, *viite*, or *viiten*. Typical Latvian clothing was also used. Kreevin women used headscarves similar to those worn by Votes and Izhorians in Ingria, and a decorated scarf was often placed on top of the headscarf. Younger girls wore their hair plaited.

### Architecture

The sources describe the construction of Bauska Castle and state that Votic prisoners of war were transferred to work on it. After the castle was finished, the Votes settled in the area and became farmers.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention decorated scarves, headscarves, and the plaiting of younger girls’ hair.

## Music & performance

They contain a language sample presented as the Lord’s Prayer, beginning “Meģģi ise taiwâs!” and ending “Amen!”.

## Foodways

The sources state that, after settling near Bauska Castle, the Votes became farmers.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that the Latvian poet Rainis and the Latvian actor Uldis Dumpis have Kreevin ancestry.

## Language & religion

The Kreevins spoke their own dialect of Votic. The sources identify the Kreevins as Votes and state that their language sounded foreign to Latvians; the name *krieviņi* means “little Russians” in Latvian in its diminutive form. In 1805, an estimated 1,200 Kreevins lived in Bauska and its surroundings, while five years later only 12–15 people could still speak Votic, all of them elders. Anders Johan Sjögren’s research trip in 1846 concluded that Votic had almost disappeared from the region.
