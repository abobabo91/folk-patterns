## Overview

The Torbeši are a Macedonian-speaking Muslim ethnoreligious group in North Macedonia and Albania. They are also called Macedonian Muslims or Muslim Macedonians and have remained religiously distinct from the Orthodox Christian Macedonian community for centuries. Their main regions are Debarska Župa, Dolni Drimkol, Reka, and Golo Brdo. Concentrations occur in western North Macedonia, eastern Albania, the Debar region, the Struga municipality, Dolna Reka, Torbešija in the Marko's River Valley south of Skopje, and around the Plasnica and Dolneni municipalities. The exact population is difficult to establish: the 2021 North Macedonia census recorded 4,174 people as Torbeši and a further 455 as “Muslim Macedonians.” Their folk-cultural significance in the supplied sources lies chiefly in their language, religious identity, local communities, cuisine, marriage practices, and political history.

## Music & performance

They identify Jašar Ahmedovski, Ipče Ahmedovski, and Menil Velioski as Macedonian singers, and Aki Rahimovski as a Croatian singer.

## Festivals & rituals

The supplied sources state that Torbeši cuisine shares commonalities with neighboring Muslim groups in dishes prepared for weddings and religious holidays. They also report that interethnic marriages are considered acceptable among Macedonian Muslims, while interreligious marriages are not; families generally prefer marriage with people from nearby villages, and arranged marriage, once common, is now rare. No annual festival calendar, seasonal ritual, birth ceremony, coming-of-age rite, funeral custom, or festival date is described.

## Foodways

The Torbeši consider their local cuisine to be “Macedonian cuisine.” The supplied source says that it shares commonalities with neighboring Muslim groups, particularly in dishes prepared for weddings and religious holidays.

## Language & religion

The Torbeši speak Macedonian and are religiously Muslim. They are linguistically distinct from the larger Muslim ethnic groups in the greater region of Macedonia, including Albanians, Turks, and Romanis. The sources describe their religious distinction from the Orthodox Christian Macedonian community and state that many Torbeši are largely descendants of Orthodox Christian Slavs from the region of Macedonia who converted to Islam during the centuries of Ottoman rule in the Balkans. The Khalwati, Rifa'is, and Qadiris Sufi orders all played a role in the conversion of Slavic and Paulician populations.
