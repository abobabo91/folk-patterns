## Overview

Germans (*Deutsche*) are the natives or inhabitants of Germany and, in broader usage, people of German descent or native speakers of German. About 100 million Germans live worldwide, most of them in Germany; sizable diasporas are found in the United States, Brazil, the countries of the former Soviet Union, Switzerland, Austria, Argentina, Canada, Spain, Australia, the United Kingdom, and South Africa. Germany is made up of sixteen Länder. German is a West Germanic language and the principal marker of modern German ethnic identity. German culture is regionally diverse because of a long history of political fragmentation, while the arts and sciences have remained important parts of German identity.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention German cities and urban centers, including Aachen, Cologne, Trier, Gdańsk, and Berlin, and describe the growth of urban centers, trade, arts, crafts, commerce, and banking.

## Music & performance

The sources identify music-related figures. Named composers associated with German culture include Johann Sebastian Bach, Ludwig van Beethoven, Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, Joseph Haydn, Johannes Brahms, Franz Schubert, Richard Strauss, and Richard Wagner.

## Festivals & rituals

Carnival, called *Karneval*, *Fasching*, or *Fastnacht*, is described as an important part of German culture, particularly in Southern Germany and the Rhineland. Oktoberfest is identified as an important German festival. Christmas and Easter are celebrated by many Germans.

## Foodways

Popular German dishes include brown bread and stew. Germans consume a high amount of alcohol, particularly beer, compared to other European peoples.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources identify writers who contributed significantly to German culture, including Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, Friedrich Schiller, Johann Gottfried Herder, Friedrich Hölderlin, E. T. A. Hoffmann, Heinrich Heine, Novalis, and the Brothers Grimm.

## Language & religion

German is a West Germanic language and the native language of most Germans. Historically, many northern Germans spoke Low German, which is closely related to German. German is related to Dutch, Frisian languages, Luxembourgish, and English. Modern Standard German is based on High German and Central German. The German endonym *Deutsche* derives from the Old High German term *diutisc*, meaning “ethnic” or “relating to the people.” A steadily shrinking majority of Germans are Christians: about one third are Roman Catholics, one third adhere to Protestantism, and another third profess no religion. The sources also mention a growing number of Muslims and a notable Jewish community that was decimated in the Holocaust.
