## Overview

The Megleno-Romanians, also known as Meglenites, Moglenite Vlachs, or Vlachs, are an Eastern Romance ethnic group traditionally inhabiting seven villages in the Moglena region of Central Macedonia, Greece, and Huma in North Macedonia. The Moglena region lies between the Vardar river, the Kožuf and Voras mountains, the plains of Giannitsa and Edessa, and the Mariansca Mountains. They are traditionally sedentary agriculturalists rather than transhumants. Their language is generally called Megleno-Romanian or Meglenitic; speakers call it *vlahește*. Estimates of their number range from 5,213 to 20,000, with communities also recorded in Romania, Turkey, Serbia, and North Macedonia. Their importance in folk culture lies especially in their distinct language, settlement history, religious diversity, and customs.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They do record that Megleno-Romanians in Cerna use the Romanian tricolor as a *furglița*, or wedding flag, during weddings, while the traditional white-red colors are used very rarely. The source presents this practice as evidence that Megleno-Romanians in Romania identify themselves as Romanians.

### Architecture

They state that there were several small hamlets and cottage settlements at high elevations at Mount Paiko before the founding of the present Megleno-Romanian villages.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

One recorded household custom involved an oak log: the head of the household placed it in the hearth just before Christmas and burned it bit by bit until Epiphany.

## Music & performance

Vasile Șirli is listed as a musical composer and producer of Megleno-Romanian descent.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources record a traditional custom called *bondic*. Just before Christmas, the head of a household took an oak log and placed it in the hearth, where it was burned bit by bit until Epiphany. The resulting charcoal was placed under fruit trees to make them fertile. A similar custom called *bavnic* existed among Aromanians, some Romanians, and Latvians, while related customs were found in Orthodox South Slavic cultures, including Serbian *badnjak*, Bulgarian *budnik*, and Macedonian *badnik*. The sources state that some observers regarded these customs and other cultural archetypes as evidence that Megleno-Romanians came from a mountainous region.

Most Megleno-Romanians are Orthodox Christians. The population of Nânti, also called Nótia, converted to Islam in the 17th or 18th century; the source also states that they converted in 1759. This was described as the only case among Eastern Romance populations of an entire community converting to Islam. The population was expelled by force to Turkey in 1923 during the population exchange between Greece and Turkey.

In Romania, the Balkan Romanianness Day was established in 2021 as a holiday for ethnic Romanian subgroups living south of the Danube, including the Megleno-Romanians. It is officially celebrated every 10 May and commemorates the establishment of the Ullah millet in 1905.

## Foodways

They do state that Megleno-Romanians were traditionally sedentary agriculturalists and that the charcoal from the *bondic* oak log was placed under fruit trees to promote fertility.

## Oral tradition & literature

They list Theodor Minda as a poet, folklorist, and activist in Romania, and Vasile Șirli as a musical composer and producer, but provide no descriptions of their works or of a Megleno-Romanian literary revival.

The sources do record several competing historical explanations for the group’s origins. Ovid Densusianu and Konstantin Jireček considered the Megleno-Romanians descendants of Romanians mixed with Pechenegs settled in Moglen by the Byzantine Emperor Alexios I Komnenos in 1091. Gustav Weigand and George Murnu connected them with the Romanian-Bulgarian Empire, while Pericle Papahagi proposed that they descended from Romanians who came to the Meglen area during the times of Dobromir Chrysos. Theodor Capidan argued that they had spent time in the Rhodope Mountains before moving to Moglen.

## Language & religion

Megleno-Romanian is an Eastern Romance language. It is also called Meglenitic in English, while Greek uses βλαχομογλενίτικα (*vlakhomoglenítika*) and μογλενίτικα (*moglenítika*). Speakers call their language *vlahește*, and the Megleno-Romanian diaspora in Romania also uses *meglenoromână*. The group once used a self-designation derived from the Latin *Romanus*, but later adopted *Vlasi* or *Vlashi*, derived from “Vlachs.” The name Megleno-Romanians was given in the 19th century by scholars studying their language and customs.

Most Megleno-Romanians are Orthodox Christians. The community of Nânti converted to Islam in the 17th or 18th century, with 1759 also given as the date of conversion. The sources state that Megleno-Romanian and Aromanian show Greek influence and lack a Slavic influence. No historical script is identified in the sources.
