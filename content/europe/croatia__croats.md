## Overview

Croats are a South Slavic ethnic group native to Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and neighboring countries in Central and Southeastern Europe. They share a Croatian culture, ancestry, history, and language, and are predominantly Catholic. In Croatia, 3.9 million people identify as Croats, constituting about 90.4% of the population. Another 553,000 live in Bosnia and Herzegovina, where Croats are one of three constituent ethnic groups, concentrated mainly in Western Herzegovina, Central Bosnia, and Bosnian Posavina. About 70,000 Croats live in Serbia, mostly in Vojvodina. Croat communities also exist in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Italy, Slovenia, Montenegro, Romania, Australia, New Zealand, and the Americas. Their history includes Slavic settlement, medieval duchies and kingdoms, and later political unions.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention hilltop settlements, forts, castles, churches, and towns, including the fort at Sisak, Ostrovica Castle, Zrin Castle, the Basilica of Saint Peter and Moses at Solin, and the church of Saint Peter in Rome.

## Music & performance

 The sources do mention solemn divine service in the church of Saint Peter in Rome in 879, during which Pope John VIII gave his blessing to Duke Branimir and the Croatian people, but they provide no musical description.

## Festivals & rituals

 The historical sources do describe political and religious events, including the coronation of Dmitar Zvonimir on 8 October 1076 at Solin in the Basilica of Saint Peter and Moses, and the celebration of Tarara Day every 15 March among the Tarara people in New Zealand, who are described as having mixed Croatian and Māori Ngāpuhi descent.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention the Chronicle of the Priest of Duklja, the Frankish Annals, the Royal Frankish Annals, Annales Carinthiæ, Chronica Hungarorum, and De Administrando Imperio as historical or literary records concerning Croat history. The Chronicle of the Priest of Duklja is described as an unreliable record, while the reliability of De Administrando Imperio has also been questioned.

## Language & religion

Croatian is described as a South Slavic language and is official in Croatia, the European Union, and Bosnia and Herzegovina. It is also a recognized minority language in Croatian communities and minorities in Montenegro, Austria, Italy, Romania, and Serbia. The earliest preserved mentions of the Croat ethnonym in stone inscriptions and written documents in the territory of Croatia date from the 8th–9th century. The earliest writing in the native Croatian language is associated with the Baška tablet, dated to around 1100 and written in Glagolitic script. Croats are predominantly Catholic. The sources also describe the historical importance of relations with the Byzantine Empire, Rome, the Holy See, and the Roman rite.
