## Overview

Bosniaks are an ethnic minority in Montenegro, first introduced as a census category in 2003. According to the 2023 census, 58,956 Bosniaks lived in Montenegro, representing 9.45% of the population and making them the country’s third-largest ethnic group after Montenegrins and Serbs. They primarily live in the Sandžak area of northern Montenegro and form the majority in Rožaje, Petnjica, Plav and Gusinje. The sources describe Bosnian dialects in Gusinje and Plav, including strong structural influence from Albanian.

## Music & performance

The sources identify several Bosniaks of Montenegro as singers: Danijel Alibabić, Senida Hajdarpašić, Dado Polumenta, Šako Polumenta and Ekrem Jevrić.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources state that the majority of Bosniaks of Montenegro supported Montenegrin independence in the 2006 independence referendum. They also state that the majority are predominantly Sunni Muslim and adhere to the Hanafi school of jurisprudence.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources identify Sait Orahovac and Faruk Međedović as writers, and Rifat Rastoder as a politician, writer and journalist.

## Language & religion

The sources describe Bosnian dialects in Gusinje and Plav as showing very high structural influence from Albanian. They explain this language contact by stating that most Bosnian speakers there are of Albanian origin. Religiously, Bosniaks are described as predominantly Sunni Muslim and adherent to the Hanafi school of thought and jurisprudence.
