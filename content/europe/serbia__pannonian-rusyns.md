## Overview

Pannonian Rusyns, also known as Pannonian Rusnaks, are ethnic Rusyns from the southern regions of the Pannonian Plain. Their communities are located mainly in Vojvodina, Serbia, and Slavonia, Croatia, where they are officially recognized as an ethnic minority. They descend from eighteenth-century migrant communities from northeastern Carpathian regions. In Serbia, they mostly live in Vojvodina, with important concentrations in Ruski Krstur, Kucura, Bikić Do, Đurđevo, and Novi Sad. The total population in Serbia and Croatia is estimated to be less than 13,000, while the 2022 census recorded 11,483 ethnic Rusyns in Serbia. Their cultural significance in the sources lies chiefly in their recognized language, institutions, settlement history, religious communities, and minority organizations.

## Material culture

### Architecture

They identify Ruski Krstur as a cultural centre of Rusyns in Serbia and mention a Greek Catholic parish church of St. Peter and Paul in Novi Sad, but provide no architectural description.

## Music & performance

They do state that the publishing house Rusyn Word, founded in Novi Sad in 1945, became a centre for publishing Rusyn newspapers, literary works, and school textbooks.

## Festivals & rituals

They do record that the agreements governing the settlement of Rusyn colonists in Krstur and Kucura required Eastern Catholic religious affiliation. The sources also mention the founding of the first Ruthenian Greek Catholic parish in Petrovci in 1836 and the Greek Catholic parish church of St. Peter and Paul in Novi Sad.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do document several institutions connected with written cultural production. Rusyn Word was founded in Novi Sad in 1945 and published Rusyn newspapers, literary works, and school textbooks. A Rusyn high school opened in Ruski Krstur in the same year. The Society for Rusyn Language and Literature was established in Novi Sad in 1970. Rusyn studies began at the Faculty of Philosophy of the University of Novi Sad in 1972, and the lectureship for Rusyn Language and Literature was established in 1983. The Department of Rusynistics was established there in 2002, and the Institute for Culture of Rusyns in Vojvodina was founded in Novi Sad in 2008.

## Language & religion

Pannonian Rusyns in Serbia and Croatia consider their linguistic variety, Pannonian Rusyn, to be one of four standardized versions of the Rusyn language; some linguists classify it as a microlanguage. They use a standardized Rusyn Cyrillic alphabet. Rusyn is one of the six official languages of provincial administration in Vojvodina and is officially used in two settlements in Croatia. In Serbia, the language’s recognition was confirmed by the provincial statutes of Vojvodina. In the 1969 Constitutional Law, Rusyn was regulated as one of five official languages in Vojvodina.

Religiously, the 2022 census recorded 94.9% of Serbia’s ethnic Rusyns as Christians: 75.4% Catholics, 18.5% Eastern Orthodox, and 0.7% Protestants. The eighteenth-century agreements concerning settlement in Krstur and Kucura required Rusyn colonists to be Eastern Catholics. The sources also record Ruthenian Greek Catholic parish institutions in Petrovci and Novi Sad.
