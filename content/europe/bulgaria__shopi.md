## Overview

The Shopi or Šopi are a regional group in the Balkans. Their traditional area, called Shopluk or Šopluk, is a mesoregion located mainly in Western Bulgaria, with smaller parts in Southeastern Serbia and Northeastern North Macedonia, where the three countries meet. The boundaries are debated: the narrowest definition includes only the surroundings of Sofia, while wider definitions include Central Western Bulgaria, Bulgarian-populated areas in Serbia, and occasionally Northwestern Bulgaria. Shopi identify variously as Bulgarians, Serbs, or ethnic Macedonians according to the country and historical territory in which they live. They maintain a regional or ethnographic identity as Shopi while speaking dialects identified as Bulgarian, Serbian, or Macedonian.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe the Shopluk as one of the centres of the Bulgarian National Revival during the 19th century. The region was included in the Bulgarian Exarchate when it was established in 1870, following active participation in the Bulgarian Church struggle. The sources also mention that the first rebellion against a Greek bishop took place in Vratsa in 1828.

## Oral tradition & literature

They do record historical descriptions by European travellers and ethnographers. Stephan Gerlach described Bulgarian towns and villages along a route from Constantinople toward Central Europe in 1578, including Sofia, Dragoman, Dimitrovgrad, Pirot, Bela Palanka, and Niš. Other travellers and writers discussed the ethnographic boundary between Bulgarians and Serbs, often placing it around Niš. These accounts concern historical observation and classification rather than a documented Shopi oral-literary tradition.

## Language & religion

Shopi dialects belong to the western group of Bulgarian dialects and are part of Balkan Slavic. The dialect area includes varieties identified by Serbian dialectology as Torlak and northern Macedonian dialects, while the population may identify as Bulgarian, Serbian, Macedonian, or Shopi. The dialects are not uniform. They show three recorded reflexes of the big yus, three reflexes of Old Bulgarian’s syllabic r, and five reflexes of Old Bulgarian’s syllabic l. Western Bulgarian dialects are divided into Southwestern, Northwestern, and Outer Northwestern groups; the Outer Northwestern group is spoken along the border with Serbia and by the Bulgarian minority in the Western Outlands in Serbia.

Shopski speech is described as extremely analytical. It has no cases apart from personal pronouns, definite articles, old Slavic tenses supplemented by inferential forms, and analytical comparative and superlative forms using *po* and *nay*. The preposition *u* is used instead of *v* in one example. The first-person singular pronoun is *ja*, while the third-person forms include *on*, *ona*, *ono*, and *oni*. The sources discuss Bulgarian, Serbian, and Macedonian linguistic classification.
