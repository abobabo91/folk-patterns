## Overview

The Walser are speakers of Walser German dialects, a variety of Highest Alemannic. They originate from the Swiss canton of Valais, particularly the upper Rhône valley, also called the upper Wallis or Upper Valais. Walser communities inhabit Alpine regions of Switzerland and Liechtenstein, as well as parts of Italy and Austria. Their settlements include communities in the cantons of Valais, Ticino and Grisons, and in places such as Triesenberg, Gressoney-La-Trinité, Formazza and Großes Walsertal. Around 1300, Walser groups began migrating from the upper Wallis into other Alpine valleys. The total number of speakers was estimated at 22,000 in 2004, including about 10,000 in Switzerland. Their importance for folk culture lies in the survival and diversification of their isolated mountain dialects and settlement communities.

## Language & religion

Walser German, also called *Walserdeutsch*, and Walliser German, also called *Walliserdeutsch* or locally *Wallisertiitsch*, are groups of Highest Alemannic dialects. They are spoken in parts of Switzerland, Italy, Liechtenstein and Austria. The term Walser refers to speakers whose ancestors migrated into other Alpine valleys in medieval times, while Walliser refers to a speaker from Upper Valais. The dialects are most closely related to those of the Bernese Oberland and Central Switzerland, including Uri, Schwyz, Unterwalden and Glarus. Walser German has preserved archaisms from Old High German in isolated high-mountain valleys and is considered endangered because language shift to French, Italian and Standard German has occurred. In Italy, it is most commonly used privately and within families, while younger people often respond in Italian.
