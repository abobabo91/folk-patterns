## Overview

The Äyrämöiset, also called Evrimeiset by the Russians, were a Finnish language-speaking people of the Saint Petersburg Oblast, with earlier settlement in the Finnish part of the Karelian Isthmus. They were one of the two Finnish-speaking groups of Ingrian Finns in St. Petersburg Oblast, alongside the Savakot. Most Äyrämöiset are Lutherans. Their name derives from the ancient county of Äyräpää, also known as Äkräpää, in the western Karelian Isthmus. The group formed the majority of the population of the Province of Ingria when it was established in the 17th century. Their South Karelian dialect of Finnish, settlement history, and relationship with the Savakot and Izhorians are the cultural features documented by the sources used here.

## Language & religion

The Äyrämöiset were Finnish language-speaking people, and their dialect belonged to the South Karelian dialects of Finnish. Most of the Äyrämöiset are Lutherans. The source states that the name Äyrämöiset, or *äkrämöiset*, comes from the ancient county of Äyräpää, also called Äkräpää, in the western part of the Karelian Isthmus. That territory was part of the kingdom of Sweden after 1323 AD. In earlier times, Äkräs, or Ägräs, was an agricultural deity associated with beans, peas, and hemp and regarded as the mythological forefather of the Äyrämöiset.
