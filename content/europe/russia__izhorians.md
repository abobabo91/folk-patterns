## Overview

The Izhorians are a Finnic indigenous people native to Ingria. Small numbers live in the western part of Ingria, between the Narva and Neva rivers in northwestern Russia. They are also called Ingrians, although that term can also refer to the Ingrian Finns or Baltic Finnic residents of Ingria more generally. In 2002, 327 Izhorians were recorded in Russia, including 177 in Leningrad oblast and 53 in St Petersburg; 812 were recorded in Ukraine in 2001 and 358 in Estonia. Their language belongs to the Northern Finnic group of the Uralic languages. The available sources document their historical movements, language, religion, and population history.

The sources state that the Izhorians are generally Eastern Orthodox and that some pre-Christian traditions also exist.

## Language & religion

Izhorian, also called Ingrian, is close to Karelian and is used primarily by members of the older generation. Along with Finnish, Ludic, Karelian, and Vepsian, it belongs to the Northern Finnic group of the Uralic languages. A Latin-based orthography existed from 1932–1937 and was taught in schools of the Soikinsky Peninsula and the area around the mouth of the Luga River. Several textbooks were published, including a grammar in 1936, but the Izhorian written language was abolished in 1937. The Izhorians and the Votes are generally Eastern Orthodox, unlike the Ingrian Finns, who are generally Lutheran. Some pre-Christian traditions also exist.
