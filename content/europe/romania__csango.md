## Overview

The Csángó are ethnic Hungarians living mostly in the Romanian region of Moldavia, where they have maintained their own dialect, religion, and folk traditions for centuries. Their Roman Catholic faith, unusual in a predominantly Orthodox area, has been central to their identity alongside their Hungarian language. Only about 43,000 people still speak Csángó, with the most archaic form known to roughly 4,000 elderly speakers. The Council of Europe has recognized them as bearers of exceptional European folk culture.

## Material culture

Csángó material culture centers on hand-woven textiles—skirts, belts, headscarves, and household linens—decorated with colorful stripes, diamonds, and geometric patterns in wool, cotton, and silk.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Csángó weavers create patterned textiles in twill and tabby weaves, combining bold stripes and diamonds in reds, blacks, yellows, greens, and blues.

- **Woman's skirt wrap** (*katrinca*) — A black wool twill wrap bordered with brown and multicolored stripes of pink, green, yellow, and blue.
- **Man's wide belt** (*öv*) — A herringbone-pattern twill belt in red, blue, yellow, and green stripes, woven from wool and cotton.
- **Woman's outer headscarf** (*kerpa ruva*) — A long orange tabby-woven rectangle with fine white bands and patterned borders of diamonds and gauze weave.
- **Wall-cover or protector** (*falvedö*) — A multicolored chequered wool tabby in red, black, and yellow against green, pink, and blue wefts.
- **Woman's bag** (*traistă*) — A twill-woven bag in red and polychrome stripes with printed cotton loops and a plaited wool handle.

### Clothing & dress

Women wear white cotton blouses under embroidered waistcoats, with wrapped skirts and headscarves; men wear long cream cotton shirts and hand-woven trousers with wide belts.

- **Woman's sleeveless sheepskin waistcoat** — Brown fleece inside and cream leather outside, embroidered in wool with circular and leaf motifs, fastened with leather toggles.
- **Man's long shirt** — Cream cotton reaching to the knee, with A-line cut and embroidery in yellow, pink, and lilac, plus drawn-thread work.
- **Man's summer trousers** — Hand-woven tabby cotton with a folded diamond crutch panel and white cotton drawstring.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The recorded objects are domestic textiles—wall-covers, cloths, towels, and cushion covers—rather than ceramics or metalwork.

- **Interior decoration cloth** (*kerchief*) — Woven with wide polychrome borders at each warp end for interior decoration.
- **Floor runner** — A twill weave in hemp and wool in cream, grey, brown, and black.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Csángó jewelry consists of glass bead necklaces in bright colors, tied with twisted wool in contrasting hues.

- **Yellow glass bead necklace** — Six double-twisted strands of graduated length in yellow glass, fastened with twisted purple and pale blue wool.
- **Red glass bead necklace** — Five double-twisted strands of red glass beads tied with twisted red wool.
- **Two-color cylinder bead necklace** — Silver beads cased in clear glass, tied with orange, green, and purple wool strands.

## Music & performance

Csángó music follows Hungarian musical traditions, with songs mostly in Hungarian dialect that repeatedly reference Csángó identity.

- **Csángó anthem** — A song that repeatedly refers to Csángó Hungarians.

## Festivals & rituals

Roman Catholic faith, influenced by Orthodox rites, is a foundational pillar of Csángó identity and has distinguished them from the Orthodox majority for centuries.

- **Catholic mass in the Csángó language** — As of 2001, the Vatican hoped the Csángó could celebrate mass in their own dialect as their liturgical language.

## Foodways

The Csángó are historically a rural and agricultural people, farming corn, potatoes, and hemp for fiber.

- **Agricultural crops and stock** — Historically raised sheep and cows and farmed corn, potatoes, and hemp.

## Oral tradition & literature

Very few Csángó today can write in their mother tongue, as the dialect is not taught in Csángó villages; the group has a scholarly record documenting dialect decline.

- **Munich Codex** — A Hussite translation of the New Testament into Hungarian, dated 1466 in Moldavia.
- **Vilmos Tánczos fieldwork** — Documentation of Csángó dialect decline and speaker numbers from 2008–2010, the most recent scholarly record.

## Language & religion

Csángó is a Hungarian dialect now severely endangered, with about 4,000 speakers of the archaic Northern form; Roman Catholicism distinguishes the group from the Orthodox majority.

- **Northern Csángó dialect** — The most archaic form of Csángó, regularly used by about 4,000 speakers, all over fifty years old.
- **Language vitality status** — UNESCO classifies Csángó as 'Severely Endangered,' with speaker numbers declining from 60,000–70,000 in 2001 to roughly 43,000 by 2010.

## Glossary

- *katrinca* — Woman's rectangular skirt wrap woven in twill from wool
- *öv* — Man's wide belt in herringbone twill with red and blue stripes
- *kerpa ruva* — Woman's outer headscarf in orange tabby weave with white bands
- *falvedö* — Rectangular wall-cover or protector woven as multicolored chequered wool tabby
- *traistă* — Woman's bag made from folded twill cloth with polychrome stripes
- *Csángó* — Hungarian ethnic group living in Romania and their dialect
- *Northern Csángó* — The most archaic and conservative form of the Csángó dialect
- *tabby weave* — A basic over-under weaving pattern
- *twill weave* — A weaving pattern creating diagonal lines
- *drawn-thread work* — An embroidery technique where threads are drawn out and remaining threads wrapped
- *Moldavia* — The region in northeastern Romania where most Csángó live
- *Roman Catholic* — The Christian faith of most Csángó, distinguishing them from the Orthodox majority
