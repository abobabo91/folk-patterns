## Overview

Bosniaks are a South Slavic ethnic group and nation native to Bosnia and Herzegovina, where they constitute the largest ethnic group alongside Serbs and Croats. They also constitute significant native communities in Serbia, Montenegro, Croatia and Kosovo, and a diaspora across Europe, the Americas and Oceania. Their common ancestry, culture, history and language emanate from the Bosnian historical region. Bosniaks are traditionally and predominantly Sunni Muslims, although “Bosnian Muslims” is described as an imprecise ethnic designation today. Their history includes Slavic settlement from the 7th to 9th centuries, the medieval Bosnian state and kingdom, Ottoman rule, Islamisation, and the formation of modern national self-identification in the late 20th century.

## Language & religion

Bosniaks are described as a South Slavic nation whose common language emanates from the Bosnian historical region. The sources discuss Bosnian, Slavic, Serbo-Croatian and related linguistic forms, including the historical ethnonym *Bošnjanin* and the modern form *Bošnjak*. They connect Bosniak identity with Bosnia and Herzegovina through *bosanstvo* (Bosnianhood) and *bošnjaštvo* (Bosniakhood). Bosniaks traditionally and predominantly adhere to Sunni Islam. Before and during the medieval period, Bosnia contained Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and independent Bosnian Church communities. The Bosnian Church was known by names including *dobri Bošnjani*, *Krstjani*, *dobri mužje*, *dobri ljudi* and *boni homines*.
