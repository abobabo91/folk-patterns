## Overview

Luxembourgers are a Germanic ethnic group native to their nation state of Luxembourg, where they make up around half of the population. Most ethnic Luxembourgers live in the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, a small landlocked country in Western Europe situated between Germany, France, and Belgium. Their origins are described as Celtic/Gallo-Roman and Germanic (Frankish). They share the culture of Luxembourg and speak Luxembourgish, a West Germanic language. Luxembourgers were historically considered a regional sub-group of ethnic Germans, and some viewed themselves as such until the collapse of the German Confederation. Luxembourg became independent while remaining in personal union with the Netherlands after the Treaty of London in 1839; that union ended in 1890.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources do state that Luxembourg City shared the status of European Capital of Culture with the Romanian town of Sibiu in 2007.

## Language & religion

Luxembourgish is the only native language of Luxembourgers as taught by parents, and it is a West Germanic language. Nearly all Luxembourgers learn French and German in school and can communicate in those languages from an early age. The sources also describe the Transylvanian Saxon dialect as very close to Luxembourgish.
