## Overview

The Jász, also known in English as Jassic, are an Iranian ethnic group of Eastern Iranic descent who have lived in Hungary since the 13th century. They live mainly in Jászság, a region comprising the north-western part of Jász-Nagykun-Szolnok county, whose cultural and political center is Jászberény. The Jász originated as a nomadic Alanic people from the Pontic steppe and came to the Kingdom of Hungary together with the Cumans after their eastern lands were invaded by the Mongol Empire. They settled near the rivers Zagyva and Tarna in the central part of the Pannonian Plain. Their original language, Jassic, belonged to the East Iranian group and was related to modern Ossetian, but became extinct as the Jász adopted Hungarian.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe historical and political observances. The Jász people were granted special privileges and maintained regional autonomy in Jászság; this autonomy was preserved until 1876, when the area populated by the Jász was administratively included in Jász-Nagykun-Szolnok County. In 1995, Jászberény hosted the 250th Anniversary of the Act of Redemption, with the President of Hungary as guest of honor and with foreign dignitaries present.

## Foodways

The only food-related material is linguistic: the Jassic glossary includes words associated with products of agriculture, including types of grain and cattle, and was probably compiled for fiscal or mercantile purposes. The glossary also contains terms translated or associated with bread, meat, cheese, grain, barley, oats, milk, flour, cattle, sheep, goat, and eggs.

## Oral tradition & literature

The only recorded Jassic linguistic document is a one-page glossary containing 34 words. It was found in the 1950s in the Hungarian National Széchényi Library and was interpreted with the help of Ossetian analogues from the Digor dialect. The glossary was probably compiled for fiscal or mercantile purposes and is concerned mainly with agricultural products, including types of grain and cattle.

## Language & religion

Jassic is the common English name for the original language of the Jász. It was an extinct dialect of Ossetian and belonged to the East Iranian language group. The Jász adopted Hungarian after their original language disappeared, reportedly by the 16th century. The only literary record is the one-page glossary found in the Hungarian National Széchényi Library in the 1950s; the language was reconstructed with the help of Ossetian analogies, especially analogues from the Digor dialect.
