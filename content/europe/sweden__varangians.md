## Overview

The Varangians were Viking warriors, traders, and settlers, mostly from present-day Sweden. From the 8th and 9th centuries, they settled in territories corresponding to present-day Belarus, Russia, and Ukraine, helping establish Kievan Rus' and the principalities of Polotsk and Turov. They operated along the Volga, Dnieper, and Dniester trade routes, linking the Baltic, Caspian Sea, Black Sea, Constantinople, the Islamic world, and Byzantium. Varangians also served as mercenaries and formed the Byzantine Varangian Guard.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Ahmad ibn Fadlan described the Rus as wearing neither coats (*qurtāq*) nor caftans, but a garment covering one side of the body while leaving one hand free. He described each man as carrying an axe, sword, and knife.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

The sources mention axes, swords, knives, broad-bladed and grooved swords, iron, silver, copper, and gold brooches, Byzantine crosses carved on runestones, and silver coins. Varangians traded furs, honey, slaves, amber, Frankish swords, and walrus ivory, exchanging these goods for Arabic silver coins called dirhams.

### Jewelry & body adornment

Ahmad ibn Fadlan wrote that Rus women wore circular brooches on their bosoms, made of iron, silver, copper, or gold according to their husbands’ wealth and social position. Each brooch had a ring containing a knife attached to the bosom. He also described the Rus men as tattooed in dark green from the tips of their toes to their necks.

## Music & performance

They mention the Norse sagas as a source referring to Gardariki and record Ahmad ibn Fadlan’s account of a ship-burning funeral ceremony, including dialogue and observations of the ceremony.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources used describe a ship-burning funeral ceremony observed by Ahmad ibn Fadlan during his 921–922 travels from Baghdad to Bulghar. He presented it as a complicated ceremony and recorded details of its dialogue and participants. The sources also mention the Christianization of Kievan Rus' in 988 and the conversion of many Varangians from Norse paganism to Orthodox Christianity.

## Foodways

They state that Rus merchants sold furs, honey, slaves, amber, Frankish swords, and walrus ivory, and exchanged these goods for Arabic silver coins.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention Norse sagas, in which the areas north of the Black Sea were known as Gardariki. They also preserve Ahmad ibn Fadlan’s first-person account of the Rus and their daily habits, including the ship-burning funeral ceremony. The Primary Chronicle records that a group known as the Rus settled in Novgorod in 862 under Rurik, and that Rurik’s relative Oleg conquered Kiev in 882.

## Language & religion

They state that Medieval Greek Βάραγγος *Várangos* and Old East Slavic варягъ *varjagŭ* derive from Old Norse *væringi*, associated with “pledge” or “faith” and “companion.” Varangians initially followed Norse paganism; many later converted to Orthodox Christianity, culminating in the Christianization of Kievan Rus' in 988. The sources also mention carved runes on Scandinavian runestones and Byzantine crosses on some stones.
