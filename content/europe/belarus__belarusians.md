## Overview

Belarusians are an East Slavic ethnic group native to Belarus. They share a common culture, ancestry, history, and traditionally speak the Belarusian language. More than 9 million people proclaim Belarusian ethnicity worldwide, including nearly 7.99 million residents of Belarus. Belarusians also live in Ukraine, Poland, the Russian Federation, Lithuania, the United States, Brazil, Canada, the Baltic states, and EU countries. Their historical territory includes the upper Dnieper River basin and the Sozh, Berezina, Pripyat, and Western Dvina river regions. The majority adhere to Eastern Orthodoxy. ## Language & religion

Belarusian and Russian are the two official languages of Belarus. Belarusians traditionally speak Belarusian, and Russian became co-official with Belarusian after the 1995 Belarusian referendum. Historically, Ruthenian, Polish, and Latin were spoken by parts of the Ruthenian nobility, while Ruthenian continued in some official contexts until the late 17th century, when it was replaced by Polish. The majority of Belarusians adhere to Eastern Orthodoxy. The sources also describe Greek Catholics of the Ruthenian Uniate Church, created by the Union of Brest in 1596.
