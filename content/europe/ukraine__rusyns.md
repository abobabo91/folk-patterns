## Overview

Rusyns, also known as Carpatho-Rusyns, Carpatho-Russians, Ruthenians, or Rusnaks, are an East Slavic ethnic group native to the Eastern Carpathians of Central and Eastern Europe. Their historical region includes Subcarpathian Rus’, Prešov Region, the Lemko Region, and Maramureş. Rusyns also live in Croatia, Hungary, Poland, Romania, Serbia, Slovakia, Ukraine, and elsewhere in Europe and North America. They speak Rusyn, an East Slavic language variety treated either as a distinct language or as a dialect of Ukrainian. The majority are Eastern Catholics, while a minority practices Eastern Orthodoxy. Estimates in the source range from approximately 1.5 million Rusyns in Europe today to around 110,000 officially identified in recent national censuses.

## Music & performance

The sources state that Rusyns in interwar Czechoslovakia were permitted to maintain their own schools, theater, and anthem. Aleksander Dukhnovich wrote the unofficial Rusyn National Anthem, “I was, am, and will be a Rusyn.” The source also records that Rusyn communities in the interwar period developed their culture under liberal conditions in Czechoslovakia. Four of the 663 congress delegates who had adopted the manifesto and were still alive attended: F. Sabov, O. Lohoida, M. Moldavchuk, and J. Matlakh. ## Oral tradition & literature

The sources connect Rusyn identity with an enduring literary and political language. The term Rusyn derives from the archaic ethnonym “Rus’,” and Rusyn identity and language became subjects of political dispute, particularly between Russophile and Ukrainophile directions. Aleksander Dukhnovich wrote the unofficial Rusyn National Anthem. The sources also mention the mythical ruler Laborec in accounts of early Carpathian history and identify Rusyn press and cultural organizations in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries.

## Language & religion

Rusyn is an East Slavic language variety variously treated as either a distinct language or a dialect of Ukrainian. Its regional and historical names include Rusyn, Rusnak, Lemko, Ruthenian, Carpatho-Rusyn, and Carpatho-Ruthenian. The source records several regional designations, including Vojvodinian Rusyns, Bachka-Srem Rusyns, and Uhro-Rusyn. Rusyns are predominantly Eastern Catholic, while a minority practices Eastern Orthodoxy. The most active stratum of the nineteenth-century Rusyn national movement was Greek-Catholic clergy. In Vojvodina, Rusyn was one of five official languages under the Constitutional Law of 1969.
