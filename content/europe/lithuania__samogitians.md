## Overview

Samogitians are the inhabitants of Samogitia, an ethnographic region of Lithuania. They lived in western Lithuania and were closely related to Semigallians and Curonians. Many speak the Samogitian language, which in Lithuania is mostly considered a dialect of the Lithuanian language together with the Aukštaitian dialect. The Samogitian language differs the most from standard Lithuanian. Whether Samogitians constitute a distinct ethnic group or a subset of Lithuanians varies. During the Lithuanian census of 2011, 2,169 people declared their ethnicity as Samogitian; 53.9% of them lived in Telšiai County. In the 1897 Russian Empire census, 448,022 people declared themselves Samogitians rather than Lithuanians.

## Festivals & rituals

Samogitians became the last group of Europeans to convert to Christianity in 1413. On 13 July 1260, they decisively defeated the joint forces of the Teutonic Knights from Prussia and the Livonian Order from Livonia in the Battle of Durbe.
