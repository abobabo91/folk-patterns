## Overview

The Flemish, or Flemings, are Dutch-speaking inhabitants of Flanders and form Belgium’s largest linguistic and cultural community, accounting for about 60% of the country’s population. Flanders is the Dutch-speaking northern part of Belgium, mainly flat and incorporating the whole Belgian coast on the North Sea. It borders the French department of Nord, the Dutch provinces of Zeeland, North Brabant and Limburg, and Walloon provinces including Hainaut, Walloon Brabant and Liège. More than half of Belgium’s population lives there: 6,821,770 people, or 58% of 11,763,650 Belgian inhabitants, as of January 2024. Flemish is a Low Franconian dialect cluster of Dutch. Flemish culture is associated with Dutch language and customs, urban history, weaving, painting, music, foodways and regional traditions.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

*Flemish cloth weaving* — Flanders’s trading towns became major centres of weaving wool from neighbouring lands into cloth for domestic use and export. Flemish weavers also migrated to England, where they contributed to the cloth and woollen industries.

*Flemish bleaching* — The town of Whitefield near Bury claims that its name refers to Flemish cloth weavers who laid their cloths in the sun to bleach them.

*Flemish painting* — Flemish painting flourished from the early 15th century until the 17th century. The Flemish Primitives popularized oil paint, while Flemish Baroque painting flourished in the 17th century, especially in the Antwerp school.

*Early Netherlandish painting* — Up to about 1520, painting across the Low Countries was commonly treated as Early Netherlandish painting. Its Flemish south was dominant, although painters from the north were also important.

*Flemish Renaissance painting* — Antwerp became the centre of Dutch and Flemish Renaissance painting, which continued until about 1580 or later.

*Flemish Baroque painting* — The first half of the 17th century was dominated by Rubens. Anthony van Dyck and Jacob Jordaens were also important figures in the Antwerp school.

*Franco-Flemish School* — The Franco-Flemish School of musical composition flourished from approximately the same period as Flemish painting and was also known as the Dutch School.

**Motif vocabulary.** The sources name a black lion with red claws and tongue on a yellow field as the emblem of the Flemish Community.

### Clothing & dress

The sources describe Flemish cloth weaving and the migration of skilled Flemish cloth weavers.

### Architecture

Flemish architectural history is connected with the trading cities of Ghent, Bruges, Ypres and Antwerp. During the late Middle Ages, these cities formed one of the richest and most urbanised parts of Europe, and their sophisticated culture produced impressive art and architecture. Flemish architects Anthonis van Obbergen and Willem van den Blocke migrated to Poland, where they designed mannerist structures; Willem van den Blocke also sculpted decorated epitaphs and tombs.

## Music & performance

Flemish and other Netherlandish composers played a major role in European art music during the 15th and 16th centuries. The Franco-Flemish School was a current of vocal polyphony that played a central role in European art music. Johannes Ciconia, Guillaume Dufay, Gilles Binchois, Johannes Ockeghem, Josquin des Prez, Jacob Obrecht, Pierre de La Rue, Alexander Agricola, Gaspar van Weerbeke and Orlande de Lassus are named among influential composers connected with the Belgian area of the Low Countries.

The migration and influence of Netherlandish composers reached its height roughly between 1480 and 1520, when musicians from the Low Countries were recruited by courts and cathedrals across Europe. The music of Josquin des Prez was considered an aesthetic model for much of the 16th-century High Renaissance. Orlande de Lassus became one of the most versatile composers of the century and was described as one of the best-known and most widely admired musicians in Europe.

In later Belgian music, the sources mention Flemish singer Helmut Lotti, blues artist Roland van Campenhout and jazz musicians including Toots Thielemans. From the late 1960s, Flanders experienced a revival of traditional music, and to a lesser degree traditional dances, through musicians such as Wannes Van de Velde, Willem Vermandere, Walter De Buck, Hubert Boone and Herman De Wit. These musicians inspired later folk musicians through written and recorded publications, festivals, summer academies and master classes.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources mention several religious events among Flemish-descended people in the Azores, including the *imperios* and the feast of the Cult of the Holy Spirit. They also state that the suffering of the First World War is remembered by Flemish organisations during the yearly Yser pilgrimage in Diksmuide at the monument of the Yser Tower. They mention that sandwiches are traditionally eaten on Sundays or after funerals, and that speculoos is traditionally baked for consumption on or just before Saint Nicholas Day, *Sinterklaas* in Dutch and *la Saint-Nicolas* in French, on 6 December.

## Foodways

Belgian cuisine combines French cuisine with more hearty Flemish fare and reflects neighbouring French, German and Dutch cuisines. Ingredients typical in Belgian dishes include potatoes, leeks, grey shrimp, white asparagus, Belgian endive, horse meat and local beer. Regional examples include *waterzooi* from Ghent, couque biscuit from Dinant and *tarte au riz* from Verviers.

Named Flemish or Belgian dishes include *carbonade flamande*, also called *Vlaamse karbonaden*, *stoofvlees* or *stoverij*. It is a beef stew made with Belgian beer rather than red wine and is generally served with bread or fries, mustard and beer. *Moules-frites*, or *mosselen met friet*, consists of mussels cooked or steamed with onions and celery and served with Belgian fries. *Waterzooi* is a stew and soup of chicken or fish, vegetables, cream and eggs, usually associated with Ghent. *Chicons au gratin*, or *gegratineerd witloof*, consists of Belgian endives in béchamel sauce with cheese, often wrapped with ham. *Stoemp* is mashed potato with vegetables, usually carrots or cabbages, often served with sausages. *Lokerse paardenworst* consists of minced horse sausages with peeled tomatoes, onion, celery and herbs and originates from Lokeren.

Belgian fries are deep-fried chipped potatoes, traditionally fried twice. They are sold in *friteries*, *frietkot* or *frituur*, and traditionally served in a *cornet de frites* or *puntzak*. Belgium’s beer tradition includes many styles; in 2011, 1,132 varieties were being produced. Traditional dishes use regional beer, including lambic in Brussels and De Koninck in Antwerp.

## Oral tradition & literature

 They do describe a Flemish literary and political movement: the Flemish Movement developed from earlier anti-French feelings of injustice and helped facilitate the de jure social, political and linguistic equality of Dutch from the end of the 19th century. Jan Verlooy is named as a late 18th-century writer whose writings criticized the Southern Francophile elites.

## Language & religion

Flemish is a Low Franconian dialect cluster of Dutch. It is also called Flemish Dutch, Belgian Dutch or Southern Dutch. The term can refer to Dutch spoken and written in Flanders, the intermediate language *Tussentaal*, non-standardized dialects and regiolects, or dialects associated with the former County of Flanders, including West Flemish, East Flemish, Zeelandic Flanders and French Flanders. Brabantian and East Flemish are classified as Dutch dialects, while Western Flemish is treated by Glottolog as a separate language within the Southwestern Dutch family.

Dutch is Belgium’s majority language and the only official language of the Flemish Region. French has continued to be spoken by some citizens, and Jewish groups have spoken Yiddish in Antwerp for centuries. Approximately 75% of Flemish people are assumed Roman Catholic by baptism, although fewer than 8% attend Mass regularly, and nearly half of the inhabitants of Flanders are agnostic or atheist. A 2006 inquiry found that 55% called themselves religious and 36% believed that God created the universe.
