## Overview

Nağaybäks are an ethnoreligious group of Volga Tatars in Russia, recognized as a separate people under Russian legislation. Most live in the Nagaybaksky and Chebarkulsky Districts of Chelyabinsk Oblast. They speak the Nagaibak dialect, a sub-dialect of the Tatar language’s middle dialect. Russian and Tatar historians usually treat them as an integral part of Volga Tatars, although a minority considers them a separate ethnicity. In the 1989 Russian census, 11,200 people identified themselves as Nağaybäks; this fell to 9,600 in 2002. Most Nağaybäks are Christian and were largely converted during the 18th century. Their history includes service as border keepers, incorporation into the Cossack estate, defense of Menzelinsk Fort, and later settlement in villages named after battles of the Napoleonic Wars.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

Traditional Nağaybäk female clothing is similar to that of the Keräşen Tatars. Male clothing contains many elements of the Cossack uniform.

### Architecture

They record Nagaybak Fort, built in 1736 in the village of Nagaybak, and identify the fort as a hub of the “newly baptized,”.

## Festivals & rituals

They do record religious conversion during the 18th century and state that Nağaybäks are mostly Christian. Another group that settled in present-day Orenburg Oblast converted back to Islam by the beginning of the 20th century and was reassimilated into the Tatar ethnos.

## Oral tradition & literature

The historical account records several theories about Nağaybäk origins, including descent from Nogay and Kipchak people, descent from Volga Tatars baptized after the fall of the Kazan Khanate, and descent from Tatarized Finno-Ugric peoples.

## Language & religion

Nağaybäks speak the Nagaibak dialect, a sub-dialect of the middle dialect of the Tatar language. Most Nağaybäks are Christian and were largely converted during the 18th century. The sources describe a historical connection with the Russian Empire: during the Tatar and Bashkir revolts of the late 17th and early 18th century, the Nağaybäks remained loyal to the Russian Empire, after which the government transferred them into the Cossack estate and assigned them to defend Menzelinsk Fort.
