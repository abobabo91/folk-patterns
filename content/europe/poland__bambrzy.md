## Overview

The Bambrzy, also known as Poznań Bambergians, are Poles partly descended from Germans who moved from the area of Bamberg in Upper Franconia, Germany, to villages surrounding Poznań, Poland. They settled villages devastated during the Great Northern War and a subsequent epidemic of plague. About 700 Germans arrived in several waves, although surviving contracts record at least 450 to 500 men and women, and later documents suggest as many as 900 people came in four waves. The sources describe their Catholic settlement, gradual integration, Polish-language education, mixed marriages, and opposition to Germanisation. They are especially associated with extravagant female dress.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

A well-known aspect of Bamber culture is extravagant female dress.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe Catholic settlement and attendance at Catholic masses.

## Language & religion

The settlers were required to be Catholic. Many learned Polish in order to attend Catholic masses, and their children learned Polish at school. The sources describe the group as partly descended from Germans from the area of Bamberg. Integration was voluntary, and some families were already Polonised by 1800.
