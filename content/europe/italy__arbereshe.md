## Overview

The Arbëreshë are an Albanian ethnolinguistic minority historically settled in southern and insular Italy, especially Calabria and Sicily, with communities also in Abruzzo, Apulia, Basilicata, Campania and Molise. They descend from Albanian refugees who arrived in successive movements from Albania, Epirus, Attica, Thebes, the Peloponnese—then called Morea—and other islands between the fourteenth and eighteenth centuries. Italy has 50 communities of Arbëreshë origin and culture, consisting of 41 municipalities and 9 villages, with a population of about 100,000; communities also exist in the Americas and central-northern Europe. Their cultural identity is maintained through Arbërisht, the Byzantine religious rite, traditional costume, art, food, song, dance and village institutions. Their settlements are often known by both Italian and Arbëresh names.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

The sources identify traditional costume as one of the features through which Arbëreshë culture has been preserved. No source describes ceremonial costume separately from everyday clothing.

### Architecture

A distinctive social and spatial form is the *Gjitonia*, a neighborhood structure typical of Arbëresh communities. Its name comes from Greek γειτονιά (geitonía). It functions as a microsystem around which life in the *horë*, or village, revolves. A Gjitonia is commonly organized around a small square toward which the alleys are oriented, with buildings opening toward a larger square, or *shesh*, on diagonal angles. The name is usually taken from the families living there. The sources describe the Gjitonia as an ancient social structure in which hospitality and solidarity between neighborhood families created relationships described by the phrase *Gjitoni gjirì*, “neighborhood relatives.”

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention a wooden, floor-level table called *zbrilla* in connection with the traditional consumption of *Strangujët* on 14 September, but provide no further description of its construction or cultural distribution.

## Music & performance

The memory of Skanderbeg and his exploits has been preserved among the Arbëreshë through songs forming a **Skanderbeg cycle**.

The religious communities of the Byzantine rite are central institutions in Arbëreshë life. The Italo-Albanian Catholic Church includes the Eparchy of Lungro degli Italo-Albanesi, the Eparchy of Piana degli Albanesi and the Territorial Abbacy of Santa Maria of Grottaferrata. Arbëresh was used in written form in the Italo-Albanian Church, and the sources state that Arbëresh liturgies include ancient Greek elements. The Basilian Order of Grottaferrata, the Collegine Sisters of Sacra Famiglia, Piccole operaie dei Sacri Cuori and the Basilian Sisters Daughters of Saint Macrina are named Byzantine-rite institutions.

## Dance & theatre

Arbëreshë people have preserved Albanian traditional dances known as *vallje*, including the Albanian sword dance. The sources identify these dances as part of the group’s cultural traditions.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe several food-related festivals and religious observances. *Festa e Kryqit Shejt*, the Exaltation of the Cross, is observed on 14 September; traditionally, families in Piana degli Albanesi consumed *Strangujët* seated around the wooden table called *zbrilla*. *Festa e Sënda Lluçisë* is associated with eating *Grurët*. *Festa e Shën Zefit*, Saint Joseph’s Day, is associated with *Milanisë* and, in Spezzano Albanese, with the preparation of *vecchiarelle*; the latter is observed on March 19th.

Carnival is represented by *E Mart e Madh*, on whose eve *Loshkat* and *Petullat* are eaten. Easter observances include *Panaret*, basket-shaped or braided breads decorated with eggs, and *Nucia*, a doll-shaped cake with an egg representing the face. In the Byzantine Catholic tradition described by the sources, Easter follows 40 days of fasting and begins with a light meal after the midnight liturgy on Saturday. The Easter meal includes *Panaret* in the sources’ account of Piana degli Albanesi, while *Verdhët* is prepared during Easter in several food traditions.

They mention *Kalimera*, a decorated palm branch used during Easter celebrations and weddings.

## Foodways

Arbëreshë cuisine combines Albanian dishes with culinary practices and ingredients of southern Italy, including Sicilian, Calabrian and Lucanian influences. The sources describe food traditions in Calabria, Basilicata, Sicily, Molise, Campania, Abruzzo and Apulia. Common ingredients and foods include wheat flour, semolina, eggs, olive oil, lard, ricotta, sheep cheese, pork, lamb, goat, sardines, anchovies, beans, chickpeas, wild fennel, vegetables, figs, honey and local herbs.

Pasta varieties include *Strangujët*, gnocchi served with tomato sauce, basil and local cheese; *Dromësat*, small flour pellets cooked directly in sauce; *Shtridhëlat*, ribbon-like noodles served with chickpeas and beans; *Makarrunet*, long fusilli-like pasta formed around a dried willow stick; and *Tumacët*, handmade eggless ribbons. *Tumact me tulez* from Barile consists of handmade tagliatelle with tomatoes, anchovies, garlic, white wine and basil, finished with fried breadcrumbs, walnuts and parsley. In the Pollino massif, *strangùle me nenezë* is served with fresh goat ricotta and *nenesa*, while *fillatjel* may be served with tomato sauce, porcini mushrooms or Calabrian ’nduja.

Other dishes include *likëngë*, pork sausages flavored with salt, pepper and fennel seed; *Udhose* and *Gjizë*, homemade cheese and ricotta; *Groshët*, a soup of fava beans, chickpeas and haricot beans; *luljékuq ma fazuljé*, corn poppy leaves cooked with beans; and *bathë e çikour*, mashed fava beans with wild chicory. Sweets and festive foods include *Kanojët*, *giuggiulea*, *kanarikuj*, *cruscoli*, *maiatka*, *kasolle megijze*, *pitta*, *turdile*, *Panaret*, *Nucia* and *cullurielli*.

## Oral tradition & literature

The songs of the **Skanderbeg cycle** preserve the memory of Skanderbeg and his exploits.

The earliest work of Italo-Albanian literature identified in the sources is *E mbsuama e krështerë* by the Sicilian archpriest Luca Matranga, dated 1567–1619 in the biographical listing and described as a simple religious translation in Arbëresh. Giulio Variboba, also known as Jul Variboba, lived from 1724–1788 and is described as a major poet of the eighteenth century. Nicola Chetta, also known as Nikollë Keta, lived from 1740–1803 and wrote in Albanian and Greek; the source states that he composed the first Albanian sonnet in 1777. Girolamo de Rada, or Jeronim De Rada, is identified as the foremost figure among Arbëresh writers and a leading figure of the Albanian nationalist movement in nineteenth-century Italy.

Since the 1980s, efforts have been organized to preserve Arbërisht and its cultural heritage. Giuseppe Schirò Di Maggio produced the schoolbooks *Udha e Mbarë* and *Udhëtimi*, used in schools in Piana degli Albanesi.

## Language & religion

Arbërisht, also called Arbëresh, is an Albanian linguistic variety derived from Old Tosk Albanian varieties spoken in southern Albania and Greece. It developed in Italy through contact with neighboring Italo-Romance communities and retains medieval Albanian features from before the Ottoman invasion of Albania in the fifteenth century, together with Greek vocabulary and pronunciation. The varieties are closely related but are not always entirely mutually intelligible. Named varieties include Siculo-Arbëresh, Cosenza, Basilicata, Campania, Molisan-Arbëresh and Apulio-Arbëresh.

Arbëresh uses the Albanian alphabet. It was commonly called *Albanese* in Italy until the 1990s and was mostly spoken until the 1980s, apart from its written use in the Italo-Albanian Byzantine Church. Italian law number 482/99 protects the historic linguistic minorities, while regional recognition is described for Calabria, Basilicata and Molise.

Most Arbëreshë communities adhere to the Italo-Albanian Catholic Church, an Eastern Catholic Church using the Byzantine rite. The church is described as the main organization maintaining religious, ethnic, linguistic and traditional identity.
