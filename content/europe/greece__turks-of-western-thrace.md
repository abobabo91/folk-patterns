## Overview

Turks of Western Thrace (*Batı Trakya Türkleri*) are ethnic Turks who live in Western Thrace, in the province of East Macedonia and Thrace in Northern Greece. Their strongest presence is in the Komotini (*Gümülcine*) and Xanthi (*İskeçe*) departments, while they are scarcely present in the Evros prefecture. According to the Greek census of 1991, approximately 50,000 people of Turkish origin lived in Western Thrace, within a Muslim minority of approximately 98,000. Other estimates place the Muslim community between 90,000 and 120,000. Turkish is spoken alongside Greek. The community matters ethnographically because its identity, institutions and cultural rights have been shaped by the Lausanne Treaty, which defines the Muslim communities of Western Thrace on the basis of religion rather than ethnicity.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that Turkish-language newspapers, magazines and books are part of a contested media environment: some sources say publications from Turkey are not allowed entry into Western Thrace, while other sources state that the minority has full and independent access to its own media from Turkey.

## Language & religion

According to Ethnologue, in 1976 Turkish was spoken by 128,000 people in Greece, most of them in the Western Thrace portion of East Macedonia and Thrace; Greek was also widely used. The Treaty of Lausanne defines the rights of the Muslim communities in Western Thrace on the basis of religion rather than ethnicity. The Greek government generally refers to the population as the “Muslim Minority of Western Thrace” or “Greek Muslims” and does not recognize Turks as a separate ethnic minority in Western Thrace. The community is entitled to freedom of religion and to religious and charitable institutions, although the Turkish community has reported restrictions concerning mosques, muftis and charitable foundations.
