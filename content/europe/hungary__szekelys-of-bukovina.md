## Overview

The Székelys of Bukovina are a small Hungarian ethnic community with a complex history. They live today in the Tolna and Baranya counties of Hungary; in Hunedoara County, Transylvania and Suceava County, Bukovina, both in Romania; and in the Serbian province of Vojvodina. Their ancestors migrated from Transylvania to Bukovina in the second half of the 18th century, where they established new villages and retained distinctive culture and folk traditions into the 20th century. The population of the Bukovina Székelys tripled during the 19th century, reaching 9,887 in 1880 and about 16,000 by the end of the 1930s. Their isolation preserved an archaic Hungarian folklore.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe the *Siculicidium*, or Massacre of the Székelys, as a historical event rather than a festival or ritual. Székely protests against forced military conscription were forcibly dispersed at a gathering at *Madéfalva*, today *Siculeni*, by the Austrian General *Josef Siskovics* on 7 January 1764; more than 400 Székelys died.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources characterize the Bukovina Székelys as maintaining an archaic Hungarian folklore because of their isolation. The supplied bibliography includes works concerning their culture, life, and history, including *A bukovinai székelyek kultúrájáról* and *A bukovinai andrásfalvi székelyek élete és története Madéfalvától napjainkig*.

## Language & religion

The Székelys of Bukovina are identified as a Hungarian ethnic community. The sources state that, after Bukovina became part of Romania under the 1920 Treaty of Trianon, there was no teaching in or of Hungarian in their schools. After the 1941 resettlement to Bačka, those from *Andrásfalva* belonged to the Reformed Church, while the others were Roman Catholics.
