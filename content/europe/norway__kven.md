## Overview

The Kvens are a Balto-Finnic ethnic group in Northern Norway. Their settlements developed along the fjords of Finnmark and northern Troms, in the interior of Finnmark, and in places including Vadsø, Alta, Børselv, Nordreisa, Storfjord Municipality, and Porsanger Municipality. Kven migration came largely from Finnish-speaking areas in the Torne Valley, northern Ostrobothnia, and Kainuu between the 17th and 19th centuries. Estimates of the Kven population vary from about 10,000 to 15,000 in a parliamentary inquiry to 30,000–50,000 from Kven organizations. Kven is a Finnic language closely related to Meänkieli and the Peräpohjola dialects of Finnish. Kven culture is significant in folk-cultural study because it combines Finnish-related language and settlement traditions with northern Norwegian livelihoods, migration history, and cultural revitalization.

## Material culture

### Textile & pattern traditions

**Kven costume** (*Kväänipuku*) was designed in the 1990s and the beginning of the 2000s. It builds on older Kven fashions and is intended to strengthen Kven identity. Much of the costume is simple white, a color associated in the source with the historical use of white and with the reputed cleanliness of the Kvens.

### Clothing & dress

The documented Kven clothing tradition is the Kven costume, or *Kväänipuku*. It was designed in the 1990s and the beginning of the 2000s, while drawing on older Kven fashions. Its simple white color has both historical associations and an identity-forming role. The sources state that silverware is an important part of Kven clothing and that the position of *kvensølvesmed*, Norwegian for “kven silver smith,” is important.

## Music & performance

The sources document a Kven-language popular-music recording rather than a broad musical system. **Kadonu Loru** is described as the only pop music single ever recorded in the Kven language. It is based on an old Kven nursery rhyme about making sausages. The artists are Karine Jacobsen and Kine Johansen, respectively from Børselv and Lakselv, and the single was published by Iđut.

The Kven Finn Association is active in reviving folk music traditions and documenting the history of Finnish-speaking people throughout Fennoscandinavia.

## Festivals & rituals

**Baaski** is a Kven culture festival held in Nordreisa Municipality. Its first festival took place in June 2007, and it was intended to be an annual event. Nordreisa municipality was responsible for organizing it, and Johanne Gaup was the first festival director.

The Kven flag was hoisted at Kiruna City Hall in Sweden on 16 March 2013 at 11:00 in celebration and honour of the first annual Day of the Kvens. The date was associated with the signing in 1328 of a state treaty between Sweden and the Birkarls, known as the Tälje Charter, or “Tälje stadga” in Swedish. The treaty guaranteed the Birkarls’ trading rights in the north.

## Foodways

The sources state that Kven livelihoods often combined agriculture with fishing in fjords and rivers. They also describe the Kven settlers as farmers and craftsmen, and identify areas suited for agriculture as preferred settlement locations. The Kven nursery rhyme underlying **Kadonu Loru** concerns making sausages.

## Oral tradition & literature

Historical references to the Kvens appear in Norse and Anglo-Saxon literature from the 9th to 13th centuries. **Egil’s Saga** describes the Kvens as a people living east of the Norwegians, sometimes acting as allies and at other times as adversaries. The term Kven also appears in Ohthere’s tales from the 800s, alongside the terms Finn and Norwegian. The area associated with the Kvens was called Kvenland, which possibly referred to the flat areas of the Bay of Bothnia.

**Kadonu Loru** is based on an old Kven nursery rhyme about making sausages. Contemporary preservation efforts include the work of the Norwegian Kven Organization, the Kven Institute, the Kven Language Board, Ruijan Kaiku, and organizations involved in reviving folk music traditions and documenting Finnish-speaking history in Fennoscandinavia.

## Language & religion

Kven, also called *kvääni*, *kainu*, or Norwegian *kvensk*, is a Finnic language spoken in the northernmost parts of Norway by the Kven people. It is closely related to Meänkieli in the Torne Valley and to the Peräpohjola dialects of Finnish. The status of Kven as a distinct language rather than a Finnish dialect has been debated. In 2005 it received minority-language status in Norway under the European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages.

There are about 1,500 to 10,000 known native speakers, while government estimates place the number of people speaking Kven between 2,000 and 8,000 depending on the criteria used. Most native speakers are over 60, and people under 30 rarely speak or know the language. The Kven Language Board was formed in 2007, and the Kven Language Council was founded in 2008. The council developed a written standard using Finnish orthography.
