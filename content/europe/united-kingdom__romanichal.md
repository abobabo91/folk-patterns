## Overview

The Romanichal are a Romani subgroup in the United Kingdom, also known as English Gypsies. Romanichal living in England, Scotland, and Wales are part of the Gypsy (Romani), Roma, and Traveller community. Nearly all Romanichal in Great Britain live in England, with smaller communities in South Wales, Northeast Wales, and the Scottish Borders. Their origins are traced to South Asia, likely in the regions of present-day Punjab, Rajasthan, and Sindh. Romanichal culture places importance on family and extended family, traditional gender roles, birth and death rituals, hygiene and household cleanliness, respect for older generations, and a traditionally nomadic lifestyle.

## Material culture

### Clothing & dress

They state that Romanichal women historically washed clothing in rivers, keeping upper-body garments further upstream from underwear and lower-body garments.

### Architecture

Romanichal traditionally built *bender* tents from bent hazel branches covered with canvas or tarpaulin. From around the mid- to late-19th century, they used wagons with living spaces called *vardos*, often brightly and colorfully decorated inside and outside. Today, most Romanichal families live in houses of bricks and mortar, while others live in caravans, static caravans, trailers, or vardos.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

They mention horse-drawn carts, wagons, caravans, trailers, utility blocks, washing machines, and household goods and services sold door to door.

## Music & performance

The sources state that the Romanichal are part of the wider Romani community and that International Romani Day commemorates the inaugural World Romani Congress, held in London in 1971.

## Dance & theatre

They mention no named dance or theatrical form belonging to the group.

## Festivals & rituals

Important Romanichal cultural celebrations include International Romani Day, which commemorates the inaugural World Romani Congress held in London in 1971. Romanichal culture also places emphasis on birth and death rituals, family and extended family, traditional gender roles, hygiene and household cleanliness, and respect for older generations. Older members of the community may be referred to as “aunts” and “uncles,” a tradition also found in many Asian cultures. The majority of Romanichal in the United Kingdom identify as Christian, and spirituality or religion typically plays a significant role in their culture and celebrations.

## Foodways

They describe agricultural work involving seed sowing, planting potatoes and fruit trees, weeding, and harvesting crops.

## Oral tradition & literature

They state that Samuel Rid wrote two books about Romanichal in the early 17th century.

## Language & religion

Many Romanichal speak Angloromani, a mixed language combining English syntax and grammar with Romani vocabulary. Traditional Romanes, an Indo-Aryan language, was largely replaced in England by English and Angloromani in the 19th century. Two Angloromani dialects are identified: Southern Angloromani and Northern Angloromani. Southern Angloromani is spoken in the Southeast, Southwest, Midlands, East Anglia, and South Wales; Northern Angloromani is spoken in the Northeast, Northwest, Yorkshire, the Scottish Borders, and Northeast of Wales. The majority of Romanichal identify as Christian. Their social customs have traditionally been influenced by *marimé*, or *mochadi*, meaning ritual impurity.
