## Overview

The Normans (Norman: *Normaunds*; French: *Normands*; Latin: *Nortmanni* or *Normanni*) were a population that arose in the medieval Duchy of Normandy through intermingling between Norse Viking settlers and local people of West Francia. Their settlements were concentrated around the lower Seine valley, including Roumois and Pays de Caux, and later extended west into the Cotentin Peninsula and beyond the Seine. The Norman identity developed during the first half of the 10th century and continued to change over the centuries. The Normans adopted the language, religion, social customs and martial doctrine of the West Franks while retaining a distinct warrior ethos. Their cultural influence extended from Normandy to England, southern Italy, Sicily, the Crusader states, Iberia, Scotland, Wales, Ireland, north Africa and the Canary Islands.

### Clothing & dress

### Architecture

Norman architecture is described as a distinctive cultural feature, especially Romanesque architecture. The sources name castles and cathedrals associated with Norman rule, including William Iron Arm’s citadel at Squillace and Roger II’s Cappella Palatina at Palermo. These buildings are presented as part of the Norman legacy in southern Italy, where architecture accompanied the region’s Norman history.

## Festivals & rituals

 They do document religious and political changes associated with the settlement of Normandy. Rollo accepted baptism and conversion to Christianity and swore fealty to King Charles III after the treaty of Saint-Clair-sur-Epte. The descendants of the Vikings replaced the Norse religion with Catholicism and adopted the Christianity and language of the local population.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources describe Norman French as an important literary language and identify surviving regional forms in mainland Normandy and the Channel Islands. A medieval literary and geographical work associated with Norman Sicily is the *Tabula Rogeriana*, written by al-Idrisi for King Roger II of Sicily and entitled *Kitab Rudjdjar* (“The Book of Roger”).

## Language & religion

The original Norse settlers adopted the Gallo-Romance language of the Frankish lands where they settled. Their Old Norman dialect developed into Norman, Normaund or Norman French, a regional Romance language still spoken in parts of mainland Normandy and the nearby Channel Islands through forms including Cotentinais, Cauchois, Jèrriais and Guernésiais. The Norman language was formed through the adoption of the indigenous langue d’oïl branch of Romance by a Norse-speaking ruling class. The settlers adopted Christianity and Catholicism, replacing Norse religion, while the Norman rulers of Sicily governed populations including Norman, Byzantine, Greek, Arab, Lombard and “native” Sicilian communities.
