## Overview

Vlach is a term and exonym used from the Middle Ages until the Modern Era for speakers of Eastern Romance languages living in Southeast Europe, south and north of the Danube. Today it refers primarily to speakers of Aromanian, Megleno-Romanian, and Istro-Romanian living south of the Danube, in Albania, Bulgaria, northern Greece, North Macedonia, and eastern Serbia. These communities include Aromanians, Megleno-Romanians, and Timok Vlachs. The term has also been applied to Moravian Vlachs, Morlachs, and Istro-Romanians. In the Balkans, it became associated with shepherds and, in some contexts, with rural Christians.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention Vlach settlements, villages, and mountain and forest habitation, including settlements called Kimbalongu, Exeva, and Ezeba.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe several historical religious and political contexts. The uprising of Asen and Peter began on 26 October 1185, the feast day of St. Demetrius of Thessaloniki. A Byzantine church document states that in 1190 the Cumans and the Vlachs carried the relics of Saint Ryli from Sofia to Tirnovo with great pomp.

## Foodways

The sources document cheese and meat products associated with Vlachs on the Chalcidice peninsula around 1000. In 1022, Vlach shepherds from Thessaly and the Pindus mountains provided cheese for Constantinople. The sources also describe herds, livestock, mountain pastures, and pastoral movement.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention the medieval Song of Roland, Nordic sagas, the Nibelungenlied, the Gesta Hungarorum, the Primary Chronicle, and the Chronicle of the Priest of Duklja in connection with names or references associated with Vlachs. The Nibelungenlied, written around the year 1200, includes Duke Ramunc of Wallachia and mentions the Vlachs. The Gesta Hungarorum, written in the early 13th century, describes Slavs, Bulgarians, Blachij, and shepherds of the Romans in Pannonia, though its interpretation and reliability are disputed.

## Language & religion

Vlach generally designates speakers of Eastern Romance languages. The source names Aromanian, Megleno-Romanian, and Istro-Romanian, and records the endonyms armãn, armãni, rãmãn, and rãmãni among Aromanians, while Megleno-Romanians use Vla and Vlaš. The term is etymologically connected with forms including Vlah, Valah, Valach, Voloh, Blac, Oláh, Vlas, Ilac, and Ulah. The sources describe Vlachs variously as Orthodox Christians, rural Christians, or Christians opposed to Muslims and Catholics, depending on historical context. In 1020, the Archdiocese of Ohrid was responsible for “the spiritual care of all the Vlachs.”
