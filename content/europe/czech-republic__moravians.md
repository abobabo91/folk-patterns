## Overview

Moravians are a Czech ethnographic group from the Moravia region of the Czech Republic. They speak Moravian dialects of Czech, Common Czech, or a mixed form of both, and are typically considered a subgroup of the Czech ethnic group. Part of the population identifies as ethnically Moravian. Smaller pockets of people declaring Moravian ethnicity are present in neighboring Slovakia. In the 2011 census, 6.01% of the Czech population declared Moravian as their ethnicity; the same source gives 630,897 people declaring Moravian nationality in that census. The strongest sense of patriotism towards Moravia is found in the environs of Brno, the former capital of Moravia. The sources describe Moravians as historically connected with the Moravia region, Western Slovakia, and surrounding parts of Central Europe.

## Festivals & rituals

The Wikipedia source does state that Moravian nationality was banned during the Czechoslovak and communist eras and appeared again in the 1991 census after the fall of the dangers of Germanization in 1945.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Moravians source mentions Czech Chronicles, stating that Czech historical tradition developed in Moravia during the Middle Ages and that Czech Chronicles was reread and distributed there. It also refers to the Annales Regni Francorum as the first known mention of the Moravians, in 822 AD.

## Language & religion

Moravians speak Moravian dialects of Czech, Common Czech, or a mixed form of both. The historical Moravian tribe was a West Slavic tribe, and its Old Slavic self-designation was Moravljane; the source also gives Slovak Moravania and Czech Moravané. Most Czech-speaking inhabitants of Moravia identify with Czech nationality, while some identify as Moravian. The sources describe substantial changes in declared nationality between the 1991, 2001, 2011, and 2021 censuses. They state that the percentage of people without religion was lowest in the Moravian Zlín Region, followed by the Vysočina Region, the South Moravian Region, the Moravian-Silesian Region, and the predominantly-Moravian Olomouc Region.
