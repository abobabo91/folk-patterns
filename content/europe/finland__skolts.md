## Overview

The Skolt Sámi, or Skolts, are a Sámi ethnic group traditionally associated with the borderland between present-day Finland, Russia and Norway, especially the Kola Peninsula and adjacent Fenno-Scandinavian mainland. They currently live in and around Sevettijärvi, Keväjärvi and Nellim in the municipality of Inari, in several places in Murmansk Oblast, and in Neiden in Sør-Varanger Municipality. Current estimates put their number at around 1250, of whom approximately 400 can speak Skolt Sámi. Most live in Finland. The sources describe them as belonging to the eastern group of Sámi because of their language and traditions. Their livelihood traditionally included reindeer husbandry, hunting and fishing, and they are traditionally Orthodox Christians.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe Skolt Christianity. Saint Tryphon of Pechenga converted the Skolts to Christianity in the 16th century, and the majority of Skolts remain members of the Eastern Orthodox Church.

## Foodways

The sources mention reindeer husbandry, hunting and fishing as traditional sources of livelihood.
