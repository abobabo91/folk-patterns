## Overview

Hungarians, also known as Magyars, are an ethnic group native to present-day Hungary and the Carpathian Basin. They share a common culture, language, history and ancestry, and have a notable presence in parts of the former Kingdom of Hungary. There are an estimated 14.5 million ethnic Hungarians and their descendants worldwide, including approximately 9.6 million in today’s Hungary. About 2 million live in areas that belonged to the Kingdom of Hungary before the Treaty of Trianon in 1920 and are now parts of neighbouring countries. Significant groups with Hungarian ancestry also live in the United States, Canada, Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Chile, Brazil, Australia and Argentina. The Hungarian language belongs to the Ugric branch of the Uralic language family.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources discuss Hungarian historical traditions and chronicles. Tradition holds that the Hungarians were organized in a confederacy of seven tribes: Jenő, Kér, Keszi, Kürt-Gyarmat, Megyer, Nyék and Tarján. The sources state that the Hungarian ethnonym *Magyar* possibly derived from the name of the prominent Hungarian tribe Megyer.

Medieval Hungarian chronicles from the Hungarian royal court, including *Gesta Hungarorum*, *Gesta Hunnorum et Hungarorum*, *Chronicon Pictum*, *Buda Chronicle* and *Chronica Hungarorum*, claimed that the Árpád dynasty and the Aba clan were descendants of Attila. A legend also developed in medieval Hungary, based on foreign and Hungarian medieval chronicles, that the Hungarians, and the Székely ethnic group in particular, were descended from the Huns.

Árpád is quoted in *Gesta Hungarorum* as saying that the land between the Danube and the Tisza belonged to his forefather, Attila.

## Language & religion

The Hungarian language belongs to the Ugric branch of the Uralic language family, alongside the Khanty and Mansi languages. The Hungarian people call themselves *Magyar* in their own language. The sources state that the Hungarian language is linguistically isolated in Europe, while Hungarians are genetically similar to surrounding populations.

The sources describe the conversion of Hungarian leaders to Christianity and state that Stephen I, also called Szent István or Saint Stephen, was crowned King of Hungary in 1001. After acceptance into Christian Europe under Stephen I, Hungary served as a bulwark against invasions from the east and south. During the Ottoman period, Christian youths were taken or kidnapped in the devşirme system, converted to Islam and drafted into Ottoman service; the Hungarian population experienced recruitment under the Ottoman regime.
