## Overview

The Boykos, also called Boikos or Highlanders, are an ethnolinguistic group of the Carpathian Mountains in Ukraine, Slovakia, Hungary, and Poland. They are considered a subgroup of Rusyns and speak a distinct East Slavic dialect. Within Ukraine, Boykos and other Rusyns are regarded as a subgroup of ethnic Ukrainians. Their historical region is known as the Boyko Region, or *Bojkowszczyzna* and *Бойківщина* (*Boikivshchyna*). Boykos differ from neighboring Lemkos and Hutsuls in dialect, dress, folk architecture, and customs. The available source states that up to 400,000 people lived in the Boyko Region, while evidence indicates that 230,000 people of Boyko origin lived there in 1970. According to the 2001 Ukraine census, 131 people identified themselves as Boykos.

### Clothing & dress

### Architecture

Boyko folk architecture is represented by distinctive wooden churches. The characteristic church of the Boyko region is a three-domed structure, with the domes arranged in one line and the middle dome slightly larger than the others.

## Language & religion

Boykos speak a distinct East Slavic dialect. They are considered a subgroup of Rusyns, while within Ukraine they and other Rusyns are regarded as a subgroup of ethnic Ukrainians. The source lists Rusyn, Ukrainian, Polish, and Slovak names associated with the group, including *бойки*, *бойки*, *Bojkowie*, and *Pujďáci*. Some people who are otherwise identifiable as Boykos regard the name Boyko as derogatory and call themselves Highlanders, *verkhovyntsi*. The name is generally considered to derive from the particle *boiie*, specifically the exclamation “бой!, бойє!”, meaning “it is really so!”. Most Boykos belong to the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church, with a minority belonging to the Ukrainian Orthodox Church.
