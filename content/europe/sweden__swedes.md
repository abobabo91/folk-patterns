## Overview

Swedes, or Swedish people, are an ethnic group native to Sweden who share ancestry, culture, history, and language. They mostly inhabit Sweden and have also migrated elsewhere, especially to the other Nordic countries. A substantial diaspora includes Swedish Americans in the United States and Canada. Swedish is a North Germanic language descended from Proto-Norse and Proto-Germanic. Finland-Swedes, who have Finnish national identity, form an officially recognized linguistic minority in Finland. The sources describe Swedish history through archaeology, written accounts, migration, Christianity, agriculture, trade, warfare, industrialization, democratic movements, and emigration.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention boat inhumation graves at Vendel and Valsgärde, tumuli at Gamla Uppsala, and the development of Birka as a trading port around 750 AD.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

Archaeological finds at Birka included graves, coins, jewelry, and other luxury items. The sources also mention iron production, mining districts, steel, machined parts, wood pulp, paper production, and mechanical engineering.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The sources mention jewelry among the archaeological finds at Birka and encrusted garnets on gilded-bronze saddle ornaments from graves of mounted warriors.

## Music & performance

 They mention runic inscriptions on artefacts and inscriptions commemorating Viking expeditions.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources mention that 6 June is now Sweden’s national holiday. They also describe Christianity becoming prevalent during the 11th century and Sweden being counted as a Christian nation from 1050, while noting that Christianity did not fully replace paganism until the 12th century.

## Foodways

The sources describe Sweden as retaining a nearly entirely agricultural economy during the 18th and 19th centuries. They mention potatoes as one factor associated with population increase, and state that farmers in Dalsland transported butter to mining districts, exchanged it for iron, and traded the iron for fish needed as food.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention Norse mythology, which presents a line of legendary and semi-legendary kings reaching back to the last centuries BC. They also describe semi-legendary Swedish-Geatish wars in the sixth century as recounted by Beowulf, and identify Snorri Sturluson as a writer who discussed the sixth-century Swedish king Adils.

## Language & religion

Swedish is a North Germanic language. The sources state that Proto-Germanic is thought to have originated with the arrival of the Battle Axe culture in Scandinavia, while the people of south Scandinavia spoke Proto-Norse during the Roman Period. Runic script was used among the south Scandinavian elite by at least the second century AD. Christianity was introduced in 829 according to the account of St. Ansgar, became prevalent during the 11th century, and gradually replaced paganism. Sweden later rejected Catholicism and entered the Protestant Reformation under Gustav Vasa.
