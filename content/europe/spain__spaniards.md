## Overview

Spaniards, or Spanish people, are an ethnic group and nation indigenous to Spain. Spain contains diverse national and regional identities, including Castilians, Aragonese, Catalans, Andalusians, Valencians, Balearics, Canarians, Basques, and Galicians. Spanish is a Romance language descended from Vulgar Latin, while Basque is the sole surviving indigenous language of Iberia and is not Indo-European. Spanish is the official state language, and the population of Spain is described as 46 million. Spaniards matter in folk-cultural terms because Spain’s history produced overlapping regional languages, identities, religious traditions, and cultural forms, while Spanish colonial rule carried the language and elements of Hispanic cultures across the Americas.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources used identify the Alhambra of Granada and the Cordoba Mosque as masterpieces of Islamic architecture and mention the Palmeral of Elche as a World Heritage Site.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe the Catholic religious landscape. Roman Catholicism is the largest denomination in Spain, although its share of the population has decreased; a 2013 study recorded 71% of Spaniards identifying as Catholics, 2% with another faith, and about 25% as atheists or without religion, while 2019 survey data recorded 69% Catholics, 2.8% another faith, and 27% atheist-agnostic-non-believers.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources identify *Don Quixote* as the most famous Spanish literary work and state that it was published during the Golden Age of the Spanish Empire.

## Language & religion

Spanish is a Romance language and the official state language. Languages spoken in Spain include Spanish, Catalan, Galician, and Basque; the sources also name Asturian, Aranese Gascon, Aragonese, and Leonese as languages with lower levels of official recognition. Spanish developed from Vulgar Latin introduced during Roman rule and contains significant Arabic influence in its vocabulary. Catalan, Galician, and Basque are spoken by regional populations, while Basque is a non-Indo-European language. Roman Catholicism is the largest denomination present in Spain, alongside other faiths and people identifying as atheists, agnostics, or non-believers.
