## Overview

Crimean Tatars (*qırımtatarlar*, *къырымтатарлар*), also called Crimeans (*qırımlılar*, *къырымлылар*), are a Turkic ethnic group and nation native to Crimea. Their ethnogenesis took place over centuries in Crimea and the northern regions along the Black Sea coast, combining Mediterranean populations with peoples of the Eurasian Steppe. Today they constitute approximately 15% of the Crimean population, with significant diaspora populations in Turkey, Romania, and Bulgaria. They are an indigenous people of Ukraine, officially recognized as such since 2014. Their history includes the Crimean Khanate, Russian annexation in 1783, Soviet deportation in May 1944, and policies enabling return from 1989.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources mention the Khan’s archives and libraries, which were famous throughout the Islamic world by the time of the first Russian invasion of Crimea in 1736. They also mention an 18th-century chronicle, ʿUmdat al-Aḫbār, which distinguished the Crimean Tatars from the Ottoman Turks and traced the ancestry of the Crimean ruling dynasty to Alan Qo’a and Genghis Khan.

## Language & religion

Crimean Tatar is a member of the Kipchak languages of the Turkic language family. It has three dialects, while the standard language is written in the central dialect. The Steppe dialect is associated with the Bulgar subgrouping of the Kipchak family and has been influenced by Nogai. The central dialect, associated with Bağçasaray, belongs to the Cuman subgroup of Kipchak and contains Turkish, Ukrainian, and Mariupol Greek elements. Yalıboyu is an Oghuz language descended from Ottoman Turkish. UNESCO ranked Crimean Tatar as severely endangered in 2010; the Institute of Oriental Studies described its threat as critically endangered in recent years. Islam became a state religion throughout the Peninsula by the end of the 15th century, and the majority of the Crimean Khanate’s population followed the Hanafi stream.
