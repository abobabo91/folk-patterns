## Overview

The Aromanians are a southern Balkan ethnic group speaking Aromanian, an Eastern Romance language from Vulgar Latin. They live in upland settlements and towns across Greece, recognized as a linguistic minority. Their material culture centers on hand-woven textiles featuring tapestry technique, geometric patterns, and metal-wrapped threads. They are predominantly Orthodox Christians with a written literary tradition dating to the 19th century.

## Material culture

Aromanian material culture is dominated by hand-spun, hand-woven wool textiles with geometric patterns, metal-thread embellishment, and tapestry technique.

### Textile & pattern traditions

Aromanian textiles use tapestry weaving, geometric motifs, and metal-wrapped threads in wool.

- **Tapestry-woven aprons** (*apron*) — Aprons built from two equal loom-widths of wool joined along a selvedge, woven in tapestry technique with elongated diamonds and linked zigzags in dark red or dark blue.
- **Plain-ground wool aprons** (*apron*) — A near-square apron of deep yellow hand-spun, hand-woven wool with a single band of white geometric ornament near the base.
- **Patterned knitted socks** (*socks*) — Knee-length women's socks knitted in bands of white, red, blue and black wool with gold-coloured metal-wrapped thread.
- **Panelled fulled-wool outer garments** (*coat or waistcoat*) — Sleeveless coats and waistcoats cut from heavily fulled twill wool and assembled from many panels with dense couched wool braiding at necklines and hems.
- **Beaded bib** (*plastron*) — A rectangle of natural cream twill cloth decorated with a V-shape of transparent, blue, green and red glass beads, gold and black faceted beads, and silver-coloured sequins.

### Clothing & dress

Women's festive attire is layered with a chemise, beaded plastron, tapestry apron, patterned socks and panelled coat, while men traditionally wear the fustanella.

- **Women's chemise** (*chemise*) — A calf-length long-sleeved garment of beige tabby cotton muslin with natural cream seersucker stripes, opening vertically at the centre front.
- **Opanci shoes** (*opanci*) — Shoes of sewn and plaited dark brown, tan and red-brown leather with a one-piece sole-and-toe, black straps across the front and a deep red strap with a metal buckle.
- **Fustanella** — The ethnic costume for men, whose use among Aromanians can be traced to at least the 15th century and appears on the Aromanian stećak of the Radimlja necropolis.

### Architecture

The sources document nothing about Aromanian house form, building materials or architectural decoration.

### Ceramics, metalwork & everyday objects

No Aromanian ceramic or metalworking tradition is documented; everyday objects are textile, with metal entering primarily through costume.

- **Striped container** — A long narrow striped container cut down from a full-sized shepherd's bag with a plaited wool carrying string.
- **Household napkin** (*napkin*) — A single-loom-width napkin of undyed sheep's wool with narrow stripes of supplementary weft in red and dark blue, hemmed and finished with blanket chain stitching.

### Jewelry & body adornment

The shape of jewels could mark cultural affiliation and village origin, though no jewelry types, materials or ritual functions are documented.

- **Beaded plastron ornament** (*plastron*) — Glass, faceted and tubular beads, sequins and rickrack massed in a V-shape on the festive plastron and repeated along its velvet neckband.

## Music & performance

Polyphonic music is common among Aromanians, and the Metsovo festivals are the principal recurring cultural events.

- **Metsovo festivals** (*festival*) — The largest Aromanian cultural gatherings in the world, held at Metsovo and regularly drawing over 40,000 participants.

## Festivals & rituals

Aromanians follow the Eastern Orthodox liturgical calendar and observe Aromanian National Day, though Greek Aromanians commemorate the Greek Struggle for Independence instead.

- **Aromanian National Day** (*Dzua Natsionalã a Armãnjilor*) — Celebrated on 23 May 1905, marking the announcement of the Ottoman irade recognizing Aromanians as the Ullah millet, adopted by Aromanians in Albania, Australia, Bulgaria and North Macedonia.
- **Balkan Romanianness Day** (*observance*) — Marked on 10 May in Romania, extending to Megleno-Romanians and Istro-Romanians as well as Aromanians.

## Foodways

Aromanian cuisine is strongly influenced by Mediterranean and Middle Eastern cuisine, though specific staples and dishes are not documented.

## Oral tradition & literature

An Aromanian literary tradition exists from the 19th century, beginning with works using the Greek alphabet and developing through linguistic and educational texts.

- **Romanische oder Macedonowlachische Sprachlehre** (*grammar*) — A grammar printed in Vienna in 1813 by Mihail G. Boiagi, called by many scholars the most valuable for translating the ethnic name.
- **Die Aromunen** (*work*) — Published in 1894 by Gustav Weigand, the work in which the term Aromanian was coined.

## Language & religion

Aromanian is an Eastern Romance language with many Greek, Slavic and Turkish loanwords, divided into the Rrãmãnji and Armãnji groups, and speakers are predominantly Orthodox Christians.

- **Eastern Romance language** (*Aromanian*) — Very similar to Romanian, it developed from Common Romanian and the Vulgar Latin spoken by Paleo-Balkan peoples.
- **Rrãmãnji speakers** (*Rrãmãnji*) — One main group of Aromanian speakers, divided into Muzãchiars, Fãrshãrots, and Moscopolitans.
- **Armãnji speakers** (*Armãnji*) — One main group of Aromanian speakers, divided into Pindeans of the Pindus and Gramustians of the Gramos mountains.

## Glossary

- *Armãnji* — One main group of Aromanian speakers, including Pindeans and Gramustians
- *Rrãmãnji* — One main group of Aromanian speakers, including Muzãchiars, Fãrshãrots, and Moscopolitans
- *Vlachs* — Medieval exonym for Aromanians, still used widely
- *opanci* — Sewn leather shoes with a one-piece sole-and-toe and metal buckle
- *fustanella* — Traditional male ethnic costume, traceable to at least the 15th century
- *plastron* — Beaded bib worn at the throat with beads, sequins and rickrack decoration
- *stećak* — Medieval tombstone, as seen at Radimlja necropolis
- *tabby* — Plain weave textile structure
- *Dzua Natsionalã a Armãnjilor* — Aromanian National Day, celebrated on 23 May
- *Ullah millet* — Ottoman administrative designation for Aromanians recognized in 1905
- *Common Romanian* — Shared stage of Eastern Romance varieties that evolved from Vulgar Latin
- *Eastern Romance* — Language family including Aromanian, Romanian, Megleno-Romanian and Istro-Romanian
- *Vulgar Latin* — Common Latin spoken by ordinary people in the Romanized Balkans
