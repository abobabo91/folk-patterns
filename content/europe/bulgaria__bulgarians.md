## Overview

Bulgarians are a South Slavic ethnic group and nation native to Bulgaria and its neighbouring regions. They share a common culture, ancestry, history, and the Bulgarian language. Bulgarians form the majority of the population in Bulgaria and exist as historical communities in North Macedonia, Ukraine, Moldova, Serbia, Albania, Romania, Hungary and Greece. Most Bulgarians live in Bulgaria, where they number around 6 million and constitute 85% of the population. The total number of Bulgarians stood at over 9 million. Their historical formation involved Thracians, Early Slavs, Bulgars, Romans, ancient Greeks and other peoples who settled or lived on the Balkans. Bulgaria became a cultural centre of Slavic Europe after the adoption of Orthodox Christianity in 864 and the development of Cyrillic literacy.

## Festivals & rituals

 They do describe major historical and religious developments. The Bulgarians adopted Orthodox Christianity in 864, and most Bulgarians are at least nominally members of the Bulgarian Orthodox Church, founded in 870 and autocephalous since 927. The church was abolished during Ottoman rule, revived as the Bulgarian Exarchate in 1873, and later raised again to the Bulgarian Patriarchate. The National awakening of Bulgaria was initiated in 1762 under the influence of the Enlightenment in Western Europe. The April Uprising broke out in 1876 and led, through the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878, to the foundation of the third Bulgarian state after the Treaty of San Stefano.

## Oral tradition & literature

Bulgarian literary and cultural activity developed through the literary schools of Preslav and Ohrid. In the first half of the 10th century, the Cyrillic script was devised in the Preslav Literary School, Bulgaria, based on the Glagolitic, Greek and Latin alphabets. Authors associated with the literary schools included Constantine of Preslav, John Exarch, Chernorizets Hrabar, Clement and Naum of Ohrid. Bulgaria exerted similar influence on neighbouring countries in the mid- to late 14th century through the Tarnovo Literary School and the work of Patriarch Evtimiy, Gregory Tsamblak and Constantine of Kostenets.

## Language & religion

Bulgarian is a South Slavic language mutually intelligible with Macedonian and, to a lesser degree, with Serbo-Croatian, especially its eastern dialects. It was influenced lexically by medieval and modern Greek and Turkish, and later borrowed words from German, French and English. Bulgarian is written in the Cyrillic script. In the first half of the 10th century, Cyrillic was devised in the Preslav Literary School. Bulgarian demonstrates linguistic developments shared with Romanian, Albanian and Greek, and Bulgarian and Macedonian have lexical similarities of 86%.

Most Bulgarians are at least nominally members of the Bulgarian Orthodox Church. In 2021, the church had a total of 4,219,270 members in Bulgaria, or 71.5% of the population. In the 2021 Bulgarian census, about 85% of Bulgaria’s population identified themselves as ethnic Bulgarians; the rest were mostly Turks and Roma. The sources also describe partial or complete Islamisation among some Bulgarians under Ottoman rule and the existence of Bulgarian Catholic communities.
