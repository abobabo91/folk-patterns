## Overview

The Arbanasi are an ethnic community of Albanian origin living in and around Zadar in northern Dalmatia, Croatia. They traditionally speak Arbanasi Albanian, a Gheg Albanian dialect whose speakers originated in the region of Kraja, now in modern Montenegro, and moved to Croatia during the early eighteenth century. Their original settlements included Arbanasi of Zadar and villages around Zadar, including Zemunik, Gračac, Dračevac, Crno and Ploča. About 4000 Arbanasi remain in Croatia, while fewer than 200 people are competent in Arbanasi Albanian and approximately 500 understand it to some degree. The community is significant for the preservation of a distinctive Albanian dialect shaped by Croatian, Italian, Venetian and Chakavian contact, as well as oral traditions, folktales, customs and songs.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The Arbanasi settled on the outskirts of Zadar on lands provided by the Venetian landowner Erizzo. The Venetian government took charge of constructing many homes and initially supplied meals. Their settlement, called *Arbënesh* by its inhabitants, Borgo Erizzo by Italians and Varoš Eričina by Croats, later became known in local languages as Arbanasi. The community first worked to reclaim marshy areas near the settlement, which had originally been an island and was later connected to the mainland, and obtained the right to cultivate the land. Saint Mary of Loreto was built from 1734 and founded in 1737.

## Music & performance

The Arbanasi preserved customs and songs after their settlement in Venetian Dalmatia. Their language was transmitted through oral traditions, including the recitation of folktales at social gatherings. The sources name no Arbanasi musical instruments, song genres, performance repertories or specialized musical occasions. The notable people listed in the sources include composers, singers, a tenor singer, a music conductor and a cellist.

## Festivals & rituals

The community’s documented religious history includes Catholic origins: the ancestors of the Arbanasi were Catholic Albanians who left villages in the Skadarska Krajina region, and Saint Mary of Loreto was built from 1734 and founded in 1737.

## Foodways

During the initial settlement of the Arbanasi, the Venetian government took charge of construction of many homes and, at first, even meals.

## Oral tradition & literature

Oral tradition played an important role in the transmission of Arbanasi Albanian. At social gatherings, women recited folk stories, helping maintain the language across generations. The language also survives in collections of Arbanasi lore and in a few publications, including the journal *Feja*. The Arbanasi dialect has been represented in a linguistic example attributed to At Justin Rrota and Italian philologist Tagliavini; the example tells of a very old donkey who could no longer work, planned retaliation by his owner and an encounter with a dog. In 1974, Aleksandar Stipčević published *Gli Illyri*, a work translated into English, Italian and Albanian.

## Language & religion

Arbanasi Albanian is a Gheg Albanian dialect, also known natively as *Arbëneshë*. Its speakers originated from Kraja and moved to Croatia in the early eighteenth century. The dialect preserves archaic features of Medieval Gheg Albanian and has influences from Croatian, Italian and Venetian. It has an alveolar tap as its only rhotic, while Albanian palatals have merged into affricates; Gheg nasal vowels have been denasalized, and /h/ is deleted wherever it occurs. Arbanasi historically spoke Albanian, Croatian and Venetian, while later speakers were usually bilingual in Croatian. The language was taught in some schools in 1896 and 1901, tolerated and then banned during the interwar period, and not taught by the school system in communist Yugoslavia. Standard modern Albanian was introduced as an optional language class in a Zadar high school in 2016. The ancestors of the Arbanasi were Catholic Albanians.
