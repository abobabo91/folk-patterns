## Overview

The Nekrasov Cossacks, also called Nekrasovite Cossacks, Nekrasovites, or Nekrasovtsy, descend from Don Cossacks who fled to the Kuban in September 1708 after the defeat of the Bulavin Rebellion of 1707–1708. They were led by Ignat Nekrasov, from whom their name derives. At that time, the Crimean Khanate ruled the Kuban. Other fugitives from the Don and runaway Russian serfs later joined them. Their settlements included the right bank of the Bolshaya Laba River, near its mouth, and the Taman Peninsula. The sources describe their Don Cossack language, songs, and ethnic identity.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention three townlets (*gorodoks*) on the Taman Peninsula: Bludilovsky, Golubinsky, and Chiryansky.

## Music & performance

The Nekrasov Cossacks preserved Don Cossack songs despite their separation from other Cossacks for more than 200 years. They state that there is ongoing work to preserve the heritage of the Nekrasov Cossacks.

## Festivals & rituals

They identify the Nekrasovites as Old Believers and state that they were persecuted by Orthodox Russian authorities.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that the Nekrasov Cossacks preserved Don Cossack songs and their ethnic identity. The sources also mention ongoing work for preserving the heritage of Nekrasov Cossacks.

## Language & religion

The Nekrasov Cossacks preserved the Don Cossack language.
