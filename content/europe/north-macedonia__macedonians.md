## Overview

Macedonians are a South Slavic ethnic group native to Macedonia in Southeast Europe. They speak Macedonian, a South Slavic language, and the large majority identify as Eastern Orthodox Christians. About two-thirds of ethnic Macedonians live in North Macedonia, with other communities in several countries. The region associated with them includes the Vardar basin, the Pelagonian plain, Ohrid–Prespa, Skopje, Bitola, Ohrid, Veles, and Prilep. Macedonian identity emerged comparatively recently, with early expressions appearing during the second half of the 19th century among Slavic-speaking intellectuals. It was consolidated through developments in language, literature, religious institutions, and national historiography during and after the 20th century.

## Music & performance

The sources identify Macedonian as a South Slavic language and mention Slavonic liturgy. In the 860s, Byzantine missionaries Cyril and Methodius created the Glagolitic alphabet and Slavonic liturgy based on the Slavic dialect around Thessaloniki for a mission to Great Moravia. After the mission ended, their students brought the Glagolitic alphabet to the Bulgarian Empire. The Preslav Literary School and Ohrid Literary School taught Slavonic liturgy and the Glagolitic and subsequently Cyrillic alphabet.

## Festivals & rituals

They do document the religious and institutional history of Macedonian Orthodox Christians. In the Ottoman period, Eastern Orthodox Christians belonged to the Rum Millet under Graeco-Byzantine jurisdiction. In 1870, the Bulgarian Exarchate was created, and in 1874 the Christian populations of the bishoprics of Skopje and Ohrid participated in plebiscites concerning association with the Exarchate.

## Foodways

They mention thriving economies based on mining in ancient Paeonia and the movement of various populations through Macedonia, but they provide no ethnographic account of foodways.

## Oral tradition & literature

Macedonian literary development is connected in the sources with the emergence of a separate Macedonian language and national literature. Gjorgjija Pulevski published in Belgrade a *Dictionary of Three languages: Macedonian, Albanian, Turkish* in 1875 and in Sofia a *Grammar of the language of the Slavic Macedonian population* in 1880. Krste Misirkov published *On Macedonian Matters* in Sofia in 1903, arguing that the dialect of central Macedonia should form the basis of a standard Macedonian literary language. The Macedonian Literary Society, established in Saint Petersburg in 1902, published *Vardar* in 1905, described as the first scholarly, scientific, and literary journal in the central dialects of Macedonia. In 1913–1914, Dimitrija Čupovski published the newspaper *Македонскi Голосъ* (*Macedonian Voice*).

## Language & religion

Macedonian is a South Slavic language. The South Slavic languages from which Macedonian developed are described as having expanded in the region during the post-Roman period, although the exact mechanisms remain debated. In the 860s, Cyril and Methodius created the Glagolitic alphabet and Slavonic liturgy based on the Slavic dialect around Thessaloniki. The Ohrid Literary School later taught Slavonic liturgy and the Glagolitic and Cyrillic alphabets. The large majority of Macedonians identify as Eastern Orthodox Christians and share an “Orthodox Byzantine–Slavic heritage” with their neighbours. The sources describe efforts to establish a separate Macedonian Orthodox Church, including Theodosius of Skopje’s attempt in 1890 to restore the Archbishopric of Ohrid as a separate Macedonian Orthodox Church.
