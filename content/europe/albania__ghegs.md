## Overview

The Ghegs, also spelled Gegs, are one of the two main dialectal subgroups of Albanians, alongside the Tosks. They predominantly live north of the Shkumbin river and in the mountainous north of Albania, as well as in Kosovo, North Macedonia, Serbia and Montenegro. Little more than half of ethnic Albanians from Albania are Ghegs. They speak Gheg Albanian, one of the two main dialects of the Albanian language. Their social organization was traditionally tribal, with distinct tribal groups, clan loyalties, dispersed fortified homesteads, traditional laws, hospitality and blood feud. Their culture is also associated with epic poetry and with a historical pattern of autonomy from regular Ottoman civil administration.

## Material culture

### Architecture

Gheg settlement traditionally consisted of separate, scattered, mostly fortified homesteads. The sources also name *kula* as a type of tower house whose defense system Gheg communities sought to preserve during revolts under the Young Turk government. No further information on building materials, roofs, decoration, or internal arrangements is provided.

## Festivals & rituals

They describe several social and life-cycle practices: marriage was arranged as an economic and political agreement among members of the tribe; girls were married without their consent; bride stealing existed to some extent until the early 20th century; sworn virginity was occasionally practised; and child betrothal sometimes occurred before birth.

## Foodways

They state that the land belonged to the clan and that Gheg families were traditionally extended, but give no further information about food production or domestic meals.

## Oral tradition & literature

The Ghegs are known for their epic poetry. The sources also describe the development of Albanian-language literature in the late Ottoman period: after the Tanzimat reforms in the second half of the 19th century, Austria-Hungary, with Ottoman approval, opened and financed schools in the Albanian language, Franciscan seminaries and hospitals, and trained native clergy. The culture of the Ghegs blossomed at the beginning of the 20th century, with Gjergj Fishta and the Scutarine Catholic School of Letters led by Fishta contributing significantly to this development. No specific epic, folktale, proverb, riddle, or storytelling form is named.

## Language & religion

The Ghegs speak Gheg Albanian, one of the two main dialects of Albanian. The Albanian communist regime based standard Albanian mostly on Tosk Albanian, a policy criticized by Arshi Pipa. The source states that language is the main criterion for Albanian self-identification and that the change in literary language had significant political and cultural consequences. Today, the majority of Ghegs are Sunni Muslims, with a large Catholic minority. There are also Ghegs who practise Orthodox Christianity, mainly in the southwest of the Gheg-speaking region, and some who practise Bektashism in areas such as Kruja and Bulqiza. Many people do not identify with any faith or usually attend religious services.
