## Overview

The name *sum* appears in medieval Russian chronicles in connection with the people now identified in the source as Finns proper. Finns proper are a historic people and a modern subgroup of the Finnish people. They live in the areas of the historical province of Finland Proper and Satakunta, and speak the southwestern dialects of Finnish. The names Finland and Suomi originally referred only to the southwestern region inhabited by Finns proper before expanding to the whole area of modern Finland.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention a town called Lyuderev and identify the Polna River with the Aurajoki.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources state that the name Finn meant Sami people, especially in older Norse sagas. They also discuss the appearance of the name sum in the Novgorod First Chronicle and the Sofia First Chronicle.

## Language & religion

Finns proper speak the southwestern dialects of Finnish. The sources do not classify Finnish within a language family or describe scripts, religious traditions, or spiritual practices. They state that Finns proper had strong connections to Scandinavia throughout their history. The Russian name sum, derived from Suomi, appears in three entries of the Novgorod First Chronicle and the Sofia First Chronicle, and the name later disappeared from Russian after Finland was incorporated into the Swedish realm.
