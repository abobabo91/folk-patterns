## Overview

The Danish minority in Southern Schleswig, Germany, has existed by this name since 1920, when the Schleswig Plebiscite divided Schleswig between Northern Schleswig, which was united with Denmark, and Southern Schleswig, which remained part of Germany. The minority’s historic roots are connected with Danish settlement after the emigration of the Angles. The first ethnic Danes settled in Southern Schleswig in the 7th century, and Hedeby was founded around the year 800. The group commonly describes itself as *danske sydslesvigere*, or Danish South Schleswigians; the German name is *Dänische Südschleswiger*. Official sources give its present size as 50,000 members. Danish schools and organizations operate in Flensborg and throughout the wider region, alongside cultural, sporting, religious, educational and political institutions.

## Material culture

### Architecture

The sources mention the Danevirke, a Danish border wall between Hollingstedt and the Eckernförde bay.

## Music & performance

They mention cultural and sports clubs, churches, libraries, a museum, scout groups and a Danish daily newspaper.

## Festivals & rituals

They record the Schleswig Plebiscite in 1920, the Copenhagen-Bonn Declarations in 1955 and the political and organizational history of the minority, but these sources do not present those events as festivals or rituals.

## Language & religion

Old Danish was spoken north of a line between the Eider, Treene and Eckernförde Bay, while a language shift occurred in the 17th, 18th and up to the 19th centuries from Danish and North Frisian dialects to Low German and later High German as common speech in Southern Schleswig. The sources state that Danish was still spoken north of a line between Schleswig and Husum up to around 1800, after which the line shifted northward. Between 8,000 and 10,000 members speak Danish every day, and between 10,000 and 20,000 have Danish as their mother tongue. The sources mention the Danish Church in Southern Schleswig.
