## Overview

Pomaks are Bulgarian-speaking Muslims inhabiting Bulgaria, northwestern Turkey, and northeastern Greece. In Bulgaria, the government officially recognizes the approximately 220,000-strong ethno-confessional minority as Bulgarian Muslims, although no ethnic Pomaks are recorded officially. They mainly inhabit the Rhodope Mountains, especially Smolyan Province, Kardzhali Province, Pazardzhik Province, and Blagoevgrad Province. Other communities live in Turkey, particularly Eastern Thrace, and in Greece, especially the eastern regional units of Xanthi, Rhodope, and Evros. The term has also been applied to Slavic Muslim populations in North Macedonia, Albania, and Kosovo. Pomak identity is diverse: members identify as Bulgarian, Pomak, ethnic Muslims, Turkish, and other identities.

## Festivals & rituals

The sources describe religious conversion and several historical ceremonies rather than an annual festival calendar. According to verbal tradition, a large ceremony of mass circumcision took place near the old mosque of Philippoupolis, and according to another account Grand Vizier Köprülü Mehmed Pasha threatened the Bulgarians of Chepino Valley with execution if they did not turn to Islam. The sources also record Islamic revolts in the Eastern Rhodopes and Western Thrace during the Balkan Wars: an Islamic revolt began on 16 August 1913, and the “Provisional Government of Western Thrace” was established in Komotini on 1 September 1913. No Pomak birth, wedding, funeral, seasonal, or annual festival rites are described.

## Oral tradition & literature

The sources refer to verbal traditions concerning the conversion of Bulgarian villagers in Philippoupolis and the Chepino Valley. They also mention that the historical sincerity of converts has been treated as a subject of suspicion and interrogation, and that explanations of mass conversions in the 17th century include the tenfold increase of the Jizya tax.

## Language & religion

Bulgaria recognizes Pomak speech as a Bulgarian dialect, while in Greece and Turkey members of the community self-declare their language as the Pomak language. Within Bulgaria, Pomaks speak dialects also spoken by Christian Bulgarians, including Eastern Bulgarian dialects, the Rup dialects in Southern Bulgaria, and the Balkan dialects in Northern Bulgaria. In the Bulgarian Rhodopes, named varieties include the Smolyan, Chepino, Hvoyna, Zlatograd, Babyak, and Gotse Delchev subdialects. In Greece, the Rup dialects of Western Thrace are called Pomak language, or *Pomaktsou*, and are taught at primary-school level using the Greek alphabet. The sources describe Islam as central to Pomak history: conversion occurred gradually from the Ottoman occupation in the early 15th century to the end of the 18th century. Pomaks subsequently became part of the Muslim community of the millet system. The sources also describe forced-assimilation campaigns in Bulgaria in 1912, the 1940s, and the 1960s and 1970s, including changes from Turkish-Arabic names to ethnic Bulgarian Christian Orthodox ones and, during the first campaign, conversions from Islam to Eastern Orthodoxy.
