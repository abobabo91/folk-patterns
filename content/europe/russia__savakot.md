## Overview

The Savakot were one of the two main subgroups of Ingrian Finns; the other was the Äyrämöiset. Their singular name is *Savakko*, and they are also known in Russian as Савакоты. They descended from Finnish, specifically Savonian, peasants who migrated from Savonia in Eastern Finland to Swedish Ingria, now part of Russia, during the 17th century. In the middle of the 19th century, Peter von Köppen recorded 43,000 Savakot on the Karelian Isthmus. In 1929, about 115,000 “Leningrad Finns” lived in Leningrad Oblast, including both Savakot and Äyrämöiset but excluding “Finland Finns.” Their self-identification later disappeared.

## Foodways

The sources identify agriculture as the major occupation of the Savakot. Shoreside populations engaged in fishing, and a small part of the population engaged in logging.

## Language & religion

The sources identify the Savakot as a subgroup of Ingrian Finns descended from Finnish, specifically Savonian, peasants. They do not specify a language family, dialects, historical scripts, religious landscape, or spiritual practices. In 1929, the literacy level of the “Leningrad Finns” was 72%.
